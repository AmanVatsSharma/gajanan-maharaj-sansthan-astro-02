# Welcome to Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

The Shegaon Junction signal changes from green to red just as the Samadhi Mandir dome comes into view over the flat Vidarbha horizon. For lakhs of devotees arriving by train each year, that first sight of the white marble dome rising above the surrounding town is the moment the yatra becomes real — not an idea on a screen or a note in a calendar, but a destination that was already waiting for you. Everything that follows, the darshan, the prasad, the quiet hours at Anand Sagar, builds on that initial feeling of arrival.

If you are reading this before your trip, you are already ahead of the planning curve. This guide covers everything a first-time visitor to Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan needs: what to expect when you step off the train at Shegaon, how the Mandir complex is laid out, how darshan flows from entry to prasad, what Anand Sagar offers beyond the Mandir, how the Bhakta Niwas accommodation works, and the essential history that gives every step of your visit deeper meaning. Bookmark it, share it with your travel group, and return to it once you are back home.

## Arriving at Shegaon

Shegaon is a small but spiritually dense town in the Buldhana district of Maharashtra, sitting at approximately 20.7934 degrees North and 76.6992 degrees East. It is well connected by rail and road. The Shegaon Junction railway station (code: SEG) on the Bhusawal–Nagpur broad-gauge line receives express trains from Mumbai, Nagpur, Pune, Bhopal, and Delhi. Khamgaon is 40km away, Akola about 100km, Nagpur roughly 300km, and Mumbai and Pune each approximately 570km. Aurangabad sits about 220km to the west.

The Sansthan maintains a visible volunteer presence at the station during festival periods, guiding devotees toward the Mandir or the Bhakta Niwas booking counter. Outside festival windows, the station is quieter and you will want to have your accommodation sorted before you arrive. The temple complex has designated parking that fills quickly during utsavs, so arriving before 8am on a festival morning makes a real difference. MSRTC buses connect Shegaon with Nagpur, Khamgaon, Akola, and Buldhana on regular schedules.

## The Samadhi Mandir: Layout and Darshan Flow

The heart of Shegaon is the Samadhi Mandir, the shrine where Shri Gajanan Maharaj took Mahasamadhi on 8 September 1910, the day of Rishi Panchami. Understanding the layout before you enter helps you move through darshan smoothly.

Your approach typically begins at the Pragat Kadamb tree, the very spot where Shri Gajanan Maharaj made his first appearance in February 1878. The tree is ancient, heavily revered, and devotees pause here for a few moments of quiet before joining the queue. Many offer a small prayer or simply stand in silence, absorbing the significance of the place where everything began.

From the Kadamb tree, the path opens into the main mandir hall. The Samadhi sits at the centre, and the darshan queue moves in an organized single file managed by Sansthan volunteers. On normal days the wait ranges from a few minutes to about half an hour. On festival days like Pragat Din or Punyatithi, the queues extend much further, and the Sansthan uses a token-based entry system. The [Pragat Din Darshan Planning guide](/blog/pragat-din-darshan-planning) covers the specific schedule.

Near the main mandir, you will find the samadhi of Dasganu Maharaj, the beloved disciple who composed the Gajanan Vijay Granth — 21 adhyays in Ovi meter. The entire mandir complex follows a clockwise circumambulation. Following that direction keeps the crowd flow smooth. After darshan, the exit path leads through the prasad distribution counters. Mahaprasad is offered free of cost at the Sansthan's Annakshetra, typically 12pm to 2pm and 7pm to 9pm. The prasad experience is itself a form of seva.

## Anand Sagar: The Complex Beyond the Mandir

Anand Sagar is Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan's sprawling spiritual and recreational complex, located roughly 8 to 9km from the main Samadhi Mandir. The Sansthan operates a dedicated shuttle bus service between the Mandir and Anand Sagar at regular intervals, making the journey convenient even for elderly devotees and families with small children. The drive takes 15 to 20 minutes.

The complex opens from 10am to 5pm daily, and entry is priced at approximately 30 to 50 rupees per person — an intentionally accessible rate that keeps the experience open to everyone. Inside, you will find a landscaped garden with seasonal flowers, a water park area for children, a quiet meditation hall, and several sculptural installations depicting scenes from the Gajanan Vijay Granth. The meditation area is one of the quieter, more reflective spaces in the entire region, and it is worth setting aside at least an hour for it. Many devotees find that the stillness deepens the darshan experience in a way that the bustling Mandir simply cannot.

## Bhakta Niwas: Your Home During the Stay

The Sansthan operates the Bhakta Niwas accommodation network for pilgrims who want to stay overnight or for the full duration of a festival. The room inventory covers shared dormitory halls for solo travelers, two-bed and three-bed rooms for small families, four-bed configurations for larger groups, and AC and non-AC variants at every tier. Rates are kept deliberately modest — two-bed non-AC at 1,250 rupees per night, four-bed AC at 2,550 rupees, deluxe and luxury suites going up to 3,150 and 4,150 rupees respectively. Every room type includes bedding, towels, and morning tea, and the all-inclusive pricing means no hidden charges at check-out.

Check-in operates on a flexible 24-hour cycle from the time of arrival, and check-out is likewise 24 hours from check-in. This arrangement means arriving devotees are not penalized for early morning trains or late-night buses. Aadhaar card or Voter ID is mandatory at check-in for every adult. Bookings are handled through the [Booking page](/booking) or via WhatsApp by sending your travel dates, group size, room preference, and ID details. The [Phone and WhatsApp Booking Best Practices](/blog/phone-and-whatsapp-booking-best-practices) explains exactly what information to include so the Sansthan team can confirm your reservation without delays.

## The History That Shapes Every Visit

Shri Gajanan Maharaj appeared to the people of Shegaon in February 1878, and his teachings of surrender, simplicity, and seva drew a following that has only grown in the century since. He took Mahasamadhi on 8 September 1910, on the day of Rishi Panchami. The Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan was formally established as a registered trust in 1908, two years before the Mahasamadhi, to manage the shrine and the growing devotional infrastructure around it — one of the older religious trusts in Vidarbha.

The Gajanan Vijay Granth, composed by Shri Dasganu Maharaj, remains the definitive spiritual text of the tradition. Its 21 adhyays in Ovi meter recount the saint's life from his appearance at Shegaon through his final days and the miracles that followed. Reading even a few chapters before your visit deepens the darshan experience considerably, and printed copies are available at the Mandir book stall along with Hindi and English translations.

Today the Sansthan manages the Samadhi Mandir, the Bhakta Niwas network, Anand Sagar, the goshala, Anna Daan services, and an annual festival calendar that includes Pragat Din in February, Punyatithi Aradhana in September, Chaitra Navratri, Ganesh Chaturthi, and the weekly Dindi processions. The [Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan Complete Guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-sansthan-complete-guide) covers these in more depth.

## What to Pack and How to Prepare

Dress modestly and comfortably. Cotton clothing works well in Shegaon's Vidarbha climate, especially between October and March. Remove leather items like belts, wallets, and watch straps before entering the Mandir. Footwear is left at the designated shoe racks near the entrance, so wear easily removable shoes or sandals. Carry a small scarf or shawl, a water bottle for the queue, and a cap and sunscreen if you are visiting during summer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best time of year to visit Shegaon?**
The most popular yatra season is October through March, when daytime temperatures are comfortable and the post-monsoon landscape is at its greenest. April through June brings very hot weather that makes extended queuing physically demanding. The monsoon months see lighter crowds but occasional heavy rainfall. Pragat Din in February and Punyatithi in September are the two largest festivals.

**How far is Anand Sagar from the main Mandir?**
Anand Sagar is approximately 8 to 9km from the Samadhi Mandir. The Sansthan operates a shuttle bus service throughout the day, with the journey taking roughly 15 to 20 minutes. The complex is open from 10am to 5pm daily, entry around 30 to 50 rupees per person.

**Do I need to book Bhakta Niwas in advance?**
Yes, especially during festival periods when demand significantly exceeds available rooms. For normal days, booking 2 to 3 days in advance is usually sufficient. For major festivals, book at least 2 to 4 weeks ahead through the [Booking page](/booking) or WhatsApp.

**Is there a dress code for entering the Mandir?**
Modest clothing is expected. Avoid shorts, sleeveless tops, and any leather items including belts and wallets. Men are advised to wear a shirt with trousers or a dhoti; women should wear a saree, salwar kameez, or a long skirt with a blouse. A scarf is useful to have on hand.

**Are there food options inside the Mandir complex?**
Mahaprasad is served free at the Annakshetra during two daily windows, typically 12pm to 2pm and 7pm to 9pm. The Sansthan canteen also serves subsidized snacks and beverages throughout the day. Drinking water is available at multiple points within the complex.

**Can I visit Shegaon as a solo female traveler?**
Shegaon is generally safe for solo female travelers. The Bhakta Niwas dormitory facilities are well-supervised by Sansthan staff. Stick to official booking channels, share your itinerary with family back home, and use the Sansthan's official WhatsApp number for on-ground assistance.

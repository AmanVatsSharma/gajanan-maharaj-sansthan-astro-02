There is something about Hanuman Jayanti at Shegaon that feels different from the other festivals. The crowd is smaller than Pragat Din, the programmes are shorter than Maha Shivaratri's all-night vigil, and yet the day has a warmth and accessibility that makes it one of the most genuinely enjoyed festivals at the Sansthan. Lord Hanuman is revered across Hindu traditions as the embodiment of devotion, strength, and selfless service, and at the Sansthan — where the spirit of seva (service) runs through every aspect of the institution — his birthday is celebrated with a sincerity that children respond to immediately. Many families arrive with young children specifically for Hanuman Jayanti, drawn by the Ramayana recitations, the bhajans, and the open courtyard where children can move around freely between darshan sessions. Understanding the crowd dynamics, the timing of the main events, and the practicalities of accommodation helps you make the most of the day without feeling rushed.

## The significance of Hanuman Jayanti at the Sansthan

Hanuman Jayanti celebrates the birth of Lord Hanuman, the devoted servant of Lord Rama whose qualities of courage, loyalty, and selfless service are held up as ideals in the bhakti tradition. At Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan, the festival carries an additional resonance: Shri Gajanan Maharaj himself was known for the same extraordinary physical stamina, the same boundless energy in service, and the same unwavering devotion that Hanuman represents. Devotees who visit Shegaon on Hanuman Jayanti often find themselves connecting these two streams of devotional energy — the ancient story of the Ramayana and the living presence of Maharaj's grace at the temple.

The festival falls on the full moon day (Purnima) of the Hindu month of Chaitra, which typically corresponds to April in the Gregorian calendar. The exact date shifts each year based on the lunar calendar, and the Sansthan announces its programme schedule several weeks in advance. The festival at Shegaon is not as large as Pragat Din or Samadhi Utsav — it draws between 80,000 and 1.5 lakh devotees over a two-day window — but the smaller crowd size is part of what makes the experience so pleasant. Devotees can move through the queue more quickly, find seating for the evening programmes more easily, and enjoy a more personal connection with the temple's rituals. ## The darshan and programme schedule

The Mangala Aarti on Hanuman Jayanti begins around 4:00 AM, and the morning session includes a special abhishek of the Hanuman idol. The priests perform the ritual with the traditional offerings — sindoor, flowers, and the chanting of the Hanuman Chalisa — and the assembled devotees join in with a devotion that is audible from outside the temple walls. The morning programme typically concludes by 9:00 AM, after which many devotees return to their accommodation for breakfast and rest.

The evening programme is the main event for many families. A Ramayana recitation and extended bhajan session run from approximately 4:30 PM to 7:00 PM, followed by the evening aarti. The Ramayana storytelling is particularly engaging for children, who sit attentively through the narratives of Hanuman's courage and devotion. The courtyard fills up approximately thirty minutes before the programme begins, so arriving by 4:00 PM gives you time to find comfortable seating. ## Crowd management strategies

Hanuman Jayanti is one of the better-managed festivals at Shegaon in terms of crowd size. The queue for darshan typically moves at a comfortable pace, with waits ranging from thirty to sixty minutes during the morning hours and slightly longer during the evening programme. The key strategy is timing: arriving at 4:00 PM for the evening programme lets you complete darshan in the less crowded early evening window and then settle in for the Ramayana recitation without rushing.

For devotees travelling with children, the open courtyard and the storytelling format of the Ramayana recitation make the evening programme particularly enjoyable. The smaller crowd compared to Pragat Din means that children can move around more freely, and the short programme duration keeps their attention. ## Arrival and transport

Shegaon railway station is approximately one kilometre from the temple, and the walk takes about fifteen minutes at a comfortable pace. Auto-rickshaws are available at the station for INR 30 to INR 50, which is the most practical option if you are carrying luggage or travelling with elderly family members. For devotees arriving by bus, the Shegaon bus stand is also within easy reach of the temple complex. The [shegaon-travel-guide](/blog/sheogaon-travel-guide) covers all transport options and routes from major cities.

If you are driving from Nagpur, the journey takes approximately three hours via the Amravati road. Leave early enough to arrive before the morning aarti, and allow extra time for parking during the busier periods. The Sansthan manages designated parking near the complex, and volunteers direct arriving vehicles to available spaces.

## Preparing for the visit

The practical preparation for Hanuman Jayanti is simpler than for the major winter festivals at Shegaon. April weather is warm but not oppressive — temperatures typically range from 25 to 35 degrees Celsius — so lightweight cotton clothing is the most comfortable choice. A small towel, sunscreen, and a water bottle are sensible additions for the daytime queue. The [winter-festival-packing-for-devotees](/blog/winter-festival-packing-for-devotees) covers general packing principles for festival visits, but the monsoon-specific considerations from [guru-purnima-darshan-planning](/blog/guru-purnima-darshan-planning) are less relevant for April travel.

Many devotees bring offerings for the temple — flowers, fruits, or coconut — which can be purchased from vendors outside the main gate. The Sansthan's volunteers are accustomed to guiding visitors to the appropriate counters for different offerings, and the process is straightforward. If you are bringing offerings for a specific puja or ceremony, contact the temple office in advance to confirm what is needed and whether advance arrangements are required. ## Extending your visit

Hanuman Jayanti does not require an extended stay, but many devotees find that adding one extra day transforms the visit from a single ceremony into a more complete pilgrimage experience. The day after the festival is noticeably quieter, and the darshan experience during this period is more relaxed and personal. If your schedule allows, arriving the evening before Hanuman Jayanti and leaving the morning after gives you a comfortable two-day window that includes the main programmes without rushing.

The Anand Sagar complex, located adjacent to the temple, is worth visiting during your stay. The lake, gardens, and cultural centre provide a peaceful complement to the intensity of the temple programme, and many devotees spend an hour or two there between morning and evening aarti.

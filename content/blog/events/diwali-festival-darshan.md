The temple at Shegaon has a particular quality on Diwali that is impossible to describe through photographs alone. It is the quality of thousands of small oil lamps arranged in rows across the temple courtyard and along the pathways that lead to the Samadhi, each flame casting a warm, wavering light across the sandstone walls and the faces of the devotees who have gathered from across Maharashtra. It is the sound of bhajans and aarti chants that continues from the early evening through the night, interwoven with the crackle of firecrackers in the distance and the quiet murmur of families moving together through the crowd. Diwali at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan is not merely a festival of lights — it is a festival that illuminates the entire devotional calendar of the year, marking the transition from the Navratri observances of October to the winter season of temple visits that begins in November with the major festivals.

## The Programme: Five Days of Luminous Devotion

Diwali at the Sansthan is not a single-day event. The Sansthan follows the traditional five-day Diwali programme, beginning with Dhanteras two days before the main festival and continuing through Bhai Dooj two days after. Each day has its own specific programme, and devotees who can spend the full five days at Shegaon experience a progression of devotional activities that builds in intensity through the main Diwali day and concludes with the familial celebration of Bhai Dooj.

The main Diwali day begins with special puja programmes at the temple in the morning, followed by the Lakshmi puja in the evening — the ceremony that is the spiritual centrepiece of Diwali across Hindu tradition. The Sansthan's Lakshmi puja is conducted with full Vedic ceremony by the temple priests, and the evening closes with a special aarti accompanied by bhajan performances. The temple and its grounds are illuminated with thousands of oil lamps, and the atmosphere is one of deep spiritual celebration. The [shegaon-darshan-timing-guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-timing-guide) provides the complete schedule for all five days of the Diwali programme.

## The Lakshmi Puja at Shegaon

The Lakshmi puja on Diwali is particularly significant at the Sansthan, as Shri Gajanan Maharaj's own legacy is closely associated with the Goddess of prosperity and abundance. The puja involves traditional offerings of flowers, fruits, and sweets to the deity, accompanied by Vedic mantras that invoke the Goddess's blessings. Devotees who arrive for the evening programme can witness the complete puja ceremony, and many offer personal prayers alongside the temple priests' recitation. The prasad distribution following the Lakshmi puja is considered especially blessed, and many devotees carry portions back home to distribute to family members.

The Lakshmi puja is typically conducted between 7:00 PM and 8:30 PM on the main Diwali day. This timing is calculated according to the traditional Hindu astrological rules — the Pradosh Kaal — that are followed across Maharashtra for the Lakshmi puja. Devotees who arrive in the temple courtyard by 6:30 PM can secure a comfortable position for witnessing the ceremony, and the temple provides seating arrangements for those who cannot stand for the duration of the puja.

## Dhanteras: The Beginning of the Festival

The Diwali programme at the Sansthan begins with Dhanteras, two days before the main festival. Dhanteras is traditionally observed as a day for the purchase of gold, silver, and new household items, and many devotees travel to Shegaon specifically for this auspicious day. The temple programme on Dhanteras includes a special abhishek ceremony and the distribution of a special prasad that is prepared for this occasion. The [major-utsav-crowd-planning-checklist](/blog/major-utsav-crowd-planning-checklist) provides preparation tips for the Dhanteras observance.

## Naraka Chaturdashi: The Pre-Diwali Purification

The day before Diwali — Naraka Chaturdashi — is observed at the Sansthan with an early morning abhishek and aarti. The ceremony commemorates the victory of Krishna over the demon Narakasura, and the temple programme includes recitations of the relevant scripture passages from the Bhagavata Purana. Many devotees take an oil bath at the temple in the morning, following the traditional practice for this day.

## The Main Diwali Day

The main Diwali day is the centrepiece of the festival at the Sansthan. The day begins with Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM, followed by a series of abhishek and aarti ceremonies throughout the morning. The afternoon is reserved for the special puja programmes at the temple, and the evening begins with the Lakshmi puja at the traditional astrological time. The illumination of the temple and its grounds is most spectacular on this evening, and devotees who arrive for the Lakshmi puja are rewarded with the sight of the temple in its full Diwali glory.

The temple is decorated for Diwali over the course of several days, with the lighting installations completed by the evening of the main Diwali day. The traditional illumination uses rows of small earthen oil lamps arranged in geometric patterns across the courtyard and along the temple's exterior walls. Modern electric lighting has been added in recent years, but the traditional oil lamp illumination remains the signature feature of Diwali at the Sansthan, and devotees travel from across Maharashtra specifically to witness this unique devotional display.

## Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj

The day after Diwali — Govardhan Puja, also known as Padwa — is observed at the Sansthan with special programmes that commemorate Krishna's lifting of the Govardhan mountain. The temple priests conduct a symbolic puja that involves the offering of small hill-shaped structures made of cow dung, and devotees participate in the ceremony with traditional offerings. The day is also associated with the Hindu New Year in many parts of India, and the Sansthan marks this with special announcements and a brief cultural programme.

Bhai Dooj, the final day of the Diwali programme, is a celebration of the brother-sister relationship. Sisters visit brothers and offer prayers for their wellbeing, and the temple programme on this day includes a special puja that honours this relationship. Many devotees conclude their five-day Diwali visit to Shegaon on Bhai Dooj, having participated in the complete programme.

## Darshan Strategy for Diwali

Diwali at Shegaon draws between 40,000 and 80,000 devotees across the five-day programme, with the main day being the most crowded. Darshan queue times during peak hours range from 45 to 90 minutes, and the early morning darshan before 8:00 AM typically involves a wait of 20 to 40 minutes. Arriving the evening before and staying at Bhakta Niwas ensures that you can participate in the pre-dawn Mangala Aarti and complete your darshan before the crowd builds.

Rishi Panchami at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan is one of the more contemplative observances of the year, celebrated in the quiet dedication of the Samadhi grounds with special programmes that honour the Saptarishi — the seven great sages of the Vedic tradition. The fifth day of the bright half of Bhadrapada — the month that follows Shravana — arrives at Shegaon in the depth of the monsoon season, when the Anand Sagar is full from the rains and the banyan trees around the Samadhi are heavy with foliage. The atmosphere of the Samadhi grounds during Rishi Panchami is one of quiet reverence, with devotees gathering at the place where Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained mahasamadhi to honour the sages whose teachings form the foundation of the Hindu spiritual tradition. The combination of the monsoon setting, the quiet devotional atmosphere, and the historical significance of the observance creates an experience that is deeply contemplative and spiritually meaningful.

## The Significance of the Saptarishi

The Saptarishi — the seven great sages — are the foundation of the Hindu spiritual tradition. They are the authors of the Vedic texts, the composers of the hymns that form the basis of Hindu devotional practice, and the spiritual teachers who established the guru-disciple tradition that Shri Gajanan Maharaj exemplified in his own life. The seven sages — Kashyapa, Atri, Bharadvaja, Vishwamitra, Gautama, Jamadagni, and Vashishtha — are honoured on Rishi Panchami because of their role as the original gurus whose teachings have been passed down through the generations. The observance of Rishi Panchami at the Sansthan is therefore a recognition of the guru-disciple tradition that the saint represented, and it is celebrated with the specific intention of honouring the sages who established that tradition.

The festival is also associated with the concept of purification and the renewal of spiritual practice. The traditional observance includes specific rituals for honouring the Saptarishi and for seeking their blessings for the coming year, and the Sansthan has adapted these traditional observances into the programme that is conducted at the Samadhi. The combination of the traditional rituals and the Samadhi setting creates an observance that is both historically grounded and spiritually focused.

## The Samadhi Programme

The Rishi Panchami programme at the Sansthan is centred around the Samadhi, the place where Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained mahasamadhi. The morning begins with a special puja that is conducted by the temple priests at the Samadhi, and the programme includes the recitation of the traditional mantras that honour the Saptarishi. The atmosphere at the Samadhi during the programme is one of quiet devotional concentration, and many devotees describe the experience of participating in the Rishi Panchami programme at the Samadhi as one of the most spiritually significant moments of the year. The main temple also conducts a special puja during Rishi Panchami, and devotees who participate in both programmes — at the main temple and at the Samadhi — report a comprehensive devotional experience that engages both the intellectual and the emotional dimensions of spiritual practice. The morning programme at the main temple includes the Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM, followed by the abhishek ceremony and the special puja for Rishi Panchami.

## The Samadhi Experience

The Samadhi at Shegaon is a unique devotional space — the place where Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained mahasamadhi and where his presence is most directly felt by devotees who visit. The Samadhi grounds are surrounded by old banyan trees, peaceful gardens, and the quiet atmosphere that is conducive to contemplative practice. The Rishi Panchami programme at the Samadhi takes advantage of this setting, with the morning programme conducted in the garden surrounding the Samadhi and the afternoon programme conducted in the temple adjacent to the grounds.

Devotees who arrive before 6:00 AM for the Rishi Panchami programme can participate in the complete morning schedule — the Mangala Aarti at the Samadhi, the abhishek ceremony, and the special puja that is conducted for the festival. The early morning programme is particularly well-attended, and many devotees describe the experience of participating in the morning programme at the Samadhi as one of the most spiritually significant moments of the year. ## The Monsoon Setting

Rishi Panchami falls during the peak monsoon season at Shegaon, when the countryside is at its most lush and the Anand Sagar is full from the rains. The early morning programme at the Samadhi is conducted in the monsoon air — cool, humid, and fragrant with the scent of wet earth and flowering plants. The monsoon setting adds a particular beauty to the devotional atmosphere of the Rishi Panchami observance, and many devotees describe the combination of the contemplative programme and the monsoon landscape as one of the most aesthetically and spiritually rewarding temple experiences of the year.

## The Monsoon Considerations

The monsoon season at Shegaon brings heavy rainfall, and devotees planning a visit for Rishi Panchami should prepare for wet conditions. The temple grounds are covered with temporary shelters and walkways that protect devotees from the rain during the programme, and the temple office provides raincoats to devotees who arrive without adequate rain protection. ## Weather and Preparation

Rishi Panchami falls in August or September, which is the peak monsoon season at Shegaon. Daytime temperatures range between 24 and 30 degrees Celsius, with moderate to heavy rainfall. Devotees should bring rain protection and wear waterproof footwear for the outdoor portions of the programme. ## The Devotional Atmosphere

The devotional atmosphere during Rishi Panchami at the Samadhi is distinct from other observances at the Sansthan. Rather than the loud, celebratory energy of Pragat Din or the elaborate ritual programme of Mahashivratri, the Rishi Panchami atmosphere is one of quiet, focused reverence.

The ninth of September holds a different quality at Shegaon. It is not the celebratory energy of Pragat Din in February, which marks the saint's appearance, nor the communal warmth of Ganesh Chaturthi. Punyatithi — the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained Samadhi in 1910 — carries an atmosphere of deep reverence, quiet devotion, and contemplative stillness that surprises first-time visitors who expect the intensity of a major festival. The crowd is large, often between one and two lakh devotees over the observance period, but the mood inside the temple complex is quieter, more focused, and more personal than on almost any other day of the year. Planning your Punyatithi visit means preparing for the practical realities — the extended darshan hours, the accommodation pressure, and the travel logistics of a post-monsoon pilgrimage — while also arriving with the right inner disposition for a day that is fundamentally about surrender and remembrance.

## Understanding the spiritual character of Punyatithi

Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained Samadhi on Rishi Panchami, the fifth day of the bright half of Bhadrapad month, which falls in September. The Sansthan observes this day with a programme of extended prayers, abhishek, and aartis that run from early morning until late evening. The bhajans during Punyatithi focus on the saint's teachings and his departure from the world, and many of the devotional songs performed on this day are specific to the observance — they are not sung at any other time of the year.

The Samadhi Mandir is the undisputed focal point of the day. Devotees queue for extended periods to offer flowers, perform paduka (footprint) darshan, and participate in the collective aarti. The evening aarti, held as the sun sets behind the Samadhi, is widely regarded as the most spiritually powerful moment of the entire observance. Arriving early enough to secure a position near the front of the queue is important for devotees who want to experience the aarti without the visual and auditory obstruction of a large crowd. ## Planning your visit timing

The Sansthan typically announces the Punyatithi programme schedule four to six weeks before the observance. Darshan timings are extended on the day, with the main gate opening around 3:30 AM for the Mangala Aarti. The queue builds rapidly after that, and by 7:00 AM the holding area near the entrance can extend to several hundred devotees deep. The two anchor events of the day are the midday abhishek and the evening aarti, and planning your visit around one of these works well.

Many devotees choose to arrive the evening before Punyatithi. They attend the preliminary evening aarti, rest overnight at their accommodation, and join the morning darshan fresh. This two-day approach is particularly helpful for elderly family members and young children, who may find the early wake-up time and extended standing difficult if attempted in a single day. The [managing-accommodation-during-pragat-din](/blog/managing-accommodation-during-pragat-din) guide provides accommodation strategies that apply equally to Punyatithi, as both are among the busiest days of the year at the Sansthan.

## Booking accommodation in advance

Accommodation for Punyatithi needs to be booked earlier than for most other festivals. Bhakta Niwas rooms must be reserved at least three weeks in advance, and the best rooms — particularly the 3-Bed AC and 4-Bed AC options — tend to be taken within the first week after bookings open. The [bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) explains the booking process in detail, including how to specify your preferred room type and dates when calling the Sansthan office.

The room range at Bhakta Niwas includes the 2-Bed Non-AC at INR 1,250 per night for budget-conscious pilgrims, the 3-Bed AC at INR 2,050 and 4-Bed AC at INR 2,550 for families wanting comfort during the post-monsoon season, and the Deluxe Suite at INR 3,150 and Luxury Suite at INR 4,150 for those seeking additional space. The Family Room at INR 3,850, which sleeps up to six guests, is ideal for multi-generational families attending Punyatithi together. All prices include breakfast, lunch, dinner, applicable taxes, and hot water — with no hidden charges.

Standard private rooms typically range from INR 1,500 to INR 2,500 per night during Punyatithi, with premium options costing more. Booking private accommodation early is equally important, as the town's limited hotel capacity fills up quickly during the observance period.

## Transport and travel logistics

Punyatithi falls during the post-monsoon period, when the roads from Mumbai, Nagpur, and Akola to Shegaon are in good condition and the weather is pleasant for travel. Daytime temperatures typically range from 28 to 34 degrees Celsius, and the early mornings are comfortably cool. If you are arriving by train from Mumbai, services on the Bhusaval division stop at Shegaon station, and booking your tickets at least three to four weeks in advance is advisable, as Punyatithi falls during a period of high travel demand across Maharashtra.

From the railway station, the temple is approximately one kilometre away. The walk takes about fifteen minutes, and auto-rickshaws are available for INR 30 to INR 50. If you are driving from Nagpur, the journey takes roughly three hours via the Amravati road. The [shegaon-travel-guide](/blog/sheogaon-travel-guide) covers all transport options and routes in full detail, including bus services from major cities and the shared taxi options available from Akola and Amravati.

## On the day: darshan and practical tips

Punyatithi darshan follows the same tiered queue system used during other high-rush days at the Sansthan. Volunteers manage roped barriers that guide devotees through successive holding areas, and the queue moves steadily but slowly. Keep your belongings minimal — a small bag with your phone, a water bottle, and your ID is sufficient. Photography is not permitted inside the main hall, and phones should remain on silent.

Inside the sanctum, the atmosphere during Punyatithi is noticeably quieter than during Pragat Din. Devotees speak softly, the aarti rhythm is measured and devotional, and the focus is on personal prayer and spiritual reflection rather than the communal celebration that characterises Pragat Din. Many devotees spend time in the outer courtyard after darshan, sitting quietly near the Samadhi or walking the circumambulatory path in a slow, contemplative rhythm.

For families with elderly members, request assistance at the entry gate. The Sansthan maintains a priority queue for senior citizens and persons with mobility challenges, and the volunteers are experienced at guiding these devotees through a smoother, faster process. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is Punyatithi the same as Pragat Din?**

**A:** No. Punyatithi marks the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained Samadhi (departed this world), while Pragat Din celebrates his divine appearance at Shegaon. Both are deeply significant but have different spiritual character and timing.

**Q: How many days in advance should I book Bhakta Niwas for Punyatithi?**

**A:** At least three weeks in advance. Demand is high, and rooms at Bhakta Niwas can be fully booked by two weeks before the observance. Use the [bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) to secure your reservation, or reach out directly via our [contact page](/contact).

**Q: Are there special darshan arrangements for Punyatithi?**

**A:** No separate VIP category exists. All devotees use the same queue system, with priority assistance available for elderly and mobility-challenged visitors at the entry gate. Arriving before 5:00 AM is the most effective way to reduce wait times.

**Q: Can I attend the evening aarti without staying overnight?**

**A:** Yes, many devotees arrive in the afternoon and leave after dinner. However, traffic and parking are busiest in the evening, so arriving two to three hours before the aarti is advisable. Book your return travel in advance, as trains and buses from Shegaon fill up quickly after the observance.

**Q: What is the dress code for Punyatithi darshan?**

**A:** Modest, traditional attire is recommended. Men should wear a kurta, dhoti, or simple cotton pants. Women should wear a saree, salwar kameez, or long dress covering shoulders and knees. Avoid leather items, as these are not permitted inside the temple complex.
**Are there fasting restrictions I should know about?**
Many devotees observe a full or partial fast on Punyatithi. The Mahaprasad served at the Sansthan is suitable for those breaking their fast after the aarti. If you have specific dietary requirements, confirm with the Mahaprasad Hall volunteers on the day.

Punyatithi — the sacred departure anniversary of Shri Gajanan Maharaj — is one of the most emotionally significant dates in the Sansthan calendar. Unlike Pragat Din, which celebrates the saint's appearance at Shegaon, Punyatithi marks the day he left his physical body, an event devotees observe with deep reverence, collective prayers, and an atmosphere of contemplative devotion rather than festivity. The quiet intensity of Punyatithi draws a large crowd nonetheless, often between one and two lakh devotees over the observance period, and planning your visit around this day requires understanding both the spiritual character of the event and the practical realities of darshan, accommodation, and travel.

## What Punyatithi Means for Devotees

Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained Samadhi on the eleventh day of the bright half of Bhadrapad month, a date that corresponds to September in the Gregorian calendar. The Sansthan observes this day with a special programme of prayers, abhishek, and aartis that run from early morning until late evening. The mood throughout the temple complex is distinctly devotional and reflective — bhajans focus on the saint's teachings, the crowd tends to be quieter and more focused, and many devotees fast throughout the day as an act of spiritual discipline.

The Samadhi Mandir becomes the focal point of the day's observances. Devotees queue for extended periods to offer flowers, perform paduka (footprint) darshan, and participate in the collective aarti. The evening aarti, held as the sun sets behind the Samadhi, is widely considered the most spiritually powerful moment of the day, and arriving early enough to secure a position near the front of the queue is important for devotees who want to experience it fully.

## Planning Your Visit Timing

The Sansthan typically announces the Punyatithi programme schedule four to six weeks in advance. Darshan timings are extended on the day, with the main gate opening around 3:30 AM for the Mangala Aarti. The queue builds quickly after that, and by 7:00 AM the holding area near the entrance can extend to several hundred devotees deep. The midday abhishek and the evening aarti are the two anchor events of the day, and planning your visit around one of them works well.

Many devotees choose to arrive the evening before Punyatithi, attend the preliminary evening aarti, rest overnight at their accommodation, and then join the morning darshan fresh. This two-day approach is particularly helpful for elderly family members and young children, who may find the early wake-up time and extended standing difficult if attempted in a single day. If you are travelling from Akola — approximately 105 kilometres from Shegaon — leaving around 9:00 PM gets you to Shegaon by midnight, giving you a few hours of rest before the 3:30 AM gate opening.

## Accommodation During Punyatithi

Booking accommodation early is even more critical for Punyatithi than for regular festival weekends, because the observance combines both the spiritual significance of Pragat Din and the seasonal timing of the post-monsoon period, when many families prefer to travel. Bhakta Niwas rooms must be reserved at least three weeks in advance. The [Bhakta Niwas booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) explains the process in detail, but the practical reality is that calling the Sansthan office directly — or using the [phone booking process guide](/blog/phone-booking-process-detailed-guide) — is the most reliable way to secure a room.

For those unable to get a room at Bhakta Niwas, the [Shegaon accommodation guide](/blog/shegaon-accommodation-guide) lists private options within the town. Rates at private hotels increase during Punyatithi, with standard rooms going from INR 800 on a normal day to INR 1,500 to INR 2,500 during the observance. Comparing options and booking early helps manage costs. The Sansthan's own accommodations remain the most affordable option, and the three-week advance booking window is worth following even if it means finalising your plans earlier than you might prefer.

## Transport and Travel Logistics

If you are coming by train from Mumbai, the Dadar-Akola Express and other trains on the Bhusaval division stop at Shegaon station. Book your tickets at least three to four weeks in advance, as Punyatithi falls during a period of high travel demand across Maharashtra. From the station, the temple is approximately one kilometre away, and walking is feasible for most devotees. Auto-rickshaws are available at the station for INR 30 to INR 50.

From Nagpur, the drive to Shegaon takes roughly three hours via the Amravati road. If you are driving, leave early enough to avoid arriving during the peak heat of midday, particularly if you plan to attend the evening aarti. The parking arrangements near the temple complex are managed by the Sansthan, and volunteers guide arriving vehicles to designated parking plots. The [Shegaon travel guide](/blog/shegaon-travel-guide) covers transport options in more detail, including bus services, shared taxis, and routes from cities across Vidarbha and Maharashtra.

## On the Day: Darshan, Etiquette, and Practical Tips

Punyatithi darshan follows the same general process as other high-rush days at the Sansthan. Volunteers manage a tiered queue system with roped barriers guiding devotees through successive holding areas. The queue moves steadily but slowly, and patience is the most useful quality you can bring to the experience. Keep your belongings minimal — a small bag with your phone, a water bottle, and any necessary documents is sufficient.

Inside the sanctum area, the atmosphere is quieter than during Pragat Din. Devotees speak softly, the aarti节奏 is measured and devotional, and the focus is on personal prayer and spiritual reflection. Photography is not permitted inside the main hall, and phones should remain on silent. After completing darshan, many devotees spend time in the outer courtyard, sitting quietly near the Samadhi or walking the circumambulatory path. The [darshan queue time optimisation guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-queue-time-optimization) provides specific strategies for minimising wait times during busy periods.

For families with elderly members, request assistance at the entry gate. The Sansthan maintains a priority queue for senior citizens and persons with mobility challenges, and the volunteers are experienced at guiding these devotees through a smoother, faster process. If you are travelling with children, keep them close in the queue and write your contact number on a card they carry, as a simple but effective safety measure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Punyatithi the same as Pragat Din?**
No. Punyatithi marks the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained Samadhi (departed this world), while Pragat Din celebrates his divine appearance at Shegaon. Both are significant but have different spiritual character and timing.

**How many days in advance should I book Bhakta Niwas for Punyatithi?**
At least three weeks in advance. Demand is high, and rooms at Bhakta Niwas can be fully booked by two weeks before the observance.

**Are there special darshan arrangements for Punyatithi?**
The Sansthan does not typically offer a separate VIP or special darshan category for Punyatithi. All devotees use the same queue system, with priority assistance available for elderly and mobility-challenged visitors at the entry gate.

**Can I attend the evening aarti without staying overnight?**
Yes, many devotees arrive in the afternoon, attend the evening aarti, and leave after dinner. However, traffic and parking are busiest in the evening, so arriving two to three hours before the aarti is advisable.

**What is the dress code for Punyatithi darshan?**
Modest, traditional attire is recommended. Men should wear a dhoti, kurta, or simple cotton pants. Women should wear a saree, salwar kameez, or long dress covering shoulders and knees. Avoid leather items, as these are not permitted inside the temple complex.

**Are there fasting restrictions I should know about?**
Many devotees observe a full or partial fast on Punyatithi. The Mahaprasad served at the Sansthan is suitable for those breaking their fast after the aarti. If you have specific dietary requirements, confirm with the Mahaprasad Hall volunteers on the day.

The eleventh day of the bright half of Chaitra — the first month of the Hindu calendar — arrives at Shegaon with a particular spiritual clarity. The spring season is in full bloom, the cotton fields around the Sansthan are fresh with new growth, and the Anand Sagar lake reflects the blue sky of the post-winter period with a brightness that mirrors the devotional energy of the Ekadashi observance. Chaitra Ekadashi is one of the twenty-four Ekadashis — the eleventh lunar day observances — that are dedicated to Lord Vishnu, and at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan it is observed with a focused programme that includes special abhishek, the recitation of Vishnu's names, and the distribution of prasad to all devotees who participate. The spring timing of Chaitra Ekadashi makes it one of the most physically comfortable observances of the year, and many devotees plan their first temple visit of the spring season around this festival.

## The Significance of Ekadashi

Ekadashi — the eleventh lunar day — is one of the most significant devotional observances in the Hindu tradition. It is dedicated to Lord Vishnu, and it is observed twice each month, once during the bright half and once during the dark half of the lunar month. Of the twenty-four Ekadashis of the year, Chaitra Ekadashi holds a special place because it falls in the month that marks the beginning of the Hindu calendar year, and it is considered one of the most auspicious Ekadashis of the year. The observance involves fasting, the recitation of Vishnu's names, the performance of puja and abhishek, and the distribution of prasad. At the Sansthan, the Ekadashi programme includes all of these elements, creating a day of focused devotional practice that is both spiritually deep and physically accessible.

The [shegaon-darshan-timing-guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-timing-guide) provides the complete schedule of the Chaitra Ekadashi programme. The morning begins with the Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM, followed by a special abhishek ceremony in the main temple that is unique to the Ekadashi observance. The abhishek is conducted with traditional offerings including milk, honey, and flowers, and the temple priests recite the Vishnu Sahasranama — the thousand names of Vishnu — as part of the ceremony.

## The Fasting Practice

Fasting on Ekadashi is a traditional devotional practice that has been observed across India for centuries. The fast is typically a partial fast — many devotees abstain from grains and cereals while consuming fruits, milk, and nuts. The Mahaprasad Hall at Shegaon provides fasting-compliant meals throughout the Ekadashi programme, and devotees who are observing the fast can receive prasad from the temple at the conclusion of the morning programme. The [shegaon-canteen-and-mahaprasad-guide](/blog/shegaon-canteen-and-mahaprasad-guide) covers the complete range of fasting-friendly options available at the Sansthan.

The fasting practice on Ekadashi is considered spiritually beneficial because it creates a state of physical lightness that is conducive to devotional practice. The combination of fasting, the abhishek ceremony, and the recitation of Vishnu's names creates a devotional day that is both physically manageable and spiritually deep. Devotees who observe the Ekadashi fast at the Sansthan report a sense of spiritual renewal that is distinct from their regular devotional practice.

## The Morning Programme

The Ekadashi programme at the Sansthan is structured around the early morning hours, with the Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM followed by the abhishek ceremony and the distribution of prasad. Devotees who arrive before 7:00 AM can participate in the complete morning programme and then spend the rest of the day in contemplation or community service at the temple. The afternoon programme includes a devotional discourse on the significance of Ekadashi, and the evening closes with a special aarti.

The morning programme at the Samadhi is particularly well-attended on Ekadashi, and many devotees describe the experience of standing at the Samadhi in the early morning hours as one of the most spiritually significant moments of their visit. The quiet atmosphere of the Samadhi — surrounded by the old banyan trees and the peaceful garden — is well-suited to the contemplative nature of the Ekadashi observance.

## The Vishnu Sahasranama Recitation

The Vishnu Sahasranama — the thousand names of Vishnu — is recited continuously at the Sansthan throughout the Ekadashi programme. The recitation is conducted by trained temple priests and is accompanied by traditional musical instruments. The atmosphere during the recitation is one of focused devotional attention, and devotees who listen to the complete recitation describe the experience as deeply meditative. Many devotees bring their own copies of the Vishnu Sahasranama to follow along during the recitation, and the practice of listening to the recitation is considered spiritually beneficial in the Hindu tradition.

## The Philosophical Significance

Ekadashi is associated with the concept of austerity and the discipline of devotional practice. The Sanskrit word "ekadashi" literally means "eleven," and the observance is associated with the eleven senses and the practice of controlling the senses through fasting and meditation. At the Sansthan, the Ekadashi observance is understood as an opportunity to deepen devotional practice through the combination of fasting, prayer, and meditation. The temple priests often emphasize the philosophical significance of Ekadashi in their discourses, and devotees who participate in the complete programme report that they receive both spiritual and philosophical insights from the observance.

## Weather and Preparation

Chaitra Ekadashi falls in March or April, which is the transition period between the cool winter and the hot summer at Shegaon. Daytime temperatures range between 28 and 35 degrees Celsius, with the morning and evening hours being noticeably cooler. Warm-weather clothing is appropriate for the day, and sun protection is recommended.

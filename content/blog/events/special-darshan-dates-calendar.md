Planning your pilgrimage to Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan starts with knowing the dates. The temple observes a rich calendar of festivals, special observances, and enhanced darshan days throughout the year that draw devotees from across Maharashtra and beyond. Each of these dates carries specific spiritual significance and specific logistical implications for your visit, from the crowd pressure of major festivals to the peaceful reflection of smaller observance days. Having a reliable reference of special darshan dates allows you to plan your pilgrimage with precision, booking accommodation and arranging travel around the most spiritually significant moments of the year. This calendar covers the major special darshan dates at Shegaon, with practical guidance on what to expect and how to plan around each date.

## Pragat Din — The Most Significant Date of the Year

Pragat Din, the manifestation day of Shri Gajanan Maharaj, is the single most important date in the Sansthan's calendar. The festival draws three to four lakh devotees, making it the most crowded day of the year and the most demanding logistically. The Sansthan's darshan schedule is extended on Pragat Din, with temple doors opening as early as 4:00 AM. The Mangala Aarti on Pragat Din morning is considered especially potent, and the entire day is filled with special programming including abhishekam, bhajans, and spiritual discourses that continue from dawn through the evening.

The specific date of Pragat Din varies each year according to the Hindu lunar calendar, typically falling in February or March. The Sansthan announces the exact date well in advance, allowing devotees to plan their pilgrimage and accommodation. If Pragat Din is your only opportunity to visit the Sansthan, plan around this date with the strategies outlined in the [gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide). The booking window for Pragat Din accommodation is the narrowest of any festival, requiring action four to six weeks in advance.

## Maha Shivaratri — The Night Vigil

Maha Shivaratri, the great night of Lord Shiva, falls in February or March and is one of the most spiritually intense nights at Shegaon. The Sansthan observes Maha Shivaratri with an all-night vigil that begins at 9:00 PM and continues through the night until 6:00 AM. The temple is illuminated by oil lamps, and devotional singing continues throughout the night, creating an atmosphere of profound spiritual energy. The [shivaratri-night-darshan-planning](/blog/shivaratri-night-darshan-planning) provides detailed guidance for the Shivaratri night experience, including packing, energy management, and timing strategies.

## Navratri — Nine Nights of the Goddess

Navratri spans nine nights in September or October, with each night carrying different spiritual significance tied to the nine forms of the goddess. The Sansthan observes Navratri with special morning and evening aarti ceremonies, abhishekam of the goddess, and cultural programs that include devotional music and discourses. The most significant nights are Ashtami and Navami, which draw the largest crowds and feature the most elaborate programming. The [navratri-festival-season-booking-guide](/blog/navratri-festival-season-booking-guide) provides Navratri-specific booking and planning guidance.

## Diwali — The Festival of Lights

Diwali, the five-day festival of lights, falls in October or November. The main Diwali day, Lakshmi Puja, is the most significant date within the festival. The Sansthan decorates the entire complex with thousands of lamps, and the evening aarti on Lakshmi Puja day is one of the most visually stunning events of the year. The [diwali-darshan-planning-tips](/blog/diwali-darshan-planning-tips) provides Diwali-specific planning guidance including crowd patterns and accommodation strategies.

## New Year Weekend — December 31 and January 1

The New Year weekend at Shegaon, spanning December 31st through January 1st, is marked by a special midnight aarti on December 31st that draws thousands of devotees who stay awake through the night to welcome the new year in the temple's spiritual atmosphere. The Sansthan extends its darshan schedule to accommodate the crowd, and the family-friendly atmosphere makes this one of the most accessible festival periods. The [new-year-weekend-darshan-planning-guide](/blog/new-year-weekend-darshan-planning-guide) provides New Year-specific planning guidance.

## Gudi Padwa — The Marathi New Year

Gudi Padwa, the Marathi New Year, falls in March or April and marks the beginning of the Hindu lunar calendar. The Sansthan observes Gudi Padwa with a special morning program that begins at 4:30 AM, including the ritual raising of the Gudi, special abhishekam ceremonies, and a devotional discourse. The atmosphere is hopeful and uplifting, making it a particularly meaningful day for setting intentions for the year ahead. The [gudi-padwa-visit-planning](/blog/gudi-padwa-visit-planning) provides detailed guidance for Gudi Padwa visits.

## Ekadashi Days — Bi-Monthly Spiritual Observances

Ekadashi, the eleventh lunar day of each fortnight, is observed as a special fasting day at the Sansthan. Every Ekadashi carries spiritual significance, with enhanced abhishekam and aarti ceremonies. Devotees who observe Ekadashi fasting often combine their fast with a visit to the temple, making Ekadashi days slightly busier than regular days. The most significant Ekadashi is Karthik Ekadashi, observed in November or December, which combines the holiness of the Karthik month with the sanctity of Ekadashi. The [kartik-ekadashi-pandharpur-darshan-guide](/blog/kartik-ekadashi-pandharpur-darshan-guide) covers Karthik Ekadashi specifically.

## Guru Purnima — Honoring the Guru Tradition

Guru Purnima, the full moon day dedicated to spiritual teachers, falls in July or August and is observed at Shegaon with special programs honoring the guru tradition. Many devotees visit the Sansthan on this day to seek blessings for their spiritual growth and to honor the teachings of their own gurus. The Guru Purnima observance is particularly significant at Shegaon because of the central role of the guru-disciple tradition in Shri Gajanan Maharaj's teachings.

## Other Notable Darshan Dates

The Sansthan's annual calendar includes several other important dates that devotees may wish to mark. The anniversary of Shri Gajanan Maharaj's punyatithi, observed on the lunar date corresponding to his passing, draws devotees seeking to honor his legacy and seek his continued blessings. Monthly observance days tied to the lunar calendar include the full moon days (Purnima) and new moon days (Amavasya), which carry spiritual significance for many devotees. The [festival-season-what-to-expect](/blog/festival-season-what-to-expect) provides a comprehensive overview of all festivals and observances throughout the year.

## Regular Darshan Days

Outside the special dates listed above, every day at Shegaon is a good day for darshan. The regular darshan schedule includes the Mangala Aarti at 5:00 AM, midday aarti at noon, and the evening aarti at sunset. For devotees who can visit on non-festival days, the regular darshan experience is peaceful and focused, allowing for deeper spiritual practice without the pressure of crowds and queues. Many devotees establish a routine of visiting during off-peak periods for a more contemplative connection with the temple, finding that the quiet atmosphere of a regular day allows for deeper meditation and a more personal connection with the divine presence at Shegaon.

## Practical Planning Tips for Festival Visits

Planning your visit around a special darshan date involves more than just marking the calendar. Once you identify the date you want to visit, begin planning immediately. Mark your travel and accommodation bookings in a calendar so you can track your preparation timeline. Inform your family and travel companions about the specific dates and the nature of the festival so everyone understands what to expect. Pack according to the season and the specific demands of the festival — the [winter-festival-packing-for-devotees](/blog/winter-festival-packing-for-devotees) and [summer-holiday-rush-booking-guide](/blog/summer-holiday-rush-booking-guide) provide seasonal guidance. Arrive the evening before your target festival day to settle into your accommodation and rest before the early morning program.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is Pragat Din celebrated?**
Pragat Din dates are announced by the Sansthan administration each year based on the Hindu lunar calendar. Check the [gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide) for specific dates and planning guidance.

**Do I need to book accommodation for regular darshan days?**
Booking is recommended during weekends and holiday periods but not always necessary on regular weekdays. The [shegaon-accommodation-guide](/blog/shegaon-accommodation-guide) provides accommodation details for regular visits.

**Which festival should I prioritize if I can only visit once?**
Pragat Din is the most spiritually significant, but any festival visit creates powerful devotional memories. Choose a festival that aligns with your personal schedule and devotional priorities.

**Does the Sansthan publish a festival calendar?**
Yes, the Sansthan publishes an annual festival calendar with all major dates and special observances. Check the official website or contact the Sansthan office for the current year's calendar.

The annual festival calendar at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan is one of the richest devotional programmes in Maharashtra, spanning the entire year with observances that range from intimate morning pujas to major community celebrations that draw tens of thousands of devotees from across India. Understanding this calendar is essential for planning a visit that aligns with the festival that most resonates with your devotional practice, whether that is the luminous Diwali celebrations, the devotional intensity of Ashadhi Ekadashi, the harvest joy of Makar Sankranti, or the contemplative observance of Mahashivratri. This guide maps out the complete festival calendar, providing the approximate dates, the devotional significance of each observance, and the practical advice needed to plan your pilgrimage around the festival season.

## January to March: The Winter Festival Season

The winter months at Shegaon are the most comfortable season for temple visits, and the Sansthan's programme during this period includes some of the most widely celebrated observances of the year. Makar Sankranti in mid-January marks the beginning of the winter festival season, with kite-flying competitions at the Anand Saga grounds, the distribution of til-gud prasad, and a special morning abhishek. The atmosphere is communal and celebratory, making it an ideal time for families to visit the Sansthan together.

The [makar-sankranti-uttarayan-guide](/blog/makar-sankranti-uttarayan-guide) provides complete details about the Makar Sankranti programme. February brings Mahashivratri, the night-long vigil that honours Shiva and connects to the formless devotional tradition that Shri Gajanan Maharaj embodied. The [mahashivratri-complete-guide](/blog/mahashivratri-complete-guide) covers the overnight programme structure, the abhishek ceremony, and the practical advice needed for the all-night vigil. Holi in March is celebrated at the Sansthan with a community gathering that includes the temple's morning programme and a festive atmosphere in the afternoon.

## April to June: The Summer Season

The summer months at Shegaon are hot, with daytime temperatures regularly exceeding 38 degrees Celsius. The festival programme during this period is lighter than the winter season, and most devotees visit the Sansthan during the early morning hours when the temperatures are moderate. Gudi Padwa in April marks the Maharashtrian New Year, and the Sansthan observes this day with special morning aartis and a community gathering. The [gudi-padwa-new-year-celebration](/blog/gudi-padwa-new-year-celebration) provides complete details.

The [hanuman-jayanti-crowd-management](/blog/hanuman-jayanti-crowd-management) guide covers the Hanuman Jayanti observance, which falls in the summer months and draws moderate crowds. Akshaya Tritiya in late April or early May is observed with special puja and the distribution of prasad, and many devotees visit the Sansthan specifically for the auspicious timing of this day. The [akshaya-tritiya-special-darshan](/blog/akshaya-tritiya-special-darshan) guide provides the complete schedule.

## July to September: The Monsoon and Ashad Observances

The monsoon season at Shegaon is one of the most spiritually charged periods of the year. The countryside is lush and beautiful, the Anand Sagar lake is full, and the temple gardens are at their peak. Guru Purnima in July falls during this period, and the [guru-purnima-celebration](/blog/guru-purnima-celebration) provides complete details about the devotional discourses and puja programmes. The Ashadhi Ekadashi Wari in August is the major pilgrimage event of the year, with lakhs of devotees walking to Shegaon as part of the Pandharpur Wari. The [ashadhi-ekadashi-pandharpur-wari-guide](/blog/ashadhi-ekadashi-pandharpur-wari-guide) covers the complete Wari experience.

Raksha Bandhan in August is celebrated with community programmes, and many devotees visit the Sansthan with their families for the day. The [raksha-bandhan-temple-visit](/blog/raksha-bandhan-temple-visit) provides practical advice. Rishi Panchami in September falls at the end of the monsoon season, when the countryside has its highest visual beauty. The [rishi-panchami-shegaon-samadhi-utsav-guide](/blog/rishi-panchami-shegaon-samadhi-utsav-guide) covers this ancient Vedic observance.

## October to December: The Pre-Winter Festival Season

October brings Navratri — the nine-night celebration of the Goddess — which is one of the most widely observed festivals at the Sansthan. The [navratri-durga-puja-guide](/blog/navratri-durga-puja-guide) covers the complete programme, including the nine-night aarti schedule, the Devi Mahatmya recitations, and the Dandiya nights. Diwali in October or November follows immediately after Navratri, and the five-day programme includes the Lakshmi puja, Dhanteras, Naraka Chaturdashi, Govardhan Puja, and Bhai Dooj. The [diwali-festival-darshan](/blog/diwali-festival-darshan) provides the complete five-day programme schedule.

The pre-winter period also includes Kartik Ekadashi in November, which is observed with special programmes at the Sansthan. The [kartik-ekadashi-pandharpur-darshan-guide](/blog/kartik-ekadashi-pandharpur-darshan-guide) covers this observance. December brings cooler temperatures and the Anniversary Utsav at the Sansthan. The [anniversary-utsav-guide](/blog/anniversary-utsav-guide) provides complete details about the anniversary observance.

## The Pragat Din Celebration

Shri Gajanan Maharaj's Pragat Din — the day of his manifestation or appearance — is one of the most significant observances of the year at the Sansthan. The exact date varies according to the Vikram Samvat calendar, but it typically falls in the pre-monsoon period of May or June. The Pragat Din observance includes special programmes that are among the most attended events of the year, with devotees travelling from across Maharashtra to participate. The [pragat-din-darshan-planning](/blog/pragat-din-darshan-planning) and [managing-accommodation-during-pragat-din](/blog/managing-accommodation-during-pragat-din) guides provide comprehensive planning advice for this major observance.

## Weather Considerations for Each Festival Season

The weather at Shegaon varies significantly across the festival calendar. The winter months — November to February — offer the most comfortable conditions, with daytime temperatures between 18 and 28 degrees Celsius. This is the ideal season for long darshan queues and extended outdoor programmes. The summer months — March to June — are hot, with temperatures regularly exceeding 35 degrees Celsius, and the early morning programme remains the most comfortable time for darshan. The monsoon months — July to September — bring moderate to heavy rainfall, but the countryside is at its most beautiful and the spiritual atmosphere of the Ashad observances makes the inconvenience of the rain worthwhile for most devotees. The [shegaon-best-time-to-visit](/blog/shegaon-best-time-to-visit) guide provides a comprehensive overview of the seasonal pattern at Shegaon.

## Booking Accommodation During the Festival Season

Accommodation demand at Shegaon varies significantly across the festival calendar. The highest demand occurs during the monsoon observances — Ashadhi Ekadashi and the Pragat Din celebrations — when the crowds at the Sansthan are at their peak. The winter festivals — Makar Sankranti, Diwali, and Mahashivratri — also draw substantial crowds, though the demand is slightly less intense than during the monsoon season. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most important festival at the Sansthan?**
Ashadhi Ekadashi and the Pragat Din celebration are the most significant observances of the year, drawing the largest crowds and featuring the most extensive programmes. However, the significance of each festival depends on your devotional practice and the traditions you follow.

**When should I book accommodation for peak festivals?**
For Ashadhi Ekadashi and Pragat Din, booking eight to twelve weeks in advance is advisable. For winter festivals like Diwali and Mahashivratri, four to six weeks in advance is sufficient. The [bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) covers the reservation process.

**What is the best season to visit Shegaon for the first time?**
The winter months — November to February — offer the most comfortable weather and a rich festival programme. Makar Sankranti, Mahashivratri, and Diwali are excellent first visits because the programmes are accessible and the crowds are manageable.

**How can I plan a visit that covers multiple festivals?**
The monsoon season in July to September covers Guru Purnima, Raksha Bandhan, and Rishi Panchami, which can be combined into a single visit. The [monsoon-festival-calendar-pilgrimage-planning](/blog/monsoon-festival-calendar-pilgrimage-planning) guide provides planning advice for multi-festival visits.

**What should I pack for different festival seasons?**
Winter festivals require warm clothing for the early morning and evening hours. Summer festivals require sun protection and light, breathable clothing. Monsoon festivals require rain protection and waterproof footwear. The [what-to-carry-pilgrimage-essentials](/blog/what-to-carry-pilgrimage-essentials) provides a complete packing checklist.

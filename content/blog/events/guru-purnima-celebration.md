The full moon of Ashadha — the monsoon month that begins in late June or early July — rises over Shegaon with a particular luminescence that devotees of the Sansthan have learned to recognize. It is the night of Guru Purnima, the sacred observance that honours the guru-disciple tradition, and the temple at Shegaon is filled with devotees who have gathered to express their gratitude to their spiritual teachers and to renew their commitment to the path of devotion. At Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan, Guru Purnima has a particular significance because of the saint's own role as a guru to countless devotees across Maharashtra and beyond. The day is observed not as a celebration of a single individual but as a celebration of the guru-disciple relationship itself — a relationship that is considered the foundation of spiritual growth in the Hindu tradition.

## The Significance of Guru Purnima

Guru Purnima is one of the most important observances in the Hindu spiritual calendar. It falls on the full moon of Ashadha, and it is observed across India as a day to honour the guru — the spiritual teacher who guides the disciple from ignorance to knowledge, from bondage to liberation. The festival has particular resonance at the Sansthan because Shri Gajanan Maharaj's legacy is fundamentally about the guru-disciple tradition. The saint himself was a guru to many, and the Sansthan continues to function as a centre where devotees come to receive spiritual guidance and to deepen their devotional practice. The guru-disciple relationship that the festival honours is one that Shri Gajanan Maharaj exemplified in his own life, and the observance of Guru Purnima at Shegaon is therefore a direct continuation of the tradition that the saint established.

## The Guru Stotra and Guru Gita

The morning programme at the Sansthan on Guru Purnima includes the recitation of the Guru Stotra — a devotional text that is composed of thirty-four verses that honour the qualities of the spiritual teacher. The recitation is conducted by the temple priests with appropriate musical accompaniment, and devotees who arrive early in the morning can participate in the complete recitation. The Guru Stotra describes the guru as the one who removes the darkness of ignorance and illuminates the path of knowledge, and the verses are recited with a devotional fervour that reflects the deep respect that the tradition holds for the spiritual teacher.

The afternoon programme includes discourses on the Guru Gita — a section of the Skanda Purana that is dedicated to the philosophical significance of the guru-disciple relationship. The Guru Gita consists of sixty-four verses that explain the nature of the guru, the qualities of the disciple, and the process of spiritual transmission that occurs through the guru-disciple relationship. The discourses at the Sansthan on Guru Purnima draw on this text to explain the philosophical foundation of the festival, and many devotees report that the discourses deepen their understanding of the devotional tradition that Shri Gajanan Maharaj represents.

## The Morning Programme

The Guru Purnima programme at the Sansthan begins in the early morning with the Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM, followed by a special puja in the main temple that honours the guru-disciple tradition. The temple priests conduct the puja with traditional materials and recite the mantras that are associated with the guru, and devotees participate by offering their own prayers of gratitude to their teachers. The [shegaon-darshan-timing-guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-timing-guide) provides the complete schedule of the Guru Purnima programme.

The morning programme at the Samadhi is particularly well-attended on Guru Purnima. Many devotees visit the Samadhi to offer their prayers at the place where Shri Gajanan Maharaj attained mahasamadhi, and the experience of standing at the Samadhi on this day carries a particular devotional depth. The quiet contemplative atmosphere of the Samadhi — surrounded by the old banyan trees and the peaceful garden — is well-suited to the reflective nature of the Guru Purnima observance. Devotees who arrive before 6:00 AM can participate in the morning programme at both the main temple and the Samadhi, and this early visit is one of the most meaningful devotional practices of the year.

## The Devotional Discourses

The afternoon programme at the Sansthan on Guru Purnima includes devotional discourses on the significance of the guru in the Hindu spiritual tradition. The speakers draw on traditional texts including the Guru Gita — a section of the Skanda Purana that is dedicated to the guru — to explain the philosophical foundation of the guru-disciple relationship. The discourses are open to all devotees and are typically conducted in Marathi, with summaries available for devotees who speak other languages. The afternoon discourses typically last for approximately two hours, and they include time for devotees to ask questions and to share their own experiences of the guru-disciple relationship.

## The Evening Programme

The evening programme on Guru Purnima includes a special aarti and the distribution of prasad. The prasad on Guru Purnima is considered especially blessed, and many devotees take portions home to share with family members and to offer to their own spiritual teachers. The Mahaprasad Hall serves a special meal that includes traditional festive items, and the [shegaon-canteen-and-mahaprasad-guide](/blog/shegaon-canteen-and-mahaprasad-guide) covers the complete range of food offerings during the festival. The evening aarti at the Samadhi is particularly well-attended, and devotees who participate in the complete programme describe the experience of the evening aarti in the quiet atmosphere of the Samadhi as one of the most spiritually significant moments of their visit.

## The Monsoon Atmosphere

Guru Purnima falls at the beginning of the monsoon season at Shegaon, when the first heavy rains have typically arrived and the countryside is lush and green. The Anand Sagar lake is filling with the early monsoon rains, and the temple gardens are at the beginning of their seasonal growth. The cool monsoon air and the lush green landscape create an atmosphere that many devotees describe as one of the most spiritually charged of the year. The combination of the devotional programme and the natural beauty of the monsoon countryside creates an experience that is both spiritually meaningful and aesthetically rewarding.

## Weather and Preparation

Guru Purnima falls in July, which is the early monsoon season at Shegaon. Daytime temperatures range between 24 and 30 degrees Celsius, with moderate to heavy rainfall. Devotees should bring rain protection and wear waterproof footwear for the outdoor portions of the programme.

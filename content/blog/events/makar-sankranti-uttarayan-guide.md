There is a particular quality to the sunlight on January 14th at Shegaon that anyone who has spent a Makar Sankranti at the Sansthan will recognize. It is the first day of the sun's northward movement — Uttarayan in the traditional astronomical language — and the warmth of the sunlight has a clarity that is distinct from the colder December days that preceded it. The sesame sweets — til-gud laddus — are being prepared in homes across the Buldhana countryside, and the fragrance of fresh sesame seeds roasting with jaggery fills the morning air near the temple. Kites are being flown from rooftops and open grounds in Shegaon town, and the sky above the Sansthan is often criss-crossed with the coloured threads of kite strings from early morning until sunset. This is the festival of Makar Sankranti at Shegaon — a celebration that combines harvest thanksgiving, the astronomical transition of the seasons, and the cultural joy of kite flying in a way that is uniquely Indian and specifically Maharashtrian.

## The Cultural Programme

Makar Sankranti at the Sansthan is one of the most community-oriented festivals of the year. The morning begins with the regular Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM, followed by special abhishek and aartis in the main temple. The Anand Saga grounds host the cultural programme from late morning until sunset, including kite-flying competitions, traditional games, and community meals. Devotees who arrive early in the morning can witness the darshan and then spend the rest of the day participating in the cultural programmes that make Makar Sankranti at Shegaon a festival that is both devotional and genuinely enjoyable.

The [shegaon-darshan-timing-guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-timing-guide) provides the complete schedule of aarti and darshan times for Makar Sankranti. The Sansthan arranges a special programme that includes the distribution of til-gud prasad to all devotees, which is the characteristic sweet of the festival. The prasad is prepared by the temple priests according to traditional methods and is considered especially blessed on this day.

## The Sesame Tradition

Til-gud — sesame seeds and jaggery — is the characteristic food offering of Makar Sankranti across Maharashtra. The combination of sesame and jaggery is associated with the quality of sweetness and warmth that the festival represents, and devotees exchange these sweets as a gesture of goodwill. The Mahaprasad Hall at Shegaon serves a special Makar Sankranti meal that includes til-gud laddus, sesame ladoos, and other traditional festive items. The [shegaon-canteen-and-mahaprasad-guide](/blog/shegaon-canteen-and-mahaprasad-guide) covers the complete range of food offerings during the festival.

The tradition of til-gud has a deeper significance that many devotees appreciate. Sesame seeds — til — are associated with the warmth and goodness of the sun, and their consumption during the cold month of January is considered both nutritionally and spiritually beneficial. The combination with jaggery — gud — adds the quality of sweetness that represents the joy and abundance of the harvest season. The preparation of til-gud at the Sansthan follows traditional recipes that have been passed down through the generations, and the prasad that is distributed on Makar Sankranti is made by hand by the temple kitchen staff starting early in the morning.

## Kite Flying at Shegaon

The kite-flying tradition of Makar Sankranti is one of the most distinctive cultural features of the festival in Maharashtra. At Shegaon, the Anand Saga grounds become a vast open-air kite-flying venue from late morning until sunset. Devotees and local Shegaon residents participate in the kite competitions, and the air is filled with colourful kites of every shape and size. For families with children, this is one of the most memorable aspects of a Makar Sankranti visit to Shegaon. The [shegaon-kids-friendly-visit-guide](/blog/shegaon-kids-friendly-visit-guide) provides specific advice for families planning to participate in the kite-flying programmes.

The kite-flying tradition of Uttarayan has deep roots in Indian cultural history, and the kite competitions at Shegaon follow the traditional format. The most common type of kite is the patang — the traditional diamond-shaped kite — and the competition involves cutting down other kites by manoeuvring one's own kite. The ground-level competition is accompanied by the excitement of the crowd watching from the sidelines, and the prizes are awarded to the kite that remains aloft the longest and the kite that successfully cuts the most opponents.

## The Harvest Significance

Makar Sankranti is fundamentally a harvest festival, and at Shegaon — located in the cotton-growing region of Vidarbha — the festival marks the end of the cotton harvesting season. The countryside around the temple is dotted with cotton fields, and the festival is celebrated by the farming community with particular enthusiasm. The Sansthan's programme includes recognition of the agricultural community, and many farmers travel from across the Buldhana district to participate in the temple programmes on this day.

## The Temple Decoration

The Sansthan decorates the temple for Makar Sankranti with traditional rangoli designs at the temple entrance and along the pathways. The rangoli at Shegaon during Makar Sankranti is typically larger and more elaborate than during other festivals, reflecting the special significance of the day. The designs are created by the temple volunteers and community members in the early morning hours, and the completed rangoli is a visual highlight of the festival. Many devotees photograph the rangoli designs as a memento of their visit.

## Weather and Preparation

Makar Sankranti falls in mid-January, which is the coldest month at Shegaon. Daytime temperatures range between 18 and 25 degrees Celsius, and the morning and evening hours are noticeably cool. This is the most comfortable weather of the year for outdoor programmes, and devotees should bring warm clothing for the early morning and evening hours. The cool January weather is ideal for spending long hours outdoors, and many devotees arrive at the Anand Saga grounds in the morning and remain there throughout the kite-flying programme. The mid-day sun is warm enough for comfortable sitting, and the breeze from the lake helps to keep the temperature pleasant.

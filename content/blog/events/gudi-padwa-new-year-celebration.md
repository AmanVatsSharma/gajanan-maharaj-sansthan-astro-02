The first day of the Chaitra month arrives at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan with a quality of fresh beginning that devotees who have experienced Gudi Padwa at Shegaon will recognise instantly. The temple grounds are decorated with fresh flowers and rangoli patterns, the sound of traditional devotional music fills the Anand Saga grounds, and families from across Maharashtra have gathered to mark the Marathi New Year with a combination of devotional programme and festive celebration. Gudi Padwa — the traditional new year celebration of Maharashtra — falls at the time when the spring season is at its most vibrant, and at the Sansthan the observance includes the raising of the Gudi — the sacred flag that symbolises victory and prosperity — alongside the regular temple programme that gives the festival its devotional foundation.

## The Significance of Gudi Padwa

Gudi Padwa is the traditional new year celebration of Maharashtra, and it is observed on the first day of the Chaitra month of the Hindu lunisolar calendar. The festival is associated with several mythological and historical events — the coronation of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, the beginning of the spring season, the victory of good over evil — and it is celebrated across Maharashtra with the raising of the Gudi in every household. The Gudi is a decorated flag that is raised on the first day of the festival, and it is accompanied by the distribution of sweets and the performance of puja ceremonies. At the Sansthan, the Gudi Padwa observance includes the raising of the Gudi at the temple alongside the regular devotional programme, creating a festival that combines the festive atmosphere of the new year celebration with the focused devotional practice of the temple programme.

## The Gudi Hoisting Ceremony

The Gudi hoisting ceremony at the Sansthan is one of the central events of the Gudi Padwa programme. The ceremony is conducted at the main temple and at the Samadhi, and it involves the ritual raising of the Gudi — a decorated flag that is accompanied by traditional materials including neem leaves, flowers, and sacred thread. The temple priests conduct the hoisting ceremony with appropriate Vedic mantras, and the atmosphere during the ceremony is one of festive devotional energy. The Gudi hoisting at the Samadhi is particularly well-attended, and devotees gather at the Samadhi grounds to witness the complete ceremony.

The Gudi hoisting ceremony is typically conducted at approximately 8:00 AM on the main day, after the Mangala Aarti has been completed. The ceremony is accompanied by the blowing of conch shells and the ringing of temple bells, and the Gudi is raised to a specific height that is determined by the temple priests according to the traditional guidelines. The raised Gudi remains in place throughout the festival period, serving as a visible marker of the devotional atmosphere at the Sansthan.

## The Devotional Programme

The Gudi Padwa programme at the Sansthan includes the regular morning aarti at 4:00 AM, followed by the special abhishek ceremony that is conducted for the Gudi Padwa observance. The temple priests conduct the abhishek with traditional materials including milk, honey, and flowers, and the ceremony is accompanied by the recitation of traditional mantras. The afternoon programme includes a special puja that invokes the blessings of the new year, and many devotees participate in the puja by making personal offerings to the deity. ## The Community Programme

The community programme at the Anand Saga grounds during Gudi Padwa includes traditional Marathi cultural performances, community games, and the distribution of festive sweets. The programme is designed to be accessible to devotees of all ages, and it creates an atmosphere of communal celebration that complements the devotional programme at the temple. The community programme typically begins at approximately 10:00 AM and continues until late afternoon, with the Anand Saga grounds filled with families who have come to celebrate the new year at the temple.

## The Significance of the Gudi

The Gudi — the sacred flag that is the central symbol of Gudi Padwa — represents victory, prosperity, and the triumph of good over evil. The Gudi at the Sansthan is typically a tall bamboo pole topped with a bright cloth, decorated with garlands, neem leaves, and a copper or silver kalash. The raising of the Gudi at the temple is accompanied by Vedic mantras that invoke blessings for the new year, and the ceremony is considered especially auspicious when performed at a temple that is associated with a revered saint. Many devotees consider the Gudi hoisting ceremony at the Sansthan to be more spiritually significant than the Gudi hoisting at their homes, and they make the pilgrimage to Shegaon specifically for the ceremony.

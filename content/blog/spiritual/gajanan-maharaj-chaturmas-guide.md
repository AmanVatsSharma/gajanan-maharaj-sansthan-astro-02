# Gajanan Maharaj Chaturmas Guide

Chaturmas — the four holy months from Devshayani Ekadashi in the month of Ashadha to Prabodhini Ekadashi in Kartik — is the year's most sustained season of devotion at Shegaon: **Shravan Mondays and ekadashis, parayans of the Gajanan Vijay, Ganesh Chaturthi, the Gajanan Vijay Utsav of Bhadrapad, Navratri and Diwali, all falling inside one continuous arc of practice**. In 2026 the season runs from Ashadhi Ekadashi on 6 July to Kartik Ekadashi on 2 November, per the Sansthan's festival calendar. This guide explains what the four months ask of a household, how Shegaon lives through them, and how to plan a monsoon-season pilgrimage around them.

## The four months, plainly explained

Chaturmas means simply "four months". Tradition holds that with Devshayani Ekadashi — the ekadashi on which Vishnu is said to rest — the earth enters a quieter register: wandering sants halt in one place, auspicious new beginnings are deferred, and the energies of devotion turn inward. The season ends on Prabodhini Ekadashi in Kartik, the awakening, when movement resumes. The four months thus have a shape of their own: a gathering-in, a long sustained middle, and an opening-out.

For household devotees the shape translates into practice. The weeks of Shravan carry daily worship and Monday observance; the ekadashis that arrive roughly fortnightly punctuate the whole season with fasts and special darshan; and the festival cluster — Ganesh Chaturthi, the Gajanan Vijay Utsav, Navratri, Diwali — gives the middle months their crescendos. Nothing in the season is compulsory; everything in it is cumulative. Families often describe chaturmas as the one period when their scattered devotional habits briefly become a single rhythm.

The singing year follows the same arc, as our guide to the [bhajans of the tradition](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-bhajans-collection) describes — the singing of Shravan is not the singing of Navratri, and neither is the singing of the Kartik awakening.

## Why chaturmas suits this saint's story

There is a quiet fitness in keeping chaturmas at Shegaon. The tradition remembers Shri Gajanan Maharaj as a saint who had, in effect, made chaturmas permanent: he arrived in February 1878 and, apart from his wanderings around the town's lanes, stayed put — three decades of daily morning darshan from one small place. Devotees mark the irony gently. The season when all wandering sants are asked to halt was, for him, simply the manner of his entire ministry.

The granth's episodes give the season its local texture. Tradition recounts his indifference to rain and sun, his presence at the places where food was cooked and served, his attention to the town's animals during the wet months. A devotee keeping chaturmas at Shegaon is therefore not importing a generic observance; the four months read differently when walked in the lanes where their subject lived them. The fuller chronology is in our [biography of the saint of Shegaon](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-biography-life-story-shegaon).

The monsoon also deepens the season's traditional associations with food and shelter. Chaturmas is, across Maharashtra, the season of annadan — the rains were historically the hungry months between sowings, and the sants who halted in one place were fed by, and fed, the households around them. At Shegaon the tradition's own annadan continues through the season at its largest scale, and devotees commonly treat a chaturmas contribution to the kitchen as part of the observance itself. It is one of the few elements of the season that can be kept from anywhere, at any distance, and families far from Vidarbha often name it as the piece of chaturmas they would least willingly drop.

A third suitability is quieter. The four months ask for staying, and staying — through weather, through tedium, through the temptation to move on — was itself the saint's demonstration. Devotees who struggle to hold a steady practice through a long season take the point personally: the tradition they are keeping was founded by someone who showed, over three decades in one town, what staying looks like.

## A household chaturmas, step by step

The season rewards a plan made once, at the beginning, rather than resolve renewed weekly.

1. **Make the sankalp on Devshayani Ekadashi.** Decide, as a family, what the four months will hold — one parayan, one ekadashi discipline, one act of seva — and write it down. The tradition treats a spoken sankalp as the season's true opening; the same logic is explained in our article on [sankalp before yatra](/blog/importance-of-sankalp-before-yatra).
2. **Set the parayan arc.** Many families begin a Gajanan Vijay parayan in Shravan and conclude it at Prabodhini, letting the granth's twenty-one adhyays span the season. The formats and vidhi are in the [Gajanan Vijay parayan guide](/blog/gajanan-vijay-granth-parayan-complete-guide).
3. **Keep the fortnightly ekadashis.** Fasting as health and household allow, a simple meal, evening aarti — the ekadashi rhythm is what makes the season feel continuous rather than a run of festivals.
4. **Anchor one seva.** Chaturmas is the traditional season for giving; devotees commonly dedicate one recurring act — a monthly annadan contribution, service at a local gathering — for the duration.
5. **Close deliberately at Prabodhini.** The concluding ekadashi deserves its own small ceremony: the parayan's final adhyay, a family review of what the four months actually held, and the next season loosely imagined.

A word on failure, because four months is long. The tradition's own guidance, reflected in how parayan lapses are handled, is to continue from where you stand rather than restart in a bid for perfection — a missed ekadashi is a missed ekadashi, not a broken season. Households that build recovery into the plan keep the whole arc; households that demand an unbroken record usually abandon it by Bhadrapad. The season is a monsoon, not a machine: it accumulates.

Two small patterns make the household season real. The first is visibility — the granth kept where the family actually sits, the sankalp paper on the shrine, the calendar marked. What is visible is kept; what is filed is forgotten. The second is pairing: assign each element of the plan to a person. The parayan belongs to whoever rises first; the ekadashi meal to whoever cooks; the children hold the aarti refrain. A season carried by one tired adult does not usually reach Prabodhini, and a season carried by everyone does.

## Visiting Shegaon inside the season

Chaturmas is the monsoon at Shegaon, and the rains change both the town's face and a pilgrim's arithmetic. The countryside of the Buldhana district turns green; the air cools; and the crowds move in the pattern the table above implies — light on ordinary weekdays, gathering on every Shravan Monday and every ekadashi, and at their heaviest around the September utsav. Rooms through the season are requested via the [booking page](/booking), with the September window planned months rather than weeks ahead; the [Bhakta Niwas complete booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) walks through the request itself.

For the rains, the practicalities are ordinary and worth respecting: footwear with grip, a compact umbrella per adult, one full change of clothing per person packed dry, and basic cold remedies within reach. The full checklist is in our [pilgrimage essentials guide](/blog/what-to-carry-pilgrimage-essentials). Trains reach the Shegaon–Akola belt from Mumbai, Pune and Nagpur; the routes are detailed in the [complete Shegaon travel guide](/blog/shegaon-travel-guide), and the location details of the complex — rooms, facilities, placement — are on the [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas page](/locations/shegaon-bhakt-niwas).

Families travelling with elders should read the season generously. A monsoon chaturmas visit asks for slower mornings, a room within easy reach of the temple approach, and a plan that treats the evening aarti as the day's fixed point rather than chasing every programme. The non-AC rooms that suit the Vidarbha winter are equally the natural choice in the wet months, when daytime temperatures sit well below their April peaks; the AC rows earn their premium only in the crowded festival windows, when a cool room to recover in becomes part of the day's strategy. Under the 24-hour check-out cycle counted from entry, a family arriving by an evening train holds its room through the following evening — which frames a two-night chaturmas stay around aartis rather than around checkout anxieties.

One scheduling counsel belongs here rather than in a footnote: do not try to see everything in one chaturmas visit. The season's density defeats the attempt, and the families who return year on year — arriving for one Shravan Monday this year, the utsav the next, Prabodhini after that — speak of the season as a relationship rather than a checklist. Shegaon rewards the returning; the rooms are requested the same way every time, through the [booking page](/booking), with anything unusual routed through the [contact page](/contact).

## Frequently asked questions

### What exactly is chaturmas?

The four months from Devshayani Ekadashi in Ashadha to Prabodhini Ekadashi in Kartik, during which tradition holds that wandering sants stay in one place and devotional life turns inward. In 2026 the season runs from 6 July to 2 November per the Sansthan's festival calendar.

### Is chaturmas a good time to visit Shegaon?

It is the most rewarding season and the most demanding one. The town is green and the observances are dense, but Shravan Mondays, ekadashis and the September utsav fill rooms early. Ordinary weekdays of the season are among the year's pleasantest visits.

### What should a household actually do during chaturmas?

The classic pattern is a sankalp at Devshayani, a Gajanan Vijay parayan spread across the season, the fortnightly ekadashis kept, one recurring act of seva, and a deliberate close at Prabodhini. The parayan mechanics are in our [parayan guide](/blog/gajanan-vijay-granth-parayan-complete-guide).

### How busy is the Gajanan Vijay Utsav within chaturmas?

It is the heaviest single date in the Shegaon year — lakhs of devotees, with accommodation requested months ahead. The full picture, including how the utsav is observed, is in our [punyatithi guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-punyatithi-guide).

### Any special packing for a monsoon-season visit?

Grippy footwear, rain protection, dry-packed clothing and basic remedies; the complete list is in the [pilgrimage essentials guide](/blog/what-to-carry-pilgrimage-essentials). Anything date-specific — darshan windows, programme details — should be confirmed with the Sansthan office via the [contact page](/contact).

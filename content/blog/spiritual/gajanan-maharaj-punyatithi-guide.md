# Gajanan Maharaj Punyatithi Guide

The punyatithi of Shri Gajanan Maharaj — the anniversary of his mahasamadhi of 1910 — is kept at Shegaon as the **Gajanan Vijay Utsav, observed on Bhadrapad Shukla Chaturdashi; in 2026 the festival calendar marks it on 8 September**, when lakhs of devotees converge on the town for special darshan, bhajans and mahaprasad. It is the heaviest single date of the Shegaon year, and the one the Sansthan's own guidance asks devotees to plan months rather than days ahead. This guide explains the observance's meaning, what happens at Shegaon in that window, and how to plan a family's visit or a home observance around it.

## What a punyatithi holds in this tradition

A punyatithi is the anniversary of a great soul's passing — the tithi on which a life of devotion is understood to have merged into what it worshipped. For saints in the bhakti traditions of Maharashtra, the punyatithi is not a mourning; it is the louder of the two annual anniversaries, the jayanti being the quieter remembrance of arrival. The logic is devotional rather than morbid: what the devotee celebrates is the completeness of a life and the continued availability of its grace. At Shegaon this general logic has a particular edge, because the tradition's own theology of its saint — a jnani, one never truly born — makes the punyatithi almost the natural festival: the day the visible form was folded away and what the form pointed to remained.

Devotees who keep both anniversaries describe the difference in register precisely. The February window around Pragat Din, described in our [jayanti celebration guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-jayanti-celebration-guide), feels like a family remembering a patriarch's arrival; the Bhadrapad utsav feels like the town itself in full voice. Neither displaces the other, but families with one long visit a year to give usually choose the Bhadrapad window for its intensity.

The pattern is not unique to Shegaon. Across Maharashtra, the punyatithis of the great saints have historically outgrown their jayantis — the wari to Pandharpur gathers on Ashadhi Ekadashi, and the great Datta observances cluster around Datta Jayanti — because a saint's passing is when the community, rather than the saint, becomes the visible actor. A punyatithi is in this sense the devotees' festival: the day the tradition demonstrates that it can carry what it received. At Shegaon, where the saint left no successor and founded no institution, the point is structural. Everything the pilgrim sees in the utsav week — the queues marshalled, the kitchens running, the parayans held, the singing organised — is the work of the community the grace called into being, doing annually what it does every day, at the year's largest scale.

## The morning tradition remembers

The saint's passing in 1910 is remembered in the tradition's accounts as quiet. The Gajanan Vijay and the devotional retellings around it recount a saint in declining health over his final months, settling affairs with characteristic brevity, and then a morning on which he sat in meditation and did not return — no crowd, no announcement, no ceremony at the moment itself. Devotees present described a stillness that stayed. We narrate this as the tradition recounts it; the quietness of the account is itself part of what devotees draw from it, the final demonstration of a life that never needed spectacle.

What followed, over the following years, was the counter-movement the quiet deserved: the gathering of devotees, the composition and spread of the granth by Das Ganu Maharaj, and eventually the Samadhi Mandir itself, around which the town's spiritual life now orbits. The punyatithi utsav is best understood as that counter-movement made annual — the community's answer, every Bhadrapad, to a silence it has never finished hearing. The full chronology of the samadhi period is in our [biography of the saint](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-biography-life-story-shegaon), and the Bhadrapad fortnight's companion observance — the Rishi Panchami programme at the Samadhi — is described in its own [Samadhi Utsav guide](/blog/rishi-panchami-shegaon-samadhi-utsav-guide).

## The utsav as Shegaon lives it

The festival calendar's own description of the day — special darshan, bhajans and mahaprasad for lakhs of devotees — understates the transformation. For roughly a week on either side of the tithi, Shegaon functions as a temporary city. Trains deposit pilgrims through the night; the walking routes from the station to the temple complex fill before dawn; the Bhakta Niwas blocks run at capacity; and the annadan scales to a size that has to be seen to be credited. The darshan queues move for hours in lines that the Sansthan's volunteers marshal with practiced patience.

Three features give the utsav its particular character. First, the density of parayan: the Gajanan Vijay is recited continuously through the window, and a household concluding its parayan at the utsav is a common and deliberate pattern — the granth's concluding dedication of merit, as our [parayan guide](/blog/gajanan-vijay-granth-parayan-complete-guide) explains, is felt most fully in the granth's own town. Second, the singing: bhajan groups from across Vidarbha and beyond arrive as groups, and the corridor-and-courtyard culture described in our [bhajan collection guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-bhajans-collection) reaches its annual maximum. Third, the mahaprasad, which devotees treat not as catering but as the day's central sacrament — the leelas of the unfailing kitchens, recounted in our [miracles and stories guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-miracles-stories), made present tense.

A first-timer's account of the day usually runs in hours. The town is awake before it is light; by the time the morning darshan queues have their shape, the singing has started and does not really stop; the middle of the day belongs to the parayans and to the long, good-humoured business of feeding everyone; and the evening aarti gathers the day into a single flame-lit hour that even devoted regulars describe as unlike the evening aarti of any ordinary week. What surprises visitors most, they tend to say, is not the scale but the composure — a town of lakhs moving slowly, and a volunteer system that has done this many times before. The right mental preparation is not endurance but surrender of the clock: build nothing into the day that requires being somewhere on time, and the day will organize itself around you.

One honest caveat belongs beside the praise. The utsav window is demanding for anyone unwell, very small, or averse to dense crowds, and the tradition itself would counsel such devotees to the quieter Bhadrapad days on either side of the tithi — or to the February window instead. The grace, devotees insist, does not check the calendar; the planning should simply respect the bodies making the journey.

## A devotee's day at the utsav

1. **Arrive the evening before, not the morning of.** The pre-dawn hours of the utsav day are its best; a family that slept in Shegaon wakes inside the festival rather than commuting into it.
2. **Take the morning darshan early and unhurriedly.** Expect queues measured in hours on the peak day; the tradition's own counsel is to treat the queue as the darshan's first part — the singing usually handles the waiting, as our [chanting during travel and waiting](/blog/chanting-during-travel-and-waiting) article reminds devotees.
3. **Sit with a parayan if one is running.** Listening to a stretch of the granth in company is the utsav at its most authentic, and no Marathi is needed to sit.
4. **Take the mahaprasad seated, and give if you can.** The annadan lines are the day's heart; contributing to them from wherever you are is equally part of the observance.
5. **Keep the evening aarti as the day's close.** The day's aartis and their sequence are set out in our [Shegaon aarti timings guide](/blog/shegaon-aarti-timings-daily-schedule); confirm the day's schedule with the temple office via the [contact page](/contact), since utsav days bend the ordinary pattern.

## Keeping the punyatithi at home

Distance does not disqualify a household from the utsav. The classic home observance combines three elements: a Gajanan Vijay parayan concluding on or near the tithi, the aarti sung at the household shrine in the evening, and an annadan contribution made through the Sansthan — reached via the [contact page](/contact) — as the outward seal. Many families add a fast or a single simple meal, and a reading of the samadhi chapters of the granth. The parayan formats and the concluding vidhi are fully set out in the [parayan guide](/blog/gajanan-vijay-granth-parayan-complete-guide); what the home observance lacks in scale it recovers in steadiness, and devotees commonly alternate — a home year, a Shegaon year.

A note on children and the home observance, since the tithi falls inside the school term: keep their part small and physical. A child asked to hold the lamp, wave the aarti plate, or recite the refrain has a role; a child asked to sit through two hours of reading has a sentence. Families who let the youngest member choose the day's one abhang, and lead its refrain, report that the utsav becomes the date the child asks about by name — which is the entire tradition secured for another generation, one September at a time.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is Gajanan Maharaj's punyatithi observed?

On Bhadrapad Shukla Chaturdashi, as the Gajanan Vijay Utsav; the festival calendar marks it on 8 September in 2026. The mahasamadhi it commemorates took place in 1910.

### How is the punyatithi different from the jayanti?

The jayanti window in February remembers the saint's appearance in Shegaon in 1878 and keeps a contemplative register; the punyatithi in Bhadrapad is the town-transforming utsav — the larger, louder of the two annual observances.

### How far in advance should rooms be booked for the utsav?

Months. The Sansthan's own guidance for this window is the longest lead time of the Shegaon year; requests sent three to four months out behave completely differently from ones sent weeks out. Begin at the [booking page](/booking).

### Can we observe the punyatithi without travelling?

Yes — a concluding parayan, the evening aarti at home, and an annadan contribution made through the office constitute a complete observance. Many families alternate a home year with a Shegaon year.

### Is the utsav suitable for elderly devotees and small children?

With planning, yes — but the peak day is genuinely crowded. Families with elders commonly choose the days either side of the tithi, hold a room within easier reach of the complex, and treat the evening aarti as the one non-negotiable. The [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas page](/locations/shegaon-bhakt-niwas) carries the placement details.

The evening aarti on the Narmada river at Omkareshwar is the spiritual climax of any visit to the Jyotirlinga. As the sun sets over the Narmada valley, the ghats come alive with the sound of bells, conch shells, and devotional chanting. The priests perform the aarti with great devotion, and the entire temple complex across the river is visible from the ghat. The floating lamps on the river, the reflection of the temple lights on the water, and the collective chanting of the assembled devotees create an atmosphere that many describe as unforgettable. This guide covers the aarti timing, the procedure, what to expect, and the practical tips that help you make the most of this experience. ## Aarti Timing and Procedure

The Narmada river aarti at Omkareshwar typically takes place around sunset, with the timing varying throughout the year. During summer, the aarti typically starts around 7:00 to 7:30 PM, and during winter, it starts around 6:00 to 6:30 PM. The aarti lasts around 30 to 45 minutes. The priests perform the aarti with five diyas, flowers, incense, and the chanting of Shiva mantras. The aarti is followed by the distribution of prasadam to the assembled devotees. The procedure is consistent across seasons, with minor variations during festival periods. The Sansthan arranges for the aarti to be performed at a specially decorated platform on the ghat, with the priests facing the river and the temple across the water.

## The Ghat Experience

The ghats along the Narmada at Omkareshwar are the best vantage point for the evening aarti. The ghats are well-maintained, with steps leading down to the river and space for hundreds of devotees to sit. The view from the ghats is panoramic — the Mamleshwar temple on the mainland, the Omkar Mandhata temple on the island, and the Narmada river connecting the two. As the aarti begins, the river comes alive with floating lamps, and the reflection of the temple lights on the water creates a breathtaking visual. The ghats are also the starting point for the boat ride to the island, and the boatmen continue to operate the service during the aarti, providing a unique view from the water. Devotees who have experienced the aarti from both the ghat and the island say that the ghat experience is more immersive, as it includes the sound of the river, the wind, and the collective chanting of the assembled crowd.

## Seasonal Variations

During the monsoon season, the aarti experience is particularly dramatic. The Narmada is in full flow, and the sound of the river mixed with the chanting creates a powerful atmosphere. The water level is higher during the monsoon, and the current is stronger, making the floating lamps drift away faster. The priests adjust their technique to compensate for the current, and the aarti becomes a dynamic, ever-changing performance. The monsoon clouds add to the dramatic atmosphere, and the ghats are often crowded with devotees who have come specifically for the monsoon aarti. During Karthik Purnima, the aarti is extended and special arrangements are made for the large crowds. During Mahashivratri, the aarti continues throughout the night, with priests performing continuous worship on the ghats. For festival planning, see our [omkareshwar-festival-season-guide].

## What to Expect During the Aarti

Arrive at the ghat at least 15 to 20 minutes before the aarti begins to secure a good spot. The ghats fill up quickly, particularly during festivals and weekends. Bring a small offering of flowers or fruits if you wish to participate in the ritual. The priests typically accept flowers and distribute prasadam after the aarti. The aarti is open to all devotees, regardless of background or tradition, and everyone is welcome to participate. Devotees who attend the aarti regularly say that each experience is unique — the combination of the chanting, the river, the lamps, and the assembled crowd creates a different spiritual moment every time. The collective energy of the assembled devotees is palpable, and even first-time visitors often feel a deep sense of peace and connection during the aarti. ## Photography During the Aarti

Photography is permitted during the aarti from the ghats, but avoid using flash, as it can be disruptive to the spiritual atmosphere. The floating lamps, the temple reflections on the river, and the assembled devotees make for compelling photographs, but remember to be respectful of the devotional space around you. If you want to capture the aarti from the water, take the last boat ride before the aarti begins and ask the boatmen to take you close to the ghat. Some devotees prefer to experience the aarti without photography, believing that the moment is too precious to be captured through a lens. This is a personal choice, and the priests do not pressure devotees either way.

## After the Aarti

After the aarti concludes, many devotees walk along the ghats, enjoying the evening breeze and the peaceful atmosphere. The local markets near the ghat open up in the evening, offering prasadam, snacks, and religious items. For many devotees, the walk along the Narmada after the aarti is the most peaceful part of the Omkareshwar experience. The temple bells continue to ring softly, and the river reflects the last light of the setting sun, creating a scene of deep tranquility. Devotees who stay late at the ghat after the aarti often describe it as the most memorable part of their visit.

## Devotee Preparation Tips

Before attending the aarti, visit the Omkar Mandhata temple on the island for darshan. This creates a complete spiritual circuit — the temple darshan in the morning and the aarti on the ghat in the evening. Wear comfortable clothing, as the ghat can be crowded and the steps can be slippery. Bring a small towel to wipe your hands after the aarti, as you may have touched flowers or other offerings. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: At what time does the Narmada aarti start at Omkareshwar?**
The aarti starts around sunset, typically around 7:00 to 7:30 PM during summer and 6:00 to 6:30 PM during winter. Check the official Sansthan website for the exact timing during your visit.

**Q: Is the Narmada aarti open to all devotees?**
Yes, the aarti is open to all devotees, regardless of background or tradition. Everyone is welcome to participate and receive prasadam.

**Q: Can I take photographs during the aarti?**
Yes, photography is permitted from the ghats. Avoid using flash, and be respectful of the devotional atmosphere. Photography is not allowed from within the temple during the aarti.

**Q: Is there an entry fee for the aarti?**
No, the aarti is free for all devotees. There is no entry fee or ticket required. The only cost is for the boat ride to the island if you want to experience the aarti from the temple side.

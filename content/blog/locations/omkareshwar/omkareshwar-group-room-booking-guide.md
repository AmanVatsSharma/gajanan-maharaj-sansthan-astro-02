# Omkareshwar Group Room Booking Guide

A group — a busload of bhajan mandals, a temple society's annual yatra, three families travelling as one party — books at Omkareshwar by the same honest arithmetic as everyone else: rooms of two, three, and four beds from the network tariff card, requested together as a single party with a single voice, so that the Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan desk can hold the cluster in one gesture rather than juggle a dozen strays. This guide is the coordinator's handbook: how a crowd is housed, how the room arithmetic scales, why one correspondent beats five enthusiastic ones, and what the festival calendar does to a group's chances. The group request begins at the [booking page](/booking); coordinators are heard at the [contact page](/contact).

The yatra-side of group travel — the itinerary, the bus, the darshan sequencing for a crowd — has its own pages: our [group yatra planning guide for Omkareshwar](/blog/omkareshwar-group-yatra-planning) and the [group darshan booking](/blog/omkareshwar-group-darshan-booking) companion. This page keeps strictly to the beds.

## When the party outgrows the family room

Draw the line first, because groups waste energy on both sides of it. The Sansthan's rooms are reserved for families — it is the network's founding arrangement, and it is what keeps every compound quiet, safe, and early-rising. A party of ten relatives across three households is a group of families, and the compound receives it gladly as such: three family rooms, requested together. A party of ten friends or society members, none of them a family unit, is a different kind of pilgrim body, and the desk will guide it toward the shared dormitory-style shelter the network keeps for exactly this devotion — the walking-party tradition in which strangers become congregation by nightfall.

Neither path is second-class; they are different doors into the same courtyard. But the coordinator who understands the distinction before writing spares the office a round of explanation and the group a round of expectation. When in doubt, state the party's true composition plainly and let the desk place it — the office's answer to an honest description is always better than its answer to a vague one.

## How the compound houses a crowd

A group's shelter at Omkareshwar is a cluster, not a wing. The [location](/locations/omkareshwar) holds its family rooms across a compact compound minutes from the island's ghats, and a group of thirty occupies it as a constellation: four-bed rooms next to three-bed rooms next to two-bed rooms, families and family-rooms paired by the coordinator's list, the whole party inside one another's calling distance. There is no banquet hall of identical chambers, and groups that expect one have mistaken a sansthan for a hotel — the compensation being that the desk knows every guest by name, which no hotel desk in the district does.

The cluster's texture is the group's friend. The bhojan kaksha seats a party in waves at meal hours — plain, sattvic, and forgiving of large numbers — which is how bus crowds actually eat. The parking takes the bus. The hot water, in every attached bathroom, absorbs a crowd's sequential baths across the dawn without ceremony. And the desk, warned honestly about a group's size and arrival hour, stages the check-in so that thirty pilgrims do not hit one register at once. Each stay runs its own full day from the hour of entry, so a group arriving together holds its cluster of rooms until the same hour next day — an ordinary fact that becomes logistically beautiful when the departure is also communal.

## The arithmetic of many rooms

The tariff card, scaled by the coordinator's ledger:

| Party | One way to house it | Nightly total, ventilated rows |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (two families) | 4 Bed + 4 Bed | ₹4,500 |
| 10 (two families + a couple) | 4 Bed + 4 Bed + 2 Bed | ₹6,150 |
| 12 (three families) | 4 Bed + 4 Bed + 4 Bed | ₹6,750 |
| 16 (four families) | Four 4 Bed rooms | ₹9,000 |
| 20+ (society or mandal) | Family rooms for the family units; shared shelter for the rest | By the list |

Read the table as a starter, not a verdict — the cooled rows add their premiums in the hot months (₹2,050 and ₹2,550 against ₹1,750 and ₹2,250), the exact mix follows the party's true composition, and the office will check any proposed arithmetic against the chart before confirming. The numbers exist to teach the coordinator the one lesson that matters: the group booking is a list, not a number. "Rooms for sixteen" is a hope; "four four-bed rooms, one household to a room, named" is a booking.

## One voice: choosing the coordinator

Every group has several natural writers and exactly one useful one. The desk's files on failed group bookings are nearly all the same file: five committee members sent five requests for the same bus, the office answered the first, confirmed the second, and the other three became contradictions the group discovered at the register. The network's rule for groups is the network's rule for everything, sharpened — one channel, one message, one correspondent.

Choose the coordinator for virtues the role actually needs: the person who has the complete list, answers their phone, and will still be enthusiastic on the return journey. Give that one person the party's names, the rooming intentions, the dates, and the authority to accept the office's answer on the group's behalf. Everyone else's job is the harder one — patience. The etiquette between acknowledgement and confirmation is a waiting virtue at family scale and a group-scale discipline, and the coordinator who follows up once, politely, with the reference in hand, is served faster than the committee that writes daily. The full grammar of requests, acknowledgements, and confirmations stands in the [complete Bhakta Niwas booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide).

## The group request, written properly

The coordinator's message, assembled in the office's preferred order:

1. **The party, truthfully composed** — how many, in what family units and singles, travelling as what kind of body (a temple society, a bus party, an extended household).
2. **The dates and the single arrival window** — including the bus's expected hour, which lets the desk stage the check-in.
3. **The rooming list** — household by household, room row by room row, using the tariff card's honest arithmetic.
4. **The needs stated as requests** — same-floor clustering, ground-floor rooms for named elders, rooms near or far from the courtyard as the party's sleep demands.
5. **The festival named if the dates touch one** — Sawan Mondays, Maha Shivaratri, Kartik Purnima — and alternates offered inside the same message: adjacent dates, a shifted mix of rows, whatever the group can honestly flex.
6. **One channel, one sending** — through the [booking page](/booking), from the coordinator, with the promise that every later change also travels through the same single voice.

What comes back is the acknowledgement, then the confirmation — the second is the group's held shelter, and its reference is the talisman the coordinator carries to the register with photo identification for every adult. ## Buses, festivals, and the group calendar

The group calendar is crueler than the family's, for one plain reason: groups travel when groups can travel — weekends, festival dates, the Sawan Mondays — and so does every other group in three districts. On the great dates the compound's family rows are claimed by family parties weeks ahead, and a bus party writing at a fortnight's notice is asking for the leftovers of the year's busiest charts. The counsel that survives every season: the group fixes its festival date against the lunar calendar first, writes its single request a month or more out, and keeps its alternates honest. The reasoning, window by window, is laid out in the [festival advance booking window guide for Omkareshwar](/blog/omkareshwar-festival-advance-booking-window); the temple hours that pace a group's day are kept current in the [Omkareshwar darshan timings hub](/blog/omkareshwar-darshan-timings).

The bus itself deserves a sentence. Parking at the compound is one of the quiet advantages of a sansthan over a bazaar hotel, and the driver — every group's unsung pilgrim — is a person the desk counts and feeds like everyone else. Tell the office the vehicle's size in the same request; it rounds out the picture the desk plans to, and it prevents the one avoidable chaos of group arrival, the bus with nowhere to stand.

The arrival day, too, is worth staging in the request rather than improvising on the road. A group that reaches the town in the evening checks in as one unhurried wave, takes the aarti as its opening darshan, and begins the pilgrimage the next dawn already rested; a group that arrives mid-morning checks in against the temple's thickest hours and spends its first collective energy in the longest queue of the day. The same day-count that serves families serves the bus twice over — the whole cluster of rooms runs to the same hour, so the group rises as one body for the dawn it came for. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many rooms can a group book at once?

As many as the party's honest list requires — groups of thirty and more are housed as clusters of family rooms when the party is composed of families, or guided to shared shelter for parties of unrelated pilgrims. The limit is arithmetic, not policy: the list must add up.

### Can a society or bhajan mandal take rooms?

Family units within the society take the family rooms as households; the mandal's unrelated members are guided to the shared dormitory-style shelter, which the network keeps for exactly this tradition. State the party's composition plainly and let the desk place each pilgrim rightly.

### Who should send the group booking request?

One named coordinator, through the official booking page, on behalf of the whole party — with the complete rooming list, the dates, and the authority to receive the office's answer. Five writers produce five contradictions; one produces a confirmation.

### How far ahead should a bus party book?

A month or more for ordinary weeks, and further for the festival calendar — Sawan Mondays, Shivaratri, Kartik Purnima — when family parties claim the large rows early and a late group request meets a bare chart. Name the festival in the request and give alternates.

### Is there parking for our bus at the compound?

Yes — parking is among the Omkareshwar location's amenities, and telling the office the vehicle's size in the request lets the desk plan its standing. The driver is received and fed like every pilgrim in the party.

### What if part of the group must cancel?

Write through the same single channel, as early as the plan changes, with the reference at hand — partial cancellations and date shifts are ordinary office work when they arrive honestly and in one voice.

The parikrama around Mandhata island at Omkareshwar is one of the most spiritually rewarding walks in central India. The island, shaped like the sacred symbol Om, is entirely walkable, and the circumambulation path takes devotees through forested sections, along the Narmada riverbank, and past several smaller shrines and meditation spots. The complete parikrama takes around 45 minutes to an hour at a relaxed pace, and many devotees complete it barefoot as a form of devotion. The Narmada views from the northern tip of the island are particularly beautiful, and the combination of the forest path, the river, and the distant temple creates a meditative experience that stays with pilgrims long after they leave Omkareshwar. This guide covers the parikrama route, the points of interest along the way, the duration, and the practical tips that help you make the most of this experience. For first-time visitors, our [omkareshwar-first-time-visitor-guide] provides a complete introduction to planning your Omkareshwar visit.

## The Om-Shaped Island

Mandhata island, named after King Mandhata who is said to have performed intense penance here, is shaped like the sacred symbol Om. The island is around 2 to 3 kilometres in circumference, and the parikrama path runs along the entire perimeter. The island is forested, with banyan trees, peepal trees, and flowering plants that attract birds and butterflies. The Narmada river surrounds the island, and the ghats on both sides provide access to the boat service. The Om shape of the island is visible from the boat, and many devotees note the significance of completing the parikrama around an island shaped like the most sacred symbol in Hinduism. For the complete island experience, see our [omkareshwar-travel-guide].

## The Parikrama Route

The parikrama starts from the boat ghat on the eastern side of the island. From there, the path runs north along the riverbank, passing through a forested section with several small shrines dedicated to Lord Shiva. The path continues along the northern tip of the island, where the Narmada views are most spectacular. From the northern tip, the path turns south along the western bank, passing through agricultural fields and more forested sections. The path returns to the starting point via the southern and eastern banks, completing the circumambulation. The entire route is marked with small signs and painted markers, making it easy to follow. The path is well-maintained by the Sansthan and local volunteers.

## Duration and Pace

The complete parikrama takes around 45 minutes to an hour at a relaxed pace. Many devotees walk barefoot as a form of penance and devotion, and the barefoot walk adds to the meditative quality of the experience. The path is mostly flat, with only minor undulations, and is suitable for devotees of all ages. Elderly devotees or those with mobility challenges should walk at their own pace and take breaks as needed. The Sansthan has placed benches at several points along the route for devotees who need to rest. For elderly devotees planning a visit, see our [omkareshwar-family-safety-and-comfort-tips].

## Points of Interest Along the Route

Along the parikrama path, there are several points of interest. Near the northern tip of the island, there is a small shrine dedicated to Lord Shiva, where devotees often stop to offer prayers. There are also several meditation spots along the path, with benches and shade under the trees. The Narmada river views from the northern tip are the highlight of the parikrama, and many devotees pause here to take in the view and reflect on their spiritual journey. The western bank of the island passes through agricultural fields, and devotees can see farmers working in the fields from the path. The southern bank passes through a more densely forested section, with large banyan trees that provide shade.

## Best Time for the Parikrama

The best time for the parikrama is early morning or late afternoon, when the sun is not too harsh and the light is soft. Early morning, around 7:00 to 8:00 AM, is particularly recommended, as the island is peaceful and the morning light creates beautiful views of the Narmada. The birds are most active in the early morning, and the forest comes alive with sound. Late afternoon, around 4:00 to 5:00 PM, is also good, as the setting sun creates golden reflections on the river. Avoid the midday hours, particularly during summer, as the sun can be harsh and the path offers limited shade. ## What to Bring

Wear comfortable footwear or walk barefoot if you prefer. Carry a water bottle, as there are limited water sources on the island. Bring sun protection — a hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen — if you are walking during the day. A camera is useful for capturing the Narmada views, but remember to be respectful of the devotional atmosphere. For photography tips, see our [omkareshwar-photography-tips-for-devotees]. A small offering of flowers or fruits can be offered at the shrines along the route. The Sansthan provides drinking water at the boat ghat, but it is wise to carry your own supply for the parikrama.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long does the parikrama take?**
The complete parikrama takes around 45 minutes to an hour at a relaxed pace.

**Q: Is the parikrama path suitable for elderly devotees?**
Yes, the path is mostly flat and well-maintained. Elderly devotees should walk at their own pace and take breaks as needed. Benches are available at several points along the route.

**Q: Can I do the parikrama barefoot?**
Yes, many devotees walk barefoot during the parikrama as a form of penance and devotion. The path is clean and safe for barefoot walking.

**Q: Are there facilities on the island for rest breaks?**
Yes, there are benches and shaded areas along the parikrama path where devotees can rest. The Sansthan has placed several benches at key points along the route.

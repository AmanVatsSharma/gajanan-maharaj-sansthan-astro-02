# Omkareshwar Family Room Booking Guide

Family rooms at the Omkareshwar compound of the Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan are its first purpose: the network's founding rule reserves its rooms for families, the tariff card runs ₹1,650 to ₹2,550 by beds and cooling, and a household of any size from a couple to a joint family of eight finds its exact fit by honest arithmetic — one room for four, two rooms for six, adjoining doors where the chart allows. This page is the family's complete booking companion: how to read the card as a household, how the joint-family question is solved, what children and elders each need from a room, and how the request is written so the desk can grant it while the chart is still open. The request itself begins at the [booking page](/booking); the family's questions go to the [contact page](/contact).

The yatra-side of travelling with a household — the itinerary, the children's pace, the elders' days — is covered in our [family yatra planning guide for Omkareshwar](/blog/omkareshwar-family-yatra-planning) and the [family stay tips](/blog/omkareshwar-bhakta-niwas-family-stay-tips) page; this guide stays on its own ground, which is the room: choosing it, configuring it, and booking it.

## A compound built around families

Begin with the rule, because it explains everything else about the place. The Sansthan's accommodation exists for families — the network's booking rules state it plainly, and the desk at Omkareshwar administers it with warmth: rooms are allocated to family parties, while walking parties and groups of unrelated pilgrims are guided toward the shared shelter that suits their kind of travel. The rule is not a prejudice; it is protection. A family with children and elders, sleeping in a temple town far from home, is sheltered best by a compound whose whole population is other such families — early to quiet, early to rise, and governed by the courtesies of a shared devotional life.

What that means practically: the rooms are sized as families are sized. The two-bed row holds two; the three-bed rows hold three; the four-bed rows hold four. There are no improvised fifth mattresses, no suites with hidden sofas — the arithmetic is honest because the shelter is honest, and a family that books its true count receives rooms that truly hold it. Around the rooms, the family estate: the bhojan kaksha for plain meals at plain hours, hot water at every attached bath, the desk that receives tired arrivals, and the [Omkareshwar location](/locations/omkareshwar) itself — a short walk from the island's ghats, which is the family advantage that matters most at dawn.

## Reading the tariff card as a household

The card, with the household's eye applied to it:

| Room row | Beds | Tariff a night | The household it holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bed AC | 2 | ₹1,650 | A couple; a parent and child; two elders travelling together |
| 3 Bed Non-AC | 3 | ₹1,750 | Parents and one child in the cool season |
| 3 Bed AC | 3 | ₹2,050 | The same small family in the hot months |
| 4 Bed Non-AC | 4 | ₹2,250 | Parents and two children — the standard family row |
| 4 Bed AC | 4 | ₹2,550 | The same four in the Malwa summer |

Read as a household, the card teaches two lessons. The narrowness of the gaps — ₹400 between the smallest and largest cooled rooms, ₹500 between the ventilated rows — means no family should distort its size to chase a price; book the room that holds you, and the differences look after themselves. And the card's pairs — each bed-count in a cooled and a ventilated version — mean the season, not the budget, usually makes the choice: the cooled rows from the spring's end through the monsoon, the ventilated rows from autumn's start through winter. ## One room or two: the joint-family question

The joint family — six, eight, ten souls across three generations — is the desk's most regular family booking, and its most miswritten. The wrong request arrives as one line: "rooms for eight". The right request arrives as arithmetic: "a four-bed and a four-bed", or "a four-bed and a two-bed, adjoining where possible", with the party's generations named.

| Joint family of… | The rooms that hold it | Notes for the request |
|---|---|---|
| Five | 4 Bed + 2 Bed, or two 3 Bed rows | Ask for adjoining or same-floor placement |
| Six | 4 Bed + 2 Bed, or 4 Bed + two singles' arrangements | State the children's ages; infants share parents' beds |
| Eight | 4 Bed + 4 Bed | The classic two-room joint booking; request same floor |
| Ten | 4 Bed + 4 Bed + 2 Bed | Book as one party with one arrival window |

Three counsels make the joint booking work. First, request the rooms as one party in one message — two branches of a family writing separately for the same dates produces two confirmations that the desk cannot know belong together, and the adjoining request dies in the gap between them. Second, ask for adjacency as a preference, not a condition — the chart is generous but not infinite, and a request the office can partially grant is a request it serves gladly. Third, name the elders in the party and their floor preference at booking time; ground-floor rooms exist in limited number and are granted to the families that asked in time.

## The smallest pilgrims: rooms and children

Families write to the digital desk most often about children, and the answers are steadier than the anxieties. Children are counted in the party's true headcount — the request carries every soul, the small ones included, because the room's arithmetic and the desk's register both depend on it. Infants travel lightest of all: too young for a bed of their own, they share the parents', and the office should simply be told. Children of bed age are counted as beds — a family of two parents and two children is a four-bed row, whatever the children's size.

The room serves children in ways the tariff card does not print. The compound's quiet hours are a family's ally: children who sleep by the evening's end wake for the dawn darshan, which is the pilgrimage's whole design. The bhojan kaksha's plain food suits the smallest stomachs better than any restaurant's. The attached bathroom with hot water turns the cold-morning bath from a battle into a routine. And the island itself — the boats, the ghats, the river visible from the lanes — is a children's geography, small and walkable and endlessly interesting; our [kids-friendly yatra guide for Omkareshwar](/blog/omkareshwar-kids-friendly-yatra-guide) builds a whole day around it. One honest caution belongs to the booking stage: request a room away from the courtyard's gathering corners if your children are light sleepers, and say so at booking rather than at the desk.

## Elders and the room that fits

The elder in the party changes the family booking more than any other member, and the desk's counsel is consistent. The ground-floor preference, stated at booking. The cooled row for the hot months, since elders feel the Malwa afternoon first and hardest. The hot-water bathroom, which in the cool dawn before the river's snana matters more than every other facility combined. And the day-count — the night is counted from your entry, not from a printed hotel hour — which lets an arriving elder rest immediately and keeps the room through the following day without a forced morning departure. ## Writing the family request

Assembled for the household, in order:

1. **Count everyone once, honestly** — every adult, every child of bed age, every infant noted as sharing; families are the party the rule was written for, and family arithmetic is the booking's spine.
2. **Choose the room or rooms by the card** — the rows that hold your true count, cooled or ventilated by your travel month.
3. **Write one message for the whole household** through the [booking page](/booking) — even when two branches travel separately to the same town — with the dates, the arrival window, and every need stated as a request: adjoining doors, ground floor, a quiet corner.
4. **Name the festival if you travel for one** — the Sawan Mondays, Maha Shivaratri, and Kartik Purnima each tighten the family chart early, and the family that names the window is served inside it.
5. **Keep the acknowledgement; travel on the confirmation** — the second message carries the booking; its reference, with every adult's photo identification, meets the desk.
6. **At check-in, verify the household's hours** — the next day's darshan timings, the kitchen's meal hours, and any elder's request confirmed in place.

## Festival weeks and family charts

The family calendar and the temple calendar meet hardest on the great dates, and one paragraph of honesty belongs here. On the Sawan Mondays the compound fills with family parties from across the Malwa; on Maha Shivaratri the town is a sea of them; through the Kartik Purnima window the lamps draw households from whole districts. In each of these weeks the family chart tightens weeks ahead, the larger rows go first, and the joint-family booking that arrives late is the desk's hardest file to answer well. The counsel is the calendar run backwards: fix the festival date against the lunar year, subtract a month or more, write the whole household's request in one sending, and give the office alternates — adjacent dates, the other rows — inside the same message. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can a family of five book a single room at Omkareshwar?

Not one room — the rows hold their bed counts exactly, so a party of five books a four-bed plus a two-bed, or two three-bed rows, requested together as one household so the desk can try for adjacency.

### Are rooms at the compound only for families?

Yes — the network's rules reserve the rooms for family parties, which is precisely what makes the compound what it is: quiet, early-rising, and safe for the households it shelters. Walking parties and groups are guided to the shared shelter that suits them.

### Do children count in the booking headcount?

They do. Children of bed age are counted as beds in the arithmetic; infants who share the parents' bed are named in the request without taking a bed. The honest count is the count the desk can serve.

### Can we get two rooms next to each other?

Adjoining or same-floor placement is requested at booking and granted wherever the chart allows — which is most of the year when the request is early, and a kindness the desk stretches for on festival weeks. Ask as a preference, in the one family message.

### Which room should a family of four take in summer?

The 4 Bed AC at ₹2,550 — the hot months leave little room for heroism, and the family that rests cool at noon is the family at the evening aarti. Once October arrives and through February, the 4 Bed Non-AC at ₹2,250 serves the same household better and cheaper.

### How early should a joint family book for Shivaratri?

Weeks to months ahead, in one message for the whole party. The great festival dates take the large rows first, and the joint-family booking written late finds the desk apologetic and the chart bare — a sorrow that an early request prevents entirely.

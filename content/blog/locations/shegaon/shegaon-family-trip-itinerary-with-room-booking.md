# Shegaon Family Trip Itinerary With Room Booking

The family Shegaon trip that works is a **two-day plan built around three fixed points — the Samadhi Mandir's aartis, the Annakshetra's meals, and a room block booked as one request through the official channels** — with everything else left soft around them. Book rooms that cover every head you are travelling with, align the booking with your train, and let the itinerary bend where families always bend: around elders' knees, children's naps, and the unexpected queue. The itinerary below assumes a party of six to eight across two or three generations, which is the shape we see most; adapt the numbers to your own dinner table. Start the room request at the [booking page](/booking) once the plan is settled.

This guide differs from our other Shegaon pieces in one deliberate way: it treats the booking and the itinerary as a single object, because that is how families actually experience them. An itinerary without a room plan collapses at two in the afternoon when the children need shade and the grandmother needs her medicines and a quiet chair. A booking without an itinerary wastes a 4-bed room on a party that is never indoors. Written together, each makes the other work.

## A yatra that holds three generations together

Before the hour-by-hour, the philosophy in one paragraph, because it decides everything downstream. A family pilgrimage is not a group of tourists who happen to share a surname; it is a small moving society with different speeds, different appetites, and a single shared purpose. The plans that succeed are the ones that give each generation its due — real darshan for the elders, real play for the children, real rest for the parents who organised it all — without fragmenting the party into separate trips. Shegaon is unusually kind to this ambition: the distances are short, the food is common, the rooms scale from two beds to four, and the town's rhythm itself — aarti, meal, garden, aarti — is a rhythm a family can keep together. Our wider advice on [pilgrimage with children](/blog/family-pilgrimage-with-children-tips) and the [Sansthan's guidance for senior devotees](/blog/senior-citizen-special-needs-guide) both feed into the plan below.

## Seven things to settle before you leave home

1. **Count the party precisely, children included.** Capacity at the Sansthan is counted in beds, and the whole room block flows from this number. An approximate family is the commonest booking mistake we see.
2. **Choose the dates around the calendar, not just the leave calendar.** A weekend raises crowds; an ekadashi transforms the town; a Tuesday adds the evening Dindi. Our [one day versus two day comparison](/blog/shegaon-one-day-vs-two-day-visit-planning) helps if you are still deciding the length.
3. **Pick the complex by what your family loves.** Bhakta Niwas for the temple-centric, Anand Vihar for garden-loving families with children, Visawa for train-heavy plans. All three appear in our [complete Shegaon accommodation guide](/blog/shegaon-accommodation-guide).
4. **Match the train to the room, not the other way round.** An early-hours arrival means the room request should say so, in the remarks, in plain words.
5. **Allocate one organiser and one backup.** One person holds the confirmation, the payment records, and the plan; one more knows where everything is. Never let the paperwork live in a single phone.
6. **Prepare the elders' kit and the children's kit.** Medicines and prescriptions on one side; snacks, a small game, and a familiar comfort on the other. The packing detail for temple towns is in our [family accommodation checklist for Shegaon](/blog/shegaon-family-accommodation-checklist).
7. **Send the room request well before the travel week** — especially if the dates touch a festival window — through the [official booking page](/booking), and keep the acknowledgement when it comes.

Families who complete these seven items report the same thing: the trip felt light from the beginning, because every decision that could be made at a kitchen table was made there.

## The two-day skeleton

The itinerary itself, in the shape that has served families best. Hours are deliberately approximate — confirm the aarti and meal timings for your dates with the temple office — and we treat them here the way a good family cook treats a recipe.

| Block | Day one | Day two |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-dawn | Train arrives; luggage to reception; Kakad Aarti if the date allows | Wake without alarm; optional second Kakad Aarti for the keen |
| Morning | Darshan; Annakshetra breakfast and mahaprasad; room handover as ready | Slow darshan; Paduka Mandir; bazaar for books and prasad |
| Midday | Rest hours in the rooms; elders nap; children play quietly | Pack; luggage to reception; mahaprasad lunch in the rows |
| Afternoon | Anand Sagar — gardens, lake walk, children's corner | Final walk; tea at the canteen; photograph at the Mandir gate |
| Evening | Aarti, lamps, bazaar; early dinner; children to bed | Evening aarti if the train allows; platform with the whole party |

Two notes on reading the table. First, the rest block on day one is not wasted time — it is the block that makes the evening aarti possible for grandparents, and families that delete it pay for the deletion at seven in the evening. Second, the whole skeleton rides on the room block being right, which is the next section's business.

## Beds for every branch of the family

The room block is where the itinerary either funds itself or falls apart. The published tariffs, with capacity counted in beds:

| Room type | Beds | Indicative tariff | Who it usually takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bed AC | 2 | ₹1,650 | One grandparent pair, or parents with an infant |
| 3 Bed Non-AC | 3 | ₹1,750 | Cooler months; one couple plus a child |
| 3 Bed AC | 3 | ₹2,050 | Summer months; same party |
| 4 Bed Non-AC | 4 | ₹2,250 | The classic family room |
| 4 Bed AC | 4 | ₹2,550 | Summer travel for the fuller branch |

A party of eight — grandparents, two parents, two teenagers, two younger children — is typically served by a 4-bed and a 4-bed, or a 4-bed and a 3-bed with the youngest sharing, as the office guides. Three-generation parties should request adjoining or same-floor rooms at booking; the office accommodates such requests where the chart allows, and a request made in advance is a different thing from a request made at a crowded counter. Every room carries an attached bathroom with hot water, and the tariff is the tariff — no festival surcharge, no hidden extras. What each category includes in fuller detail is in [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas room types and facilities](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-room-types-and-facilities).

The choice of AC versus non-AC is seasonal, not social. From April to June, with children and elders in the party, the AC categories earn their difference; in the monsoon and winter months the non-AC rooms are the natural choice and the saving is real. Families split across categories — the grandparents in AC, the parents in non-AC — are common and easily arranged in one request.

## The pace layer: grandparents and small children

Every family yatra runs on two clocks, and the plan that acknowledges this travels twice as well. The elders' clock wants short walking distances, a chair at regular intervals, shade at midday, and the dignity of unhurried darshan. The children's clock wants food before hunger arrives, water before thirst does, a run after every stillness, and something to look forward to after every queue. Shegaon serves both clocks unusually well, and the plan above is already shaped around them; these refinements sharpen it further.

For the elders: request the ground floor at booking; keep the walking days short — the Mandir side on day one, the garden on day two's schedule only if the legs vote yes; use the canteens for tea breaks rather than pushing to the next sight; and give the aarti the priority over everything, because for most grandparents it is the purpose of the trip. Our dedicated advice for [senior citizens travelling to Shegaon](/blog/shegaon-senior-citizen-travel-tips) carries this further.

For the children: let Anand Sagar be promised early and delivered honestly — the lake walk and the play area are the trip's reward block; carry the small snacks that bridge the gap between appetite and the Annakshetra's window; and involve them in the ritual itself — a flower to offer, a lamp to hold, a story of the saint told at their height. The [kids-friendly yatra guide for Shegaon](/blog/shegaon-kids-friendly-yatra-guide) is written for exactly this.

For the parents in the middle, one sentence of mercy: your job is not to enforce the itinerary; it is to keep the party fed, watered, and together, and to know where the confirmation is. The itinerary serves you, not the reverse.

## Meals the Sansthan already handles

A family does not need to plan food at Shegaon so much as align with it. The Annakshetra serves free mahaprasad through the main meal windows — eating in the rows is the experience itself, and children learn the discipline of it faster than adults expect. The Sansthan canteens bridge the gaps with simple food through the day. The practical family rules are three: eat early in the window with children, carry the water bottles everywhere, and treat the bazaar's snacks as occasional seasoning rather than the meal itself. ## One booking thread for the whole party

The room block, requested cleanly, is a ten-minute exercise that pays for itself the whole trip:

1. **Compose the request as one family, not three.** One submission covering the whole room block — the party, every room type, the dates, the complex preference — with the arrival train and hour in the remarks.
2. **Name one booker and one backup**, and make the payment through the route the office specifies, keeping the reference and the receipt. How advances and balances work is covered in our [advance payment guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-advance-payment-guide), within the request-to-receipt mechanics the [complete Bhakta Niwas booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) sets out for every Sansthan stay.
3. **State the special requests now** — adjoining rooms, ground floor for the grandparents, AC for the summer dates — not at the desk.
4. **Save the acknowledgement, then the confirmation, and forward both** to the backup person and to any family group, so the plan survives any single phone.
5. **Carry the confirmation and the IDs to the desk**, let one adult complete the entry while the party waits in the shade, and note the entry time — your checkout is counted a day from that moment, not from any hotel's noon.
6. **If the family's plans change, change through the same thread.** One reference, one conversation, one truth — the discipline our [common booking mistakes guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-common-booking-mistakes) teaches through other families' stumbles.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I book rooms for a large joint family at Shegaon?

Make one request through the official booking route covering the entire party — every room, every date, with the full headcount — rather than parallel requests from each branch. One thread keeps the rooms together in the chart and the paperwork sane at the desk.

### Can we get adjoining or same-floor rooms for three generations?

Yes, request it at booking in the remarks. The office accommodates such requests where the chart allows, and an advance request is far stronger than one made at a crowded counter on arrival day.

### Which complex suits a family with young children best?

Anand Vihar, for most — beside Anand Sagar, where the garden becomes an extension of the stay. Families centred on the temple choose Bhakta Niwas for the walking distance; train-heavy plans choose Visawa. All three are covered in the complete accommodation guide.

### Do children need ID at check-in?

Photo ID is asked for adults; for children a school ID or copy of an Aadhaar where available speeds the register, but the desk guides families case by case and children are never a problem at Shegaon.

### What if our family's train reaches before the rooms are ready?

This is the standard early-hours pattern: the desk holds your luggage, the family goes straight to the aarti or darshan, and the room is handed over as it readies. State the early arrival in the booking remarks so the office plans for you.

### Is two days really enough with a full family?

Two days is the shape that suits most families — long enough for the aartis, the garden, the bazaar and the rest blocks, short enough for leave calendars and school schedules. The full weighing of one day against two, including festival-date considerations, is in our dedicated comparison guide.

# Shegaon Parking and Local Transport

Shegaon does not have the traffic infrastructure of a major city, and that is precisely what makes it manageable — most of the time. The town's compact size, the concentration of the temple complex in a single area, and the Sansthan's own shuttle and parking arrangements mean that getting around is straightforward for visitors who arrive with even a basic plan. The exceptions are the festival days, when tens of thousands of additional vehicles and pilgrims converge on a town that was designed for a fraction of that volume. Understanding how parking and local transport work in both scenarios — regular days and festival days — saves you from the kind of logistical stress that can overshadow an otherwise beautiful pilgrimage.

## Reaching Shegaon by train

Shegaon Junction (station code SGO) is the primary railhead for devotees visiting the Samadhi Mandir. It sits directly on the busy Mumbai-Howrah trunk railway line, which means most express and superfast trains running between Mumbai and Nagpur, Mumbai and Kolkata, or Mumbai and Hyderabad stop here. The station is well-connected and receives trains from major cities including Pune, Aurangabad, Amravati, and Akola.

The distance from Shegaon Junction to the Samadhi Mandir is walkable — approximately 1.5 kilometres — and many pilgrims make the walk on foot as part of their spiritual preparation. For visitors with heavy luggage, elderly family members, or children, the Sansthan operates free shuttle vehicles from the station to the Bhakta Niwas complex during daylight hours. The shuttle frequency increases during festival windows when hundreds of arriving pilgrims need transport.

If you are booking a train ticket, aim for a daytime arrival. Arriving in Shegaon after dark, particularly during summer or monsoon, means navigating unfamiliar roads with limited transport options. The last MSRTC bus services to the temple area typically cease by 8:00 pm, leaving private auto-rickshaws as the only option. Always confirm the last bus timing if you are arriving by public transport in the evening. For a complete picture of reaching Shegaon from major cities, see our [Shegaon travel guide](/shegaon-travel-guide).

## Reaching Shegaon by road

The Mumbai-Pune expressway network, connected via the Samruddhi Mahamarg, has dramatically reduced driving times to Shegaon. The journey from Mumbai now takes approximately six to seven hours via the Aurangabad or Jalna feeder interchanges. From Pune, the drive is around seven to eight hours. From Nagpur, Shegaon is roughly five to six hours by road via State Highway 6. From Akola, the distance is around 60 kilometres, making it the most convenient base for visitors arriving from the eastern Vidarbha region.

State Highway 24 and the regional road network converge near Shegaon, connecting the town with nearby Khamgaon, Jalgaon, Buldhana, and Malkapur. MSRTC buses operate regular services on these routes, and private operators run additional services during festival periods. If you are travelling by bus from a nearby town, confirm the last departure time in advance — rural bus services often stop running by 8:00 pm, and arriving after dark without a pre-arranged pickup can be inconvenient.

The approach to Shegaon town is straightforward: follow the signs for the Samadhi Mandir or the Sansthan office, and you will reach the accommodation complex within a few kilometres of the main road. Google Maps works reliably on the highway approach but can lose signal in the narrower lanes near the temple. Download offline maps before you leave home, particularly if you are navigating to Bhakta Niwas or Anand Vihar after dark.

## Parking at Bhakta Niwas and the temple complex

The Bhakta Niwas complex has its own on-site parking for guests and day visitors. The slots are allocated on a first-come, first-served basis, and they fill up quickly on weekends and festival days. The practical advice is to arrive by evening on the day before your intended darshan, park your vehicle in the designated slot, and lock it properly. During festival weeks, the Sansthan sometimes reallocates parking space for visiting officials and volunteers, so unmarked or informally parked vehicles may be shifted.

If the on-site parking is full, the Sansthan designates overflow parking lots on the outskirts of town. These lots are connected to the temple complex by the free shuttle service, which runs at increased frequency during peak periods. The overflow option is perfectly workable — many experienced pilgrims use it deliberately during festival days to avoid the congestion near the main entrance.

For two-wheelers, parking is less of an issue. The temple complex and Bhakta Niwas have designated two-wheeler parking areas that rarely reach capacity, even during festivals. The only precaution is to use a disc brake lock and park in a well-lit area.

If you arrive during a major festival like Samadhi Utsav and cannot find parking anywhere near the temple, park your vehicle at the nearest designated lot on the main road and walk. The walk from the outer parking areas to the temple gates typically takes 15 to 30 minutes, and many devotees treat it as part of the pilgrimage experience. During the annual Gajanan Vijay Utsav (September 8), which commemorates the punyatithi of Shri Gajanan Maharaj, lakhs of devotees arrive by road, and the parking situation requires significant patience and early arrival.

## Auto-rickshaws and local transport within Shegaon

Auto-rickshaws are the primary mode of local transport in Shegaon for visitors who are not walking. They are readily available near the railway station, near the temple main gate, and near the Bhakta Niwas complex. The typical fare for a short trip within the temple area is nominal — most journeys cost between ₹20 and ₹50 depending on the distance and the time of day. During festival periods, auto drivers may quote higher fares for longer distances or during peak hours, but the increases are usually modest.

Auto-rickshaws are also the most practical way to reach Anand Vihar from Bhakta Niwas, or to travel between the temple complex and the Anand Sagar park. The journey between Bhakta Niwas and Anand Vihar takes around 10 to 15 minutes by auto, and the fare is typically ₹30 to ₹50. Ask the driver to wait if you are making a return trip — many auto drivers are happy to arrange a round trip with a waiting charge, which saves you the hassle of finding transport back.

The free shuttle service operated by the Sansthan is another valuable transport option. It connects Bhakta Niwas with the railway station and runs at regular intervals during daylight hours. During festival windows, additional shuttle services are deployed to handle the increased pilgrim flow. The shuttle is free for all Sansthan guests and can be identified by its branding. If you are unsure about the shuttle schedule, ask at the Bhakta Niwas reception.

## Transport tips for festival visits

The single most important piece of advice for festival visits is to arrive early. If you are attending Pragat Din or Samadhi Utsav, aim to reach Shegaon the evening before the main day. This gives you time to park, check in, and settle in before the crowds build. Arriving on the morning of the festival, particularly by road, is a recipe for frustration — the roads leading to the temple are often diverted or restricted, and the parking situation becomes chaotic by mid-morning.

Consider carpooling with other devotees from your city or community. Many pilgrimage groups organise shared vehicles, and this reduces the parking pressure at the temple while also splitting the fuel and toll costs. The Sansthan's booking desk can sometimes connect you with other pilgrims travelling from the same region.

If you are travelling by hired taxi from a nearby city, confirm the drop-off point in advance. Most taxi drivers are familiar with the Bhakta Niwas complex, but some may not know the exact location of Anand Vihar. Provide the driver with the address and, if possible, the Google Maps coordinates (20.7934, 76.6992 for Bhakta Niwas, 20.7919, 76.6937 for Anand Vihar).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is parking available at the Bhakta Niwas complex?**
A: Yes, on-site parking is available for guests and day visitors on a first-come, first-served basis. The slots fill up quickly on weekends and festival days. During peak periods, the Sansthan designates overflow parking lots on the outskirts, connected to the temple by a free shuttle service.

**Q: How far is Shegaon Junction railway station from the temple?**
A: Approximately 1.5 kilometres, which is a walkable distance. The Sansthan operates free shuttle vehicles from the station to the Bhakta Niwas complex during daylight hours, with increased frequency during festivals.

**Q: Are auto-rickshaws readily available in Shegaon?**
A: Yes, auto-rickshaws are available near the railway station, the temple main gate, and the Bhakta Niwas complex. Typical fares for short trips within the temple area range from ₹20 to ₹50. During festival periods, fares may increase slightly for longer distances or peak hours.

**Q: How do I get from Bhakta Niwas to Anand Vihar?**
A: The most practical option is an auto-rickshaw, which takes approximately 10 to 15 minutes and costs around ₹30 to ₹50. You can ask the driver to wait for a return trip or book a separate auto for the return journey.

**Q: Is there a free shuttle service?**
A: Yes. The Sansthan operates free shuttle vehicles connecting Bhakta Niwas with Shegaon Junction railway station and key stops around the temple precincts. The shuttle runs at regular intervals during daylight hours and increases frequency during festival windows. Ask at the Bhakta Niwas reception for the current schedule.

**Q: What should I do if parking is full during a festival?**
A: Use the designated overflow parking lots on the outskirts of town, which are connected to the temple by the free shuttle service. Alternatively, arrive the evening before the main festival day to secure an on-site parking slot. Walking from outer parking areas to the temple gates takes 15 to 30 minutes and is a common practice during the busiest periods.

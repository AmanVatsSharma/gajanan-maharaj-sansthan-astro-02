# Shegaon Bhakta Niwas Advance Payment Guide

An advance at Shegaon Bhakta Niwas is a **partial payment that holds your room against a confirmed booking**, with the remaining balance settled at the reception desk when you arrive. The exact amount is communicated by the Sansthan office when your request is confirmed — it is guided by your room type and dates rather than a fixed percentage — and every rupee you pay, at booking or at the desk, deserves a receipt. Start your request on the [official booking page](/booking), and keep the [contact page](/contact) handy for any payment question the office can answer directly.

We wrote this guide because payment questions reach the digital desk every single week. Devotees ask how much to pay in advance, whether a message counts as a receipt, what happens to the money if a train is missed, and who in a joint family should make the payment. This article answers each of those in plain language, walks through the advance sequence step by step, and closes with the records you should be able to produce months later if anyone in the family asks.

## Why an advance is part of the process

Sansthan accommodation exists to serve pilgrims, not to profit from them, and the advance exists to protect that service. When a family confirms a room, the office sets aside a real bed on a real night. An advance signals that the party intends to arrive, which lets the office plan its chart honestly for the thousands of devotees who travel to Shegaon each month for darshan at the Samadhi Mandir.

Three things follow from this purpose, and understanding them removes most confusion:

- The advance is **part of your room cost, not a fee on top of it**. Whatever you pay before travel is adjusted against your final bill at the desk.
- The advance **travels with the booking, not with the person**. If dates shift and the office accommodates the change, the advance moves with the request as guided by the prevailing policy.
- The advance **does not buy priority**. It confirms intent; it does not jump the office's processing queue ahead of families who booked earlier.

Devotees sometimes worry that an advance makes cancellation costly. In practice, the Sansthan's approach to adjustments is pastoral rather than commercial, and genuine circumstances are heard with sympathy. The full mechanics of cancellations and date changes live in our [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas refund and cancellation guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-refund-and-cancellation-guide), which we keep as the canonical word on the subject.

## What the advance sits against: published room tariffs

An advance only makes sense against the tariff it belongs to. The Sansthan publishes its indicative room tariffs openly, and the amount payable for your dates is settled with the office at booking. The current published options across the network:

| Room type | Capacity | Indicative tariff | Typical party |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bed AC | 2 | ₹1,650 | Couples, elderly parents travelling alone |
| 3 Bed Non-AC | 3 | ₹1,750 | Small families in the cooler months |
| 3 Bed AC | 3 | ₹2,050 | Small families travelling in summer |
| 4 Bed Non-AC | 4 | ₹2,250 | The standard family option |
| 4 Bed AC | 4 | ₹2,550 | Larger families, April to June travel |

Every room, whatever its category, comes with an attached bathroom and hot water, and the tariff you confirm is the tariff you are billed against — the Sansthan does not layer hidden charges onto devotee accommodation. What each room type includes in greater detail — bedding, support hours, and the differences between wings — is laid out in [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas room types and facilities](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-room-types-and-facilities).

A practical note on capacity: it is counted in beds. A party of five books a combination that covers five sleeping places, usually a three-bed and a two-bed room or a four-bed plus adjustments as the office advises. The advance follows the combination, so settle your headcount before you settle your payment.

## The advance sequence, from request to receipt

Families who follow the sequence below rarely have a payment question left over. Those who improvise usually create the questions later.

1. **Submit the room request first.** Payment enters the picture only after the office has processed your request through the [booking flow](/booking) and communicated a confirmation with the amount payable. Paying before a request exists leaves you holding money with nothing attached to it.
2. **Read the confirmation carefully.** It names the room type, the dates, the party, and the advance the office has guided. If any detail reads differently from what you asked for, raise it now, before paying — corrections are simplest before money moves.
3. **Pay only through the route the office specifies.** The confirmation tells you the accepted mode and destination for the advance. Treat any other route — a number forwarded on a family group, a link from a search result — as unreliable. Our [official contact page](/contact) is the place to verify anything you are unsure about.
4. **Preserve the proof of payment exactly as it comes.** A screenshot of the transaction, the reference number, the date, and the amount. If the office issues an acknowledgement of receipt, save that alongside. These small artefacts are what you will show at the desk.
5. **Carry the whole correspondence to Shegaon.** Either on your phone or printed, bring the confirmation and the payment proof together. The desk reconciles the two, and a family that arrives with both is through the desk in minutes.
6. **Settle the balance and take the final receipt.** At check-in, the advance is deducted from the bill, you pay the remainder as guided, and the desk issues the receipt for the stay. Ask for this final receipt even if the amount feels small — it is the closing entry of the whole transaction.

Where your request stands at any moment — submitted, acknowledged, confirmed — is easiest to track using the stages described in our [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas booking confirmation timeline](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-booking-confirmation-timeline), and the fuller context of how requests, advances, and receipts work across every Sansthan location is gathered in the [complete Bhakta Niwas booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide).

## Reading your advance receipt

A receipt is not a formality; it is the version of your booking that the desk can act on. Devotees sometimes show us photographs of transfers with no accompanying details, and while the office can usually help reconstruct them, the reconstruction costs everyone time. A usable advance receipt carries five elements:

| Element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Name of the payee as booked | Ties the money to the register entry for your party |
| Amount and date | Establishes what was paid and when, for adjustment at the desk |
| Reference or transaction identifier | The unique thread the office uses to trace the payment |
| Room type and dates | Confirms the money is attached to the right booking |
| Mode of payment | Speeds up reconciliation if any query arises later |

If any of these are missing from what you receive, a short polite message to the office asking for a complete acknowledgement is entirely reasonable. Offices appreciate devotees who keep clean records — it makes every later interaction, from early check-in to a date change, faster and friendlier.

One habit worth teaching younger family members: forward the receipt to at least two people in the party before travel day. Phones are lost, batteries die on overnight trains, and a pilgrimage is a poor place for a single point of failure. The record should live in more than one pocket.

## Settling the balance at the Shegaon desk

The balance payment is deliberately simple. At check-in, after your identity and booking are verified, the desk calculates the tariff due, deducts the advance already paid, and states the remainder. You pay the remainder as the office guides, and the desk hands you the final receipt for the stay.

Three situations deserve a little forethought:

- **Extra members of the party.** If an extra traveller joins after booking — an aunt who decided at the last moment, a cousin extending his trip — mention it at the desk rather than absorbing it silently. Capacity follows the bed count, and the office will guide whether the room as booked can accommodate the change or a small addition is needed.
- **A shorter stay than booked.** If you paid for two nights and circumstances end the stay after one, the desk guides the adjustment per the prevailing policy. Do not simply skip the conversation; the desk cannot adjust what it does not know.
- **Payments by another family member.** If a son booked from abroad and the parents are travelling, ensure the travellers carry the confirmation, the payment proof, and ideally a note of who paid. The desk reconciles bookkeeping and people with patience when the paper trail is clear.

Whatever the situation, the closing principle is the same one that opened this guide: every payment, of any size, in any direction, receives a receipt. Collect them in one envelope and the financial side of your pilgrimage is closed the moment you leave the desk.

## Mid-course corrections: date shifts and partial stays

Plans bend on a pilgrimage. Monsoon floods a route, a wedding is postponed, a grandparent's health asks for gentler dates. When a confirmed booking needs to change, the advance is handled as guided by the office and the prevailing policy — the money is not forfeited by default, and requests are heard on their merits.

The cleanest path for any change is the same: write to the office through the channel that confirmed your booking, quote your reference, state the change plainly, and wait for the office's guidance before making any new payment. Families who follow this rhythm keep one thread and one truth. Families who send parallel requests through three channels create the situations our [guide to common booking mistakes](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-common-booking-mistakes) was written to prevent.

The stage between request and confirmation also has its own rhythm, and payment sent during that gap — before the office has confirmed — is the single most avoidable complication we see. Patience for those two or three days protects both your money and the office's ability to match it correctly to your booking.

## Records devotees regret not keeping

Looking back over a season of devotee correspondence, a pattern emerges in the queries that took longest to resolve: almost every one involved a missing artefact. The screenshot that was never taken. The reference number typed into a chat instead of saved. The receipt left on the reception counter at check-out.

Build the habit early and the problem never arises. Keep a single folder on your phone — confirmation, payment proof, office messages, final receipt. Bring a printed copy if elders in the party prefer paper. At the end of the yatra, the folder becomes a small family archive: proof of the room you rested in on the night before Ashadhi, the bed your children slept in after their first Kakad Aarti. Devotees write to us years later asking to confirm an old stay for a family record; the ones who kept the envelope answer their own question in seconds.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the advance for a Shegaon Bhakta Niwas room?

The amount is communicated by the Sansthan office with your booking confirmation and is guided by your room type and dates. It is a part-payment towards the tariff, adjusted in full at the desk — not a separate charge.

### Can I pay the full tariff in advance instead?

Many devotees prefer to, and the office can guide whether that suits your booking. If you do, carry the proof of the full payment so the desk can close the account cleanly and issue the final receipt at check-in.

### Is a screenshot of my payment enough as proof?

A screenshot of the transaction with the reference number, date, and amount is a sound working record. If the office sends a written acknowledgement, keep that too — the two together answer every question a desk can ask.

### What happens to my advance if I cancel the trip?

Cancellations are handled per the prevailing policy, and genuine circumstances are heard with sympathy. Raise the request through the channel that confirmed your booking, quote your reference, and follow the office's guidance — our [refund and cancellation guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-refund-and-cancellation-guide) walks through the full flow.

### Who should make the payment in a joint family booking?

Whoever manages the family's arrangements comfortably. What matters is that the name on the payment, the name on the booking, and the travellers who arrive at the desk can be reconciled — carry the proof and a note of who paid, and the desk takes care of the rest.

### Do I pay anything extra at check-out?

No hidden extras are added at check-out. If your stay matched the booking, the account was already closed at check-in with the final receipt. Charges only arise if your party or dates changed during the stay, and the desk will have discussed those with you when the change happened.

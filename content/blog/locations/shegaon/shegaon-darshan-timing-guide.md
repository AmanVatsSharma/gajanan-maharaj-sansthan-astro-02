# Shegaon Darshan Timing Guide

Planning when to visit the Samadhi Mandir at Shegaon is the single most important decision in your pilgrimage itinerary. The darshan experience changes dramatically depending on whether you arrive at dawn, midday, or during one of the major festivals. A well-timed visit can mean a peaceful 20-minute darshan; a poorly-timed one can mean standing in a queue for three hours on a hot afternoon. This guide covers the regular daily schedule, the special schedules for Pragat Din and Samadhi Utsav, the different darshan queues, and practical tips for timing your visit well.

## Daily darshan timings at the Samadhi Mandir

The Samadhi Mandir is open for darshan every day of the year. The standard schedule is:

- **Morning darshan:** Opens at 4:00 am; closes around 12:00 noon (or 11:30 am depending on the season)
- **Evening darshan:** Opens at 4:00 pm; closes around 9:00 pm (or 9:30 pm during summer)

Between the morning and evening sessions, the mandir closes for about 4 hours — typically from 12 noon to 4 pm — during which the priests perform the midday rituals and the sanctum is prepared for the next session. During this gap, devotees can visit the outer courtyards, the Anand Sagar complex, or the nearby shops.

The aarti (prayer ceremony with lamps) happens at both the morning and evening sessions. The morning aarti begins shortly after the gates open, around 4:30 am. The evening aarti is typically around 7:00 pm to 8:00 pm, depending on the season and the crowd size. The aarti is the most spiritually charged moment of the day — the chanting, the lamps, and the gathered devotees create an atmosphere that many first-time visitors find unexpectedly powerful. If you can choose one thing to prioritize during your visit, it is attending the evening aarti.

## Darshan queues: what to expect

Shegaon operates several darshan queues simultaneously, which means you can choose between speed and proximity to the sanctum.

**General queue:** This is the free queue, open to everyone. It moves steadily on regular days and is the best option for most visitors. During festivals, this queue can stretch to 2 or 3 hours. On a regular weekday morning, the wait is typically 15 to 30 minutes.

**Special darshan:** The Sansthan offers a paid special darshan option that bypasses the general queue and takes devotees directly to the sanctum within 10 to 15 minutes. The fee is small (typically ₹51 or ₹101 — check at the counter for the current rate). This is the recommended option for: senior citizens, visitors with mobility issues, families with young children, and anyone on a tight travel schedule.

**Online booking:** The Sansthan has been expanding its digital booking infrastructure. For the most current status of online darshan booking, check the official Sansthan website or the [accommodation booking page](/booking). Online booking is not yet available for all darshan slots, so plan to arrive in person for the special darshan counter.

## Crowd patterns by time of day

The flow of devotees at the mandir follows a predictable rhythm. Understanding this helps you plan your visit:

**4:00 am to 7:00 am — the best window.** The gates have just opened, the queue is shortest, the temperature is pleasant, and the early morning light makes the temple complex feel peaceful. The morning aarti around 4:30 am is attended by a relatively small group. If you are staying at the Bhakta Niwas, plan to leave the accommodation by 3:45 am so you can be at the gate by 4:00 am.

**7:00 am to 11:00 am — the main morning rush.** Day-trippers from nearby towns arrive during this window. The queue lengthens progressively, and by 10 am on a Saturday or Sunday, the general queue may be 45 minutes to an hour. The temple is busiest between 9 am and 11 am.

**11:00 am to 4:00 pm — the temple closes.** The sanctum closes for the midday break. The outer courtyards remain open, and many devotees spend this time at the Anand Sagar complex or the shops near the temple. This is also a good time to visit if you are mainly interested in the temple architecture and history rather than darshan. See our [Samadhi Mandir guide](/shegaon-samadhi-mandir-history-architecture) for what to observe during the midday closure.

**4:00 pm to 6:00 pm — the calm evening window.** The gates reopen, and the queue is again manageable for the first couple of hours. If you missed the morning, this is your best opportunity for a relatively quick darshan.

**6:00 pm to 9:00 pm — the evening peak and aarti.** This is the most popular darshan window. The evening aarti (around 7:00–8:00 pm) draws the largest crowd of the day. The queue can be 60 to 90 minutes during this window, but the experience of the aarti makes it worthwhile. Arrive by 6:30 pm to get through the queue in time for aarti seating.

## Darshan on Pragat Din (February 12)

Pragat Din — the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj appeared (pragat) in human form — is celebrated on Magha Shuddha Panchami, which falls in February (usually February 12). It is the single biggest festival at Shegaon after Samadhi Utsav.

On Pragat Din:

- The mandir opens early (typically 2:00 am) and stays open continuously through the night.
- The queue can stretch for several kilometres; arrive the night before.
- Special darshan bookings are available but sell out weeks in advance.
- The Sansthan arranges for additional counter staff, security, and crowd management.
- Free annadan (community meals) are served throughout the day at multiple locations around the temple complex.
- Parking near the temple is extremely limited; arrive early or park at designated lots on the outskirts and walk.

See our [Pragat Din darshan planning guide](/pragat-din-darshan-planning) for a complete festival-day itinerary.

## Darshan during Samadhi Utsav (Rishi Panchami)

Samadhi Utsav commemorates the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj took samadhi — September 8, 1910, on Rishi Panchami. This is the largest festival at Shegaon, typically lasting 5 to 7 days around August–September.

During Samadhi Utsav:

- The mandir opens at midnight and operates continuously for the full festival period.
- The crowds are at their annual peak — 100,000+ devotees on the main day.
- Special trains and buses are arranged by the Sansthan and the railways.
- Advance booking of Bhakta Niwas is essential — rooms are typically booked out 30 to 60 days before the festival. See our [Rishi Panchami Samadhi Utsav guide](/rishi-panchami-shegaon-samadhi-utsav-guide) and our [Bhakta Niwas tariff guide](/bhakt-niwas-room-tariff-room-types-2026) for rates and availability.
- Free meals are served continuously in the large pandals near Anand Sagar.
- The evening aarti during Samadhi Utsav is especially elaborate, with thousands of devotees holding lamps.

## What to expect inside the mandir

The sanctum (garbhagriha) of the Samadhi Mandir is compact — it was designed so that the devotee is close to the saint's samadhi. The structure is simple: a black stone platform marks the spot, and the sanctum is surrounded by a narrow circumambulatory path. On busy days, the darshan experience is brief — a few seconds of proximity to the samadhi before moving on. This is normal and expected; the temple architecture has been designed for volume rather than prolonged individual devotion inside the sanctum.

Outside the sanctum, there are spaces for longer sitting and prayer — the outer mandapa (hall) has seating areas where you can sit and chant or meditate after darshan. Many devotees spend 15 to 30 minutes outside the sanctum in quiet reflection after the formal darshan.

## Photography and video policy

Photography is generally not permitted inside the sanctum. Photography is allowed in the outer courtyards and the Anand Sagar complex. During festivals, the Sansthan may impose additional restrictions — check with volunteers at the gate if you are unsure.

## Tips for a smooth darshan experience

**Arrive early for the best experience.** This cannot be overstated. The 4:00 am window on a regular day is incomparably better than any other time.

**Use special darshan if you are short on time.** The fee is small and the time saved is significant. It is especially worth it during Pragat Din and Samadhi Utsav.

**Check the calendar for festival days.** If your visit coincides with a festival, plan for longer queues and reduced comfort. If you want a peaceful experience, avoid festival days or plan to arrive a day before or after.

**Keep your ID ready.** The Sansthan requires photo ID for room booking at Bhakta Niwas and occasionally at the temple gate during peak periods. Carry one valid government-issued ID per adult.

**Combine with annadan.** The free meal at the Sansthan kitchen is a meaningful part of the visit. See our [free food guide](/shegaon-annadan-mahaprasad-free-food-guide) for timings.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What are the earliest darshan timings at Shegaon?**
A: The gates open at 4:00 am for morning darshan. On festival days like Pragat Din and Samadhi Utsav, they may open earlier (around midnight or 2:00 am). The 4:00 am window on a regular day is the best time to visit — shortest queues, peaceful atmosphere, and the morning aarti.

**Q: Is there a dress code for darshan?**
A: Modest dress is expected. Men should wear long pants or a dhoti. Women should wear a sari, salwar kameez, or a long skirt with a top. Shoulders should be covered. Shorts, sleeveless tops, and revealing clothing are not appropriate inside the temple.

**Q: How long does the darshan queue usually take?**
A: On a regular weekday morning, 15 to 30 minutes. On a weekend morning, 30 to 60 minutes. During Pragat Din and Samadhi Utsav, the queue can be 2 to 4 hours. The special darshan queue reduces this to 10 to 15 minutes.

**Q: Can I get darshan for my elderly parents who cannot stand in a queue?**
A: Yes. The special darshan counter is designed for devotees with mobility or health limitations. You can also inform the volunteers at the general queue entrance — they will often direct elderly visitors to a shorter path or offer assistance.

**Q: Is the darshan area accessible for wheelchair users?**
A: The main sanctum entrance has a few steps and is not fully wheelchair-accessible. The outer courtyard is accessible, and volunteers can help carry a wheelchair up the short flight of steps to the sanctum entrance. For a fully accessible visit, contact the Sansthan office in advance to arrange assistance.

**Q: Are there separate darshan queues for men and women?**
A: No. All devotees use the same queue regardless of gender. However, there are separate prasad counters for men and women at some entry points.

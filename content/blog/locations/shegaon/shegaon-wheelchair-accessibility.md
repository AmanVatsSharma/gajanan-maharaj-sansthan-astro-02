# Shegaon Wheelchair Accessibility

For devotees with reduced mobility, a pilgrimage to Shegaon is not just a spiritual journey — it is also a logistical challenge that requires advance planning. The Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan has made significant efforts to make its facilities accessible, but the reality of a historic temple complex with stone steps and narrow circumambulatory paths means that a completely barrier-free experience is not yet possible. Understanding what is accessible, where assistance is available, and how to plan ahead makes the difference between a frustrating visit and a deeply meaningful one.

## The temple complex: what is accessible

The outer courtyard of the Samadhi Mandir is largely accessible. The approach from the main road to the temple gates is on a paved surface that accommodates wheelchairs without difficulty. The outer courtyard, where devotees wait in the queue and where the evening aarti is held, is also on level ground. During regular days, a wheelchair user can navigate from the gate to the queue area without assistance.

The main challenge begins at the sanctum entrance. The garbhagriha — the innermost chamber where the samadhi is located — is accessed via a short flight of stone steps. This is a structural feature of the temple that predates modern accessibility standards, and it cannot be modified. For wheelchair users who cannot navigate even a few steps independently, the Sansthan's volunteers are trained to assist by carrying the wheelchair up to the sanctum entrance. This assistance is offered routinely during regular darshan hours, and the volunteers are experienced in handling the procedure with respect and care.

The circumambulatory path (parikrama) around the sanctum is narrow and involves a few uneven sections. Wheelchair users who wish to complete the parikrama will need assistance from a companion or a volunteer. The path is short — typically a few metres — but the width and the surface condition make independent navigation difficult.

Outside the main temple, the Anand Sagar complex is considerably more accessible. The park has wide, paved walkways, ramps at key points, and a level terrain that is easily navigable by wheelchair. The garden areas, the water features, and the seating zones around the park are all designed for accessibility. For wheelchair users who want a peaceful space for reflection away from the temple crowds, Anand Sagar is an excellent alternative. Our [Anand Sagar visit guide](/shegaon-anand-sagar-visit-guide) provides more detail on what the park offers.

## Bhakta Niwas accommodation: accessibility features

The room allocation policy at Bhakta Niwas allows for ground-floor rooms to be assigned to guests with mobility needs. These rooms are closer to the dining hall and the main exit, reducing the distance that needs to be covered in a wheelchair. The attached bathrooms in these rooms typically have wider doorways than the standard configuration, though they do not have grab rails or walk-in shower stalls by default. If you have specific accessibility requirements for the bathroom, mention them when you call to book.

The dormitory hall at Bhakta Niwas is on the ground floor, which means it is accessible without stairs. However, the shared bathroom facilities in the hall may not be fully wheelchair-accessible, and you should discuss your specific needs with the booking desk before confirming a dormitory booking.

Anand Vihar, the premium property near Anand Sagar, has a higher proportion of ground-floor rooms and a garden setting with paved walkways. The overall environment is more accessible than the main Bhakta Niwas complex, making Anand Vihar a better choice for wheelchair users who want a calmer, more navigable stay. For a comparison of both properties, see our [Bhakta Niwas vs Anand Vihar guide](/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-vs-anand-vihar).

Both properties include all meals in the tariff, with no hidden charges. The dining hall at Bhakta Niwas is on the ground floor and accessible, with seating that accommodates wheelchairs. The Mahaprasad canteen operates in the same building as the dining hall, so meal times are straightforward from an accessibility perspective.

## Planning ahead: what to do before you arrive

The single most important step for a wheelchair user planning a Shegaon visit is to contact the Sansthan in advance. Call or WhatsApp +91 80531 90691 and explain your specific mobility requirements. The booking desk will note your request and ensure that the accommodation and temple teams are prepared for your arrival. Advance notice allows the Sansthan to:

Assign a ground-floor room near the dining hall and exit. This is not always possible during festival season when every room is booked, but during regular months the team can almost always accommodate the request.

Brief the volunteer team at the temple gate about your arrival. The volunteers at the sanctum entrance are then ready to provide wheelchair assistance as soon as you reach the steps.

Arrange for a volunteer escort from the accommodation to the temple. During festival periods, when the crowds are dense, having a volunteer guide you through the queue saves significant time and reduces the physical effort of navigating the crowd.

Arrange special darshan if needed. The paid special darshan option bypasses the general queue and takes devotees directly to the sanctum within 10 to 15 minutes. This is particularly useful for wheelchair users who would find standing in a long queue physically demanding. The fee is typically ₹51 or ₹101 — confirm the current rate when you call.

## During your visit: practical tips

Arrive at the temple during the quieter windows. The 4:00 am to 7:00 am morning darshan session is the least crowded, and the volunteers are more available to provide individual assistance. The 4:00 pm to 6:00 pm evening window is also manageable. Avoid the 6:00 pm to 9:00 pm peak if possible, as the crowds make wheelchair navigation more difficult and the volunteer team is stretched thin.

Carry a companion if you can. While the Sansthan's volunteers are helpful and experienced, having a family member or friend alongside you during darshan provides both practical assistance and emotional comfort. If you are travelling alone, the dormitory warden or the reception desk can arrange for a volunteer to accompany you to the temple.

Use the free shuttle service. The shuttle between Bhakta Niwas and the railway station is accessible and avoids the need to walk long distances with a wheelchair. During festival windows, the shuttle frequency increases, and you can ask the driver to drop you closer to the temple entrance if needed.

Stay hydrated and take breaks. The Shegaon climate is hot and dry for much of the year, and navigating a crowded temple complex in a wheelchair requires physical effort. Carry water, take breaks in the shaded areas of the outer courtyard, and do not attempt too many darshan rounds in a single visit.

## What the Sansthan is doing to improve accessibility

The Sansthan has been progressively improving its accessibility infrastructure. The outer courtyard, the Anand Sagar complex, and the Bhakta Niwas accommodation have been upgraded in recent years to include ramps, wider pathways, and ground-floor room options. The volunteer training programme now includes specific modules on assisting devotees with mobility needs. The special darshan counter, which reduces queue time from hours to minutes, is itself an accessibility measure for devotees who cannot stand for long periods.

These improvements are ongoing. The Sansthan's approach has been pragmatic: rather than undertaking a major structural modification to the historic temple — which would be difficult, expensive, and potentially controversial from a heritage perspective — the focus has been on improving the approaches, the volunteer support, and the accommodation facilities. If you have specific feedback about accessibility after your visit, share it with the Sansthan office. Their responsiveness to practical suggestions has been one of the more positive aspects of the institution's management style.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Can wheelchair users get darshan at the Samadhi Mandir?**
A: Yes. The outer courtyard is fully accessible, and Sansthan volunteers assist wheelchair users up the short flight of steps to the sanctum entrance. Special darshan is available for devotees with mobility needs and reduces queue time to 10 to 15 minutes. Contact the Sansthan in advance at +91 80531 90691 to arrange assistance.

**Q: Are the Bhakta Niwas rooms wheelchair-accessible?**
A: Ground-floor rooms can be assigned upon request. The booking desk will note your mobility requirements when you call. The room bathrooms typically have wider doorways but may not have grab rails or walk-in showers. Mention your specific needs at the time of booking.

**Q: Is Anand Vihar better suited for wheelchair users than Bhakta Niwas?**
A: Anand Vihar has a higher proportion of ground-floor rooms and a garden setting with paved walkways, making it more navigable for wheelchair users. If accessibility is a primary concern, Anand Vihar is often the better choice. You can book both properties through the same number: +91 80531 90691.

**Q: Is the Anand Sagar complex wheelchair-accessible?**
A: Yes. Anand Sagar has wide, paved walkways, ramps at key points, and level terrain throughout the park. It is an excellent alternative to the temple for wheelchair users who want a peaceful spiritual space. See our [Anand Sagar visit guide](/shegaon-anand-sagar-visit-guide) for more information.

**Q: Should I inform the Sansthan about my mobility needs before arriving?**
A: Yes, absolutely. Calling or WhatsApp-ing +91 80531 90691 before your visit allows the Sansthan to prepare a ground-floor room, brief the volunteer team, and arrange darshan assistance. Advance notice makes a significant difference to the quality of your experience.

**Q: Are there accessible toilets at the temple complex?**
A: Accessible toilet facilities are available at the Bhakta Niwas accommodation complex and at Anand Sagar. Inside the main temple area, the facilities are more limited. The booking desk can provide specific information about the current accessible toilet locations when you call to book.

# Trimbakeshwar Darshan Timing Guide | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

Stepping off the MSRTC bus at Trimbakeshwar town, the first thing you notice is not the temple — it is the mountain. The Sahyadri range closes in around the town on three sides, and the Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga Temple sits at their foothills at coordinates 19.9418 degrees North, 73.5298 degrees East, 28 kilometres from Nashik. The temple is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas and holds a unique position in the Shaiva tradition: it is also where the Godavari River begins its long journey to the Bay of Bengal.

Arriving with a clear plan for darshan timing transforms the experience. The temple keeps a rigorous daily schedule of aartis, queues stretch and compress in predictable patterns across the day, and the right window of arrival can mean the difference between a calm 20-minute darshan and a two-hour wait. This guide covers operating hours, each aarti, queue strategy, Mahashivratri timings, dress code, nearby attractions, and the Sansthan's Trimbakeshwar accommodation.

## Temple Operating Hours and Daily Aarti Schedule

The Trimbakeshwar temple generally opens around 5am and remains accessible to devotees until approximately 10pm. Timings can shift by 15 to 30 minutes during festival periods; confirm with the temple administration on arrival.

The Kakad Aarti at roughly 5:30am is the first aarti of the day and the one most seasoned pilgrims prioritize. The temple is quiet at this hour, the air is cool coming off the Sahyadri slopes, and the queue is at its shortest. Arriving by 5am virtually guarantees darshan within 15 to 20 minutes. The Madhyahna Aarti at around 12pm is the midday offering, busier but manageable on a weekday. The Godavari Aarti at approximately 6pm draws the largest evening crowd, particularly on Mondays and during Shravan. The Shej Aarti at around 10pm is the final aarti of the day, a quieter closing ceremony.

On Mondays, expect crowds throughout the day, especially at the Kakad and Godavari aartis. The same holds during Shravan (July to August). If you are visiting during either of these peak windows, target the Kakad Aarti at 5:30am.

## Queue Strategy and Crowd Patterns

The darshan queue at Trimbakeshwar is organized but can stretch to over an hour during peak periods. Several factors determine your wait time, and understanding them lets you plan around them rather than simply waiting it out.

Early morning is consistently the best window. The Kakad Aarti queue at 5:30am typically processes devotees quickly, partly because the overall crowd is thin at that hour and partly because the temple committee dedicates more volunteer staff during the first two hours of the day. Weekdays — Tuesday through Thursday — are lighter than Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, when the temple receives a larger share of local visitors from Nashik, Mumbai, and Pune. The Godavari Aarti at 6pm on a weekday is comfortable; the same aarti on a Saturday evening during Shravan can mean a two-hour wait.

Shivaratri is in a category of its own. Mahashivratri at Trimbakeshwar sees the temple open continuously for 24 hours, with aartis at irregular intervals throughout the night. The crowd density is unlike any other day of the year, and the queue for darshan can extend for several kilometres. If you are planning to visit on Mahashivratri specifically, arrive at least 6 to 8 hours before your desired aarti time, carry water and snacks, and dress in layers — the mountain air turns cold after midnight even in late winter. The Sansthan often coordinates group transport from Nashik to Trimbakeshwar on Shivaratri, and booking through the [Booking page](/booking) well in advance is strongly recommended.

## Dress Code and What to Carry

The dress code at Trimbakeshwar temple is simple but enforced: men should wear a shirt with trousers or dhoti, and women should wear a saree, salwar kameez, or a long skirt with a blouse. Shorts, sleeveless tops, and western dresses are not permitted inside the main sanctum area, and security staff at the entrance will ask you to cover up if necessary. Head covering is not mandatory but many devotees choose to bring a scarf anyway, especially for the morning aartis when the mountain air is cool.

Leather items are strictly prohibited. Remove leather belts, wallets, watches with leather straps, and any leather accessories before entering. The temple complex has a cloakroom system for storing these items, but it is more convenient to leave them in your accommodation or vehicle. Carry a small amount of cash for prasad and donation boxes inside the temple; the card machines are not always functional during busy periods. A water bottle helps during the queue, particularly in the warmer months between March and June.

## Beyond the Main Temple: Gorakhnath Cave and Kushavarta Kund

The Trimbakeshwar complex includes several important sites beyond the main Jyotirlinga shrine, and most devotees visit them as part of the same darshan round. The Gorakhnath Cave, named after the Nath yogi Gorakhnath who meditated here, is a small rock-cut cave located a short walk from the main temple. It requires a separate, much shorter queue and offers a quiet space that many pilgrims find more introspective than the busy main sanctum. The cave is accessible during temple hours, and there is no separate fee.

The Kushavarta Kund is the sacred reservoir where the Godavari River first surfaces before flowing toward Nashik and onward to the sea. Taking a dip here — or at least walking around the kund in clockwise circumambulation — is considered spiritually significant, particularly for devotees performing tirtha yatras or shraddha ceremonies. The water is cold even in summer and the stone steps around the kund can be slippery, so wear sturdy footwear and move carefully. Many pilgrims combine the kund visit with the main temple darshan and complete the entire circuit in about 90 minutes if the queues are light.

## Sansthan Accommodation in Trimbakeshwar

The Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan operates accommodation facilities in Trimbakeshwar for pilgrims, primarily designed to support devotees who are doing multi-location yatras that include Shegaon as their base. The facility provides basic, clean rooms at nominal rates, and booking through the Sansthan's official channels guarantees that you are not overcharged during peak periods — a real concern during Mahashivratri and Shravan, when private hotels in Trimbakeshwar town routinely double or triple their rates.

If the Sansthan facility is fully booked, Nashik city offers a much wider range of hotels and lodges, located approximately 28km from Trimbakeshwar by road. The drive takes about 45 minutes to an hour depending on traffic. Many devotees prefer staying in Nashik and making a day trip to Trimbakeshwar, particularly during quieter months. ## Practical Tips for First-Time Visitors

The mountain climate at Trimbakeshwar is noticeably cooler than Nashik city or Shegaon, especially during early morning and evening hours. Even in peak summer, a light jacket or shawl is useful for the Kakad Aarti queue. The best months to visit are October through February, when the post-monsoon air is clear, the Godavari is flowing well, and the Sahyadri slopes are green.

Photography inside the main sanctum is not permitted, and phones should be switched to silent mode before joining the queue. Many devotees find that putting their phone away entirely and approaching the darshan without screens allows for a deeper, more focused experience. The temple has a designated photography area outside the main hall for those who want to capture the temple facade and the surrounding mountain views.

If you are combining Trimbakeshwar with the other Jyotirlingas on your itinerary, allow at least one full day here. The morning at Trimbakeshwar, a midday drive or train to Nashik, and an evening at one of the Nashik vineyards make for a well-rounded day that balances the intense spiritual focus of the temple with a gentler, more relaxed pace.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the exact temple opening and closing times?**
The temple generally opens at 5am and closes at 10pm, though these timings can shift by 30 minutes on festival days or during special observances. During Mahashivratri, the temple remains open continuously for the full 24 hours. Confirm current timings with the temple administration or the Sansthan office if your visit coincides with a major festival.

**Is there a separate fee for darshan, or is it free?**
Darshan at Trimbakeshwar is free for all visitors. There is no VIP darshan ticket system at the main temple, and the queue is open to everyone. Donations are voluntary and accepted at designated boxes inside the complex.

**How long does the darshan queue typically take?**
On a normal weekday morning during the Kakad Aarti around 5:30am, the queue processes in 15 to 20 minutes. During Godavari Aarti on weekends or Shravan Mondays, expect 45 minutes to over an hour. During Mahashivratri, the wait can extend to several hours. Arriving early is the most reliable way to keep the wait short.

**Can I take a dip in the Kushavarta Kund?**
Yes, the Kushavarta Kund is open to all devotees for ritual bathing and circumambulation. There is no separate entry fee, though small donations to the kund maintenance fund are welcome. Be aware that the steps can be slippery and the water cold; move carefully, particularly if you are visiting during winter or the post-monsoon months.

**Are there accommodation options near the temple?**
Yes. The Sansthan operates a basic accommodation facility in Trimbakeshwar for pilgrims. Private dharamshalas and budget hotels are also available in the town. Nashik city, 28km away, has a much wider selection of hotels at various price points. The [Trimbakeshwar Accommodation Guide](/blog/triambakeshwar-accommodation-guide) covers all available options in detail.

**What is the significance of Trimbakeshwar among the 12 Jyotirlingas?**
Trimbakeshwar is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas and is uniquely significant because the Godavari River originates here. It is also associated with Lord Shiva's association with the three puras (tri + ambaka), and the temple's self-manifested Jyotirlinga is believed to embody all three of Lord Shiva's eyes. The site is deeply connected to the mythology of Gautama Rishi, who performed intense penance here to bring the Ganga down to Earth.

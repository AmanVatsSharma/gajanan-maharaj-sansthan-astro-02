# Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan Room Booking Phone Number Guide

The right way to reach the Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan room booking office by phone is through the numbers published on the Sansthan's own [contact page](/contact) — the single canonical source the office keeps current for all four centres. This guide deliberately repeats no digits, because copied numbers go stale and stale numbers strand pilgrims; the page is where the live detail lives. What we do give you is everything around the number: when calling works best, what to say so the office can help in one call, how the phone fits with the [online booking request](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-sansthan-online-room-booking), and — most importantly — how to recognise the fake "booking numbers" that circulate on lookalike websites and social pages, which have cost devotees real money and real tears.

The Sansthan digital desk handles the fallout of tout numbers every festival season, so this guide is written with some feeling. Read it once before your next pilgrimage, and share it with the elder in the family who does the booking calls.

## Why the contact page is the only number worth trusting

A phone number is a fact that decays. Offices change lines, festival temporary numbers come and go, and a digit typed wrong in a forwarded message replicates faster than any correction. The Sansthan therefore maintains the [contact page](/contact) as the canonical, continuously checked source for room booking and office contact across Shegaon, Pandharpur, Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar — and asks every guide, including ours, to point there rather than freeze digits in print.

The contrast with unofficial listings is sharp. Search results and social pages aggregate numbers from years-old directories; lookalike sites sometimes publish numbers that route to travel agents or outright touts posing as the Sansthan. Devotees who dial a number found outside the official channels have reported being asked for immediate transfers to "confirm" rooms that the Sansthan never held. The office's rule of thumb is simple enough to teach a child: the number is genuine if you got it from the Sansthan's own page, and everything else is to be verified before trusted. For the full picture of legitimate contact channels — phone, WhatsApp and office visits — our [complete Sansthan contact channels guide](/blog/all-sansthan-contact-numbers-complete-list) and the article on [how to verify official Sansthan contacts](/blog/how-to-verify-official-sansthan-contacts) are the two references to keep.

## When a call is the right tool — and when it is not

Phone contact suits some jobs better than others, and knowing which saves the office's lines for true phone work.

| The task | Best channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A new room booking request | The [booking page](/booking) | The written request creates the file the office acts on; calls create memory, not records |
| Checking whether dates are open | Phone, from the contact page's numbers | A quick live answer suits availability questions |
| Following up a request already submitted | Phone or WhatsApp, quoting your reference | The reference lets the office pull your file in seconds |
| Changing headcount, dates or room type | Phone followed by written confirmation of the change | Verbal requests need the office's written reply to become firm |
| Verifying a doubtful message or "confirmation" | Phone, using only the contact page's number | Verification must run through a channel the tout cannot stand between |
| Emergency during travel | The emergency channels on the contact page | Emergencies bypass ordinary queues; our [emergency contact during pilgrimage](/blog/emergency-contact-during-pilgrimage) guide covers this |

Notice the pattern: the phone excels at live, small, specific questions, while the request itself and anything that must endure belongs in writing. Devotees who phone in a full booking as a stream of details, and then phone again the next day to amend it, are the office's hardest files — not because the office is unwilling, but because memory is not a register.

## Making the one-call booking call

When a call is genuinely the right tool, five minutes of preparation turns it into the only call you need.

1. **Dial only the number on the contact page.** Open the [contact page](/contact), find the room booking line for your centre, and call that — not a number forwarded in a family group.
2. **State the centre first.** "Shegaon", "Pandharpur", "Omkareshwar" or "Trimbakeshwar" — the network serves all four, and the opening word routes you correctly.
3. **Give the party facts plainly.** Headcount including children, arrival date, number of nights, and room preference if any. If you have a reference from a submitted request, give it before anything else.
4. **Ask your specific questions while the office has the file open.** Availability for your dates, whether your party fits one room or two — capacity equals bed count, so a party of five needs a 4-bed room plus a 2-bed, or two smaller rooms — and anything about access needs.
5. **Write down what the office says, and ask for the written follow-up.** The note anchors your memory; the written confirmation from the office anchors the booking itself.
6. **Close with your name and a callback number.** If the line drops — festival days are busy — the office can reach you back.

One honesty note from experience: office lines are busiest in the days around each ekadashi and the big utsavs. A call placed mid-week outside festival windows usually reaches a calmer desk. Persistence with politeness is the devotee's proven strategy.

## The fake-number problem, and how to see through it

Tout numbers work by imitation, so the tells are learnable. Warn the family, especially elders booking for the whole group.

- **The demand for urgent payment.** A caller pressing you to transfer money immediately to "hold the last room" is the classic tout move. The Sansthan's real flow is a request, an office confirmation, and payments against receipts — never a panic transfer.
- **A number that is not on the contact page.** Whatever the story — "new booking wing", "festival desk", "VIP seva line" — if the [contact page](/contact) does not carry it, treat it as unverified until the office confirms it from the official number.
- **Unrealistic promises.** Guaranteed AC rooms during Ashadhi week, instant confirmations during Mahashivratri — the real office manages scarcity honestly and speaks in possibilities, not certainties.
- **WhatsApp display-picture impersonation.** Touts copy the Sansthan's logo and even its posts. Verification always runs phone-to-office through the official number, never through the app that the tout controls. Our [WhatsApp booking guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-sansthan-room-booking-whatsapp-guide) covers this channel's own cautions.
- **"Agents" outside the temple and station.** In person, the same rule applies: the booking desk inside the Sansthan premises is the real one. The [guide to reaching the Sansthan office at Shegaon](/blog/how-to-reach-sansthan-office-shegaon) describes the genuine path.

If a family has already paid a suspected tout, two steps: stop further payment immediately, and report the number to the office through the contact page so other devotees are spared. The [common booking mistakes guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-common-booking-mistakes) gathers these hard lessons in one place — worth reading before any festival trip.

## Phone plus page: the workflow that never fails

Families who never lose a room combine both channels deliberately, and the workflow is worth copying exactly.

1. **Submit the request in writing first** through the [booking page](/booking), so the file exists.
2. **Call the office a reasonable interval later** — from the contact page's number, reference in hand — to confirm receipt and ask anything live.
3. **Keep the written confirmation** when it comes, and file it with every subsequent call note.
4. **Call before travel only for genuine changes** — a delayed train, an extra traveller — not for reassurance loops; the confirmation you hold is the assurance.
5. **Save the contact page, not a copied number**, so every future call starts from the current, canonical source.

This rhythm respects both sides: the office's time and the family's certainty. Elders who prefer voice get their call; the household gets its paper trail. ## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I find the official Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan room booking phone number?

On the Sansthan's own [contact page](/contact), which the office maintains as the single current source for all four centres. This guide and every genuine Sansthan guide points there rather than printing digits, because a copied number goes stale while the page stays live.

### Why will no Sansthan article print the booking number directly?

Because numbers change and copies do not. A digit printed in an article or forwarded in a message keeps circulating after the office changes the line — and lookalike sites exploit exactly that confusion. The canonical page cannot be outrun by a screenshot.

### Someone called offering to book my rooms for a fee. Is this the Sansthan?

No. The Sansthan does not operate through fee-charging booking agents or cold callers. Verify any doubtful call against the office using the contact page's number, and report the caller. Urgent payment demands are the surest sign of a tout.

### Is it better to phone or to use the online booking request?

Use both in their roles: the [booking page](/booking) to create the request in writing, and the phone for live questions and follow-ups. A booking that exists only as a phone conversation has no file; a question that needed a live answer has no substitute for the call.

### What should I do if the office phone is busy during a festival?

Call mid-week or outside peak hours if you can, and fall back on the written channels — the request page for bookings, and the contact page's other routes for queries. Festival weeks strain every line; politeness and patience, repeated, get through.

### Can I call to check availability for all four centres in one call?

You can ask, but expect the clearest answers one centre at a time — Shegaon, Pandharpur, Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar each have their own sheets and seasons. State the centre first, finish one enquiry, then move to the next.

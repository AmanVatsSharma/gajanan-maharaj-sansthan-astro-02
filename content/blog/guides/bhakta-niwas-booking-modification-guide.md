# Bhakta Niwas Booking Modification Guide

Changing a Bhakta Niwas booking — a new date, a different room row, one more pilgrim on the register — is done through the same office that confirmed the original request, in writing, with your reference number at the head of the message. There is no self-service button that silently rewrites a stay, and that is deliberate: every modification touches an allocation chart that a human being at the Sansthan office balances by hand. Send the change through the [contact page](/contact) quoting your confirmation, let the office reply with the amended terms, and keep the new message as your truth thereafter. The published rows — ₹1,650 to ₹2,550 — and their arithmetic simply recalculate around whatever the change turns out to be.

This guide covers what a modification can touch, the request sequence, the money arithmetic of moving dates or rows, the festival-window differences, and the point at which a modification becomes a cancellation.

## What a modification can change, and what it cannot

The office's authority over a confirmed booking is broad, but it is not magic, and devotees plan better when they know its edges.

| Change | Modifiable? | The honest note |
|---|---|---|
| Travel dates, within availability | Yes | The most common change, and the one charts absorb most readily in plain weeks |
| Room row — non-AC to AC, 3-bed to 4-bed | Yes, subject to the chart | The tariff difference is recalculated at published rates |
| Arrival window — evening to dawn | Yes | Worth doing before travel day; it shapes room readiness |
| Adding a pilgrim within bed capacity | Yes | Capacity still equals the bed count — a 3-bed room cannot become a 4-head room |
| Names on the register | Yes, with ID carried | Bring the new pilgrim's photo ID to the desk |
| Headcount beyond the room's beds | No | A second room must be requested; capacity rules do not bend |
| Transferring a booking to another party | Ask the office | Handled case by case, never assumed |

Read the table's spirit rather than its letter: the office moves heaven for a grandfather's knee and a delayed train, but it cannot print a fifth bed in a 4-bed room. Where a party grows beyond its row, the honest modification is a second room — often the 2 Bed AC at ₹1,650 beside the family's original booking — requested in the same message so the rooms travel together, as the [family room booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-family-room-booking-guide) advises.

## Modifications the office handles every week

Because devotees imagine their case to be uniquely complicated, a short catalogue of the ordinary ones helps everyone. A train cancelled the night before travel, and the whole yatra sliding by a day — handled as a date change, usually absorbed when the new date is a plain weekday. A wedding invitation that turns a two-night Shegaon stay into one night — handled as a shortening, with the office recalculating the tariff and settling the difference against a receipt. A booking made in the cool season that now falls in April, and the family wants AC — handled as a row change, the ₹300 per-night premium recalculated from the change date onward. An elder joining the yatra at the last hour — handled as a register addition where the beds allow, or a second room where they do not. A devotee who booked for the ekadashi and now reads the crowd forecasts and wishes to arrive a day earlier — handled gladly, and often to the pilgrim's considerable benefit.

What unites the catalogue is that none of these requests was made by shouting, by escalation, or at the counter at midnight. Each was a message with a reference number, answered by an office that handles a hundred like it. Devotees who fear that their change is an imposition should read the catalogue twice: the imposition is not the request, it is the request made carelessly.

## Sending the modification: the sequence

A well-formed modification is answered faster than a vague one, and the difference is five minutes of care.

1. **Open with your reference.** The confirmation number, the original dates and the room row, stated in the first line, lets the office find your file in one motion.
2. **State the change as a complete sentence** — "please move our 4 Bed AC from 14 to 21 of the month, same party of four" — rather than a fragment the office must reconstruct.
3. **Give your reason where it helps.** A delayed train, a medical need, a family wedding: context moves requests up the office's queue of judgements.
4. **Wait for the office's amended confirmation before acting on it.** The modification is real when the office says it is, not when you send the message; the pathway a confirmation travels is mapped in the [Bhakta Niwas booking confirmation process](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-confirmation-process).
5. **Save the amended message and let go of the old one.** Two confirmations in two inboxes is how families arrive holding a cancelled room and a confirmed one in the wrong order.

The follow-up etiquette — one polite nudge, with the reference, after a reasonable interval — is identical to a fresh booking's, and the [Omkareshwar booking follow-up tips](/blog/omkareshwar-booking-follow-up-and-confirmation-tips) describe it from a desk that handles many such threads.

## The arithmetic of moving dates and rows

Modifications recalculate against the same published table, and the desk encourages devotees to do the sum before requesting the change — it makes the office's reply a confirmation rather than a surprise.

| Change of row | Tariff movement per night | Over a two-night stay |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Bed Non-AC to 3 Bed AC | ₹1,750 to ₹2,050 — up ₹300 | Up ₹600 |
| 4 Bed Non-AC to 4 Bed AC | ₹2,250 to ₹2,550 — up ₹300 | Up ₹600 |
| 3 Bed Non-AC to 4 Bed Non-AC | ₹1,750 to ₹2,250 — up ₹500 | Up ₹1,000 |
| 2 Bed AC to 4 Bed AC | ₹1,650 to ₹2,550 — up ₹900 | Up ₹1,800 |
| 4 Bed AC to 4 Bed Non-AC | ₹2,550 to ₹2,250 — down ₹300 | Down ₹600 |

The same arithmetic governs date changes whenever the row stays constant — the tariff is per night, so a stay moved from two nights to three at 4 Bed AC simply becomes ₹7,650 rather than ₹5,100. Where an advance was paid against the original terms, the adjustment is settled through the office against a receipt, never in cash at the counter without one; the structure of advances is explained in the [advance amount guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-advance-amount-guide), and the timing of confirmations around them in the [Shegaon booking confirmation timeline](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-booking-confirmation-timeline).

Two worked examples make the arithmetic concrete. First, the family that booked a 3 Bed Non-AC for two nights — ₹3,500 — and modifies to a 3 Bed AC for the same dates: the new total is ₹4,100, and the difference of ₹600 is settled when the office confirms the change. Second, the family that booked a 4 Bed AC for three nights — ₹7,650 — and shortens to two nights: the recalculation returns ₹2,550 to the file's balance, handled per the office's settlement practice against a receipt. In both cases the devotee could have predicted the office's answer to the rupee before sending the message, and that predictability is the point — a modification under these tariffs is arithmetic, not negotiation, and nobody's case needs to be argued.

## Festival windows treat modifications differently

A modification is a request for a room the chart may no longer have, and in festival windows the chart answers to the calendar before it answers to you. Moving a plain-week booking by a day is usually absorbed; moving a booking into or across an ekadashi, a Navratri window or the Pragat Din period is a different proposition — the target date is typically committed weeks ahead, and the office can only offer what cancellation releases. Devotees planning festival-adjacent changes should read the [festival dates and booking windows guide](/blog/festival-dates-and-booking-windows) before choosing the new date, and the ekadashi mechanics are documented for Shegaon in the [ekadashi and festival booking guide](/blog/shegaon-room-booking-during-ekadashi-and-festivals).

The honest posture for a festival-window modification is the one devotees call "ask early, hold lightly": send the change request the moment the need appears, because you stand in a queue of cancellations that is worked in order; and hold every plan — train tickets above all — lightly until the office's amended confirmation arrives. The Pandharpur wari season sharpens the counsel further, since the town's rooms are spoken for in a rhythm of their own; pilgrims moving dates around that season should read the [Pandharpur festival season guide](/blog/pandharpur-festival-season-guide) before they choose the destination date, not after.

The reverse move deserves a line of praise: shifting out of a festival date into a plain week is often the easiest modification the office handles, and the traveller gains both a confirmed room and a quieter darshan. The [peak versus off-season guide](/blog/peak-season-vs-off-season-guide) quantifies the contrast.

A closing thought on the modification habit itself. Devotees who modify frequently — and families with trains in their blood modify more than they admit — should keep a single note on their phone with every live reference number, the current dates and the row booked, updated the moment each change is confirmed. The note takes seconds to keep and saves the one genuinely painful modification scenario: the family that arrives holding a confirmation from two versions ago, certain of dates the office last saw a month past. The register is kind, but it is honest, and honesty runs in both directions.

## When the change is really a cancellation

Some modifications arrive at the desk already dead — the yatra postponed beyond the horizon, the family plan dissolved. At that point the honest instrument is a cancellation, handled under the Sansthan's cancellation and refund practice rather than as a date change; the terms are set out in the [refund and cancellation policy](/blog/bhakta-niwas-refund-and-cancellation-policy), with the Shegaon-specific handling described in the [Shegaon refund and cancellation guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-refund-and-cancellation-guide). Our counsel is uniform: if the new date exists, request a modification; if it does not, request a cancellation early, because an early release returns the room to another devotee and improves whatever the refund arithmetic turns out to be.

And when the modification succeeds but a different centre now suits the journey better, the request travels the same channel — the location pages for [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas](/locations/shegaon-bhakt-niwas), the [Pandharpur Math](/locations/pandharpur-math), [Omkareshwar](/locations/omkareshwar) and [Trimbakeshwar](/locations/trimbakeshwar) describe each complex, and the [Trimbakeshwar booking follow-up tips](/blog/trimbakeshwar-booking-follow-up-and-confirmation-tips) show the same discipline in the hill town.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I change my booking dates after confirmation?

Yes — send the request through the office with your reference number, and treat the office's amended confirmation, not your own message, as the booking's new truth; plain-week changes are absorbed readily, festival-window changes depend entirely on the target chart.

### Can I upgrade from non-AC to AC after booking?

Where the chart holds an AC row, yes: the ₹300 per-night premium at the same bed count is recalculated and settled against a receipt, and requesting it at modification time beats requesting it at the counter.

### What if one more person joins our yatra?

If the party stays within the room's bed count, the office adds the pilgrim to the register — carry their photo ID; if the party exceeds the beds, request a second room in the same message so both allotments move together.

### Is there a fee for modifying a booking?

The rows' published tariffs are the arithmetic of any change — a modification that raises the row raises the tariff accordingly, and any adjustment of an advance is settled through the office against a receipt; no separate charge hides inside the process.

### How do I cancel instead of changing?

Request the cancellation early and in writing through the same channel, then read the [refund and cancellation policy](/blog/bhakta-niwas-refund-and-cancellation-policy) — early releases help the next devotee and your own file alike.

## Change the plan, keep the booking honest

A modification is a small pilgrimage of its own — reference in hand, change stated plainly, patience while the office balances its chart. Do the arithmetic before you write, wait for the amended confirmation before you book trains around it, and let the old dates go once the new ones are real.

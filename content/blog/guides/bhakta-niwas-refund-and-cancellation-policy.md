# Bhakta Niwas Refund And Cancellation Policy | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

Last Diwali, a family from Nagpur booked a 4-Bed AC at Bhakta Niwas three months ahead, then lost their grandmother unexpectedly a week before travel. They emailed the Sansthan office expecting a partial refund and were surprised to get the entire amount back within eight working days — because they had cancelled 22 days ahead. A month earlier, another family had cancelled their booking two days before check-in and received nothing back. The difference was timing, and timing is the heart of this policy.

Bhakta Niwas is run by a Sansthan that exists for seva, not revenue. The room rates — ₹1250 for a 2-Bed Non-AC, ₹2550 for a 4-Bed AC, ₹4150 for a Luxury Suite — are already subsidised, and the cancellation framework exists to balance two competing needs: protecting devotees who must change plans, and protecting the Sansthan's ability to hold rooms for genuine pilgrims during peak seasons. Understanding the policy before you book is the single best way to avoid surprises later.

## The standard cancellation framework

For non-festival dates, the policy is straightforward and tiered by notice period. If you cancel seven or more days before check-in, you receive a full refund to the original payment method. If you cancel between three and seven days before check-in, you receive a 50% refund. If you cancel less than 72 hours before check-in, no refund is processed. The thresholds are counted in calendar days, not working days, so a Saturday cancellation six days before a Friday check-in counts as six days' notice.

The reason for the tiered structure is operational. Once a room is allotted, the Sansthan holds it against your arrival — and a private room at Bhakta Niwas cannot be resold at the last minute because walk-in demand at a temple town is unpredictable. A 50% refund in the 3-to-7-day window covers the cost of preparing the room (bedding, sanitation, ID verification) while still being fair to the devotee. Below 72 hours, the room is essentially lost to the Sansthan, which is why the refund closes off entirely.

## Festival periods — stricter, but fair

During major festivals, the framework tightens because demand outstrips supply by an order of magnitude. Pragat Din in February, the anniversary of Shri Gajanan Maharaj's first appearance, sees Bhakta Niwas effectively full for the entire surrounding week. The same is true for Rishi Panchami in September, marking the day He took samadhi in 1910, and Ganesh Chaturthi in August or September. Mahashivratri in February or March, while primarily a Trimbakeshwar event, also pulls large crowds to Shegaon.

For these festival windows, cancellations made within 15 days of check-in are non-refundable. Cancellations between 15 and 30 days ahead receive a 50% refund, and cancellations 30 days or more ahead receive a full refund. The extended window exists because festival bookings often begin two to three months ahead, and devotees who plan early need flexibility if personal circumstances change. The festival windows also require a small advance deposit at the time of booking — typically 30% of the room tariff — to confirm the reservation. This deposit is non-refundable if you cancel, but it counts toward your final bill on arrival.

## Modifications — treated like cancellations

There is no separate modification policy. Changing your check-in date, your room type, or the number of occupants is treated as a cancellation followed by a fresh booking. Send a WhatsApp message quoting your existing booking reference and stating the new request. If your new dates have availability, the office will cancel the old booking, refund you according to the standard tier (full refund if 7+ days ahead), and create a new reservation. If availability is tight, the office will put you on a waitlist for the new dates and confirm your original booking remains valid until the waitlist resolves.

This approach may feel bureaucratic, but it is the only way the Sansthan can keep its room allocation fair across hundreds of devotees during peak seasons. For deeper context on the system, the [Bhakta Niwas Complete Booking Guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) explains how allocation works in practice.

## No-show policy

If you miss your check-in entirely without notifying the Sansthan, the booking is forfeited. A no-show is defined as a failure to arrive within 24 hours of the stated check-in time without any prior communication. If your train is delayed and you know you will arrive late, send a WhatsApp update the moment you realise — the office routinely holds rooms until the next morning for devotees facing genuine travel disruptions. A no-show without communication is treated differently because the Sansthan has no way to distinguish it from a deliberate abandonment of the booking.

## Partial-stay refunds — not supported

Because Bhakta Niwas runs on a 24-hour check-in and 24-hour check-out cycle, partial-stay refunds are not available. If you book three nights and leave after two, you forfeit the third night's charge. This is not a penalty; it reflects the fact that the room cannot be reassigned to another devotee mid-stay, and the bedding, towels, and Mahaprasad hall access for the missed night were already provisioned. The same 24-hour cycle also applies to early check-in requests — if you arrive at 6am and your booking was for 2pm, the room may not be ready, and the office will store your luggage while you attend darshan.

## Refund timeline

Once a refund is approved, expect the amount to reach your original payment method within five to ten working days. UPI refunds usually clear in two to three days; bank transfers take five to seven; card refunds can take the full ten days because of the bank processing cycle. The Sansthan does not charge any cancellation fee beyond what the tiered policy specifies — there is no "administrative charge" or "processing fee." If you do not see the refund within ten working days, send a follow-up message with your booking reference, and the office will trace the transaction with the bank.

## Force majeure — medical emergencies, train cancellations

Genuine emergencies are handled on a case-by-case basis. If a train is cancelled by Indian Railways, or if a medical emergency in your family prevents travel, send documentation to the [Sansthan office](/contact) — a railway cancellation receipt, a hospital discharge summary, or a doctor's note — and request a goodwill refund. The office reviews these requests weekly and routinely approves full refunds even when the timing would normally fall outside the policy window, provided the documentation is genuine.

This is one of the genuine advantages of booking through the Sansthan directly rather than through a third-party platform. A third-party site has rigid refund rules; the Sansthan can apply judgement. For more on this distinction, the [Phone and WhatsApp Booking Best Practices](/blog/phone-and-whatsapp-booking-best-practices) post explains how direct communication with the office opens these flexibility pathways.

## How to cancel — the practical steps

The fastest way to cancel is a WhatsApp message. Open the chat with the official Sansthan number, quote your booking reference, state clearly "I wish to cancel this booking," and provide a brief reason (the reason is optional but speeds up processing). Within a few hours during the working day, the office will reply with a cancellation confirmation and an estimated refund timeline. For urgent cancellations close to check-in, call the [Sansthan office](/contact) directly — the desk staff can cancel the booking in real time while you are on the line.

## What if you cannot cancel at all

Sometimes life happens faster than policy can handle. A relative passes away, a child falls sick the morning of travel, a work crisis demands your presence at the office. If you cannot cancel and cannot travel, you have one alternative: transfer the booking to a family member. Send the booking reference and the name of the substitute to the office, and at check-in the substitute presents their ID. The room is reassigned to them with no change in tariff. This is the closest thing Bhakta Niwas has to a no-penalty transfer, and it works for blood relatives and, with documentation, for in-laws.

## Subsidy desk — when standard rates are unaffordable

If the standard Bhakta Niwas rates are genuinely difficult for your family, the Sansthan operates a separate subsidy desk next to the Bhakta Niwas reception. Open daily from 9am to 1pm, the desk reviews written applications for further reduction on grounds of financial need. Bring ID proof, a brief letter explaining your circumstances, and any supporting documents (ration card, income certificate). Approvals are not guaranteed and depend on availability, but the Sansthan's mandate is seva, and the subsidy desk exists precisely to ensure that no devotee is turned away for lack of funds.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the standard notice period for a full refund at Bhakta Niwas?** Seven or more calendar days before check-in. Inside seven days, refunds drop to 50%, and inside 72 hours no refund is processed.

**Does the festival-period policy apply to all Sansthan locations?** The festival tiering applies at Bhakta Niwas Shegaon. Other Sansthan-supported locations like Omkareshwar, Pandharpur, and Trimbakeshwar have their own accommodation frameworks; check the [Bhakta Niwas Complete Booking Guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) or the [contact page](/contact) for location-specific terms.

**Can I get a partial refund if I leave a day early?** No. Bhakta Niwas runs on a 24-hour check-in and check-out cycle, and partial-stay refunds are not supported under the standard policy.

**What if my train is cancelled by Indian Railways?** Document the cancellation with the railway's SMS or PRS confirmation, send it to the Sansthan office, and request a goodwill refund. These cases are routinely approved in full even when the timing is short.

**Is there a cancellation fee separate from the refund tier?** No. The tiered refund (full, 50%, or none) is the entire financial outcome. The Sansthan does not charge administrative or processing fees on cancellations.

**Can I transfer my booking to a family member if I cannot travel?** Yes. Send the booking reference and substitute name to the office, and the substitute presents their ID at check-in. The tariff and room allocation remain unchanged.

## Closing reflection

The Bhakta Niwas cancellation and refund policy is built to be predictable rather than lenient. A devotee who understands the timing thresholds and books accordingly will rarely face a frustrating outcome. A devotee who books late for a festival and cancels inside the 15-day window should expect the deposit to be forfeited — that is the price of certainty in a system that serves hundreds of thousands of pilgrims a year. Read the policy once, plan around it, and you will find the system fair, transparent, and quietly generous in genuine emergencies.

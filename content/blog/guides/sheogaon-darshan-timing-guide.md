# Shegaon Darshan Timing Guide | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

The first time you approach the Samadhi Mandir at Shegaon, the energy of the place surprises most visitors. Thousands of devotees move through the queue with a quiet purpose that reflects the deep faith Shri Gajanan Maharaj inspired during his lifetime. But that same devotion means crowds, and crowds mean waiting. Understanding the darshan timings, crowd patterns, and queue dynamics before you arrive transforms your experience from a stressful wait into a contemplative one. This guide covers everything you need to know about planning your darshan at Shegaon.

## Daily Darshan Timings

The Samadhi Mandir opens at 4:00 AM for the pre-dawn Mangala Aarti. This is the most spiritually charged time of day, and for many devotees, it is the only darshan slot worth planning around. The early morning queue begins forming around 3:00 AM, and by 4:00 AM the line can stretch several hundred metres from the main entrance down the road. Mangala Aarti concludes around 5:00 AM, after which the temple closes for a short rest period.

The temple reopens at 8:00 AM for morning darshan. This slot is significantly less crowded than the pre-dawn aarti, particularly on weekdays. Queue times at 8:00 AM typically range from 30 to 60 minutes, compared to 90 to 120 minutes during festival days at the Mangala Aarti. The 8:00 AM slot is ideal for families with children or elderly devotees who want darshan without the physical demands of a 3:00 AM arrival.

Midday darshan hours run from 12:00 PM to 2:00 PM. This period sees moderate crowds, mostly consisting of devotees who arrived by bus or train and are visiting the temple after settling into their accommodation. The queue moves reasonably quickly during these hours, typically 20 to 40 minutes.

Evening darshan begins at 6:00 PM and continues until 9:00 PM. The Shej Aarti at 9:00 PM is the final aarti of the day, and devotees who wish to attend should be in the queue by 8:30 PM. Evening darshan can be the most peaceful time of day, as the temple is lit with lamps and the summer heat has broken.

## Festival Day Timings

During major festivals, the schedule expands significantly. On Gajanan Maharaj Pragat Din Utsav (typically in February), the temple remains open continuously from 4:00 AM on the festival day until late night. Multiple additional aartis are performed, and the queue can take 3 to 5 hours on peak festival days. The Sansthan sets up separate queue lines for senior citizens and differently-abled devotees during these periods, and it is worth asking security staff at the entrance about the current queue arrangement.

Ashadhi Ekadashi, Kartik Ekadashi, and Guru Purnima are the other major calendar events that dramatically increase crowd sizes. On these days, arrive at the temple by 5:00 AM if you want Mangala Aarti darshan, or wait until after 11:00 AM when the initial rush has cleared. The Mahaprasad distribution hall operates extended hours during festivals, serving up to 50,000 devotees per day.

## Queue Management Strategies

The queue at Shegaon moves in a single serpentine line that winds through the temple compound. During normal days, the line moves at approximately one person every 10 to 15 seconds, which translates to about 30 to 50 metres of forward movement per minute. On festival days, the movement slows considerably, and devotees should expect to stand for extended periods.

Here is what experienced devotees recommend for managing the queue efficiently. Wear comfortable, open footwear that is easy to remove at the footwear counter. Avoid carrying large bags or handbags — the security check slows everyone down. Carry only your phone (in silent mode) and a small amount of cash for donations. If you are visiting during a festival, arrive with a small bottle of water and a light snack, but avoid eating in the queue area.

Senior citizens, pregnant women, and devotees with medical conditions can use the special queue lane on the right side of the main entrance. This lane is clearly marked and is monitored by Sansthan volunteers. If you need assistance, approach any volunteer in the bright orange uniform — they are trained to help devotees navigate the queue. During major festivals, the Sansthan also arranges for wheelchairs at the entrance; ask the security desk when you arrive.

## Darshan Experience Inside the Temple

The darshan itself at Shegaon is remarkably quick — you are in front of the Samadhi for perhaps 15 to 30 seconds. This is by design. Shri Gajanan Maharaj's Samadhi is in a small inner sanctum, and the queue moves continuously. The experience is not about lingering; it is about being present in the moment. Many devotees find that a single glimpse is enough to feel the connection, while others attend multiple aartis across their stay.

After darshan, you will exit through a separate door that leads to the prasad distribution counter. Here, you can collect a small packet of prasad (typically a dry laddu and a piece of coconut) for INR 10-20. The counter also accepts donations of any amount. If you wish to make a larger donation, approach the donation desk near the exit, where volunteers can provide receipts for contributions above INR 500.

## Best Times to Visit Shegaon

The most comfortable weather for a Shegaon visit is between October and February, when daytime temperatures range from 20 to 30 degrees Celsius. The pre-dawn Mangala Aarti during these months is genuinely cool, and a light shawl is recommended. The worst months for darshan are April and May, when temperatures can exceed 40 degrees and the queue becomes physically demanding. If you must visit during summer, the 8:00 AM or 6:00 PM slots are far more comfortable than the pre-dawn aarti.

The monsoon months of July and September bring heavy rain to the Buldhana region. The temple remains open during rain, but the queue area can become slippery. Carry an umbrella and wear footwear with good grip. The silver lining is that the temple and its surroundings look spectacular during the monsoon, with lush greenery and water-filled drains that add to the sacred atmosphere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the earliest darshan time at Shegaon?** The Mangala Aarti begins at 4:00 AM, and the temple opens for darshan around 3:45 AM. The queue typically starts forming from 2:30 AM on regular days and from midnight on festival days.

**How long does the darshan queue take during normal days?** On a regular weekday, expect 30 to 60 minutes at the 8:00 AM slot, 20 to 40 minutes at midday, and 20 to 30 minutes in the evening. Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM takes 45 to 75 minutes on regular days.

**Is there a separate queue for senior citizens?** Yes. There is a dedicated queue lane on the right side of the main entrance for senior citizens, pregnant women, and differently-abled devotees. Volunteers in orange uniforms are stationed at the entrance to guide devotees to the correct lane.

**Can I leave my luggage at the temple while doing darshan?** The Sansthan operates a cloakroom near the main entrance. Luggage storage is available for a nominal fee of INR 20-50 per item. Present your cloakroom ticket when collecting your bags. This is especially useful if you are arriving by train and want to go directly to darshan before checking in.

**Are phones allowed inside the temple?** No. Phones must be deposited at the designated counter before entering the main temple area. There are lockers available, or you can hand your phone to the security personnel who will tag and store it. Many devotees keep a small amount of cash in their pocket instead of the phone to make donations.

**What happens during Pragat Din Utsav?** During the annual Pragat Din celebration (typically in February), the temple operates extended hours with multiple aartis throughout the day and night. The queue can take 3 to 5 hours. Special arrangements are made for food, water, and medical support. The [Pragat Din guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide) covers the full schedule and what to expect.

# Shegaon Darshan Timing Guide | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

The queue at Shegaon does not move slowly by accident. It moves slowly because thousands of people are each taking thirty seconds in front of the Samadhi, and that thirty seconds is the only thing that matters to the person standing behind you. Understanding the rhythm of the day — when the queue is shortest, when the aartis are most powerful, and when the midday heat makes standing outside a health risk rather than a devotional test — is the difference between a darshan that energises you and one that exhausts you. This guide covers every darshan slot at Shegaon, the actual queue times you will encounter, and the specific strategies that regular devotees use to get the most from each visit.

## The Four Daily Darshan Windows

The Samadhi Mandir opens its gates four times a day, and each window has a different personality. The pre-dawn Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM is the most spiritually intense, the 8:00 AM slot is the most practical for families, the midday window at 12:00 PM is the most relaxed, and the evening darshan from 6:00 PM is the most beautiful visually. Knowing which one to pick depends on who you are travelling with, how well you have slept, and whether you have the patience to stand in a queue at 3:30 in the morning.

The Mangala Aarti is the one most devotees plan their entire trip around, and for good reason. The temple opens at around 3:45 AM, and the queue starts forming from 2:30 AM on regular days. On festival days it starts forming from midnight. The aarti itself begins at 4:00 AM and concludes around 5:00 AM. The spiritual atmosphere during those forty-five minutes is unlike anything else in the Sansthan network — the pre-dawn cool, the sound of the aarti conch, the shared silence of hundreds of devotees waiting for their turn. After the aarti, the temple closes for a brief rest period before reopening at 8:00 AM. The queue time for Mangala Aarti on a regular weekday is 45 to 75 minutes; on a festival day it stretches to 90 to 120 minutes or more.

The 8:00 AM morning darshan is the sweet spot for most visitors. Queue times on regular weekdays are 30 to 60 minutes, which is long enough to get into a devotional headspace but short enough that children and elderly devotees can manage it. On Sundays and Ekadashi days, add 15 to 30 minutes. This is the slot recommended for first-time visitors, families, and anyone who does not want to set an alarm for 2:30 AM. The temple is open until midday, so you have flexibility if the queue feels longer than expected.

Midday darshan from 12:00 PM to 2:00 PM is the quietest window. The crowd is mostly devotees who arrived by morning bus or train and settled into their accommodation first. Queue times are typically 20 to 40 minutes, and the atmosphere is more relaxed. The downside is the heat — in summer, the walk back from the temple at 2:00 PM is hot, and the early-afternoon rest becomes a necessity rather than a luxury.

Evening darshan begins at 6:00 PM and continues until 9:00 PM. The Shej Aarti at 9:00 PM is the final aarti of the day and the one that most long-term devotees prefer. The queue for the Shej Aarti is shorter than the Mangala Aarti on most days, the temple is lit with hundreds of oil lamps, and the summer heat has broken. Arrive by 8:30 PM if you want to be in the queue for the 9:00 PM aarti. The 6:00 PM aarti is a good compromise if you want an earlier dinner and an earlier night.

## Festival Day Schedules

During Gajanan Maharaj Pragat Din Utsav (typically in February), the schedule expands dramatically. The temple opens at 4:00 AM on the festival morning and remains open continuously until late that night. Multiple additional aartis are performed throughout the day, each with its own queue. The peak queue at midday can take three to five hours on the main Pragat Din day, and the Sansthan sets up separate dedicated lanes for senior citizens and differently-abled devotees during these periods. Ask the volunteers in bright orange uniforms at the entrance — they will direct you to the correct lane. The [Pragat Din guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide) covers the full multi-day festival schedule and what to expect on each day.

Guru Purnima in July or August follows a similar extended-hours pattern, though the crowds are somewhat smaller than Pragat Din. Ashadhi Ekadashi and Kartik Ekadashi also increase crowd sizes, but these festivals are more prominent at Pandharpur than at Shegaon. The Mahaprasad distribution hall at Shegaon operates extended hours during festivals and serves up to 50,000 devotees per day — the logistics of that operation are genuinely impressive, and many devotees find that waiting in the meal queue after darshan is a spiritual experience in itself.

## Queue Behaviour and Efficiency

The Shegaon queue moves in a single serpentine line that winds through the temple compound and then through the inner sanctum approach. On a regular weekday, the line moves at roughly one person every ten to fifteen seconds, which translates to about 30 to 50 metres of forward movement per minute. On festival days it slows considerably. The people who manage the queue best are the ones who have thought about what they carry.

Wear slip-on footwear that is easy to remove. The footwear counter is at the entrance to the queue, and fumbling with laces while seventy people wait behind you is stressful. Avoid carrying large handbags or backpacks — the security check at the queue entrance slows the entire line. The most efficient devotee arrives with a phone in their pocket (in silent mode), a small amount of cash for donations, and nothing else. If you are carrying medication for a chronic condition, put it in an easily accessible pocket so the security check does not become a twenty-minute excavation.

Senior citizens, pregnant women, and devotees with mobility challenges should use the dedicated right-side queue lane at the main entrance. This lane is clearly marked and staffed by volunteers in orange uniforms who are trained to help. During major festivals, the Sansthan also provides wheelchairs at the entrance — ask the security desk if you need one. If you arrive with an infant or small child and are worried about the queue, the right-side lane is also available to you. Do not hesitate to use it; it exists for exactly this situation.

## Inside the Samadhi Mandir

The darshan itself is remarkably brief. You are in front of the Samadhi for perhaps fifteen to thirty seconds, and then the ushers guide you forward. This is by design. The Samadhi Mandir is small, and the continuous flow of devotees means that there is no opportunity to linger inside the inner sanctum. What the brief moment offers, though, is something that hours of standing cannot replicate: an unplanned, unguarded, unmediated encounter. Many devotees find that a single glimpse is enough to carry them for months. Others attend multiple aartis across a multi-day stay. Both approaches are valid.

After darshan, you exit through a door that opens directly into the prasad counter area. Here, a small packet of prasad — typically a dry laddu and a piece of coconut — is available for INR 10-20. The counter also accepts donations of any amount. If you wish to make a larger donation, the donation desk near the exit provides receipts for contributions above INR 500. Many devotees who receive an answered prayer or a moment of peace make a larger offering at this desk.

## The Accommodation Connection

The quality of your darshan day is directly linked to where you slept the night before. A room at [Bhakta Niwas Shegaon](/locations/shegaon-bhakt-niwas) puts you five minutes from the temple entrance, which means you can walk to the 4:00 AM Mangala Aarti without arranging transport in the dark. The route from the campus to the temple is well-lit and safe, and you will find yourself walking with other devotees heading in the same direction. If you are staying at a private lodge or a mid-range hotel 2 km away, you will need an auto-rickshaw for the 4:00 AM slot, and the availability of autos at that hour is not guaranteed.

## Choosing the Right Season

Shegaon is most comfortable between October and February. Daytime temperatures in Buldhana district during these months range from 20 to 30 degrees Celsius, the pre-dawn aarti is genuinely cool (a light shawl is recommended), and the queue experience is manageable for most people. April and May are the hardest months: temperatures can exceed 40 degrees, and standing in a queue at 3:30 AM for the Mangala Aarti is a genuine physical test. If you must visit during summer, choose the 8:00 AM or 6:00 PM slot and keep water and salt tablets with you.

The monsoon months of July through September bring heavy rain to Vidarbha. The temple stays open, and the queue area has covered sections, but the ground can be slippery. Carry an umbrella and wear shoes with good grip. The compensations are real: the temple grounds turn vivid green, the Anandanand garden is at its most beautiful, and the crowds are thinner. Many long-term devotees consider the monsoon months the most spiritually rewarding time to visit simply because the crowds are smaller and the temple feels more personal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the earliest time the temple opens?** The Mangala Aarti begins at 4:00 AM, and the queue opens around 3:45 AM. The queue line itself begins forming from 2:30 AM on regular days and from midnight on festival days.

**How long is the queue during regular days?** Mangala Aarti: 45 to 75 minutes. 8:00 AM darshan: 30 to 60 minutes. Midday: 20 to 40 minutes. Evening: 20 to 30 minutes. Shej Aarti at 9:00 PM: 15 to 25 minutes. Add 30 to 60 minutes on Sundays and Ekadashi days.

**Is there a separate queue for differently-abled devotees?** Yes. A dedicated lane on the right side of the entrance is for senior citizens, pregnant women, and devotees with mobility challenges. Volunteers in orange uniforms are stationed at the entrance to guide you to it.

**Can I leave my luggage at the temple while I do darshan?** The Sansthan operates a cloakroom near the main entrance. Luggage storage costs INR 20-50 per item. Present the receipt when you collect your bags. This is especially useful if you are arriving by train and want to go straight to darshan before checking into Bhakta Niwas.

**Are phones and cameras allowed inside the temple?** Phones must be deposited at the designated counter before entering the inner queue area. Lockers are available, or you can hand your device to a security staff member who will tag and store it. All cameras — including phone cameras — are prohibited inside the Samadhi Mandir complex.

**What should I expect during Pragat Din Utsav?** During Pragat Din (typically in February), the temple operates extended hours with multiple aartis. The peak queue can take 3 to 5 hours. Separate queue lanes for senior citizens are operational, the Mahaprasad hall serves free meals, and medical facilities are on high alert.

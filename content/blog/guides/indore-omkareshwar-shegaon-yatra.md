# Indore Omkareshwar Shegaon Yatra | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

For devotees flying into India through Indore — the commercial and cultural capital of Madhya Pradesh, home to Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport with flights from Mumbai, Delhi, Ahmedabad, and most major Indian cities — the most logical first stop is Omkareshwar. The Jyotirlinga temple on the Mandhata island in the Narmada is just 80 kilometres from the airport, making it an easy and spiritually resonant entry point into the Sansthan's temple circuit. From Omkareshwar, the natural next step is Shegaon — the Samadhi Mandir of Shri Gajanan Maharaj, approximately 260 kilometres away. This three-stop yatra (Indore airport arrival, Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga, Shegaon Samadhi Mandir) is the most practical entry route for devotees from northern and central India, and it works equally well for NRI devotees flying into Indore from international destinations. It also works for devotees from Maharashtra who want to start from the Indore side rather than the Mumbai side.

This guide provides a complete itinerary for the Indore-Omkareshwar-Shegaon yatra, with transport options, accommodation recommendations, darshan scheduling, and practical tips for each leg. If you have more time available, the itinerary can be extended to include Trimbakeshwar, creating a four-location circuit that covers the Narmada Jyotirlinga, the Godavari Jyotirlinga, and the Samadhi Mandir in a single journey. The [planning a one-week Maharashtra yatra guide](/blog/planning-a-one-week-maharashtra-yatra) covers the extended version.

## The route overview

**Indore to Omkareshwar:** 80 km, 2 hours by taxi or bus

**Omkareshwar to Shegaon:** 260 km, 5 to 6 hours by car (via Khandwa and Burhanpur)

**Total distance:** Approximately 340 km

**Best duration:** 4 to 5 days (1 day at Indore/Omkareshwar, 2 to 3 days at Shegaon)

The route flows naturally from west to east: Indore (entry point) to Omkareshwar (Narmada Jyotirlinga) to Shegaon (Samadhi Mandir). The reverse direction (Shegaon to Omkareshwar to Indore) works equally well for devotees departing from Indore airport. The road conditions are good throughout, and both legs can be completed comfortably in a single day of driving each.

## Day 1: Arrival at Indore, proceed to Omkareshwar

**Arrival at Indore:** Land at Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport (IDR). The airport is well-connected to Mumbai, Delhi, Ahmedabad, and other major cities, with both domestic and limited international services. Collect your luggage, clear customs if arriving internationally, and proceed to the pre-booked taxi or bus for Omkareshwar. If you have booked a taxi in advance through an Indore operator, the driver will be waiting at the airport arrival gate with a name board. If you are taking a bus, the MP Tourism bus stand is about 15 kilometres from the airport, and you will need a local taxi to reach it.

**Travel to Omkareshwar (80 km, 2 hours):** The most practical option is a pre-booked taxi from Indore to Omkareshwar, which costs approximately INR 1,500 to 2,000 for an AC car. The journey takes 2 hours via SH 27 and passes through pleasant countryside. MP Tourism runs AC and non-AC buses from Indore to Omkareshwar throughout the day, with a journey time of 2 to 2.5 hours. Bus tickets are available on the MP Tourism website and through RedBus. The bus drops you near the Narmada bridge on the mainland side, from where you will need to walk or take a local auto to the island temple. The taxi is the more convenient option, especially if you have luggage.

**Accommodation at Omkareshwar:** Check in at the Sansthan's Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas or a hotel near the Narmada bridge on the mainland side. The Bhakta Niwas is on the Mandhata island near the Jyotirlinga temple — the most convenient location for darshan, as you can walk to the temple in the morning without crossing the bridge. Book through +91 80531 90691 or the [booking form](/booking). The room types at Omkareshwar include 3-bed Non-AC and 2-bed Non-AC, with tariffs around INR 1,200 to 1,800 per night. The [accommodation comparison guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-across-locations-comparison) shows room types and tariffs across all locations.

**Evening darshan (4:00 PM to 8:00 PM):** Attend the afternoon darshan at the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga temple. The 4:00 PM slot is the best time for first-time visitors — short queue, relaxed atmosphere, and the temple is at its most accessible. Attend the evening Narmada aarti on the ghats at sunset. The Narmada aarti is one of the most beautiful spiritual experiences in the Sansthan circuit — the river, the temple, the aarti flames, and the devotional singing create an atmosphere that stays with you long after you leave. After the aarti, return to the Bhakta Niwas for dinner at the Mahaprasad canteen. The canteen serves simple Satvik food — rice, dal, vegetables, and roti — which is wholesome and filling after a day of travel.

**Rest:** Get an early night. The next day starts with a pre-dawn darshan, and you will want to be rested for the early morning temple visit.

## Day 2: Omkareshwar — Full day at the Jyotirlinga

**Morning darshan (5:30 AM):** Wake up early for the pre-dawn darshan at the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga temple. The morning atmosphere is distinctly peaceful — the temple is quiet, the Narmada ghats are calm, and the first light on the river and the island is beautiful. The darshan at 5:30 AM is the most intimate experience of the day — there are very few devotees, and the priests have time for a longer, more personal darshan. The [darshan timing guide](/blog/omkareshwar-darshan-timing-guide) covers the complete daily schedule at Omkareshwar.

**Narmada ritual (6:30 AM):** Attend the morning Narmada aarti and take a ritual dip in the Narmada if you wish. The morning water is clean and the temperature is comfortable, especially between October and February. Carry a separate set of dry clothes to change into after the dip — the temple and Bhakta Niwas do not have changing rooms, so plan accordingly. Many devotees bring a small towel and change into dry clothes in the auto or taxi before entering the temple.

**Mandhata island exploration (8:00 AM to 10:00 AM):** Walk around the Mandhata island after the morning darshan. The island is compact — roughly 2.5 kilometres around the perimeter — and can be explored comfortably on foot in under an hour. Visit the Siddheshwar temple on the western end, the smaller shrines along the riverbank, and the ashrams that dot the island. The island's narrow lanes, ancient banyan trees, and riverside setting create a distinctly contemplative atmosphere that is quite different from the busier temple towns.

**Breakfast (10:00 AM):** Breakfast at the Mahaprasad canteen. The canteen opens early and serves hot puri, poha, and chai.

**Late morning (10:30 AM to 12:00 PM):** Return to the Jyotirlinga temple for a second darshan, or visit the Mamaleshwar temple on the mainland side. The Mamaleshwar temple is paired with Omkareshwar in the Jyotirlinga tradition — both are considered forms of Shiva and both are visited as part of the complete Omkareshwar pilgrimage experience. The bridge connecting the island to the mainland is a short walk, and the Mamaleshwar temple has its own aarti schedule.

**Lunch (12:30 PM):** Lunch at the Mahaprasad canteen.

**Afternoon rest (1:00 PM to 4:00 PM):** Rest at the Bhakta Niwas. Use this time for reading, journaling, or simply resting. The afternoon heat makes outdoor activity uncomfortable from December to March, and the Bhakta Niwas rooms are comfortable enough for a short nap.

**Evening darshan and aarti (5:00 PM to 8:00 PM):** Attend the evening darshan and aarti. The evening Narmada aarti at sunset is the highlight of the day — the sky turns orange and purple over the river, the aarti flames reflect on the water, and the devotional singing rises from the ghats. This is the experience that most devotees remember most vividly from their Omkareshwar visit.

**Overnight:** Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas.

## Day 3: Omkareshwar to Shegaon

**Morning darshan (5:30 AM):** Final darshan at Omkareshwar. The pre-dawn aarti is a fitting spiritual send-off before the journey to Shegaon. If you are departing after breakfast, this is your last opportunity for darshan at the Jyotirlinga.

**Breakfast and check-out (8:00 AM):** Breakfast at the Mahaprasad canteen and check out of the Bhakta Niwas. Settle any outstanding payments at the reception. The checkout process is typically quick — have your booking reference ready.

**Departure (8:30 AM):** Start the drive to Shegaon (260 km, 5 to 6 hours). The route follows the same corridor as the Shegaon-Omkareshwar leg in reverse: Omkareshwar to Khandwa (30 km), Khandwa to Burhanpur (140 km), Burhanpur to Khamgaon (110 km), Khamgaon to Shegaon (35 km). The road conditions are good throughout — the Khandwa-Burhanpur stretch is on a national highway, and the Burhanpur-Khamgaon stretch is on a state highway with decent surface. The Khamgaon-Shegaon stretch is familiar Vidarbha territory and is well-maintained.

**Mid-journey stops:**
- **Khandwa (9:30 AM):** Fuel stop and rest break. Khandwa has several petrol stations near the highway.
- **Burhanpur (12:00 PM):** Lunch stop. Burhanpur has several restaurants near the bus stand, including some that serve good Maharashtrian food. If you prefer a dhaba experience, the highway dhabas between Burhanpur and Khamgaon are clean and serve hot meals.
- **Khamgaon (2:30 PM):** Tea break and rest stop. Khamgaon is a familiar stop for anyone travelling to Shegaon — the town has several tea stalls and restaurants near the highway.
- **Shegaon arrival (3:30 PM):** Arrive at Shegaon. The approach to Shegaon is marked by the increasing presence of devotees and the sound of temple bells in the distance.

**Arrival at Shegaon (3:30 PM):** Check in at the Shegaon Bhakta Niwas. The reception will ask for your booking reference and ID. Freshen up and head to the Samadhi Mandir for the evening darshan. The transition from the Narmada aarti at Omkareshwar to the Samadhi Mandir aarti at Shegaon is a powerful spiritual experience — the two temples have very different atmospheres, and the contrast between the island Narmada setting and the Vidarbha plains is striking.

**Evening darshan and aarti (5:00 PM to 8:00 PM):** Attend the afternoon darshan at 5:00 PM and the evening Mangala Aarti at 7:00 PM. The 7:00 PM aarti at Shegaon is the largest gathering of the day and is deeply moving for devotees who have just arrived from another sacred site. The Mahaprasad canteen serves dinner until around 8:30 PM.

## Day 4: Shegaon — The Samadhi Mandir

**Morning darshan (5:00 AM):** Wake up for the 5:00 AM Mangala Aarti at the Samadhi Mandir. The pre-dawn darshan at Shegaon is the most spiritually resonant time of the pilgrimage — the temple is quiet, the atmosphere is devotional, and the first light on the Samadhi Mandir dome is memorable. The 5:00 AM aarti is shorter than the evening aarti, but it has a depth and intimacy that makes it the highlight of many devotees' Shegaon visits. The [darshan timing guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-timing-guide) covers the complete daily schedule.

**Anand Sagar (8:00 AM to 10:00 AM):** Visit the Anand Sagar complex — the musical fountain, the gardens, the Anandanand museum, and the temple exhibition. Anand Sagar is most peaceful in the morning, and the museum provides valuable context about Shri Gajanan Maharaj's life and teachings for devotees who are visiting for the first time. If you are travelling with family, the gardens and open spaces provide a change of pace from the temple darshans. Children enjoy the gardens and the fountain when it is operational.

**Breakfast (10:00 AM):** Breakfast at the Mahaprasad canteen.

**Mid-morning (10:30 AM to 12:00 PM):** Return to the temple for a second darshan, or spend time at Anand Sagar. Many devotees use this time for quiet reflection, journaling, or interacting with other devotees at the Bhakta Niwas. The Bhakta Niwas common areas are a good place to meet other pilgrims and share experiences.

**Lunch (12:30 PM):** Lunch at the Mahaprasad canteen.

**Departure (1:30 PM):** Check out of the Bhakta Niwas and proceed to your onward destination. For devotees departing from Indore, the drive back to Indore takes approximately 5 to 6 hours via the same route in reverse. For devotees departing from Mumbai, the 12139 Sewagram Express departs Shegaon at 8:55 PM, giving you the afternoon for a final visit to the temple or Anand Sagar before the train departure.

## Alternative: Extending to Trimbakeshwar

If you have 6 to 7 days available, you can extend the itinerary to include Trimbakeshwar after Shegaon. The Shegaon to Trimbakeshwar leg is approximately 400 km and 7 to 8 hours by car, following the route via Aurangabad and Nashik. This creates a three-location circuit (Indore-Omkareshwar-Shegaon-Trimbakeshwar) that covers the Narmada Jyotirlinga, the Godavari Jyotirlinga, and the Samadhi Mandir. The [planning a one-week Maharashtra yatra guide](/blog/planning-a-one-week-maharashtra-yatra) covers the full four-location circuit including Pandharpur.

## Accommodation booking

**Omkareshwar (1 or 2 nights):** Book through +91 80531 90691 or the [booking form](/booking). Room types include 3-bed Non-AC (INR 1,200/night) and 2-bed Non-AC (INR 1,500/night).

**Shegaon (1 to 3 nights):** Book through +91 80531 90691 or the [booking form](/booking). Room types range from common hall (INR 300/night) to 6-bed AC (INR 4,000/night).

Book both locations at least 4 to 6 weeks in advance, especially if travelling during festival periods. The [festival rush booking strategies guide](/blog/festival-rush-booking-strategies) covers festival-period booking in detail.

## Transport logistics

**Indore to Omkareshwar:** Taxi (INR 1,500–2,000) or MP Tourism bus (INR 200–400). Pre-book the taxi through Indore-based operators. Uber and Ola do not reliably cover the Omkareshwar route.

**Omkareshwar to Shegaon:** Taxi (INR 3,000–4,500 one way). Book a round-trip taxi if returning to Indore, or ask the driver to wait at Shegaon for your return journey. The [complete transport guide](/blog/complete-transport-guide-for-devotees) covers taxi booking options and alternative routes.

**Shegaon to Indore (return):** Taxi (INR 3,000–4,500 one way). Same route in reverse, typically via Burhanpur and Khandwa.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is Indore airport the best entry point for this yatra?**

A: For devotees from northern and central India, yes. Indore Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport has flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Ahmedabad, and most major Indian cities, and it is only 80 km from Omkareshwar. For devotees from Mumbai, the Mumbai to Shegaon train route is more practical as a starting point. The [Mumbai to Shegaon weekend guide](/blog/mumbai-to-shegaon-weekend-guide) covers the Mumbai-side entry route.

**Q: Can NRI devotees use this itinerary?**

A: Yes. Indore airport has international connectivity from several Gulf countries. Carry your passport and visa at all times for Bhakta Niwas check-in. The [NRI booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-for-nris) covers documentation requirements.

**Q: Is the Omkareshwar to Shegaon drive comfortable?**

A: Yes, the 260-kilometre drive takes 5 to 6 hours on good roads via Khandwa and Burhanpur. Several dhabas along the highway provide meal and rest stops.

**Q: Should I extend this yatra to include Trimbakeshwar?**

A: If you have 6 to 7 days, extending to Trimbakeshwar is highly recommended. The Shegaon to Trimbakeshwar leg is approximately 400 km and 7 to 8 hours by car. The [planning a one-week Maharashtra yatra guide](/blog/planning-a-one-week-maharashtra-yatra) covers the full circuit.

**Q: What is the best time of year for this yatra?**

A: October to February is ideal — cool weather for both darshan and travel. The monsoon season (July to September) can disrupt travel near Burhanpur.

**Q: Can I visit the Ajanta caves on the way?**

A: Yes. The Ajanta caves are approximately 150 km from Burhanpur and can be visited as a detour. The [Shegaon and Ajanta Ellora combined trip guide](/blog/shegaon-and-ajanta-ellora-combined-trip) covers the full itinerary.

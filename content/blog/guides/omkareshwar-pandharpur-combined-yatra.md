# Omkareshwar Pandharpur Combined Yatra: Two Sacred Destinations in One Journey

Combining Omkareshwar and Pandharpur in a single trip is one of the most spiritually rewarding yatra options available to devotees in central and western India. Omkareshwar, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas perched on the Mandhata island in the Narmada River, offers the quiet gravity of a Jyotirlinga darshan steeped in ancient tradition. Pandharpur, the home of Lord Vitthal, delivers the devotional intensity of Maharashtra's varakari tradition with its centuries-old Wari processions and the living practice of daily kirtan. Together, they are about 480 kilometres apart by road, and with proper planning, you can experience both in a comfortable six to eight-day itinerary.

This guide covers the practical realities of combining these two locations: transport routes, accommodation at both Sansthan-run Bhakta Niwas, darshan logistics, and tips for families and solo devotees. If you want to extend the trip to include Shegaon as well, see the [Shegaon Pandharpur Omkareshwar Circuit](/blog/shegaon-pandharpur-omkareshwar-circuit) guide.

## Starting at Omkareshwar

Omkareshwar is best accessed through Indore, which has the closest airport and a major railway station. Direct trains run from Mumbai, Pune, and Delhi to Indore. From Indore, you have two transport options to the temple: a private taxi (INR 1,200 to INR 1,800 one way) or a state transport bus (INR 100 to INR 150 per person). Buses leave Indore's Sarwate Bus Stand every 30 minutes from 6 AM to 9 PM. Taxis offer door-to-door service, which is especially helpful if you have elderly family members or heavy luggage. The [Complete Transport Guide for Devotees](/blog/complete-transport-guide-for-devotees) has more details on reaching Indore from major cities.

The temple at Omkareshwar sits on Mandhata island, which is connected by bridges from both banks. The darshan includes a parikrama — a circumambulation of the island — which is best done in the early morning or late afternoon to avoid the midday heat. The [Omkareshwar Darshan Timing Guide](/blog/omkareshwar-darshan-timings) provides detailed information on the best darshan slots and the parikrama route. The parikrama covers approximately 8 km and takes 2.5 to 3 hours at a comfortable pace. Many devotees do half the island on one day and the rest on another, resting at the Bhakta Niwas in between.

The Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas at Omkareshwar is within walking distance of the main temple ghat. Rooms are available at nominal rates, and the booking is handled through the central +91 80531 90691 number. The [Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas Accommodation Guide](/blog/locations/omkareshwar/omkareshwar-bhakta-niwas-accommodation-guide) covers the facilities and room types in detail.

## Travel from Omkareshwar to Pandharpur

The 480-kilometre road journey from Omkareshwar to Pandharpur takes 8 to 10 hours by car. The fastest route passes through Sendhwa, Dhule, Nashik, and Ahmednagar. If you are driving yourself, plan for an overnight stop at Nashik or Ahmednagar to break the journey comfortably. The route passes through the Western Ghats and offers opportunities to visit Trimbakeshwar near Nashik if you are extending the yatra. Break the journey with stops at clean highway dhabas — the stretch from Dhule to Nashik has several good dhabas with clean restrooms and decent food. A rest stop at Nashik breaks the 480 km journey into two comfortable 4-hour drives, which is especially important for elderly travellers.

By train, the fastest route is from Khandwa (60 km from Omkareshwar) to Kurduvadi or Solapur, then onward to Pandharpur Road station. Trains on this route are infrequent, so book well in advance through IRCTC. The Khandwa to Solapur route has two or three trains per day, and the journey takes approximately 6 to 8 hours. State transport buses are not a practical option for the entire 480 km leg — the journey is too long and tiring, and there is no direct bus service.

A popular intermediate stop is **Trimbakeshwar**, near Nashik. You can spend one night at Trimbakeshwar, visit the Jyotirlinga there, and then drive the remaining 290 kilometres to Pandharpur the next day. This brings three Jyotirlingas within reach in a single trip. The [Omkareshwar to Trimbakeshwar Pilgrimage Checklist](/blog/omkareshwar-to-trimbakeshwar-pilgrimage-checklist) has specific preparation tips for this leg.

## Darshan at Pandharpur

The temple of Lord Vitthal and Rukmini at Pandharpur is one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in Maharashtra. The Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas in Pandharpur is located near the temple and offers convenient accommodation. The [Pandharpur Wari Accommodation Tips](/blog/pandharpur-wari-accommodation-tips) provides essential advice if you are planning to travel during the Ashadhi or Kartik Wari seasons.

Darshan at Pandharpur is busiest in the early morning (5 AM to 8 AM) and during the evening aarti (6 PM to 8 PM). The Pandurang sakhas — devotional music groups — are active throughout the day, and you will encounter continuous kirtan singing as you walk through the temple area. The temple has a separate darshan queue for women, which moves faster than the general queue. The darshan experience at Pandharpur is more interactive than at many temples — the priests often engage devotees in conversation, and the atmosphere is more informal and warm. Many devotees report that the Pandharpur darshan feels more personal and less regimented than at other major temples.

The Pandharpur temple area includes several smaller shrines and ghats that are worth exploring. The Bhima River ghat, where devotees take holy dips, is particularly beautiful during the early morning hours. The Pundalik shrine near the main temple marks the spot where the legendary devotee Pundalik is said to have stood for Lord Vitthal. Visiting this shrine before entering the main temple is considered auspicious by many devotees.

## Accommodation for the Circuit

Book rooms at both locations through the central WhatsApp number +91 80531 90691. Mention "Omkareshwar + Pandharpur" in a single message, and the coordinator will handle both bookings together. This approach ensures that your travel dates align properly and that you receive a single confirmation message covering both locations. ## Sample 7-Day Itinerary

**Day 1:** Arrive in Indore. Taxi to Omkareshwar (3.5 hours). Check into Bhakta Niwas. Evening ghat darshan.
**Day 2:** Morning Omkareshwar parikrama and darshan. Afternoon rest at Bhakta Niwas. Evening aarti. The [Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas Booking Process](/blog/omkareshwar-bhakta-niwas-booking-process) covers reservations for this stop.
**Day 3:** Morning darshan at Omkareshwar. Travel to Trimbakeshwar via Nashik (350 km, 7 hours). Overnight at Trimbakeshwar Bhakta Niwas.
**Day 4:** Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga darshan. Afternoon travel to Pandharpur (290 km, 6 hours).
**Day 5:** Morning Pandharpur darshan. Afternoon rest. Evening aarti.
**Day 6:** Full day at Pandharpur — visit the Bhima ghat, explore the temple area, attend kirtans.
**Day 7:** Morning darshan. Return journey to home city via Solapur or Pune.

## Extended 10-Day Itinerary Including Shegaon

If you have the time, adding Shegaon to the circuit creates a comprehensive three-location pilgrimage:

**Days 1-3:** Omkareshwar (parikrama, darshan, rest)

**Days 6-7:** Pandharpur (Vitthal darshan, Wari experience)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How far is Pandharpur from Omkareshwar by road?**
Approximately 480 kilometres. The journey takes 8 to 10 hours by car.

**Can I do the Omkareshwar to Pandharpur leg by bus?**
State transport buses are not practical for the full 480 km leg. A private taxi or a train from Khandwa is more comfortable.

**Should I include Trimbakeshwar in my itinerary?**
Yes, if you have the time. Trimbakeshwar sits between Omkareshwar and Pandharpur and adds a third Jyotirlinga to your trip. The [Indore Omkareshwar Shegaon Yatra](/blog/indore-omkareshwar-shegaon-yatra) includes Trimbakeshwar in its itinerary.

**How many days do I need for both Omkareshwar and Pandharpur?**
Six to eight days for a comfortable pace. Five days is possible if you skip Trimbakeshwar and travel directly. Adding Shegaon brings the total to 10 to 14 days.

**Is accommodation available at both locations?**
Yes. Book both through the central Sansthan WhatsApp number. The [Bhakta Niwas Complete Booking Guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) explains how to coordinate multi-location bookings.

**What is the best time of year to combine both temples?**
October to February offers comfortable weather. The winter mornings can be cold but the days are ideal for darshan and parikrama. Avoid June to August if possible — the Narmada is high during the monsoon and Pandharpur can be humid.

**How much does the transport cost for the full circuit?**
Between INR 8,000 and INR 12,000 for a sedan taxi covering all legs. Shared taxis or trains reduce the cost significantly. The exact cost depends on the type of vehicle and whether you book directly or through a travel agent.

**Can I travel this route by public transport only?**
Public transport is possible but requires significant planning. Buses from Indore to Omkareshwar run frequently, but the onward connection to Pandharpur is challenging. A private taxi or a combination of train and taxi is the most practical option for this circuit.

**What about booking accommodation in advance?**
Book all rooms in advance, especially during festival periods. The [WhatsApp Booking Step By Step Guide](/blog/whatsapp-booking-step-by-step-guide) explains the optimal booking process for this circuit.

**Are there ATMs at the Bhakta Niwas locations?**
ATMs are available in towns near each Bhakta Niwas but not at the premises themselves. Withdraw enough cash in your home city before travelling — INR 5,000 to INR 7,000 should cover a week-long trip including food and small purchases. The [Packing Light For Weekend Darshan](/blog/packing-light-for-weekend-darshan) guide has specific cash recommendations.

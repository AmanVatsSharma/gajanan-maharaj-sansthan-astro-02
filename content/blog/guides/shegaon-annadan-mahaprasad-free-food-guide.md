# Shegaon Annadan & Mahaprasad: Free Food Guide

Free food at Shegaon operates at two levels. The first is the daily annadan (community meal) served by the Sansthan's kitchen to every devotee who visits — no questions asked, no donations required. The second is the laddu mahaprasad distributed after darshan at the Samadhi Mandir. Both are organized by the Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan and are available to everyone, regardless of caste, religion, or income. This guide covers the timings, the food, the etiquette, and what to expect.

## The tradition of annadan at Shegaon

Free food for devotees has been a continuous practice at Shegaon since the lifetime of Shri Gajanan Maharaj. Accounts from the early twentieth century describe the saint distributing simple meals to whoever came to the temple. After his samadhi, the Sansthan institutionalized the practice: a large kitchen (the Prasadalay) was established, and the annadan program expanded to serve hundreds daily, then thousands.

The practice is consistent with the saint's own emphasis on feeding the hungry as the simplest and most direct form of service. The Anand Sagar complex itself was conceived, in part, as a place where devotees could eat, rest, and contemplate without paying — the water, the gardens, and the meals are all free.

## Daily annadan: timings and food

The Sansthan kitchen serves free meals twice daily, at lunch and dinner. The timings are:

- **Lunch:** Typically served from 11:30 am to 2 pm
- **Dinner:** Typically served from 6:30 pm to 8:30 pm

During festival periods, the timings extend — additional service windows are added to accommodate the larger crowds.

The meal is a simple, wholesome vegetarian thali. The typical plate includes:

- Chapati or bhakri
- Rice
- Dal (lentil curry)
- One vegetable preparation (seasonal)
- Salad or pickle
- Sweet (laddu or a simple halwa on special days)

The food is prepared in the Sansthan kitchen on a large scale, with quality control that is supervised by the trust. There are no eggs or onion-garlic in the kitchen, consistent with the sattvic diet the Sansthan follows.

## Who can avail annadan

Everyone. There is no eligibility test, no ID requirement, no donation prerequisite. Devotees of every community and background eat at the same counters. The queues are well-organized and typically move quickly.

For visitors with dietary restrictions: the food is vegetarian and prepared without onion and garlic. However, if you have a specific allergy (nuts, dairy, gluten), ask at the serving counter before eating — the kitchen staff can guide you on the day's ingredients.

## Mahaprasad: the laddu after darshan

Every devotee who completes darshan at the Samadhi Mandir receives a small laddu prasad. The laddu is the signature offering of the temple — round, about the size of a small marble, made from gram flour, ghee, and sugar.

The prasad distribution happens continuously throughout the day at the exit of the mandir. There is no special timing. After darshan, the devotee exits through a door where the prasad is handed out. Take the prasad with respect — it is considered a sacrament.

Many families preserve the prasad at home. A common practice is to keep the laddu in a clean container and consume a small piece before beginning any important task — a meeting, an exam, a journey. The practice dates back to the saint's lifetime and continues today.

## Annadan during festivals

During Pragat Din (February 12), Samadhi Utsav (Rishi Panchami, August–September), and other major festivals, the Sansthan arranges for large-scale community meals. The kitchen operates around the clock, and the number of devotees served can reach 50,000 or more during a peak day of the Samadhi Utsav. These meals are organized in large pandals near the Anand Sagar complex.

During the Samadhi Utsav specifically, the Sansthan also arranges for bhandara (community feast) every evening. This is a special, larger meal with multiple items and a wider variety than the daily thali. It is open to all and is one of the highlights of the festival.

## Donating to the annadan

The annadan is free for devotees, but it costs money to run — the Sansthan spends several lakh rupees on the kitchen every year. Devotees who want to contribute can donate to the annadan fund at the Sansthan office. Donors are recognized with a certificate and a listing in the Sansthan's annual report. There is no minimum donation amount.

Corporate groups, trusts, and individuals often sponsor specific meals — a day's lunch or dinner for all devotees — on special occasions such as birthdays or in memory of departed family members. Enquire at the Sansthan office for the sponsorship program.

## Practical tips

**Best time to eat:** If you want to avoid the largest crowds, aim for the early service window (11:30 am for lunch, 6:30 pm for dinner). As the main lunch and dinner hours approach, the queues can become long during festival periods.

**Seating:** The seating area is an open hall with long tables and benches. There is no reserved seating. During festivals, overflow seating is set up in the adjacent pandals.

**Water:** Drinking water is available at the serving counters. Carry your own water bottle if you prefer.

**Bringing food to guests:** If you are hosting guests who are visiting Shegaon, you can collect a few laddus after darshan to take back to them. The Sansthan is happy to let devotees take additional laddus for family members who could not visit.

## Other free services at Shegaon

Beyond food, the Sansthan provides several free services:

- **Cloakroom:** Free luggage storage near the mandir.
- **Shuttle bus:** Free shuttle between the railway station and the temple complex.
- **Drinking water:** Water kiosks throughout the Anand Sagar campus.
- **Medical aid:** A small first-aid facility is available near the mandir.
- **Children's area:** A free play area for children within Anand Sagar.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Do I need to register to eat at the annadan hall?**
A: No registration is needed. Just join the queue, receive your plate, and eat. During very large crowds (Samadhi Utsav peak days), the queues can be long — plan accordingly.

**Q: Is the food served in a specific area for tourists or is it the same hall for everyone?**
A: It is a single hall for everyone — devotees, tourists, locals, and visitors all eat together. This is consistent with the Sansthan's philosophy of universal service.

**Q: Can I take prasad home?**
A: Yes. The laddu prasad after darshan is yours to keep or distribute. You can also purchase packaged laddus at the shop near the exit if you want to take some for family at home.

**Q: What if I have a Jain or specific dietary restriction?**
A: The annadan kitchen is vegetarian but not Jain-specific (no root vegetables may still contain some items not permitted in strict Jain practice). If you have strict requirements, it is advisable to carry your own food or eat at a Jain-friendly restaurant in Khamgaon.

**Q: Is the annadan available every day of the year?**
A: Yes, the daily annadan operates every day without exception, including during festival periods (when additional meals are added).

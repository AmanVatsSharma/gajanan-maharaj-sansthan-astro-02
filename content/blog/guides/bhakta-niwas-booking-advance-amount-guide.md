# Bhakta Niwas Booking Advance Amount Guide

The advance on a Bhakta Niwas booking is a portion of your room's sanctioned tariff, stated by the office when it confirms your request, paid through the office's guided channels against a receipt, and subtracted from what you owe — so that the balance settled at the desk is simply the remainder, never a second charge. The exact amount due for a given stay is set by the office at confirmation; what a devotee can and should master in advance is the arithmetic the advance sits inside, the rules that govern it, and the habits that keep it safe. This guide gives you all three, with worked tariff examples at every centre, so the money conversation at booking arrives already understood.

We place this guide deliberately between its two siblings: the [payment methods guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-payment-methods-guide), which covers the channels and receipts, and the [confirmation process guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-confirmation-process), which covers the document that states the advance. The advance deserves its own page because it is the point where families most often lose the thread of who owes what, when, and why.

## The rules, stated plainly

Four rules govern every advance across the Sansthan's four centres. They are short, and every confusion we have ever been sent resolves into one of them.

1. **The office states the advance.** It arrives in the confirmation message, tied to your dates, party and room — never announced first by a caller, a link or an intermediary.
2. **The advance is part of the tariff, not beside it.** Whatever the portion, the balance at the desk is the remainder of the same published row. Two nights in a 4 Bed AC at ₹2,550 is ₹5,100 total; whether the advance is larger or smaller, that total does not move.
3. **Every advance meets a receipt.** The receipt is the advance's proof of existence; without it, the payment is a story. Photograph it the day it is issued.
4. **Adjustments run through the office.** A change of dates, room type or party size re-draws the arithmetic, and the office issues the corrected figures in the same thread — devotees never recompute the split privately and pay against their own arithmetic.

From these rules follows the deepest one, unwritten but universal: the advance is trust, extended in both directions. The family trusts the office with money before travel; the office holds a room for a party it has not yet met. Everything in this guide exists to keep that mutual trust tidy.

## The tariff arithmetic the advance lives inside

An advance can only be understood against the total it serves. The sanctioned rows, network-wide:

| Room type | Capacity | Tariff per night | Two-night total | The natural party |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bed AC | 2 | ₹1,650 | ₹3,300 | A couple; a parent and one child |
| 3 Bed Non-AC | 3 | ₹1,750 | ₹3,500 | A small family in the cool season |
| 3 Bed AC | 3 | ₹2,050 | ₹4,100 | A small family in the hot months |
| 4 Bed Non-AC | 4 | ₹2,250 | ₹4,500 | The standard family room |
| 4 Bed AC | 4 | ₹2,550 | ₹5,100 | A larger family, April to June |

Now the moves families actually make, and what each costs per night. Upgrading within the same bed count from non-AC to AC adds exactly ₹300 — the entire distance from 3 Bed Non-AC to 3 Bed AC, and likewise from 4 Bed Non-AC to 4 Bed AC. Adding a bed without air-conditioning — 3 Bed Non-AC to 4 Bed Non-AC — adds ₹500 per night. And the family that grows from a 2 Bed AC into a 4 Bed AC crosses ₹900 per night, the compound of two more beds and the air-conditioning. These three differences are the whole upgrade map; every "what would it cost instead" question at the desk is one of them.

The advance sits inside whichever row you hold. When the office states an advance and later the family upgrades the room type, the office re-draws both halves against the new row in writing — rule four above — and the receipt trail continues unbroken. ## Settling the advance: the safe sequence

1. **Receive the confirmation** stating the advance and its channel. No message before this one has the authority to name a figure.
2. **Check the anatomy** — party, centre, dates, room type — before paying anything, because the advance binds a file; bind the right one.
3. **Pay only through the stated channel**, and never through a link that arrived by forward or a "desk" that called you first. The genuine channels are anchored on the [booking page](/booking) and the [contact page](/contact).
4. **Collect the receipt** — physical or digital as issued — and photograph it beside the confirmation.
5. **Note the payment in your thread:** "advance paid as guided on this date; receipt held." The thread now carries the whole money story in one place.
6. **At the centre, settle the balance against the receipt** and watch the desk's arithmetic agree with the row you have known since planning.

Six steps, perhaps ten minutes of actual work, and the family arrives at the busiest desk in the network with nothing left to argue about. Devotees who skip step five often regret it — not because anything is wrong, but because the thread that cannot show its money history always ends up re-explaining itself at the worst hour.

## When the trip unwinds: advances and cancellations

Plans fail — illness, dates, railway timetables — and the advance's rules face their test. The handling follows the Sansthan's policy through the office, with the receipts as the working documents; the full picture, including what changes on festival dates, is in the [refund and cancellation policy guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-refund-and-cancellation-policy). Two disciplines from this desk make that process painless: cancel through the same thread that booked, as early as the plan fails, and keep the advance receipt until the matter is fully closed — it is the first document the process will ask for, and often the only one it needs.

The honest framing for families: an advance on a Bhakta Niwas room has never been a wager on the family's misfortune. It is a place held in trust, returned to the system promptly when the family cannot come — which is exactly how the waitlisted family behind you receives the room that would otherwise have stood empty.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the advance for a Bhakta Niwas room?

The office states the advance for your specific stay in the confirmation message — it is a portion of your room's sanctioned tariff, and the balance at the desk is the remainder of the same row. There is no separate schedule to memorise; there is arithmetic to understand, and this guide's tables supply it.

### Does the advance change the total I pay for the room?

No. The advance and the balance are two halves of one published tariff — for example, two nights in a 3 Bed AC at ₹2,050 total ₹4,100, whatever the split between the halves. Any demand that seems to add a figure on top of the row is either an error to query in the thread or a fraud to report.

### When should I pay the advance — before or after the confirmation message?

After. The confirmation is the document that states the figure, the channel and the deadline; a demand for payment before any confirmation exists is the classic impostor pattern, however urgent the caller sounds. Verify against the [contact page](/contact) whenever the sequence feels wrong.

### What happens to the advance if I upgrade the room type later?

The office re-draws both halves against the new tariff row and states the corrected figures in writing in your thread. The upgrade differences are fixed arithmetic — ₹300 per night for AC at the same bed count, ₹500 for 3 Bed Non-AC to 4 Bed Non-AC, ₹900 for 2 Bed AC to 4 Bed AC — so the revision is a re-split, not a renegotiation.

### Is the advance handled differently during festival bookings?

The rule is the same; the weight is different. Festival windows are contested, and a file with its advance settled stands first when rooms are allocated — families planning wari, utsav or Mahashivratri travel should settle promptly once the office states the figure.

### Can the advance be paid by one family member for the whole group?

Yes, when the thread-holder named at booking is that person or has arranged it with the office — keep the receipt in the one booking folder the group travels behind, and let the desk's arithmetic at arrival reconcile the rest. One folder, one thread, one voice: the group mantra that never changes.

## The small sum that holds the room

An advance is the smallest movement of money in a pilgrimage and the one that buys the most certainty with it.

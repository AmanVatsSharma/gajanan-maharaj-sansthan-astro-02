# Narayan Nagbali & Kaal Sarp Dosh Pooja at Trimbakeshwar

Trimbakeshwar is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and, for devotees dealing with pitru dosha (ancestral affliction) or kaal sarp dosh, it is also one of the most important ritual sites in India. The town, located about 30 km from Nashik in Maharashtra, hosts thousands of pujas every year, and two specific pujas — Narayan Nagbali and Kaal Sarp Dosh — account for a large share of those rituals. This guide explains what these pujas are, who should consider them, what the experience is like, and how to plan your visit.

## Why Trimbakeshwar for these pujas

The Narayan Nagbali puja has a specific scriptural sanction at Trimbakeshwar that is not found at other temples. The town sits at the confluence of the Godavari River (which has its origin at Trimbakeshwar) and three hills — Brahmagiri, Nilagiri, and Katharkar — making it a sacred geography that amplifies the effect of pitru (ancestor) rituals. The Kaal Sarp Dosh puja is similarly timed for specific astrological conditions that are believed to be neutralized more effectively at a Jyotirlinga site.

For devotees who have been told by an astrologer that they have kaal sarp dosh or that a Narayan Nagbali puja is required, the practical question is typically: "Should I do this at Trimbakeshwar or can it be done at home?" The tradition holds that the puja must be done at Trimbakeshwar specifically, not at any temple, because of the town's unique combination of river, Jyotirlinga, and mountain geography.

## What is Narayan Nagbali puja?

Narayan Nagbali is a pitru puja — a ritual performed for the benefit of departed ancestors. It is traditionally performed when:

- A family has experienced repeated unexplained misfortunes (deaths, financial losses, illness)
- An astrologer has identified pitru dosha in the family's horoscope
- There is a belief that an ancestor was not given proper funeral rites
- The family wants to resolve karmic debt owed to departed ancestors

The puja has two components. "Narayan" refers to the worship of Lord Vishnu in his form as the protector of all beings, while "Nagbali" refers to the ritual of offering to the nagas (serpent deities associated with ancestors and with Lord Shiva). Together, the two rituals address both the divine protection needed and the ancestral lineage that has been disrupted.

The puja is performed over a period that typically lasts three days. A qualified priest (pandit) guides the family through the rituals, which include homa (fire offering), pind daan (ritual offering to ancestors), and the reading of specific mantras.

## What is Kaal Sarp Dosh puja?

Kaal Sarp Dosh is an astrological condition identified when all seven planets in a birth chart fall between the two nodes of the moon — Rahu (north node) and Ketu (south node). The condition is considered inauspicious and is associated with delays, obstacles, and chronic misfortune. The puja at Trimbakeshwar is one of the few specific remedies prescribed in traditional astrology for this dosha.

The puja involves:

- Abhishek of the Jyotirlinga with specific offerings
- Chanting of Rahu and Ketu mantras
- Homa in a dedicated fire pit
- Pind daan and offering of milk and rice to the Jyotirlinga
- The offering of nag (serpent) effigies made of clay to the Godavari

The puja is typically done on specific dates recommended by the astrologer — an amavasya (new moon day), a Monday, or during the Shravan month are considered especially auspicious.

## Who should perform these pujas?

Both pujas are family-oriented. A married couple with children is the ideal format; the puja is not typically done by an individual. For Narayan Nagbali, the family brings the following:

- Photographs of departed ancestors
- Their names and gotra (clan lineage)
- A copy of the family tree if available

The priest uses these to direct the pind daan — the offering that is believed to reach the specific departed soul. For Kaal Sarp Dosh, the puja is done by or on behalf of the individual identified in the horoscope, with family members present.

## What to expect at Trimbakeshwar

### Arrival and priest

Most families arrive in Nashik or Trimbakeshwar a day before the puja begins. The Sansthan office at Trimbakeshwar and the many private operators in Nashik can arrange for a qualified priest. The cost of the puja varies by priest, by the family's specific requirements, and by the scale of the homa. Expect to pay anywhere from ₹5,000 to ₹20,000 depending on the package; confirm the cost before committing.

### The three-day schedule

**Day 1:** Arrival, purification at the Godavari (a bath in the river is part of the ritual), initial consultation with the priest, and a preliminary abhishek at the Jyotirlinga.

**Day 2:** The main puja day. Early morning homa, pind daan, the primary abhishek, and offerings to the nagas. The day is long — from 4 or 5 am until the evening aarti. The priest guides each step; family members participate by offering rice, flowers, and other items at specific moments.

**Day 3:** The concluding rites. Any remaining offerings are completed, the priest offers final blessings, and the family distributes prasad. Many families do a final darshan at the Jyotirlinga before departing.

### Accommodation

Trimbakeshwar has basic accommodation options, including rooms at the government-run facility near the temple and guesthouses in Nashik (30 km away). The [Trimbakeshwar accommodation guide](/trimbakeshwar-bhakta-niwas-accommodation-guide) covers these options in detail. Book at least two to three weeks in advance during festival seasons.

## Practical tips

**Best time to visit:** The months between October and February are the most comfortable for a three-day puja at Trimbakeshwar. The summer (March to June) is very hot, and the rainy season (July to September) makes travel and outdoor ritual difficult.

**What to bring:** White cotton clothing is traditional for the puja. Bring photos of departed ancestors, the names and dates, and any specific items the priest asks you to bring (typically ghee, flowers, rice, and turmeric). There are shops near the temple that sell these items if you are traveling from a distance.

**After the puja:** The puja is considered complete when the priest says it is. There is no further requirement — no follow-up puja, no annual repetition (unless the family chooses to repeat it for personal reasons). Many families do a second puja (Narayan Nagbali) at a later date if there are multiple generations involved.

## Trimbakeshwar as a broader pilgrimage

Beyond the specific pujas, Trimbakeshwar is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and a major site in the Nashik-Trimbakeshwar circuit. Visitors who come for a puja often extend their stay to include darshan at the Jyotirlinga, a visit to the Godavari source at Gangadwar, and a stop at the Anjaneri hills. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q: How do I know if I have Kaal Sarp Dosh?**
A: Consult a qualified astrologer with a full birth chart. The condition is identified when all seven planets fall between Rahu and Ketu. Your astrologer will also tell you if the dosha is partial or complete and whether a puja at Trimbakeshwar is the recommended remedy.

**Q: Can Narayan Nagbali and Kaal Sarp Dosh puja be done together?**
A: They are separate pujas with different purposes. Some families do both during the same visit to Trimbakeshwar, but they are performed as two distinct rituals with two distinct priestly preparations. Discuss with the priest about scheduling both in one visit.

**Q: Is there a cheaper way to perform these pujas?**
A: The cost depends on the scale of homa and the number of ritual elements. Some families perform the basic versions at Trimbakeshwar at a lower cost and complete the larger homa on a subsequent visit. The key is to work with a recognized priest from the Sansthan or a trusted private operator.

**Q: Can women perform Narayan Nagbali puja?**
A: Yes. There is no gender restriction on the puja. Married women perform the puja alongside their husbands, and widows or unmarried women who are performing the puja on behalf of the family also participate fully.

**Q: What is the difference between Narayan Nagbali and Pitru Paksha rituals?**
A: Pitru Paksha (the annual fortnight of ancestor rites, typically in September) is a fixed annual observance. Narayan Nagbali is a one-time puja done when there is a specific karmic need to resolve. Families do both — the annual Pitru Paksha for regular ancestor veneration and Narayan Nagbali as a targeted remedy for a specific dosha.

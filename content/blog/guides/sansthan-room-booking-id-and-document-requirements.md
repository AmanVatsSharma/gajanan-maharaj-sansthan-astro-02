# ID and Document Requirements for Sansthan Room Booking

Every adult who sleeps in a Sansthan room stands behind an original photo ID at the desk — Aadhaar most commonly, and equally a PAN card, a driving licence or a passport — presented with the booking reference at check-in, while children travel lighter: a school ID or an Aadhaar copy where one exists smooths the register, and the desk guides families case by case. That single discipline, plus a headcount that matches the beds, is the whole of the paperwork. There is no form queue, no notary, no photograph to paste; the request goes in through the [booking page](/booking), the office confirms with a reference, and the register at the desk closes the loop.

This page lists the documents that serve, the headcount rule that travels with them, the edge cases from infants to NRIs, and the small habits that keep paperwork painless across a multi-town yatra.

## The one rule, and the four cards that satisfy it

The register at every Bhakta Niwas desk — [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas](/locations/shegaon-bhakt-niwas), the [Pandharpur Math](/locations/pandharpur-math), [Omkareshwar](/locations/omkareshwar), [Trimbakeshwar](/locations/trimbakeshwar) — asks the same thing: an original, government-issued photo identity document for every adult, shown in person. The four cards devotees actually carry:

| Document | Best for | The practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Aadhaar card | Almost everyone, every centre | The default answer; the number most desks handle fastest |
| Passport | NRIs, international devotees, air arrivals | The universal document; carry it, not just a copy |
| Driving licence | Motorists who travel light | A valid photo ID, gladly accepted |
| PAN card | Business travellers who carry it anyway | Serves where it is what the bag holds |

What matters is that the document be original and photo-bearing; photocopies support a file but do not replace the card at the counter. The desk is not auditing identities so much as keeping the register honest — the same honesty that lets capacity equal the bed count and lets a ₹1,650 to ₹2,550 tariff stay flat for everyone. Devotees wanting the fuller, pan-yatra version of this list — tickets, prescriptions, concession cards — will find it in the [devotee document checklist for all locations](/blog/devotee-document-checklist-for-all-locations) and the dedicated [ID proof and document checklist](/blog/id-proof-and-document-checklist).

Choosing among the four cards is a small decision with a small best answer. Prefer the card you can afford to carry without anxiety: for most devotees that is Aadhaar, which is replaceable at speed if the worst happens on the road. Carry the passport only when the journey is already carrying it — an air arrival, an NRI's homecoming — because a passport lost between two temple towns is a month of a pilgrim's peace. The driving licence serves the driving family, already in the wallet; the PAN card serves the traveller whose purse holds it anyway. And whichever card leads, one spare card per adult in a different bag is the veteran's habit — the cheapest insurance in this whole guide, costing nothing but five minutes at the packing stage.

## Headcount discipline: book exactly who travels

The document rule's twin is the headcount rule, and the two are checked together. The register lists the party; the party must fit the beds; the beds were fixed at booking — so the headcount written on the request is the headcount the desk expects to see, IDs in hand.

- **Count children as heads.** Capacity equals the bed count, and a child sleeps in a bed; the family of five books the 4-bed plus the 2-bed, not a hopeful 4-bed.
- **Add the late joiner before travel, not at the desk.** A cousin joining the yatra is a modification — one message through the [contact page](/contact) with the reference — while a surprise ninth at the counter is a second-room negotiation on the busiest evening of your trip.
- **Match names to IDs reasonably.** The desk reads names against cards with sense, not pedantry; but a request booked under a nickname the ID never mentions earns a longer look than it needs.
- **Declare infants sharing parents' beds.** The register's honesty is its value; an infant noted at booking is welcome without a bed, and noted is the operative word.

Families working out how many rooms a party truly needs will find the two-room arithmetic ready-made in the [family room booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-family-room-booking-guide), and the terms behind the register in the [booking rules and terms guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-rules-and-terms). Headcount miscounts are, fittingly, the first entries in both the [Shegaon common booking mistakes guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-common-booking-mistakes) and its [Pandharpur counterpart](/blog/pandharpur-bhakta-niwas-common-booking-mistakes), which families routinely read after a correction they would rather have avoided.

The headcount rule deserves its defence as well as its statement, because it can read as rigidity to a family that has always travelled loosely. It is, in truth, the register's entire honesty: when the desk knows exactly who sleeps where, it can hold the right number of rooms for the parties behind you, account for every traveller in an emergency, and keep the tariffs flat for everyone by keeping the arithmetic exact. A devotee who books truthfully for four and arrives as four has subsidised nothing and hidden nothing; the system that thanks them is the same system that will hold a room for their daughter's family twenty years hence. Institutions outlive their guests, and the register is how an institution keeps its promises across generations.

## The edge cases, answered plainly

**Infants and small children** carry no obligation beyond mention: a school ID or an Aadhaar copy, where one exists, quickens the register and nothing more. **Elders** present their own ID like any adult — the ground-floor and comfort requests that matter to them belong in the booking message, not in a document, as the [senior citizen booking guide](/blog/sansthan-room-booking-for-senior-citizens) explains. **NRIs and international devotees** travel on passports, with the visa side of life handled by immigration rather than by the desk; our [Bhakta Niwas booking for NRIs](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-for-nris) guide covers the longer arc — foreign numbers, arrival planning, the works. **A party of solo adults** — colleagues, friends, padayatris — presents one card per head, exactly as families do, and the dormitory travellers among them follow the same register discipline, as the [dormitory booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-dormitory-booking-guide) records.

The lost-ID anxiety deserves its own sentence: a driving licence left on a dining table has been solved, in our experience, by any other card on the list, presented patiently. The desk's humanity is the system's quiet feature.

## Keeping documents whole across a yatra

A multi-town pilgrimage multiplies paperwork, and a short sequence of habits keeps it painless from the first desk to the last.

1. **Photograph everything before departure.** A phone photograph of every card, every confirmation reference and every prescription, taken the evening before travel, has rescued more yatras than any single item in any bag.
2. **Carry one card per adult on the person.** The pilgrim's wallet travels in a pocket, not in the suitcase riding the luggage van — the wider packing discipline is set out in our [what to carry pilgrimage essentials](/blog/what-to-carry-pilgrimage-essentials) guide.
3. **Pack one spare card per adult in a different bag.** The veteran's habit from earlier in this page, applied across the party's luggage, so that no single lost bag empties the whole party's proof.
4. **Give each reference a home you can reach in seconds.** The booking confirmation from each centre — Shegaon, Pandharpur, Omkareshwar, Trimbakeshwar — saved where the family's booking member can produce it at once, because the reference is the thread that ties the desk's register to your request.
5. **Reconcile the wallet each morning of the yatra.** Two minutes at breakfast — cards counted, references in place, photographs backed up — keeps the whole sequence honest across every town on the circuit.

First-time devotees assembling the whole picture — documents, darshan planning, what the first visit feels like — will find the [first visit checklist](/blog/first-time-visit-checklist) written for exactly them.

## Documents and the check-in moment

Check-in is where the paperwork pays its dividend. An adult party with four cards and one reference stands at the desk for a minute; the register is signed, the 24-hour cycle from entry begins its clock, and the keys follow. The same moment without the cards is a negotiation, a phone call to a family member, a photograph sent over WhatsApp and squinted at — all of it avoidable. The check-in sequence, and the day-of rhythm around it, is described for the busiest centre in the [Shegaon check-in and check-out timings guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-check-in-check-out-timings), and the confirmation pathway that precedes it in the [Bhakta Niwas booking confirmation process](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-confirmation-process).

It is worth picturing that minute from the desk's side once, because it explains why the system asks so little and gives so smoothly. The desk holds a chart that says a 4-bed room goes to a named party of four arriving by the evening train; four travellers arrive, four cards meet four register lines, the reference matches, and the day's plan and the day's reality close like a well-made box. Multiply that minute by every party of the day and the whole institution runs on it. The document you carry is not a formality the desk endures; it is the one contribution only you can make to your own smooth arrival — everything else, the office has already done.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Aadhaar compulsory for booking a Sansthan room?

No — Aadhaar is merely the most common; any original photo ID serves, and the standard four are Aadhaar, passport, driving licence and PAN, presented by each adult at the desk.

### Do children need ID proof as well?

Not in the adult sense: a school ID or an Aadhaar copy where available smooths the register, but children are counted as heads for capacity rather than carded as adults, and the desk guides families case by case.

### What documents do NRIs need for Bhakta Niwas?

A passport, carried rather than copied, is the anchor document, with the booking reference as usual; the fuller NRI picture — arrival windows, foreign contact numbers — is drawn in the [NRI booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-for-nris).

### Can I check in if I have lost my ID on the journey?

Present any other card from the standard four, and speak to the desk plainly — the register's humanity is real, though carrying a second card or a phone photograph of each document before departure is the habit that makes the question rare.

### Does the headcount on the booking have to match exactly?

It does — the party must fit the beds, and the register reconciles the headcount against the request; changes travel as modifications through the office before travel day, where they are absorbed, rather than at the counter, where they are negotiated.

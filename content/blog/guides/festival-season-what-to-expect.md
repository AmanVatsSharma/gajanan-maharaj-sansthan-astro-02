The festival season at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan is the temple's most dynamic and spiritually intense period. From the spring celebration of Akshaya Tritiya through the winter observance of Datta Jayanti, the Sansthan's annual programme calendar spans eleven major festivals and dozens of monthly utsav that provide devotees with a continuous devotional rhythm throughout the year. Understanding this calendar — not simply the dates and timings but the character of each festival, the crowd level it draws, and the preparation it requires — is the foundation of effective pilgrimage planning. It allows you to choose the festival that best matches your devotional interests, your available time, and your tolerance for crowd and intensity.

This guide covers the complete festival calendar at the Sansthan, describing each major festival, its spiritual significance, the crowd it draws, the accommodation booking window it requires, and the darshan strategy that makes your visit meaningful. The guide is organised around the annual calendar sequence, starting with the spring festivals and moving through the year to the winter programmes, so that you can use it as a planning reference at any point in the year.

## Spring festivals: Akshaya Tritiya and Pragat Din

Akshaya Tritiya, typically falling in April or May, is the first major festival of the year at the Sansthan. The [akshaya-tritiya-special-darshan](/blog/events/akshaya-tritiya-special-darshan) guide covers the complete programme and planning advice. The festival draws 15,000 to 25,000 devotees, and the special morning puja is the spiritual high point. Accommodation booking two to three weeks before the festival is typically sufficient.

Pragat Din, typically in May, is the Sansthan's most significant annual programme. The [gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-pragat-din-utsav-guide) covers the complete festival programme and provides detailed planning advice. Pragat Din draws 50,000 to 80,000 devotees over three days, and the programme commemorates the day when Shri Gajanan Maharaj appeared in physical form at Shegaon. Accommodation booking four to six weeks before the festival is essential.

## Summer transition: Guru Purnima

Guru Purnima, typically in July or August, is the monsoon festival that honours the Guru tradition. The [guru-purnima-devotee-planning](/blog/guru-purnima-devotee-planning) guide covers the complete programme. Guru Purnima draws 10,000 to 15,000 devotees, and the spiritual discourse programme is one of the most intellectually engaging events in the Sansthan's calendar. The monsoon weather adds a contemplative atmosphere to the festival, and the reduced crowd allows for an unhurried devotional experience.

## Monsoon programmes: Narsimha Jayanti and Janmashtami

Narsimha Jayanti, typically in August, is a smaller monsoon programme that honours Lord Narsimha. The festival draws 5,000 to 8,000 devotees, and the programme is intimate and contemplative.

Janmashtami, also in August, celebrates the birth of Lord Krishna. The [krishna-janmashtami-celebration](/blog/events/krishna-janmashtami-celebration) and [janmashtami-sansthan-visit](/blog/janmashtami-sansthan-visit) guides cover the complete programme. Janmashtami draws 10,000 to 15,000 devotees, and the midnight ceremony is the spiritual high point. The [ashadhi-ekadashi-pandharpur-wari-guide](/blog/events/ashadhi-ekadashi-pandharpur-wari-guide) provides information about combining a Janmashtami visit to Shegaon with the Pandharpur Wari.

## Autumn festivals: Dussehra and Diwali

Dussehra, typically in September or October, commemorates Lord Rama's victory over Ravana. The [dussehra-celebrations-at-sansthan](/blog/dussehra-celebrations-at-sansthan) guide covers the complete programme. Dussehra draws 20,000 to 35,000 devotees over the festival period, and the Ravana effigy ceremony is the centrepiece.

Diwali, typically in October or November, is the festival of lights and one of the most spiritually powerful programmes at the Sansthan. The [diwali-darshan-and-accommodation](/blog/events/diwali-darshan-and-accommodation) and [diwali-darshan-planning-tips](/blog/events/diwali-darshan-planning-tips) guides cover the complete programme and accommodation planning. Diwali draws 40,000 to 60,000 devotees over the festival period, and the evening diya illumination ceremony is unforgettable. Booking four weeks in advance is recommended.

## Winter programmes: Datta Jayanti and Punyatithi

Datta Jayanti, typically in December, celebrates the birth of Lord Dattatreya. The [datta-jayanti-visit-planning](/blog/events/datta-jayanti-visit-planning) guide covers the complete programme. Datta Jayanti draws 12,000 to 20,000 devotees, and the December weather makes the visit comfortable and pleasant.

Punyatithi, typically in December or January, commemorates Maharaj's own punyatithi — the day of his passing into samadhi. The [punyatithi-utsav-darshan-planning](/blog/punyatithi-utsav-darshan-planning) guide covers the complete programme. Punyatithi draws 20,000 to 35,000 devotees, and it is one of the most emotionally significant programmes in the Sansthan's calendar.

## Planning across the festival year

The most effective approach to festival planning is to identify two to three anchor festivals that you want to attend each year, and to build your pilgrimage calendar around those anchor points. Pragat Din in May and Diwali in October or November are the two most significant anchor points, and they are separated by approximately five months — a gap that allows for planning, saving, and spiritual preparation. Many devotees plan their annual leave and budget around these two festivals, attending both as the foundation of their devotional calendar.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which festival is the most significant at the Sansthan?**
Pragat Din in May is the most significant festival, as it commemorates the day when Shri Gajanan Maharaj appeared at Shegaon. Diwali in October or November is the second most significant, combining a major festival programme with favourable weather and a large devotional crowd.

**How far in advance should I book accommodation for major festivals?**
Four to six weeks before Pragat Din, three to four weeks before Diwali and Punyatithi, and two to three weeks for Guru Purnima, Janmashtami, and other moderate festivals. The [bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) covers the booking process and timeline.

**Which festival is best for a first-time visitor?**
Diwali or Guru Purnima are the best first major festival experiences. Both provide a spiritually engaging programme with a moderate crowd that is manageable for first-time visitors. Pragat Din is the most powerful festival but involves a larger crowd and more advance planning.

**Can I attend multiple festivals in one year?**
Yes, many devotees attend two to three major festivals per year. The Pragat Din and Diwali combination is the most popular, as the two festivals are separated by five months and together provide a comprehensive devotional calendar. Our [contact page](/contact) can provide planning advice for multi-festival annual calendars.

**What is the crowd level at each major festival?**
Pragat Din: 50,000 to 80,000 over three days. Diwali: 40,000 to 60,000 over three to five days. Punyatithi: 20,000 to 35,000. Dussehra: 20,000 to 35,000. Guru Purnima: 10,000 to 15,000. Janmashtami: 10,000 to 15,000. Datta Jayanti: 12,000 to 20,000. The [major-utsav-crowd-planning-checklist](/blog/major-utsav-crowd-planning-checklist) provides detailed crowd estimates and planning advice for each festival.

**Does the Sansthan publish the festival calendar in advance?**
Yes, the Sansthan publishes the annual programme calendar several months in advance on its official website and social media channels. Check the official channels regularly for festival dates, programme timings, and booking announcements.

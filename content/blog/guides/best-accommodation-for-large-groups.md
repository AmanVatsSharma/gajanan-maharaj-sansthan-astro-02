# Best Accommodation For Large Groups | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

Accommodating a large group of devotees — whether it is a family gathering of fifteen, a temple trust group of forty, or a community outing of sixty — requires a different approach to accommodation planning than a standard individual or family booking. The question is not simply "how many rooms do we need?" but also "which combination of room types works best for our group's composition?", "should we use the common hall or private rooms?", and "what is the most practical way to split the group across different accommodation types?" The Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas properties offer a range of options that can be mixed and matched to suit different group sizes and compositions, and understanding these options — combined with the local private accommodation options near each temple — is the key to a comfortable and well-organised group yatra. This guide covers the accommodation options available for large groups at each Sansthan location, the room allocation formulas that work for different group sizes, and the specific considerations that apply when booking for diverse groups with mixed ages and requirements.

## Understanding the group accommodation challenge

The fundamental challenge of group accommodation is that groups are rarely homogeneous. A group of twenty people typically includes: young adults who are comfortable with shared dormitory-style accommodation, middle-aged couples who prefer private rooms, senior citizens who need ground-floor access and quieter rooms, and children who need to be close to their parents. A single room type will not suit all these requirements, and the accommodation plan needs to account for this diversity.

The Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas properties provide the building blocks for a flexible accommodation plan: private rooms in multiple sizes, dormitory-style common hall accommodation, and the option to supplement with nearby private hotels when the Bhakta Niwas cannot accommodate the full group. The art of group accommodation planning is combining these building blocks into a configuration that serves every member of the group comfortably.

## Room type guide for groups

### Private rooms

The Sansthan offers private rooms in several configurations at Shegaon and Omkareshwar. These are the most comfortable option for individual families, senior citizens, and group leaders.

**2-bed rooms:** Suitable for couples, small families, or senior citizens who prefer a quiet, private space. These rooms are limited in number, and they are often the first to be allocated during festival periods.

**3-bed rooms:** The workhorse of group accommodation. These rooms comfortably accommodate small families, groups of three friends, or two couples. For a group of fifteen, a mix of 3-bed rooms and 4-bed rooms provides a good balance.

**4-bed rooms:** The most efficient option for larger groups on a budget. A 4-bed room can accommodate four single travellers or two families (two adults and one child per family). For a group of twenty, five 4-bed rooms would be the core of the accommodation plan.

**Deluxe and Luxury Suites:** Suitable for group leaders, dignitaries, or senior members of the group who prefer a higher standard of accommodation. These rooms are limited in number and are not always available during festival periods.

**Family Rooms (6-person, AC):** The largest private room option at Shegaon. These rooms are designed for extended families and can accommodate up to 6 guests. They are ideal for a single large family within a bigger group.

### Common hall dormitory

The common hall at Shegaon Bhakta Niwas is the most economical accommodation option for large groups. It accommodates up to approximately 50 guests in a dormitory-style arrangement with shared bathroom facilities. The common hall is best suited for: young devotees and students, groups who prioritise budget over comfort, and community groups where the shared living experience is part of the devotional practice.

The common hall has 24/7 security and access to all meals at the Mahaprasad canteen. The bunk beds are basic but clean, and the shared bathroom facilities are maintained by the Bhakta Niwas housekeeping staff. Many devotees who use the common hall describe the experience as spiritually meaningful — living in close proximity to fellow pilgrims from different regions creates a sense of community that is harder to achieve in private rooms.

### Nearby private hotels

When the Bhakta Niwas cannot accommodate the full group, nearby private hotels serve as an essential supplement. At Shegaon, several private hotels near the Samadhi Mandir offer rooms from INR 800 to INR 2,500 per night. At Omkareshwar, hotels near the Narmada bridge on the mainland side offer rooms from INR 800 to INR 2,000. These hotels do not include meals in the tariff, but clean vegetarian restaurants are available near each location.

Splitting the group between Bhakta Niwas and a nearby hotel is a practical solution for large groups during peak periods. The key consideration is proximity — choose a hotel that is within walking distance or a short auto ride from the temple so that group members can attend darshan together. The [last-minute booking options guide](/blog/last-minute-booking-options-guide) covers backup accommodation options for each location.

## Room allocation formulas by group size

Here are the allocation formulas that work for common group sizes:

**Group of 10:** 2 rooms of 4-bed + 1 room of 2-bed = 10 guests. Alternatively, 8 guests in the common hall + 2 guests in a private 2-bed room for the group leader.

**Group of 15:** 3 rooms of 4-bed + 1 room of 2-bed + 1 room of 2-bed = 16 guests (one extra bed). Alternatively, 10 guests in the common hall + 5 guests in private rooms.

**Group of 20:** 4 rooms of 4-bed + 2 rooms of 3-bed = 22 guests (two extra beds). Alternatively, 15 guests in the common hall + 5 guests in 2-bed and 3-bed private rooms.

**Group of 30:** 15 guests in the common hall + 15 guests in 3 rooms of 4-bed + 2 rooms of 3-bed. For groups this size, splitting between two accommodation types (Bhakta Niwas + nearby hotel) is often more practical.

**Group of 50:** 40 guests in the common hall + 10 guests in 2-bed and 3-bed private rooms. See the [50-plus devotees guide](/blog/group-booking-for-50-plus-devotees) for detailed large-group logistics.

## Group-specific considerations

### Senior citizens

Senior citizens require ground-floor rooms, minimal walking distance, quieter rooms away from common areas, and access to bathrooms without long corridors. When booking for a group with senior citizens, request ground-floor rooms at the time of booking and specify the number of senior citizens in your group. The Bhakta Niwas staff are accustomed to these requests and will allocate appropriate rooms. At Shegaon, the 2-bed AC rooms on the ground floor are ideal for senior citizens.

### Families with young children

Families with young children need rooms that are close together (so parents can reach children's rooms quickly), rooms with bathroom access that does not require children to walk alone through long corridors, and proximity to the canteen for quick meal access. The 3-bed and 4-bed rooms are well-suited for families — they provide enough space for parents and children, and the attached bathroom means children do not need to use shared facilities. The [family yatra planning guide](/blog/pandharpur-and-shegaon-family-yatra-plan) covers family-specific considerations.

### Mixed-age groups

Mixed-age groups (senior citizens, adults, teenagers, young children) benefit from a mixed accommodation plan: private rooms for senior citizens and families with young children, and common hall or shared rooms for teenagers and young adults. This arrangement respects the different comfort and privacy needs of different age groups while keeping the overall accommodation plan manageable.

## Booking process for large groups

The booking process for large groups follows the same steps as individual bookings but requires more advance planning and a single point of contact:

1. **Appoint a coordinator.** The coordinator collects member details, sends the booking request, tracks confirmations, and manages changes.
2. **Compile a complete member list.** Include names, ages, and ID details for every guest.
3. **Determine the room type mix.** Use the allocation formulas above to calculate the number and type of rooms needed.
4. **Send a single, organised booking request.** Submit all details together via WhatsApp (+91 80531 90691) or the [booking form](/booking).
5. **Confirm in writing.** Wait for written confirmation from the Sansthan specifying room types, dates, and tariffs.
6. **Distribute details to the group.** Share the confirmed arrangement with all group members at least one week before departure.

The [group booking coordination guide](/blog/group-booking-coordination-guide) covers the full coordination workflow in detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What is the largest group the Bhakta Niwas can accommodate?**

A: Shegaon Bhakta Niwas can accommodate approximately 50 guests in the common hall plus additional guests in private rooms, depending on availability. For groups larger than 50, the common hall can be supplemented with nearby private hotels. Omkareshwar, Pandharpur, and Trimbakeshwar have smaller Bhakta Niwas properties and lower maximum capacities. The [group booking for 50-plus devotees guide](/blog/group-booking-for-50-plus-devotees) covers very large group logistics.

**Q: Can we split our group between Bhakta Niwas and a nearby hotel?**

A: Yes, and this is a common and practical arrangement for large groups during peak periods. Choose a hotel within walking distance or a short auto ride from the temple so that group members can attend darshan and meals together. The [accommodation comparison guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-across-locations-comparison) shows which nearby hotels are suitable for each location.

**Q: Is the common hall suitable for senior citizens?**

A: The common hall is generally not recommended for senior citizens or guests with mobility issues. The shared bathroom facilities, the bunk bed arrangement, and the communal atmosphere are more suited to younger devotees. For groups that include senior citizens, allocate private rooms for them and use the common hall for younger group members.

**Q: Do group members have to eat at the Mahaprasad canteen?**

A: No. While meals are included in the Bhakta Niwas tariff, group members are free to eat at nearby restaurants if they prefer. This is particularly useful for groups staying in nearby private hotels where meals are not included. The canteen serves Satvik vegetarian food, which is suitable for most dietary requirements.

**Q: Can we get a group discount on the tariff?**

A: The Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas tariffs are already subsidised, and group discounts are not typically offered. However, for very large groups (50-plus) making extended stays, discuss your requirements with the booking desk — they may be able to offer a consolidated rate or special terms. Always ask.

**Q: How far in advance should a large group book?**

A: For groups of 10 or more, book at least 6 to 8 weeks in advance during regular seasons and 10 to 12 weeks in advance during festival periods. For groups of 50 or more, extend these timelines by 2 to 4 weeks. The [festival rush booking strategies guide](/blog/festival-rush-booking-strategies) covers festival-period group booking in detail.

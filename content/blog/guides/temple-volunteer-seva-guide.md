# Temple Volunteer Seva Guide: Serve at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

Volunteering at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan is one of the most fulfilling ways to deepen your connection with the temple and its community. The Sansthan relies on devotees who contribute their time and energy to keep the campus running — from kitchen seva that feeds thousands of devotees daily, to queue management during peak hours, to cleaning and maintenance of the temple complex. The volunteer culture at Shegaon is deeply ingrained, and new devotees who spend even a single day in seva often describe it as the highlight of their visit, ranking it above even the darshan itself.

This guide covers everything you need to know about volunteering at the Sansthan: what types of seva are available, how to register, what to expect on a typical day, and practical tips for first-time volunteers. Whether you are planning to volunteer for one day or during a festival period, the information below will help you prepare thoroughly and make the most of the seva experience.

## Why Seva Matters

In the tradition of the Sant tradition of Maharashtra, seva (selfless service) is considered a form of worship equal to or even greater than ritual prayer. Shri Gajanan Maharaj himself lived a simple, ascetic life and was known for his deep compassion for all beings. Volunteering at the Sansthan is a way to embody that spirit in a practical, tangible way. Every meal served, every queue guided, every floor swept — these acts of service are offerings in themselves, and they create a direct connection between the volunteer and the larger community of devotees who benefit from the seva.

Many regular devotees at Shegaon plan their visits around seva shifts. They arrive early, serve breakfast, join the darshan queue during quieter hours, and leave with a sense of purpose that stays with them long after they return home. The Sansthan recognises this spirit and actively encourages devotees to participate in seva. The seva desk near the main entrance is the starting point for all volunteer registration.

## Types of Seva Available

The Sansthan categorises seva into several broad areas, each with specific tasks and timings. When you register, the coordinators will ask about your physical ability and availability, and assign you to the role that best fits.

**Kitchen and Mahaprasad Seva** is the most sought-after and the most impactful. The Mahaprasad Hall at Shegaon serves meals to between 2,000 and 5,000 devotees every day, depending on the season. Kitchen seva involves washing and chopping vegetables, helping with cooking and fire management, serving food to devotees in the hall, and cleaning up after meals. The morning shift runs from 5:30 AM to 9:00 AM (preparing and serving breakfast), the afternoon shift from 11:00 AM to 2:00 PM (lunch), and the evening shift from 6:00 PM to 8:30 PM (dinner). This seva is physically demanding but deeply rewarding. Many volunteers describe the act of serving Mahaprasad as a direct form of worship. The kitchen follows strict hygiene standards, and new volunteers are briefed on hand-washing protocols before entering.

**Queue Management Seva** helps the temple staff manage the darshan flow during busy periods. Volunteers guide devotees to the correct queue lanes, help elderly and disabled devotees to the priority lanes, and maintain order in the queue areas. This seva runs from 7:00 AM to 10:00 AM and from 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM on regular days. During festivals, queue management operates throughout the day. The queue management team wears bright vests or armbands so that devotees can identify them easily.

**Temple Cleaning and Maintenance Seva** involves sweeping the temple complex, cleaning the ghat areas, arranging flowers and decorations, maintaining the cleanliness of the Bhakta Niwas common areas, and helping with the setup and takedown of temporary structures during festivals. This seva is available throughout the day with flexible timings, making it a good option for devotees who want to contribute without committing to a fixed shift.

**Medical and First-Aid Seva** is for devotees with medical training. During festival periods, the Sansthan sets up temporary medical camps, and volunteer doctors and nurses are in high demand. If you have a medical background, mention this when you register. The Sansthan provides basic medical supplies, and your expertise can make a significant difference during health emergencies in crowded festival conditions.

**Information and Guide Seva** is a newer category where devotees who know the temple well help new visitors navigate the campus, explain darshan procedures, and answer common questions in Hindi, English, and Marathi. This seva is especially valuable during festivals when the temple receives visitors from across India who may not be familiar with the layout or procedures.

## How to Register for Seva

Seva registration is handled at the Sansthan office at Shegaon. Walk in and speak to the seva coordinator at the volunteer desk near the main entrance. You will be asked for your name, contact number, and availability. You can also register through the main Sansthan number by asking to be connected to the seva coordinator.

No prior registration is needed for regular-day seva. Simply show up at the volunteer desk and register on the spot. For festival seva — especially Pragat Din in February and Diwali — advance registration is strongly recommended because the number of volunteers needed is much larger and the coordination is more complex. Festival seva registrations open one month in advance, and devotees are advised to register early.

When you register, you receive a volunteer badge or identification card. Wear this at all times during seva. It helps the temple staff identify you and gives you access to certain areas of the campus that are restricted to general devotees. The badge is collected at the end of your seva shift.

## What to Wear and Bring

Wear simple, modest cotton clothes that you do not mind getting dirty. A kurta or simple shirt and trousers for men, and a kurta or salwar for women. Avoid wearing valuable jewellery or accessories during seva — the kitchen and queue areas are busy, and you do not want to worry about your belongings. Remove any watches, rings, or necklaces before entering the kitchen.

Bring a water bottle, comfortable closed-toe shoes, and a small towel. The Sansthan provides gloves for kitchen seva, aprons for cleaning seva, and any specialised equipment needed for other roles. You do not need to bring tools or supplies. Most volunteers also bring a small pack of digestive biscuits or dry snacks to keep them going through long shifts.

## What to Expect on a Typical Day

Arrive at the volunteer desk 15 minutes before your shift starts. The coordinator will brief you on your specific tasks and assign you to a supervisor. Follow the supervisor's instructions carefully — the Sansthan runs on a tight schedule, especially during the Mahaprasad distribution.

During your shift, you may be asked to switch tasks depending on the crowd flow and the needs of the moment. Flexibility is important. Stay until the end of your assigned shift unless your supervisor asks you to continue. If you need to leave early, inform your supervisor before your shift ends.

After your seva, many volunteers join the darshan queue or sit for the aarti. This is a beautiful way to experience the temple — you have served the community, and now you receive the blessing yourself. The spiritual satisfaction of seva followed by darshan is one of the most powerful experiences available at Shegaon.

## Accommodation for Volunteers

Volunteers who stay overnight at the Bhakta Niwas receive discounted or free accommodation depending on the season and the type of seva. During major festivals, the Sansthan provides free accommodation to volunteers who commit to at least two to three days of seva. On regular days, nominal charges apply, and you can book through the standard [WhatsApp Booking Step By Step Guide](/blog/whatsapp-booking-step-by-step-guide). If you are coming from another city specifically to volunteer, book your Bhakta Niwas room in advance. The [Bhakta Niwas Complete Booking Guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) covers the booking process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need any special skills to volunteer?**
No. Seva at the Sansthan is open to all devotees. The coordinators assign tasks based on your physical ability and availability. No prior experience or qualifications are needed.

**Can I volunteer for just one day?**
Yes. Single-day seva is welcome, especially on regular days. For festival seva, the trust prefers volunteers who can commit for at least two to three days.

**Is there a minimum age for volunteering?**
Children can help with simple tasks like distributing flowers or serving prasad under adult supervision. Adult seva roles are open to anyone above 16 years of age.

**What are the typical seva timings?**
Kitchen seva runs from 5:30 AM to 9:00 AM and 11:00 AM to 2:00 PM. Queue management shifts are 7:00 AM to 10:00 AM and 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM. Festival seva timings are longer and communicated during registration.

**Can I bring my family while volunteering?**
Yes. Many devotees bring their families and volunteer together. Family accommodation can be booked separately at the Bhakta Niwas.

**What should I bring for seva?**
Wear simple cotton clothes, bring a water bottle, comfortable shoes, and a small towel. The Sansthan provides volunteer badges, gloves, and any specialised equipment needed for your role.

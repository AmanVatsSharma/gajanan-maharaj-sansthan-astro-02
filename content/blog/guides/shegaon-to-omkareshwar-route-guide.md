# Shegaon To Omkareshwar Route Guide | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

The journey from Shegaon to Omkareshwar is one of the more interesting legs of the Maharashtra-Madhya Pradesh temple circuit. It takes you from the flat, cotton-growing plains of Vidarbha, through the hilly terrain of the Satpura range, and finally to the Mandhata island in the Narmada River where one of the twelve Jyotirlingas stands. The route covers approximately 340 kilometres, takes roughly 6 to 7 hours by car or taxi, and crosses from Maharashtra into Madhya Pradesh — a state border crossing that requires nothing more than a quick document check but adds a subtle sense of journeying into a different cultural landscape. This guide covers the exact route, the best way to travel, intermediate stopping points, accommodation options mid-journey, and what to expect as you transition from the Samadhi Mandir at Shegaon to the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga.

## The route overview

**Distance:** Approximately 340 kilometres

**Driving time:** 6 to 7 hours by car or taxi

**Best direction:** Shegaon to Khamgaon to Mehkar to Jalgaon Jamod to Burhanpur to Khandwa to Omkareshwar

**State crossing:** Maharashtra to Madhya Pradesh at the Burhanpur border

The most practical route follows NH 753C and MP SH 27 via Khandwa. The road quality is good on the Maharashtra side and decent on the Madhya Pradesh side, though some stretches near Burhanpur and Khandwa have narrower sections and moderate traffic.

## The journey in segments

### Shegaon to Khamgaon (35 km, 45 minutes)

The journey begins on NH 53 heading north from Shegaon toward Akola. At the Khamgaon turn-off, divert north toward Mehkar. This 35-kilometre stretch is straightforward and takes about 45 minutes. Khamgaon is a useful stop if you need to refuel, buy supplies, or have breakfast before the longer drive ahead. Several dhabas near the highway serve clean vegetarian food, and the town has banks and ATMs.

### Khamgaon to Mehkar to Jalgaon Jamod (80 km, 1.5 hours)

From Khamgaon, the road continues north through Mehkar and then toward Jalgaon Jamod. The terrain remains flat — Vidarbha's characteristic black cotton soil landscape — and the road is in good condition. This stretch passes through smaller towns with limited facilities, so it is worth using the facilities at Khamgaon before continuing.

### Jalgaon Jamod to Burhanpur (110 km, 2 hours)

At Jalgaon Jamod, the road turns northwest and enters the Satpura hill country. The landscape changes from flat plains to rolling hills, and the road becomes more winding. Burhanpur is a historically significant town with Mughal-era architecture, including the Shahi Qila and the Jama Masjid. It is worth a brief stop for lunch or a rest break — several restaurants near the bus stand serve clean, affordable vegetarian food. Burhanpur is also the state border crossing point, where you will show your ID at the Maharashtra-Madhya Pradesh border checkpost. For domestic travellers, this is a routine document check that takes 2 to 3 minutes.

### Burhanpur to Khandwa (80 km, 1.5 hours)

From Burhanpur, the road continues northwest through the Nimar region of Madhya Pradesh. The landscape is hilly and green, with the Satpura ranges visible on the horizon. Khandwa is a major railway junction and the nearest railhead to Omkareshwar. The town has good facilities — hotels, restaurants, banks, and fuel stations — and it is a useful stop if you want to break the journey or stay overnight before visiting Omkareshwar the next day.

### Khandwa to Omkareshwar (30 km, 45 minutes)

The final leg from Khandwa to Omkareshwar takes you across the Vindhya ranges and down to the Narmada River. The road winds through hills and then descends to the bridge that connects the mainland to the Mandhata island. The first view of the Narmada from the bridge is one of the more memorable moments of the journey — the river is wide, the island is visible ahead, and the temple spires rise above the island's tree cover.

Once you reach the bridge, the taxi drops you at the mainland end. You cross the bridge on foot (approximately 500 metres) with your luggage, and then walk or take an auto-rickshaw from the island end to the Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas or the Omkareshwar temple.

## Transport options for this route

**By private taxi or hired car:** The most practical and comfortable option. A private AC car (Maruti Ertiga or similar) from Shegaon to Omkareshwar costs approximately INR 3,000 to 4,500 depending on the operator and the season. The driver will typically wait at Omkareshwar for your return journey if you book a round trip, or you can book a one-way transfer. Book at least 1 to 2 weeks in advance through local operators in Shegaon or Khamgaon. The [complete transport guide](/blog/complete-transport-guide-for-devotees) covers taxi booking across all four locations.

**By bus:** There is no direct bus from Shegaon to Omkareshwar. The most practical bus option is to take an MSRTC or private bus from Shegaon or Khamgaon to Khandwa, and then a local bus or taxi from Khandwa to Omkareshwar. The Khamgaon to Khandwa bus takes approximately 4 hours, and the Khandwa to Omkareshwar leg is 30 minutes by taxi or 45 minutes by local bus.

**By train:** There is no direct train from Shegaon to Omkareshwar. The train journey from Shegaon to Khandwa takes approximately 5 to 6 hours, and you would then take a taxi from Khandwa to Omkareshwar. The train option is less practical than a direct taxi for this specific route.

## Mid-journey stops

If you want to break the journey into two days for a more relaxed experience, these are the best stopping points:

**Khamgaon (35 km from Shegaon):** A comfortable first-day stop. Khamgaon has several budget hotels (INR 500–1,500 per night) and is a 45-minute drive from Shegaon. You can spend the night in Khamgaon, visit the Shegaon temple in the morning, and continue to Omkareshwar the next day. The total journey would be Khamgaon on Day 1 (after Shegaon darshan) and Omkareshwar on Day 2.

**Khandwa (240 km from Shegaon, 100 km from Omkareshwar):** A better stopping point if you want to divide the journey more evenly. Khandwa has a wide range of hotels and restaurants, and the railway station makes it accessible by train. From Khandwa, Omkareshwar is just 30 km away. A Khandwa stop works well for a two-day Shegaon to Omkareshwar itinerary, with a night in Khandwa between the two temple visits.

**Burhanpur (180 km from Shegaon):** A historical stopping point with Mughal-era monuments. If you have an interest in history, Burhanpur's Shahi Qila and Jama Masjid are worth a 1 to 2 hour visit. The town has budget hotels and dhabas for rest and refreshment.

## Accommodation at Omkareshwar

Upon arrival at Omkareshwar, your accommodation options are:

**Bhakta Niwas (Sansthan accommodation):** The Sansthan's Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas is located on the Mandhata island near the temple. It offers 2-bed and 3-bed rooms (Non-AC and AC). Book through the official booking number +91 80531 90691 or the [booking form](/booking). For availability and room types, see the [Bhakta Niwas across locations comparison](/blog/bhakta-niwas-across-locations-comparison).

**Hotels near the Narmada bridge (mainland side):** Several budget and mid-range hotels near the bridge offer rooms from INR 800 to INR 2,000 per night. They are approximately 1.5 kilometres from the temple (a 20-minute walk across the bridge). This is a practical option if the Bhakta Niwas is full.

**Budget guesthouses on the island:** Several small guesthouses are available on the Mandhata island near the temple. These are basic but well-located and typically available on short notice.

## Practical tips for the Shegaon-Omkareshwar leg

**Start early.** The 6 to 7 hour drive is more comfortable when you start at 6:00 AM and arrive at Omkareshwar by early afternoon, giving you time to freshen up and visit the temple for the evening darshan.

**Carry snacks and water.** The highway stretches between Mehkar and Burhanpur have limited food options. Pack water, fruits, and snacks for the journey.

**Carry ID for the state border crossing.** The Maharashtra-Madhya Pradesh border checkpost at Burhanpur requires ID verification for all passengers. Carry your Aadhaar card or driving licence.

**Book a round-trip taxi.** If you plan to continue from Omkareshwar to Trimbakeshwar or back to Shegaon, book a round-trip taxi that can wait at Omkareshwar for your onward journey. This saves time and avoids the hassle of finding a new taxi at Omkareshwar.

**Plan for the bridge walk.** The Narmada bridge at Omkareshwar does not allow taxis to cross onto the island. You will walk across the bridge (approximately 500 metres) with your luggage. Pack light enough that you can carry everything across the bridge in one trip.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is the road from Shegaon to Omkareshwar safe for night driving?**

A: The road is generally safe, but night driving through the Satpura hills between Jalgaon Jamod and Burhanpur is not recommended for unfamiliar drivers. The road narrows in sections, and there is limited lighting. Start early in the morning and complete the journey in daylight.

**Q: Can I take a bus from Shegaon to Omkareshwar?**

A: There is no direct bus. You would need to take a bus from Shegaon or Khamgaon to Khandwa, and then a taxi or local bus from Khandwa to Omkareshwar. The total journey time would be approximately 8 to 9 hours, which is longer than the direct taxi route.

**Q: How much does a taxi from Shegaon to Omkareshwar cost?**

A: A one-way AC car (Maruti Ertiga or similar) costs approximately INR 3,000 to 4,500. An SUV (Toyota Innova or Tata Safari) costs approximately INR 4,000 to 5,500. Always confirm the rate and whether the driver's meals and accommodation are included before booking.

**Q: Is the Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas on the island or the mainland?**

A: The Sansthan's Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas is on the Mandhata island, near the Jyotirlinga temple. Taxis drop you at the mainland end of the Narmada bridge, and you walk across to the island. The Bhakta Niwas is a short walk from the bridge end on the island.

**Q: Should I book the Shegaon-Omkareshwar leg together with the Omkareshwar-Trimbakeshwar leg?**

A: If you are doing the full Maharashtra-Madhya Pradesh circuit, booking a multi-leg taxi is more economical than booking each leg separately. A 7-day taxi for the full circuit (Shegaon-Omkareshwar-Pandharpur-Trimbakeshwar-Shegaon) costs approximately INR 17,500 to 24,500 for an AC car, which is significantly cheaper than booking each leg independently. The [multi-location pilgrimage planning guide](/blog/multi-location-pilgrimage-planning-guide) covers multi-leg taxi booking.

**Q: What is the best time of year to travel this route?**

A: The best time is between October and February, when the weather is cool and the roads are in good condition. The monsoon season (July to September) makes the Satpura hill roads slippery and less safe. Summer (March to June) is hot but manageable if you travel early in the morning.

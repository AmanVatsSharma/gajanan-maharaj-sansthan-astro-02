# Bhakta Niwas Long Stay Room Booking Rules

A long stay at a Bhakta Niwas — three nights, five, a week or more — is requested exactly the way a one-night stay is: one written request through the [booking page](/booking), with dates, headcount and room row, which the office confirms room by room. What changes with length is everything around the request: the cost arithmetic compounds night by night, the room choice starts mattering more than the tariff, and the house rules of a pilgrim rest house — visitor discipline, meal rhythms, the etiquette of a continuing stay — become the difference between a residence and a sojourn. Each night of the stay runs on the network's from-entry day cycle, so a long-stay family that arrives at a chosen hour keeps a steady daily rhythm throughout.

This guide sets out the long-stay rules as the office states them, the honest weekly arithmetic from the sanctioned tariffs, and the living wisdom that families on extended devotional stays have shared with the digital desk.

## What a long stay means at a pilgrim rest house

Sansthan accommodation exists so that devotion can take its time. Where a hotel measures a guest in nights, a Bhakta Niwas measures a devotee in darshans, aartis and unhurried meals — and the rooms are priced, ₹1,650 to ₹2,550 per night across the five rows, so that a week of devotion costs what a single city weekend might. A long stay, in this spirit, is not an unusual request to be negotiated but a familiar one to be planned: the office handles multi-night bookings routinely at all four centres, and the rules that govern them are the same gentle rules that govern every stay, merely applied across more mornings.

The right mental shift is from lodging to living. A one-night party uses the room; a week-long party inhabits it. That is why the planning below spends as much ink on routines, laundry and meals as on the request itself — the families who write back happiest about long stays are the ones who arrived prepared to dwell rather than to sleep.

It is also worth naming what a long stay is not. It is not a lease, a tenancy or a house relocation; the room remains a pilgrim accommodation with the house's rhythm around it, and the office's word — given at confirmation, refreshed at the desk — is the standing authority on what the stay may include. Families who hold their plans lightly this way, asking rather than assuming at each step, find the doors of the house open to them at every asking; the rules below are best read in exactly that spirit, as the house's honest answer to the question every long guest eventually asks: how shall we live here well?

## Asking for many nights, properly

The request itself follows six deliberate lines.

1. **State the full date range at the outset** — "arriving the fourth, departing the tenth" — because the office allots contiguous nights against the chart as one arrangement, not six separate day-decisions.
2. **Give the true headcount and its constancy**: whether all nights carry the same party, or family members join and leave mid-stay, so the register is drawn accurately.
3. **Name the room row for the whole stay, with a fallback**, remembering that a week amplifies the small comforts — the AC row that costs ₹300 more per night is ₹2,100 across a week in high summer, and worth every rupee of it.
4. **Explain the purpose in one sentence** — chaturmas observance, a parikrama, seva, a family pilgrimage with elders — because purpose helps the office choose the right wing.
5. **Ask your continuing-stay questions in the first message**: meal timings at the centre, laundry arrangements, the desk's guidance for longer holds.
6. **Keep the office's confirmation as your reference**, and send any change of plan through the same thread rather than a new channel.

## The honest arithmetic of a week

Long-stay arithmetic rewards a few minutes with the sanctioned tariffs, because the differences between rows, compounded by nights, become real money.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bed AC | ₹1,650 | ₹4,950 | ₹8,250 | ₹11,550 |
| 3 Bed Non-AC | ₹1,750 | ₹5,250 | ₹8,750 | ₹12,250 |
| 3 Bed AC | ₹2,050 | ₹6,150 | ₹10,250 | ₹14,350 |
| 4 Bed Non-AC | ₹2,250 | ₹6,750 | ₹11,250 | ₹15,750 |
| 4 Bed AC | ₹2,550 | ₹7,650 | ₹12,750 | ₹17,850 |

Read the table twice — once for the row you want, once for the row a week can live with — because the numbers reward the second reading.

Three derived figures are worth carrying in mind. The same-bed air-conditioning premium is ₹300 per night — ₹900 over three nights, ₹1,500 over five, ₹2,100 over a week. Moving from the 3 Bed Non-AC to the 4 Bed Non-AC adds ₹500 per night, which over a week is ₹3,500, roughly the cost of two extra nights in the smaller row. And lifting a couple's arrangement up to the 4 Bed AC, so the whole family shares one room, adds ₹900 per night — over five nights, ₹4,500, usually less than a second room would cost in any arrangement the maths allows. ## Rules of the house, gently stated

The rules that govern a long stay are the Sansthan's accommodation rules applied with continuity, and their spirit is simpler than any list: a Bhakta Niwas is the Maharaj's house, and guests keep it as they would a revered host's home. In practice the office states them plainly at confirmation, and they are collected in the [Bhakta Niwas booking rules and terms](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-rules-and-terms) guide and the wider [understanding Sansthan accommodation rules](/blog/understanding-sansthan-accommodation-rules) page. The essentials for a longer party: the room is registered to the named party, and the headcount the register holds is the headcount the room holds; visitors are received as the centre's discipline directs, typically in reception areas rather than bedrooms; quiet hours honour the pre-dawn aarti rhythm the house exists to serve; and payments are receipted at every stage, with the balance settled as the office directs.

Two courtesies mark the experienced long-stay family. The first is the daily touch at the desk — a greeting, a question when needed, a word of thanks at breakfast — which turns a week from anonymity into acquaintance, and often into guidance no guidebook holds. The second is the room itself, left each morning as the house's discipline expects, so housekeeping's work is easy and the stay's blessings are shared rather than consumed.

A third courtesy belongs to the long-stay weeks specifically: the family's visibility. Over many days, a household's habits become part of the wing's life — the hour the children run the corridor, the volume of the evening bhajan, the way luggage accumulates in a room now lived in. None of this is policed; all of it is noticed, by staff and by neighbouring devotee families alike. The long-stay families the houses remember with warmth are the ones who kept their week gentle by that standard — children taught the corridor's quiet hours, devotional music at a companionable volume, the room's little disorder contained by evening. It is the same discipline any family keeps in a revered host's home, applied for seven mornings instead of one.

## Choosing a room you can live in

For one night, any clean room serves; for a week, the room's character becomes the stay's character. Families on long stays should weigh four qualities in the request. A wing within easy reach of the desk and dining keeps the daily walk short, which matters more on the seventh morning than the first. The air-conditioning question belongs to the season and the town — a week in the hot months answers it firmly in the AC row's favour, while the cool season lets the ₹300-per-night premium fund prasad instead. Light and ventilation matter to a room lived in at noon; the office can advise which wings breathe best at each centre. And a ground-floor preference, worth requesting in any case, becomes near-essential for a week-long stay with elders — a truth developed further in our guides for seniors across the network. The row-by-row facilities detail, including the attached bathroom with hot water standard to every row, is tabulated in the Shegaon room types guide and holds network-wide.

## The long-stay rhythm: meals, rest and the days between

A week at a temple town finds its own liturgy of hours. Mornings begin with the aarti and breakfast from the canteen or mahaprasad serving; middays belong to darshan, reading, rest; evenings gather the family for the night darshan and an unhurried dinner. Laundry is a long stay's quiet logistics question — ask the desk at arrival how families at that centre manage it, because practice differs by town and the local answer is the true one. Medicines, if the stay serves an elder's recovery or a chronic condition, should be carried for the full duration with margin, and the daily routine built around their schedule rather than the reverse. The deeper art of pacing a multi-day visit — rest days inside the itinerary, the courage to skip a crowded morning for a calm afternoon — is drawn from devotee experience in [Shegaon one day versus two day visit planning](/blog/shegaon-one-day-vs-two-day-visit-planning), and the principle stretches to any length of stay: the yatra is served by the family's steadiness, not its exhaustion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a limit on how many nights I can book?

Multi-night requests are handled by the office per dates and availability rather than by a fixed published cap; state your full intended range in the request and the office's confirmation is the authoritative answer.

### Is the tariff lower for longer stays?

The sanctioned tariffs are per night and stand across stay lengths — ₹1,650 to ₹2,550 by row — so a long stay's economy comes from planning, not discount-hunting.

### Can family visit us during a long stay?

Visitors are received as each centre's discipline directs, typically around the reception areas; the desk at your centre will guide you on the first morning.

### What if we need to extend once the stay has begun?

Ask at the desk as early as you can — extensions depend on the chart ahead of you, and a request made two days early is a courtesy the desk can usually honour where one made at checkout cannot.

### Which room row suits a week-long family stay?

Most families choose the 4 Bed rows for the space; in the hot months the 4 Bed AC, at ₹2,550 a night — ₹17,850 for a full week — is the row that keeps the week gentle.

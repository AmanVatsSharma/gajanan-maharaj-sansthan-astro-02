# Bhakta Niwas Family Room Booking Guide

Family room booking at a Bhakta Niwas centre is a piece of arithmetic before it is anything else: count the heads travelling, match them to one of the published room rows — 2 Bed AC at ₹1,650, 3 Bed Non-AC at ₹1,750, 3 Bed AC at ₹2,050, 4 Bed Non-AC at ₹2,250 or 4 Bed AC at ₹2,550 per night — and submit the request through the [booking page](/booking) early enough for the office to confirm. A family of four fits one 4-bed room; a party of six books two rooms; capacity equals the bed count, always. That single rule, applied honestly, prevents most of the confusion families bring to our digital desk.

This guide walks through the arithmetic, the two-room combinations joint families rely on, the special requests worth making at booking time rather than at the desk, and how the family experience differs across Shegaon, Pandharpur, Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar.

## Count heads first, choose rows second

The most common family booking error we hear about is not a wrong date or a misspelt name — it is a party of five squeezing into a 4-bed request "because the child is small". The rooms are allotted strictly by capacity, and the bed count is the capacity. When the office reads a request, it reads the headcount against the row; a mismatch means an exchange of messages, a delay, and occasionally a disappointed arrival.

So begin the booking conversation, whether online or with the office, from an honest headcount:

- Count every traveller who will sleep in the room, children included.
- Decide whether grandparents, who may wake at dawn for aarti, would honestly be better in an adjoining 2-bed than in the same large room.
- Check whether your party splits naturally — two parents and a child in one room, an uncle's family in another — before choosing room types.
- Only then match parties to rows, remembering that the 2-bed row is published only in AC.

Families who book this way report the same small satisfaction: the confirmation arrives without back-and-forth, and the desk hands over exactly what was imagined. The wider spirit of travelling with the whole family is captured in our [family pilgrimage with children tips](/blog/family-pilgrimage-with-children-tips), which pairs well with this page.

A note on timing belongs beside the counting. Family rows — the 4-bed rooms above all — are the first inventory to tighten at every centre, because the family is the standard unit of Indian pilgrimage. In plain weeks a request sent a fortnight ahead is comfortably confirmed; in festival windows, family rows can close weeks out, and the ekadashi arithmetic is its own discipline at each town. The rule our desk repeats: when the date is fixed by panchang rather than by convenience, the request should precede the train tickets, never follow them. Families unsure whether their chosen week carries festival weight will find the calendar guidance they need in the [festival dates and booking windows guide](/blog/festival-dates-and-booking-windows), and those combining several towns should let the [multi-location pilgrimage planning guide](/blog/multi-location-pilgrimage-planning-guide) sequence the family's stops before any single room is requested.

## The room rows, read the way a family reads them

The tariff list is short and honest, and it rewards being read slowly — once, before the request, rather than twice at the counter.

| Room row | Beds (capacity) | Tariff per night | The family it genuinely fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bed AC | 2 | ₹1,650 | A couple, or a grandparent pair wanting quiet |
| 3 Bed Non-AC | 3 | ₹1,750 | Parents with one child in the cool season |
| 3 Bed AC | 3 | ₹2,050 | The same trio travelling April to June |
| 4 Bed Non-AC | 4 | ₹2,250 | The standard family room, most months |
| 4 Bed AC | 4 | ₹2,550 | Families of four in the hot season |

Three readings of this table matter to families. First, the jump from 3 Bed Non-AC to 3 Bed AC — and from 4 Bed Non-AC to 4 Bed AC — is ₹300 a night for air-conditioning at the same bed count, which over a two-night weekend is ₹600 for the family's sleep. Second, the climb from 3 Bed Non-AC at ₹1,750 to 4 Bed Non-AC at ₹2,250 is ₹500 a night: worth paying when a fourth head exists, wasteful when one does not. Third, moving from the 2 Bed AC row to the 4 Bed AC row adds ₹900 a night and doubles the beds — the natural step when a couple's yatra becomes a family's.

Every row carries the same backbone regardless of price: an attached bathroom with hot water, capacity equal to the bed count, and the 24-hour check-out cycle counted from the time of entry. A dearer room buys more beds and cooler air, never different terms. The full facilities picture, wing by wing, sits in the [Shegaon Bhakta Niwas room types and facilities guide](/blog/shegaon-bhakta-niwas-room-types-and-facilities) and its Pandharpur counterpart.

## Two-room combinations for joint families

A joint family of six or seven does not need a suite — the Sansthan's answer is two rooms in the same building, requested together, and this is where family booking becomes a small art.

| Party | Combination | Combined tariff per night | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 (2 elders, 3 young) | 2 Bed AC + 3 Bed AC | ₹3,700 | Elders keep early hours without waking anyone |
| 6 (two couples, 2 children) | 4 Bed AC + 2 Bed AC | ₹4,200 | One room per generation's rhythm |
| 6 (six adults) | 3 Bed Non-AC + 3 Bed Non-AC | ₹3,500 | The economical cool-season answer |
| 7 (parents, 3 children, 1 grandparent) | 4 Bed Non-AC + 3 Bed Non-AC | ₹4,000 | Everyone bedded, no one cramped |
| 8 (two families of four) | 4 Bed AC + 4 Bed AC | ₹5,100 | Both rooms booked under one request |

Read the combination table with the same honesty as the single-room table. The ₹3,700 combination suits the classic multigenerational yatra — grandparents in the 2-bed, parents and a child in the 3-bed — and leaves ₹800 a night unspent against the two-4-bed-non-AC alternative of ₹4,500. Families preparing the deeper logistics of such trips will find the [multi-generation family yatra guide for Shegaon](/blog/shegaon-multi-generation-family-yatra) worth an evening's reading, and the Pandharpur version covers the wari-season version of the same journey.

One caution from the desk's mailbag: request both rooms in a single booking with one contact name and one headcount breakdown, rather than two separate requests from two phones. Separate requests can be read as separate parties and allotted to different wings; a single request states plainly that the rooms travel together.

## Making the special requests that matter to families

Some wishes cannot be granted at the counter on arrival day, because the allocation chart is drawn earlier. The sequence below places each family request where it belongs — inside the booking itself.

1. **Write the headcount into the request exactly**, naming each room and its occupants: "one 4 Bed AC for two adults and two children; one 2 Bed AC for two senior citizens".
2. **Ask for same-floor or adjoining rooms at booking time**, not at check-in; the office notes it while the chart still has slack.
3. **Request a ground-floor room if a member struggles with stairs**, and say why in one line — the office handles such requests with visible care when it knows the reason.
4. **State your arrival window**, especially for overnight trains, so room readiness is planned the previous evening where possible.
5. **Mention an infant who will share a parent's bed**, so the register and the chart both reflect reality; infants do not change the row, but they should change the paperwork's honesty.

Families making these five requests at booking time stand at the counter for minutes, not hours. The pathway a request travels afterwards — acknowledgement, confirmation, reference number — is mapped in the [Bhakta Niwas booking confirmation process](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-confirmation-process), which we suggest the booking member of the family reads before anyone packs.

## What the family tariff covers, and what it leaves out

The published tariff is the room, whole and complete: the beds, the attached bathroom with hot water, the 24-hour cycle, and the standing support the Sansthan's offices give devotees across centres. It is not a hotel bill, and families who read it as one arrive calmer.

What the room leaves out is everything a dharamshala fairly leaves out — there is no room service to telephone, no television package to browse, no restaurant bill to sign to the room. What the tariff quietly includes instead is the thing commercial stays cannot stock: mahaprasad and the canteen culture around the temple, the short walk to darshan, and the company of families travelling for the same purpose. The eating arrangements are described warmly in the [mahaprasad and food guide](/blog/mahaprasad-and-food-guide), and families consistently tell us the food alone justifies the choice of stay.

The honest budgeting frame — what a family of four spends across two nights, meals, travel and prasad — is drawn in the [pilgrimage budget planning for families guide](/blog/pilgrimage-budget-planning-for-families), which treats the room tariff as one honest line among several.

One more expectation worth setting before the request is sent: the family room is a room, not a suite, and its comforts are the pilgrim's comforts. There will be beds enough for the heads named, an attached bathroom with hot water, light to read a passage of the Gajanan Vijay by, and walls that carry the sound of a corridor full of families doing exactly what yours is doing. Families arriving with hotel expectations arrive twice — once at the counter and once in their own understanding — and the second arrival is the happier one for being prepared. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many rooms should a family of five book?

The clean answer is a 4-bed plus a 2-bed, booked together, so the fifth sleeper has a real bed and the party stays within the capacity rule; squeezing five into a 4-bed request is the single most corrected error the office handles.

### Is the 4 Bed AC row worth ₹2,550 for a family?

Between April and June, most families who have tried both rows say yes without hesitation — ₹300 a night over the non-AC row buys the sleep that the next day's queue-standing spends; in the cool months, the non-AC row at ₹2,250 is the sensible default.

### Can we get two rooms next to each other?

Yes, when the request is made at booking time and stated plainly — one request, one contact name, both rooms named. Same-floor and adjoining allocations are handled kindly, but they are chart decisions, made before arrival day.

### Do children count towards the room capacity?

They do. Capacity equals the bed count, and the bed count is the register's truth; an infant sharing a parent's bed should be mentioned in the request so the paperwork stays honest, but older children each occupy a bed and are counted.

### Which centre should a first family yatra choose?

Most families begin at Shegaon, where the temple, the annexes and Anand Sagar sit within a walkable circuit; the [complete guide to the Sansthan](/blog/gajanan-maharaj-sansthan-complete-guide) frames all four centres for those planning further.

# Maharashtra Pilgrimage Circuit | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

Maharashtra holds a unique place in India's spiritual geography. The state is home to three of the twelve Jyotirlingas — Trimbakeshwar near Nashik, Grishneshwar near Ellora, and the Aundha Nagnath in the Marathwada region. Beyond the Jyotirlingas, the Bhima river at Pandharpur has drawn lakhs of warkaris for centuries, and the Samadhi of Shri Gajanan Maharaj at Shegaon is one of the most visited pilgrimage destinations in Vidarbha. Omkareshwar, just across the border in Madhya Pradesh, is often included in Maharashtra-centred circuits because of its proximity and shared spiritual heritage. Together, these four locations form what devotees increasingly refer to as the Maharashtra pilgrimage circuit — a journey that combines Jyotirlinga darshan, wari heritage, and the living tradition of Shri Gajanan Maharaj.

## The Four Pillars of the Circuit

**Shegaon** is the natural starting point for most devotees. Located in Buldhana district of Vidarbha, approximately 230 km from Nagpur and 550 km from Mumbai, Shegaon is easily accessible by both road and rail. The Shri Sant Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan manages the Samadhi Mandir, which houses the resting place of the revered saint who attained samadhi in 1910. The Sansthan also operates the Bhakta Niwas for devotees, a massive Mahaprasad hall that serves thousands daily, and an Anand Sagar entertainment complex. Shegaon is not just a stop; it is the organisational hub of the entire circuit, and many devotees use it as their base before branching out to the other three locations.

**Omkareshwar** sits on the Mandhata island in the Narmada river, about 340 km north of Shegaon. The island is shaped like the Om symbol, which is how it earned its name. Two temples dominate the island: the Amaleshwar (or Mamaleshwar) temple, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, and the Omkareshwar temple dedicated to Lord Shiva. The Narmada aarti at sunset, performed on the river ghats with hundreds of oil lamps, is widely considered one of the most beautiful spiritual experiences in central India. The surrounding area includes the Siddhnath temple and the historic Fort of Mandhata, which offers panoramic views of the Narmada valley. The nearest major city is Indore, about 80 km away, and most devotees fly or drive into Indore before taking a 2-hour road journey to the island.

**Pandharpur** lies on the banks of the Bhima river in Solapur district, about 230 km south of Pune and 550 km from Omkareshwar. The town is dominated by the Vitthal temple, dedicated to Lord Vitthal (a form of Krishna) and his consort Rukmini. Pandharpur is the destination of the famous Ashadhi Ekadashi wari, during which lakhs of warkaris walk from Alandi and Dehu to Pandharpur over a period of 21 days. The town also sees massive gatherings during Kartik Ekadashi in November. The Bhima river ghats, particularly the Vishnupad ghat where it is believed Lord Vitthal left his footprints, are central to the pilgrimage experience. Pandharpur's significance in the Bhakti movement, its connection to saints like Tukaram, Dnyaneshwar, and Eknath, and the living tradition of the wari make it the most culturally rich of the four circuit stops.

**Trimbakeshwar** is located about 30 km from Nashik in the Sahyadri mountain range. The Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga temple is one of the most sacred Shiva temples in India and is also the source of the Godavari river, which originates from the Brahmagiri mountain nearby. The temple complex includes the main Jyotirlinga, the Kushavarat kund where devotees perform rituals, and the hilltop temple of Nilgiri. The Brahmagiri mountain trek, about 17 km from the temple, takes you to the river's origin point and is one of the most physically demanding but spiritually rewarding side activities in the circuit. Trimbakeshwar is particularly crowded during Shravan month and Mahashivratri, when hundreds of thousands of devotees visit for the Bauli aarti.

## Recommended Circuit Itineraries

How you sequence these four locations depends on your starting point, available time, and the season. Here are three proven itineraries:

**The 7-Day Grand Circuit:** Starting from Nagpur, drive to Shegaon (3.5 hours, 230 km). Spend 2 days at Shegaon for darshan and rest. Drive to Omkareshwar (6 hours, 340 km). Spend 1 full day at Omkareshwar. Drive to Pandharpur (8 hours, 550 km) with an overnight stop at Aurangabad or Solapur. Spend 1 day at Pandharpur. Drive to Trimbakeshwar (7 hours, 420 km) via Nashik. Spend 2 days at Trimbakeshwar and Nashik. Return to Nagpur (4.5 hours, 260 km) or fly out from Nashik.

**The 4-Day Shegaon-Omkareshwar Short Circuit:** Ideal for devotees with limited leave. Drive from Nagpur to Shegaon (3.5 hours), spend 1 day, then drive to Omkareshwar (6 hours), spend 1.5 days, and return to Nagpur via Khandwa and Amla. This gives you the Shegaon Samadhi darshan, the Narmada aarti, and the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga in a comfortable 4-day trip.

**The Extended Pune-Starting Circuit:** If you are starting from Pune, consider the Pune-Shegaon-Pandharpur-Trimbakeshwar-Nashik loop. Pune to Shegaon is 580 km (10 hours by road, or fly to Nagpur and drive from there). From Shegaon, the route loops south to Pandharpur (6 hours) and then north to Trimbakeshwar (7 hours), ending in Nashik where you can catch a flight or train back. This 8-10 day itinerary is described in detail in the [week-long devotional circuit planner](/blog/week-long-devotional-circuit-planner).

## Best Seasons for Each Location

The Maharashtra pilgrimage circuit can be undertaken year-round, but each location has its own ideal season. Shegaon is most comfortable between October and February, when the Buldhana district weather is pleasant. The pre-dawn Mangala Aarti during these months is genuinely cool, and the outdoor queue experience is manageable. From March to May, Shegaon temperatures can exceed 40 degrees, and the darshan queue becomes physically demanding. Plan for the 8:00 AM or evening slots during summer.

Omkareshwar is at its best between October and March. The Mandhata island has a microclimate moderated by the Narmada river, and winter days are comfortably cool. The summer months of April to May are harsh, with temperatures reaching 42-45 degrees. The monsoon months of July to September bring the Narmada to its full glory, with the river appearing wide and dramatic, but the island paths can become slippery.

Pandharpur is best visited during the winter months of November to February. The Bhima river is low during this period, making the ghats accessible and the walking comfortable. Summer is extremely hot, and the open ghats offer little shade. The monsoon months bring the river to a dramatic high, but the wet conditions make long walks around the temple difficult. If you want to experience the wari, Ashadhi Ekadashi (usually in July) is the date to target, despite the rain and heat.

Trimbakeshwar is pleasant year-round due to the Sahyadri elevation, which keeps temperatures 3-5 degrees cooler than the surrounding Nashik plain. Winter mornings can be cool, with fog around the Brahmagiri hills in December and January. The monsoon months are spectacularly green, and the Anjaneri waterfall is at its best. Shravan month (July-August) brings massive crowds but also the most vibrant spiritual atmosphere.

## Transport and Connectivity

Each of the four locations is connected by road to major cities. Nagpur is the nearest airport to Shegaon (3.5 hours by road) and also the best departure point for Omkareshwar (via Khandwa). Indore is the nearest airport to Omkareshwar (2 hours by road). Pune has an airport with good connectivity to Pandharpur (via Solapur, about 5 hours by road). Nashik has a domestic airport with flights from Mumbai and Ahmedabad, and it serves as the gateway to Trimbakeshwar (30 minutes by road).

For devotees who prefer rail, Shegaon has its own railway station on the Bhusaval-Nagpur line with regular trains from Mumbai, Nagpur, and Bhusaval. Pandharpur has a railway station on the Miraj-Solapur line. Omkareshwar does not have a railway station; the nearest railhead is Khandwa, about 60 km away. Trimbakeshwar does not have a railway station either; the nearest is Nashik Road, about 40 km away.

State transport buses connect all four locations to their nearest major cities. MSRTC operates regular services from Pune, Mumbai, and Nagpur to Shegaon, Pandharpur, and Nashik. MP State Transport runs buses from Indore to Omkareshwar. Bus fares are significantly cheaper than private car travel, though journey times are longer.

## Spiritual Significance of the Combined Circuit

Visiting all four locations in one trip creates a spiritual narrative that each location alone cannot provide. Shegaon anchors the circuit in the living tradition of a 20th-century saint whose message of simplicity and service continues to resonate. Omkareshwar connects you to one of the most ancient Jyotirlingas and the sacred river Narmada. Pandharpur immerses you in the Bhakti movement's most powerful living expression — the wari. And Trimbakeshwar connects you to the Godavari, the Ganga of the South, and one of the most powerful Shiva temples in India.

Together, these four locations cover the devotional spectrum from nirguna (formless) to saguna (with form) worship, from the intimate to the monumental, and from the peace of the Vidarbha countryside to the energy of the Bhima river wari. Devotees who complete the full circuit often describe a sense of spiritual completion that visiting any single location cannot replicate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days are needed for the full circuit?** A comfortable pace requires 7-10 days, with 2 days at Shegaon, 1-2 days at Omkareshwar, 1 day at Pandharpur, and 2 days at Trimbakeshwar. A rushed 4-day version is possible but sacrifices the contemplative quality of the experience.

**Which location should I visit first?** Shegaon is the best starting point for most devotees, particularly if you are arriving from Nagpur or Mumbai. The Sansthan's organisational infrastructure is strongest here, and you can collect information and make bookings for the other locations from the Shegaon office.

**Can I skip one of the four locations?** Yes. If you have limited time, prioritise Shegaon and whichever other location holds the most personal significance for you. Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar are both Jyotirlingas and spiritually similar; one is sufficient if time is tight.

**Is the circuit suitable for first-time pilgrims?** Yes, but start with just Shegaon and one other location. The [sansthan location selection guide](/blog/sansthan-location-selection-guide) helps first-time visitors choose the best entry point.

**What is the total road distance of the circuit?** Covering all four locations in a loop starting and ending at Nagpur is approximately 1,790 km. Starting from Mumbai, add another 550 km to reach Shegaon.

**Are there guided tours available for the circuit?** The Sansthan does not currently run an official multi-location guided tour. However, several registered tour operators in Nagpur and Pune offer 7-day and 10-day packages. Verify the operator's credentials through the [Sansthan contact office](/contact) before booking.

# Travel Insurance Pilgrimage | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

Travel insurance is one of those things that most people know they should have but few people actually buy before a pilgrimage. The reasoning is usually the same: "It is a short trip, I am healthy, I am travelling within India, and the chances of something going wrong are small." This reasoning is not entirely wrong — the probability of a medical emergency, a lost bag, or a cancelled transport booking on a five to ten day pilgrimage is low, and the trust accommodation at the Sansthan locations is reliable and safe. But the consequences of something going wrong without insurance are disproportionately high, and the cost of a basic travel insurance policy is low enough that the decision to buy one is almost always the right one.

This guide covers the specific insurance considerations for a multi-location pilgrimage to the four Sansthan locations — what to cover, what the standard policies include and exclude, how to make a claim if something goes wrong, and how insurance fits into a broader risk management plan that includes the trust's support services and your own preparation.

## What Travel Insurance Covers

A standard travel insurance policy for domestic travel in India typically covers three categories of risk: medical emergencies, trip cancellations and interruptions, and loss or delay of baggage and personal belongings. The coverage amounts vary by policy and by insurer, and the premium is typically two to five percent of the total trip cost.

**Medical emergency coverage** is the most important component for a pilgrimage trip. The policy covers hospitalisation costs, medical treatment, and emergency evacuation if you are injured or fall ill during the trip. The coverage is particularly valuable if you have a pre-existing medical condition that requires ongoing treatment, because the policy covers the cost of emergency medical care even if the condition is pre-existing. The [Handling Medical Emergencies on Yatra](/blog/handling-medical-emergencies-on-yatra) guide covers the hospital facilities at all four Sansthan locations, and the insurance coverage supplements the trust's medical support by covering the costs of private hospital treatment if the local facilities are not adequate.

**Trip cancellation and interruption coverage** compensates you if you have to cancel or cut short your trip due to illness, family emergency, or other covered reasons. The coverage reimburses the non-refundable expenses — accommodation bookings, transport bookings, and tour deposits — that you have already paid and cannot recover. This coverage is particularly valuable for multi-location trips where the accommodation and transport bookings are made well in advance and the cancellation policies vary by provider.

**Baggage and personal belongings coverage** compensates you if your luggage is lost, stolen, or damaged during the trip. The coverage is typically limited to a maximum amount per item and a total maximum for all items, and it excludes cash, valuables, and documents. The [Luggage Storage and Lockers](/blog/luggage-storage-and-lockers) guide covers the luggage storage facilities at all four Sansthan locations, and the insurance coverage supplements the trust's storage security by providing financial compensation if your luggage is lost or damaged.

## What Standard Policies Exclude

Standard travel insurance policies have exclusions that are important to understand before you buy. The most common exclusions for pilgrimage travel are:

**Pre-existing medical conditions.** Most standard policies exclude coverage for medical treatment related to pre-existing conditions unless you purchase a specific rider. If you have a chronic condition — diabetes, hypertension, heart disease — check whether your policy covers emergency treatment related to that condition, and consider purchasing a pre-existing condition rider if it does not.

**Adventure and high-risk activities.** Some policies exclude coverage for activities that are classified as high-risk — trekking, mountaineering, water sports. The Brahmagiri hill climb at Trimbakeshwar and the Narmada Parikrama at Omkareshwar may be classified as trekking or hiking, and the policy may not cover injuries sustained during these activities. Check the policy wording and consider purchasing an adventure activity rider if you plan to participate in these activities.

**Loss of cash and valuables.** Most policies exclude cash, credit cards, jewellery, electronics, and documents from the baggage coverage. If you are carrying expensive items — a laptop, a camera, expensive jewellery — consider a separate personal articles policy or keep them in your day bag rather than in checked luggage.

** cancellations due to known events.** If you cancel your trip due to a known event — a festival that is announced before you buy the policy, a weather forecast that predicts a storm — the policy may not cover the cancellation. Buy the policy as soon as you book your accommodation and transport, to maximise the coverage for unforeseen events.

## Making a Claim

If something goes wrong during your pilgrimage and you need to make a claim, the process is straightforward but requires documentation. The key steps are:

**Notify the insurer immediately.** Contact the insurance company's emergency helpline as soon as the incident occurs. The helpline number is on your insurance card and in the policy document. The insurer will provide guidance on the next steps and may arrange direct settlement with the hospital or the service provider, which eliminates the need for you to pay upfront and claim reimbursement later.

**Collect documentation.** For a medical claim, collect the hospital admission papers, the medical reports, the bills, and the discharge summary. For a baggage claim, collect the police report, the airline or transport operator's report, and a list of the lost items with their value. For a trip cancellation claim, collect the cancellation confirmation from the accommodation and transport providers, the refund policy documentation, and the receipts for the non-refundable expenses.

**Submit the claim form.** Complete the insurer's claim form and submit it with the supporting documentation within the time limit specified in the policy — typically thirty days from the date of the incident. The insurer will review the claim and process the settlement within the time limit specified in the policy — typically fifteen to thirty days for a straightforward claim.

## Insurance as Part of a Broader Risk Management Plan

Travel insurance is one component of a broader risk management plan for your pilgrimage, and it works most effectively when combined with the trust's support services and your own preparation. The risk management framework has three layers:

**Layer 1: Prevention.** The most effective risk management is prevention. The [What to Carry Pilgrimage Essentials](/blog/what-to-carry-pilgrimage-essentials) guide covers the practical preparation that reduces the risk of illness, injury, and loss — adequate clothing for the weather, sufficient water and food, comfortable footwear, a first-aid kit, and a plan for managing your health during the trip. The [First-Time Visit Checklist](/blog/first-time-visit-checklist) covers the pre-departure preparation that reduces the risk of logistical problems — accommodation booking, transport confirmation, and document preparation.

**Layer 2: Trust support services.** The trust at all four Sansthan locations provides a range of support services that reduce the impact of problems when they occur. The trust clinic at Shegaon provides basic medical care, the trust staff can arrange transport to local hospitals, the trust accommodation provides secure luggage storage, and the trust office can help with lost documents, transport delays, and other logistical issues. The [Handling Medical Emergencies on Yatra](/blog/handling-medical-emergencies-on-yatra) guide covers the trust's medical support services in detail.

**Layer 3: Insurance.** Insurance covers the financial impact of problems that occur despite your preparation and despite the trust's support. A medical emergency that requires private hospital treatment, a lost bag that contains valuables, a cancelled transport booking that requires last-minute alternative arrangements — these are the situations where insurance provides financial protection that the trust cannot provide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is travel insurance worth the cost for a five-day pilgrimage?**

Yes, for most devotees. The premium for a five-day domestic travel insurance policy is typically one to two hundred rupees per person, and the coverage provides financial protection for medical emergencies, trip cancellations, and baggage loss. The cost is low and the potential benefit is high, and the decision to buy insurance is almost always the right one.

**What should I look for in a pilgrimage travel insurance policy?**

Look for a policy that covers medical emergencies (including pre-existing conditions if you have them), trip cancellation and interruption, and baggage loss. Check the policy limits — the medical coverage should be at least one lakh rupees, the baggage coverage should be at least twenty-five thousand rupees, and the trip cancellation coverage should be at least the total cost of your pre-paid bookings. Check the exclusions carefully, particularly for pre-existing conditions, adventure activities, and cash loss.

**Does the trust provide any insurance for devotees staying at the dharamshala?**

No. The trust does not provide travel insurance for devotees, and the trust's liability for lost or damaged luggage at the storage facilities is limited. The trust provides secure storage and helpful staff, but the financial protection for lost or damaged items comes from your travel insurance policy, not from the trust. The [Luggage Storage and Lockers](/blog/luggage-storage-and-lockers) guide covers the trust's luggage storage procedures and the liability limitations.

**Can I buy travel insurance after I have already started my trip?**

Some insurers allow you to buy a policy after departure, but the coverage is typically limited and the premium is higher. The standard approach is to buy the policy before you leave home, at the same time that you book your accommodation and transport. This ensures that the policy covers the entire trip from the moment you leave home until you return.

**What should I do if I need medical treatment during the trip?**

Contact the insurance company's emergency helpline immediately. The helpline will provide guidance on the nearest network hospital, arrange for direct settlement if possible, and provide advice on the documentation you need for the claim. The [Handling Medical Emergencies on Yatra](/blog/handling-medical-emergencies-on-yatra) guide covers the hospital facilities and the medical emergency procedures at all four Sansthan locations.

**Does travel insurance cover the Ashadhi wari at Pandharpur?**

Yes, if the wari is the purpose of your trip and you have booked accommodation and transport in advance. The trip cancellation coverage will reimburse your non-refundable expenses if you have to cancel the wari due to illness or family emergency. The medical coverage will cover emergency treatment if you are injured or fall ill during the wari. The [Pandharpur Wari Accommodation Tips](/blog/pandharpur-wari-accommodation-tips) guide covers the wari logistics and the specific risks that apply during the festival.

# Shegaon To Ajmer Spiritual Route | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

A pilgrimage from Shegaon to Ajmer is not a simple point-to-point journey. It is a cross-country circuit that takes you from the Vidarbha heartland across Maharashtra and into Rajasthan, touching two very different spiritual traditions — the Bhakti sant tradition of Maharashtra and the Sufi and Rajput heritage of Rajasthan. If you see pilgrimage as a way of threading together the diverse threads of India's devotional culture, this route is one of the most rewarding you can plan. It stretches from the Samadhi Mandir of Shri Gajanan Maharaj in Shegaon to the Dargah Sharif of Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti in Ajmer, with the sacred Brahma Lake at Pushkar just 30 minutes away, and it can be comfortably completed in 7 to 10 days.

This guide covers transport for each leg, what to see in Ajmer and Pushkar, a sensible itinerary, accommodation options, and practical tips on food, weather, and safety.

## Leg 1: Shegaon to Nagpur

The first leg of the journey takes you from Shegaon to Nagpur, a distance of roughly 300km. By train, the Shegaon Junction station (SEG) on the Bhusawal–Nagpur line offers several options including the Sevagram Express and the Vidarbha Express, with a journey time of 4 to 5 hours. MSRTC buses also ply the route, taking approximately 5 to 6 hours depending on road conditions. For most pilgrims, the train is the more comfortable option, particularly if you are carrying luggage and traveling in a group. Book an AC 2-tier or 3-tier berth for the overnight slot between Shegaon and Nagpur if your schedule allows — it means you sleep through the journey and wake up in Nagpur ready to start the next leg.

Nagpur is the largest city in Vidarbha and the most practical transit hub for onward travel to Ajmer. It has an international airport with flights to Jaipur, Mumbai, Delhi, and Hyderabad, a major railway junction with trains connecting to every part of India, and good road connectivity via National Highway 53. For the Shegaon to Ajmer circuit, Nagpur serves as the primary pivot point. The [Shegaon Travel Guide](/blog/shegaon-travel-guide) covers the Shegaon to Nagpur connection in more detail, including train timings and MSRTC bus schedules.

## Leg 2: Nagpur to Ajmer

From Nagpur, you have two practical options for reaching Ajmer.

The first is the direct train. Several express trains connect Nagpur with Ajmer, including the Nagpur–Ajmer Express. The journey takes approximately 20 to 22 hours, running overnight so that you sleep through most of it. This is the most economical option and requires no onward connection. Book an AC 3-tier berth well in advance, particularly during the October-to-March peak travel season.

The second option is a combination of flight and road. Flights from Nagpur to Jaipur take roughly 2 hours, and from Jaipur airport Ajmer is a comfortable 135km drive of approximately 2.5 to 3 hours. This is the faster overall route — total travel time from Nagpur to Ajmer by air is around 5 hours including the Jaipur drive — and it is worth considering if you are on a tight schedule or traveling with elderly family members for whom a 22-hour train journey is not practical. IndiGo and Air India both operate daily flights on the Nagpur to Jaipur route.

If you are extending your yatra from Ajmer to other Jyotirlinga destinations, the [All 12 Jyotirlinga Planning guide](/blog/all-12-jyotirlinga-planning) maps out the complete national circuit and shows where Ajmer fits within it.

## Ajmer: The Dargah and the Old City

Ajmer is dominated by the Ajmer Sharif Dargah, the final resting place of Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, one of the most revered Sufi saints in the Indian subcontinent. The Dargah is open to visitors of all faiths throughout the day. The spiritual atmosphere inside — the soft recitation of qawwali, the scent of rose petals, the quiet murmur of thousands of devotees in prayer — is unlike anything else in Rajasthan. The experience is deeply moving regardless of your religious background.

The old city around the Dargah is a dense, atmospheric warren of narrow lanes, spice shops, and eateries serving traditional Rajasthani fare. Walk to the nearby Ana Sagar Lake, built in the 12th century by King Arnoraj, and take a sunset boat ride if the water level permits. Taragarh Fort, the "Star Fort" sitting on the Aravalli ridge, offers panoramic views of Ajmer, and the climb up to the fort gate takes about 45 minutes on foot from the Dargah.

For accommodation near the Dargah, several dharamshalas offer budget rooms within walking distance. Mid-range hotels are available on the main road leading to the Dargah, and the city has good properties in the Naka Madhi and Civil Lines areas. The [Multi-Location Pilgrimage Planning Guide](/blog/multi-location-pilgrimage-planning-guide) covers Ajmer accommodation in a broader yatra context.

## Pushkar: 30 Minutes from Ajmer

Pushkar is the natural companion to an Ajmer visit. It is just 30km from Ajmer city, approximately 30 to 40 minutes by car or shared auto, and it is one of the five sacred dhams in the Hindu tradition. Pushkar Lake, surrounded by 52 ghats, is the town's defining feature, and the Brahma Temple — one of the very few dedicated to Lord Brahma in India — draws devotees from across the country.

Pushkar is also one of the most atmospheric small towns in Rajasthan. The main market is packed with cafes, handicraft shops, and the famous rose gardens that produce the attar sold across India and internationally. Do not leave without trying the kulfi at one of the lakeside vendors — the malai kulfi at Pushkar is legendary and worth the detour on its own. The town is also home to the Pushkar Camel Fair every November, one of the largest livestock fairs in Asia, which transforms the town completely for a week.

For devotees planning the Shegaon–Ajmer circuit, Pushkar is best visited as a 1-day side trip from Ajmer, staying overnight in Ajmer and making the round trip to Pushkar in a single day. The drive is easy, the distance is short, and the town is compact enough to explore comfortably on foot.

## Suggested Itinerary: 7 to 10 Days

A 7-day Shegaon to Ajmer itinerary works well for pilgrims who want to move at a relaxed pace. Day 1 and 2: Shegaon — darshan at the Samadhi Mandir, visit to Anand Sagar, and a restful stay at Bhakta Niwas. Day 3: travel from Shegaon to Nagpur and board an overnight train or early morning flight to Jaipur. Day 4: arrive in Jaipur, drive to Ajmer, and settle into your accommodation. Day 5: Ajmer — full day at the Dargah Sharif, Ana Sagar Lake, and Taragarh Fort. Day 6: Pushkar day trip — Brahma Temple darshan, Pushkar Lake ghats, and the market. Day 7: return to Jaipur for onward travel, or extend to 8 to 10 days if you want to add Jodhpur, Udaipur, or the Mount Abu Jain temples to the circuit.

## Practical Tips: Food, Weather, and Safety

Ajmer's cuisine is a distinctive blend of Rajasthani and Mughlai influences. Look for korma, dal baati churma, and the city's own version of ghevar at the old-city eateries near the Dargah. Pushkar's vegetarian-only policy (the town does not serve meat or eggs) means you will eat exceptionally well if you are vegetarian; the thalis and the rooftop cafe food scene are genuinely good. Shegaon's Mahaprasad, mentioned in the [welcome guide](/blog/welcome-to-sansthan), is free and wholesome — a practical option on the first day of your journey when you are still settling in.

The Shegaon to Ajmer route crosses a significant climate gradient. Shegaon in July to September is hot and wet. Nagpur is similarly humid through the monsoon. Ajmer is arid year-round, with summer temperatures reaching 45 degrees Celsius between April and June. The best travel window is October to March, when all four cities are in their most comfortable weather. Carry cotton clothing, sunscreen, and a reusable water bottle for the road portions, and a light jacket for Ajmer's cool winter evenings.

Safety-wise, the Shegaon to Ajmer route is well-traveled and safe for families. The Ajmer Dargah has a dedicated volunteer volunteer group that assists visitors, including women traveling alone. Pushkar is safe and tourist-friendly. Standard precautions apply: keep valuables secure in crowded markets, use registered taxis for inter-city travel, and avoid isolated stretches of road after dark.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days should I allocate for the Shegaon to Ajmer yatra?**
A comfortable pace is 7 to 10 days, which allows 2 days in Shegaon, 2 to 3 days in Ajmer, and 1 day for Pushkar plus travel days between cities. If you want to add other destinations — Jodhpur, Udaipur, or Omkareshwar on the way back — extend to 12 to 14 days.

**What is the best season to travel this route?**
October to March is ideal, when the weather is pleasant across Shegaon, Nagpur, and Ajmer. Avoid the peak summer months of April to June, when Ajmer temperatures can exceed 45 degrees Celsius, and the monsoon peak of July to August, when heavy rainfall can disrupt train and road travel between cities.

**Is Ajmer safe for solo female travelers?**
Ajmer is generally safe for solo female travelers, particularly around the Dargah area and the main market. The volunteer volunteer group at the Dargah assists women pilgrims actively. Pushkar is also very tourist-friendly. Standard precautions apply: stay in well-rated accommodation, avoid walking alone after dark in unfamiliar areas, and use app-based taxis rather than hailing random autos.

**Can I do the Shegaon to Ajmer circuit on a budget?**
Yes. The MSRTC bus network connects Shegaon with Nagpur at low cost, and the Nagpur to Ajmer train in 3-tier AC is economical. Dharamshala accommodation in Ajmer near the Dargah can cost as little as 300 to 500 rupees per night. Pushkar has budget guesthouses starting from 400 rupees. The total 7-day circuit can be done comfortably for under 8,000 rupees per person excluding train and flight fares.

**Is the Ajmer Dargah open to non-Muslim visitors?**
Yes, the Ajmer Sharif Dargah is open to visitors of all faiths. You will be asked to cover your head and remove your shoes before entering the inner sanctum, which is standard protocol for the Dargah regardless of your religion. The experience is widely described as deeply moving by visitors of every background.

The Shegaon to Ajmer route is a reminder that India's spiritual landscape is not organized by single tradition. You can stand in the quiet of the Samadhi Mandir in Shegaon and, a week later, stand in the candle-lit courtyard of the Ajmer Dargah, and both experiences leave their mark in the same part of you.

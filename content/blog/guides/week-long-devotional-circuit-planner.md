# Week Long Devotional Circuit Planner | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

A week-long devotional circuit across the Sansthan network is one of the most fulfilling trips a devotee can undertake. You begin at Shegaon with the intimate presence of Shri Gajanan Maharaj's Samadhi, move to Omkareshwar for the ancient power of a Narmada Jyotirlinga, continue to Pandharpur for the living tradition of the wari and the Bhima river, and end at Trimbakeshwar for the Godavari's origin and one of the most revered Shiva temples in India. The circuit covers approximately 1,790 km of road, requires 7 days and 6 nights, and creates a spiritual journey that touches every dimension of Maharashtra's devotional heritage.

This planner breaks down each day with specific timing, transport details, darshan recommendations, and accommodation advice. It is designed to be used alongside the [complete booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) and the [transport guide](/blog/complete-transport-guide-for-devotees). If you are travelling with family, also review the [budget planning guide](/blog/pilgrimage-budget-planning-for-families).

## Pre-Trip Checklist

Before you begin the circuit, confirm the following:

- Book accommodation at Shegaon, Omkareshwar, Pandharpur, and Trimbakeshwar. Each location requires a separate booking. Use the phone/WhatsApp numbers from the [booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide).
- Book overnight buses or trains from your starting city to Shegaon, and a return ticket from Trimbakeshwar (Nashik) or Shegaon back home.
- Confirm vehicle arrangements if you are driving. Arrange a driver if you prefer not to drive the full circuit yourself.
- Check the festival calendar. Avoid visiting Pandharpur during Ashadhi Ekadashi or Shegaon during Pragat Din unless you specifically want to experience those massive crowds.
- Carry a small first-aid kit, sufficient cash (INR 15,000-20,000 per person), your ID documents, and a refillable water bottle.

## Day-by-Day Itinerary

### Day 1: Arrival at Shegaon

**Route:** Mumbai/Nagpur to Shegaon (230 km from Nagpur, 550 km from Mumbai).
**Travel time:** 3.5 hours from Nagpur by car, 10-12 hours from Mumbai by overnight bus or train.
**Accommodation:** Bhakta Niwas Shegaon (2 nights).

Arrive in Shegaon by mid-morning. Check in at Bhakta Niwas and freshen up. Head to the Samadhi Mandir for the 8:00 AM or midday darshan. After darshan, spend the afternoon exploring the Anandanand garden and the temple museum. Evening aarti at 6:00 PM or 9:00 PM. Dinner at the Mahaprasad canteen.

If you arrive in the afternoon (from Nagpur by car), you can still attend the 6:00 PM evening aarti. This gives you a gentle first day without the demands of the 4:00 AM Mangala Aarti. Save the pre-dawn aarti for Day 2 when you are fully rested.

### Day 2: Shegaon (Full Day)

**Accommodation:** Bhakta Niwas Shegaon (second night).

Wake up at 3:30 AM and join the queue for the Mangala Aarti at 4:00 AM. This is the most spiritually significant moment at Shegaon, and many devotees plan their entire trip around it. The queue forms from 2:30 AM on regular days and from midnight on festival days. Dress warmly — the pre-dawn air in Buldhana district is cool even in summer.

After the aarti, return to Bhakta Niwas for breakfast and rest until 9:00 AM. Use the morning to visit the local market for prasad, photographs, and small souvenirs. The market near the temple has shops selling brass lamps, religious books, photographs of Shri Gajanan Maharaj, and locally made sweets.

Lunch at the Mahaprasad canteen. Afternoon rest from 1:30 PM to 4:30 PM. Return to the temple for a second darshan at 6:00 PM. Evening aarti at 9:00 PM. This is your last night at Shegaon — take time to absorb the atmosphere and say your farewells.

### Day 3: Shegaon to Omkareshwar

**Route:** Shegaon to Omkareshwar via Khamgaon, Patur, Burai, Khandwa (340 km, 6 hours by road).
**Accommodation:** Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas (1 night).

Depart Shegaon by 7:00 AM. The route passes through the Vidarbha countryside, which is green and peaceful during the post-monsoon months. Stop at Khandwa for lunch around 12:30 PM. Khandwa has several decent vegetarian restaurants on the main highway. Continue to Omkareshwar, arriving by 1:30 PM.

Cross to the Mandhata island via the bridge or cable car. Check in at Omkareshwar Bhakta Niwas. Darshan at the Amaleshwar Jyotirlinga in the afternoon. Walk the island circumference, visit the smaller shrines, and sit on the Narmada ghats for quiet reflection.

Evening: Narmada aarti at sunset on the river ghats. This is the highlight of the Omkareshwar stop. The aarti is performed with hundreds of lamps, and the reflections in the still Narmada water create an extraordinary visual. Dinner near the bridge.

### Day 4: Omkareshwar to Pandharpur

**Route:** Omkareshwar to Pandharpur via Burhanpur, Jalgaon, Aurangabad, Solapur (550 km, 8-9 hours by road).
**Accommodation:** Pandharpur Bhakta Niwas or private hotel (1 night).

This is the longest driving day of the circuit. Depart Omkareshwar by 7:00 AM. The route goes south through Burhanpur (famous for its historic Shahi Qila and Ahir Rulers' tombs), then east through Jalgaon and Aurangabad. Stop for lunch at Aurangabad, which has excellent vegetarian restaurants. Continue to Solapur and then to Pandharpur, arriving by 4:00 PM.

If the 550 km drive in one day feels too long, consider an overnight stop at Solapur (200 km from Omkareshwar). This breaks the journey into two comfortable days: Omkareshwar to Solapur (5 hours), rest overnight, then Solapur to Pandharpur (3 hours). The extra night costs INR 500-1,500 for accommodation.

Arrive in Pandharpur by 4:00 PM. Check in at Bhakta Niwas. Visit the Vitthal temple for evening darshan and aarti. The Bhima river ghats are beautiful at sunset. Dinner at a local restaurant or the Anna Kshetra community kitchen if it is operational.

### Day 5: Pandharpur

**Accommodation:** Pandharpur Bhakta Niwas (second night).

Wake up early for the pre-dawn darshan at the Vitthal temple. The temple opens at 5:00 AM, and the early morning atmosphere with the Bhima river flowing beside it is unforgettable. Spend the morning walking along the river ghats and visiting the Vishnupad, where it is believed Lord Vitthal left his footprints.

If you are visiting during the Kartik Ekadashi period (October-November), the Pandharpur temple sees a massive influx of devotees. The Kartik Ekadashi fair lasts for several days, and the town is decorated with lights and flowers. This is the second-most important pilgrimage event in the Pandharpur calendar after Ashadhi Ekadashi. If you want to experience it, plan your trip around this date. The [Kartik Ekadashi guide](/blog/kartik-ekadashi-pandharpur-darshan-guide) covers the full schedule.

Spend the afternoon resting at your accommodation. Pandharpur can be hot during the day, and the temple town atmosphere is best experienced in the cooler morning and evening hours.

Evening: Second darshan at the Vitthal temple, followed by dinner at a local restaurant.

### Day 6: Pandharpur to Trimbakeshwar

**Route:** Pandharpur to Trimbakeshwar via Solapur, Barshi, Pune, Nashik (420 km, 7 hours by road).
**Accommodation:** Trimbakeshwar Bhakta Niwas (1 night).

Depart Pandharpur by 7:00 AM. The route goes northeast through Solapur, Barshi, and then toward Pune. From Pune, take the Nashik road (approximately 200 km, 4 hours). Stop at a restaurant near Pune for lunch. Continue to Nashik and then to Trimbakeshwar (30 km from Nashik), arriving by 3:00 PM.

Check in at Trimbakeshwar Bhakta Niwas. The rooms are basic but comfortable, and the location is convenient — within 1 km of the Jyotirlinga temple. Freshen up and visit the temple for evening darshan. The Trimbakeshwar temple is smaller than Shegaon and Omkareshwar, and the queue is typically shorter. The evening aarti at Trimbakeshwar is accompanied by the sound of the Sahyadri wind through the temple complex, creating a deeply atmospheric experience.

Dinner at the trust canteen or at a restaurant in the Trimbakeshwar market area.

### Day 7: Trimbakeshwar to Departure

**Accommodation:** Trimbakeshwar Bhakta Niwas (second night).

Wake up at 5:30 AM for the pre-dawn darshan at the Jyotirlinga. The early morning mist over the Brahmagiri hills makes this one of the most visually stunning darshans of the entire circuit. After darshan, return to Bhakta Niwas for breakfast.

If you have energy and time, visit the Kushavarat kund, where many devotees perform ritual baths. The kund is the source of the Godavari river and is one of the most sacred water bodies in India. Alternatively, if you are physically able, consider the Brahmagiri mountain trek (17 km round trip from the temple) to the river's origin point. This is a moderate trek that takes about 4-5 hours and is best done in the cool morning hours.

Alternatively, for a less strenuous option, visit the Anjaneri waterfall about 15 km from Trimbakeshwar. The waterfall is most impressive during and after the monsoon season (July to October). The trail to the waterfall takes about 45 minutes of walking from the nearest road point.

Check out by 10:00 AM. Begin the return journey to Nagpur (260 km, 4.5 hours) or Nashik (30 km) for your flight or train back to Mumbai. The Nashik airport has regular flights to Mumbai, and the journey from Trimbakeshwar to Nashik airport takes about 45 minutes.

## Booking Accommodation for the Full Circuit

The key to a smooth week-long circuit is booking all four locations in advance. Here is a recommended timeline:

- **6-8 weeks before:** Book Omkareshwar (limited rooms, high demand) and Trimbakeshwar (especially if visiting during Shravan).
- **4-6 weeks before:** Book Pandharpur (especially if visiting during Ekadashi festivals).
- **2-4 weeks before:** Book Shegaon (most rooms available, but book early during Pragat Din).

Use the [complete booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-complete-booking-guide) for contact details and message templates. If you are booking from abroad, see the [NRI booking guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-for-nris) for time zone considerations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I visit the locations in this specific order?** This order (Shegaon → Omkareshwar → Pandharpur → Trimbakeshwar) minimises backtracking and is geographically logical. However, you can reverse the order or start from a different point depending on your starting city. The [location selection guide](/blog/sansthan-location-selection-guide) helps you choose the best entry point.

**Can I skip one location to save time and money?** Yes. A 5-day circuit covering Shegaon, Omkareshwar, and Trimbakeshwar is a perfectly meaningful pilgrimage. Pandharpur is the most logistically challenging stop due to its distance from the other three locations. If time is limited, consider dropping Pandharpur and spending an extra day each at Shegaon and Trimbakeshwar.

**What if I fall behind schedule?** The itinerary has built-in flexibility. Each day is designed to work even if you start an hour or two late. The main constraint is the check-in times at each Bhakta Niwas, which typically operate from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM. If you are running behind, call ahead to let the accommodation desk know your estimated arrival time.

**Is the circuit suitable for elderly devotees?** The circuit is demanding due to the long driving days. For elderly devotees, consider visiting only two locations with more rest time, or hiring a private car with a driver who handles all the driving. The [senior citizen special needs guide](/blog/senior-citizen-special-needs-guide) has additional advice.

**What should I do if a temple is closed for a private function?** Major temples occasionally close for private functions or special pujas. If the temple you planned to visit is closed, the Sansthan office at Shegaon can usually provide information about alternative temples or adjusted darshan timings. Check the notice board at Bhakta Niwas each morning for any schedule changes.

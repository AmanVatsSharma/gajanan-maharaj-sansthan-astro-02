# Senior Devotee Multi-City Itinerary | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

A devotional yatra for senior devotees requires a fundamentally different approach than a young traveller's itinerary. The pace is gentler, the resting periods are longer, the accommodation must be accessible (ground floor or with elevator access), the food must suit specific dietary needs, and the medical preparedness is essential. This 10-day itinerary covers all four Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan locations — Shegaon, Omkareshwar, Pandharpur, and Trimbakeshwar — with built-in rest days, accessible accommodation options, comfortable transport arrangements, and minimal driving hours per day. It is designed for devotees aged 60 and above, those with mobility considerations, or those travelling with senior family members who need a comfortable, restorative yatra experience rather than a rushed, demanding one. The total road distance for the full circuit is approximately 1,500 km, but the schedule distributes the driving over 5-6 driving days with 2-hour maximum driving blocks per day.

## Pre-Trip Preparation

Before you start the yatra, schedule a check-up with your physician and get a fitness clearance for travel. Discuss the destinations (some locations are at altitude near the Brahmagiri hills at Trimbakeshwar, others are at low altitude near the Narmada at Omkareshwar), the climate conditions during your travel dates, and any medications you are currently taking. Carry a full supply of all regular medications for the entire trip, plus 4-5 days of extra supply in case of travel delays. Store medications in your carry-on bag, not in checked luggage. Pack a small medical kit with band-aids, antiseptic, pain relievers, antacids, ORS packets, and any specific items your doctor recommends.

For the transport, choose a sedan or SUV rather than a smaller hatchback. The road conditions across the circuit include some rough stretches, and a sedan with good ground clearance is more comfortable for senior passengers. The [complete transport guide](/blog/complete-transport-guide-for-devotees) recommends Tata Tigor, Maruti Dzire, or Toyota Etios for sedan comfort, and Maruti Ertiga or Toyota Innova for SUV comfort with three senior passengers.

For accommodation, request ground-floor rooms at Bhakta Niwas, with attached bathrooms and walk-in showers (not tubs). The Sansthan office is responsive to these requests, and ground-floor rooms are typically allocated to senior devotees first. Request these accommodations at the time of booking, not at check-in. The [family accommodation checklist guide](/blog/shegaon-family-accommodation-checklist) covers the specifics of family room booking.

## Day 1: Mumbai to Shegaon (Arrival Evening)

Take the 12139 Sewagram Express from Mumbai CST at 8:55 PM, arriving Shegaon at 8:00 AM the next morning. Alternatively, take a flight to Nagpur (3-4 hours by air, multiple daily flights) and then a private taxi from Nagpur to Shegaon (4-5 hours by car). For senior devotees who find overnight train travel uncomfortable, the Nagpur flight + taxi option is preferable — it avoids the night journey and the senior passenger arrives refreshed at Shegaon.

If travelling by train, book a Lower Berth in Sleeper class or a 2-tier AC berth. The 12139 Sewagram Express has Lower Berth allocations for senior citizens in the quota system, and you should request this at the time of booking on IRCTC.

## Day 2: Shegaon Darshan and Rest

Arrive at Bhakta Niwas at 8:00 AM. Request early check-in, which is typically granted for senior devotees during regular seasons. Freshen up and have breakfast at the Mahaprasad canteen (poha, upma, chai, INR 40-60).

At 10:30 AM, take a slow walk to the Samadhi Mandir. The walk is approximately 400 meters from Bhakta Niwas, with a shaded pathway and benches along the way. The 10:30 AM darshan slot has a short queue (10-15 minutes), and the temple atmosphere is peaceful. The temple is accessible to senior devotees, with ramps at the main entrance, a dedicated senior darshan queue on the right side of the temple, and temple staff who assist with wheelchairs if needed.

After darshan, return to Bhakta Niwas for lunch and a full afternoon rest. The afternoon heat (especially in summer) is the most challenging time for senior devotees, and rest is essential. The Bhakta Niwas rooms are air-conditioned and quiet, and the rest period is the most restorative part of the day.

At 5:00 PM, take the auto-rickshaw to the temple for the evening Mangala Aarti. The evening aarti is the most beautiful darshan of the day, and the queue is shorter than the morning slot. The 5:00 PM slot typically has a 20-minute wait.

After darshan, walk slowly through the Anand Sagar entertainment complex for the 7:00 PM musical fountain show. Dinner at Guruprasad restaurant or the Bhakta Niwas canteen, and a quiet evening in the room.

## Day 3: Shegaon to Omkareshwar (Half-Day Drive)

This is the longest driving day of the circuit, and the schedule includes a rest stop every 2 hours. The route is approximately 340 km and 6-7 hours of driving, with breaks. Depart Shegaon at 7:00 AM, break at Khamgaon (40 km, 1 hour) for chai and restroom, break at Mehkar (90 km, 1.5 hours from Shegaon) for 30 minutes, and break at Khandwa (180 km, 3 hours from Shegaon) for lunch (45 minutes).

Arrive at Omkareshwar at 2:30-3:00 PM. Park the car on the mainland near the bridge. Check into Bhakta Niwas Omkareshwar or a mainland hotel. Rest for 1-2 hours in the room.

At 5:30 PM, take the bridge to the Mandhata island for the Narmada aarti at 6:00 PM. The walk from the bridge to the aarti ghat is approximately 200 meters on level ground, and the aarti is on the river ghats with steps leading down to the water. Senior devotees who cannot manage the steps can watch the aarti from the upper ghats, which still provide a beautiful view of the river and the lamps.

After the aarti, return to Bhakta Niwas for dinner and rest.

## Day 4: Omkareshwar — Full Day

This is a rest and darshan day at Omkareshwar. Pre-dawn darshan is optional (the 5:00 AM aarti is for devotees who are comfortable with the early morning schedule), but the morning darshan at 8:00 AM is the recommended senior slot. The morning queue is short, the temple is cool, and the staff is responsive to senior requests.

After darshan, have breakfast at the Bhakta Niwas canteen. Spend the morning walking slowly around the Mandhata island, visiting the Mamaleshwar temple (which is on the same island, 1 km walk from the main temple), and resting at the ghats.

The afternoon is a full rest period. Senior devotees who find the afternoon heat challenging should rest in the air-conditioned room.

At 5:30 PM, attend the evening Narmada aarti again if you wish, or simply rest in the room. Dinner at Bhakta Niwas canteen and an early night.

## Day 5: Omkareshwar to Pandharpur (Half-Day Drive with Buffer)

This is the longest stretch of the circuit, approximately 480 km and 9-10 hours of driving with breaks. For senior devotees, this drive should be broken into two segments with an overnight stop. The recommended overnight stop is at Akola or Washim (180 km from Omkareshwar, 4 hours), where comfortable hotels are available. This approach adds one day to the circuit but significantly reduces driving pressure.

If you prefer the single-day drive, depart Omkareshwar at 5:00 AM and break every 2 hours. The route goes through Burhanpur (90 km from Omkareshwar, 1.5 hours), Akola (200 km from Omkareshwar, 4 hours), and then the final stretch to Pandharpur (480 km total, 9 hours).

Arrive at Pandharpur by 2:00-3:00 PM. Check into Bhakta Niwas Pandharpur and rest for 2 hours. Take a slow walk to the Vitthal temple for evening darshan. The Pandharpur Bhakta Niwas is approximately 500 meters from the Vitthal temple, and the walk is on a flat, paved path.

## Day 6: Pandharpur — Full Day

This is the most relaxed day of the circuit. Morning darshan at the Vitthal temple at 8:00 AM, breakfast at Bhakta Niwas, and a slow walk along the Chandrabhaga river ghats. The Pundalik samadhi, the Rukmini temple, and the local dharamshalas are all within walking distance.

Afternoon rest at Bhakta Niwas. Evening darshan at 6:00 PM, dinner, and an early night.

## Day 7: Pandharpur to Trimbakeshwar

Pandharpur to Trimbakeshwar is approximately 600 km and 11-12 hours of driving with breaks. This is too long for a single day for senior devotees. The recommended approach is a two-day segment with an overnight stop at Nashik (300 km from Pandharpur, 6 hours).

Depart Pandharpur at 5:00 AM, break every 2 hours, arrive Nashik by 1:00 PM. Check into a comfortable Nashik hotel (good options include Hotel Panchavati Yatri, Hotel Vaishali, or similar mid-range hotels). Afternoon rest. Optional visit to the Nashik temples (Trimbakeshwar is 30 km from Nashik, but you can also visit the Sita Gufa and Ramkund in Nashik itself).

## Day 8: Nashik to Trimbakeshwar

Take a relaxed morning. Breakfast at the hotel, and depart for Trimbakeshwar at 10:00 AM. The drive is 30 km and 45 minutes. Check into Bhakta Niwas Trimbakeshwar or a local hotel, and have lunch at the canteen.

Afternoon darshan at the Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga. The temple has a senior-friendly queue with priority access for senior citizens, and the darshan typically takes 20-30 minutes. After darshan, a slow walk around the temple complex.

The Gangadwar waterfall is a 30-minute walk uphill from the temple, and it is recommended for senior devotees only if they are comfortable with the climb. The view from the base of the waterfall is beautiful without the climb.

Evening rest at Bhakta Niwas, dinner at the canteen, and an early night.

## Day 9: Trimbakeshwar to Mumbai

Depart Trimbakeshwar at 7:00 AM, drive to Nashik (45 minutes), and then continue to Mumbai (180 km, 4 hours). Break every 2 hours. Arrive Mumbai by 2:00 PM.

## Day 10: Mumbai Rest and Recovery

This is a full rest day after the yatra. Rest at home, eat light meals, hydrate well, and recover from the journey.

## Medical Considerations

Senior devotees should keep the following medical items in their carry-on: full medication supply for 15 days, blood pressure monitor (portable), glucose meter (for diabetic devotees), pulse oximeter, hand sanitizer, masks, a small first-aid kit, and any specific items your doctor recommends. Keep a list of emergency contacts in your wallet: family members, your physician, and the Sansthan contact at each location. The [medical and emergency guide](/blog/shegaon-medical-and-emergency-guide) covers the medical resources available at Shegaon specifically.

The Sansthan office is responsive to medical requests. If a senior devotee is feeling unwell, the Sansthan can arrange a doctor visit at Bhakta Niwas, recommend the nearest hospital, and assist with any urgent arrangements. Do not hesitate to contact them for any health-related concern.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can senior devotees do the full circuit?** Yes, with the 10-day slow-paced itinerary described here. The single-day drives are broken into two-day segments where appropriate, and rest days are built in at each location.

**What is the best season for senior devotees?** October to February is ideal. The temperatures are mild (15-30 degrees), the humidity is low, and the air quality is reasonable. Avoid the May-June heat and the July-August monsoon.

**Are the Bhakta Niwas rooms accessible for senior devotees?** Yes. Bhakta Niwas rooms have ground-floor accessibility, attached bathrooms, and walk-in showers. Request ground-floor rooms at the time of booking.

**Can I bring a wheelchair?** Yes. The temples have wheelchair-accessible ramps, and the Bhakta Niwas campuses are wheelchair-friendly. The Anand Sagar complex at Shegaon is also accessible.

**What about oxygen or medical equipment?** Most senior devotees do not need supplemental oxygen. If you use CPAP or other medical equipment, ensure you have backup batteries for the trip.

**Can I travel with a caregiver?** Yes. The Sansthan allows caregivers to stay in the same room at no additional cost. Request a twin-bed or double-bed room at the time of booking.

The daily darshan schedule at Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga is one of the most important pieces of practical information for any pilgrim planning a visit. The temple follows a well-established routine that has been in place for centuries, with specific timings for morning darshan, midday rest, evening darshan, and the famous Narmada aarti on the ghat. Understanding these timings helps you plan your day around the most spiritually meaningful hours, avoid the midday closure, and arrive in time for the aarti that matters most to you. This guide covers the complete daily schedule, the significance of each aarti, the best times for darshan, and the festival schedule that extends the temple hours during special occasions.

## Daily Temple Schedule

The Omkareshwar temple opens its doors at approximately 5:00 AM for the morning darshan. This early opening allows devotees who wish to attend the Mangal Aarti to arrive in time. The temple remains open throughout the morning, with a steady flow of devotees coming for darshan and puja. The morning darshan period is considered the most auspicious time for temple visits, as the atmosphere is calm, the crowds are thinner, and the priests perform the first puja of the day with particular devotion.

The temple closes for a brief rest period around 12:30 PM and reopens at 4:00 PM. During this rest period, the priests perform the Madhyahna Puja and other rituals that are not open to devotees. The temple complex and the parikrama path remain accessible during the rest period, so devotees can continue exploring the island, visiting the Mamleshwar temple on the mainland, or resting at their accommodation.

The evening darshan period begins at 4:00 PM and continues until approximately 9:30 PM. The evening darshan is popular with devotees who are on day trips from nearby towns, and the temple sees a steady stream of visitors throughout the evening. The famous Narmada aarti takes place around sunset on the ghat across from the temple, and this aarti is one of the most visually and spiritually striking experiences at Omkareshwar.

The temple closes at approximately 9:30 PM after the final Shayan Aarti, which marks the symbolic rest of the deity. After the final aarti, the temple doors close for the night, and devotees are requested to leave the temple premises.

## Mangal Aarti at Sunrise

The Mangal Aarti, performed around 5:30 AM, is the most sacred and intimate aarti at Omkareshwar. The temple is bathed in the soft light of oil lamps, and the priests chant the Rudra mantras with great devotion. The Mangal Aarti is performed to awaken the deity and mark the beginning of a new day, and devotees who attend describe it as a deeply personal and transformative experience.

The queue for Mangal Aarti darshan is typically shorter than during peak hours, as only a limited number of devotees can fit inside the temple during the aarti. Devotees who wish to attend the Mangal Aarti should arrive at the temple by 5:00 AM to secure a place in the queue. The aarti lasts approximately thirty minutes, and it is followed by a brief period for individual darshan before the temple opens fully for the morning crowd.

## Madhyahna Puja and Midday Rest

The Madhyahna Puja is performed around 12:30 PM, and it is followed by a rest period during which the main temple is closed to devotees. The Madhyahna Puja is an important ritual in the daily schedule of the temple, and it involves offerings to the deity that are not open to public viewing. During the rest period, devotees can visit the Mamleshwar temple on the mainland, which does not observe a midday closure, or they can return to their accommodation for rest and lunch.

The midday rest period is also a good time to visit the local markets near the ghat, where vendors sell prasad, flowers, and souvenirs. The market is busiest during the morning and evening darshan periods, and it is relatively quiet during the midday rest, making it a good time for a relaxed shopping experience.

## Evening Darshan and Narmada Aarti

The evening darshan period at Omkareshwar is one of the most popular times for temple visits. The temple reopens at 4:00 PM, and the evening crowd begins to build from around 5:00 PM. The evening darshan is particularly popular with devotees who are on day trips from Indore, Khandwa, and other nearby towns, as it allows them to visit the temple after completing their daytime activities.

The Narmada aarti takes place around sunset on the ghat across from the temple. This aarti is performed by priests on the ghat steps, with the Narmada river as the backdrop and the Omkareshwar temple visible across the water. The aarti involves the singing of devotional songs, the waving of lamps, and the offering of flowers to the river. The Narmada aarti is free to attend, and it is open to all devotees regardless of whether they have visited the main temple. Many devotees describe the Narmada aarti as the most memorable part of their Omkareshwar visit, and it is highly recommended for first-time visitors.

## Shayan Aarti and Closing Time

The Shayan Aarti is the final aarti of the day, performed around 9:00 PM before the temple closes at 9:30 PM. The Shayan Aarti marks the symbolic rest of the deity, and it is performed with great reverence by the temple priests. The aarti is shorter than the morning and evening aartis, and it is attended by a smaller number of devotees. After the Shayan Aarti, the temple doors close for the night, and devotees are requested to leave the premises.

## Festival Timings and Extended Hours

During major festivals at Omkareshwar, the temple timings are extended, and the temple remains open for longer periods. During Mahashivratri, which is the most significant festival, the temple remains open throughout the night with continuous aarti and puja. The temple sees large crowds during Mahashivratri, and devotees are advised to arrive early to secure a place in the queue. During Karthik Purnima, special aartis are scheduled in the evening, and the Narmada aarti is particularly grand. During Sawan Mondays, the temple opens earlier and closes later to accommodate the increased number of devotees.

## Best Times for Darshan

The best time for darshan at Omkareshwar depends on your preferences and your schedule. For a peaceful and uncrowded darshan experience, the morning hours between 5:00 AM and 8:00 AM are ideal. The crowds are thinner, the atmosphere is calm, and the priests have more time for individual interactions. For devotees who want to experience the full devotional atmosphere of the temple, the evening darshan period between 4:00 PM and 7:00 PM is ideal. For devotees who want to attend the Narmada aarti, arriving at the ghat by 6:30 PM during summer or 5:30 PM during winter will give you a good spot.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Mangal Aarti time at Omkareshwar? The Mangal Aarti takes place around 5:30 AM. Devotees should arrive by 5:00 AM to secure a place in the queue.

Is the temple closed in the afternoon? Yes, the temple closes for a brief rest period around 12:30 PM and reopens at 4:00 PM. The island parikrama path remains open during this period.

What time is the Narmada aarti? The Narmada aarti takes place around sunset, typically between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM depending on the season.

Are the timings the same during festivals? No, festival timings vary. During Mahashivratri, the temple remains open throughout the night. During Karthik Purnima, special aartis are scheduled in the evening.

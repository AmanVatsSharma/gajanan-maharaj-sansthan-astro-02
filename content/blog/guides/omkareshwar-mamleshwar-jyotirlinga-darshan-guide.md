# Omkareshwar Mamleshwar: Jyotirlinga Darshan Guide

Omkareshwar is the eighth of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the most visually distinctive — the temple sits on an island shaped like the Hindu syllable Om, formed by the Narmada River as it splits into two channels before rejoining. The island temple is called Omkareshwar. The Mamleshwar temple is on the mainland, immediately opposite. Both are equally revered and share the same Jyotirlinga tradition. This guide explains the two-temple structure, how to reach Omkareshwar, what to expect at darshan, and how to combine an Omkareshwar visit with Shegaon.

## The two temples: Omkareshwar and Mamleshwar

The confusion most first-time visitors have is the relationship between Omkareshwar and Mamleshwar. The two temples are part of the same pilgrimage tradition, but they are distinct structures on opposite sides of the river:

**Omkareshwar temple** is the island temple, accessible only by bridge or ferry. It is the more famous of the two and the one most photographs depict. It houses the Jyotirlinga in its primary sanctum and is the site of the main Mahashivaratri celebrations. The walk around the island — the parikrama — is an important ritual for devotees.

**Mamleshwar temple** is on the mainland, immediately adjacent to the bridge that leads to the island. It is older than the Omkareshwar temple and is believed by many devotees to be the original site of the Jyotirlinga manifestation. Both temples are managed by different trusts, and both are visited by most pilgrims who come to Omkareshwar.

The two-temple structure means that a complete darshan visit typically includes both. The order is up to you: many devotees do Mamleshwar first (mainland) and then walk to the bridge and cross to Omkareshwar (island). Others do it in reverse. The parikrama of the island is the concluding ritual for most pilgrims.

## The Narmada and the Om-shaped island

The Narmada River is one of the five sacred rivers of India and the only one that flows eastward (most major rivers flow west). At Omkareshwar, the river splits into two channels around the island, and the combined shape of the island is a near-perfect representation of the Om symbol. The geography of the place is considered the reason for its Jyotirlinga status — the river, the shape, and the ancient tradition of Shiva worship at this specific location all combine.

The parikrama (circumambulation) of the island is a 3 to 4 km walk along the riverbank path. It is a quiet, meditative walk that many devotees do barefoot. The path passes several smaller shrines, ghats where pilgrims take a dip in the Narmada, and resting spots. The walk takes 90 minutes to 2 hours depending on your pace.

## Darshan timings

The Omkareshwar temple on the island is open approximately:

- **Morning darshan:** 5 am to 12 pm
- **Evening darshan:** 4 pm to 9 pm

The Mamleshwar temple on the mainland has similar hours. During Mahashivaratri and other major festivals, the temples remain open through the night.

## How to reach Omkareshwar

**From Indore:** 77 km by road, approximately 2 hours. This is the most common route. Indore has an airport and regular train and bus connections from Mumbai, Delhi, and other major cities. From Indore, hire a taxi or take a bus to Omkareshwar.

**From Khandwa:** 60 km by road, approximately 1.5 hours. Khandwa is on the Mumbai–Allahabad railway line and has regular train services. From Khandwa, local buses and taxis are available.

**From Shegaon:** The Shegaon to Omkareshwar route is about 200 km and takes 4 to 5 hours by road. It is most practical as part of a multi-day pilgrimage circuit — see the [12 Jyotirlinga planning guide](/all-12-jyotirlinga-planning) and our [Shegaon to Pandharpur route guide](/shegaon-to-pandharpur-route-guide) for how to combine visits.

**By air:** The nearest airport is Indore (IDR). From Indore, a taxi to Omkareshwar takes about 2 hours.

## Accommodation at Omkareshwar

The MP Tourism Development Corporation operates hotels and guesthouses at Omkareshwar, including the Narmada Retreat and several smaller lodges near the temple. Prices are moderate. For a more devotional stay, the Mamleshwar and Omkareshwar temple trusts offer basic rooms for pilgrims — enquire at the temple offices.

Book accommodation in advance during Mahashivaratri. The town swells with visitors, and rooms are typically sold out weeks before the festival.

## What to do during your visit

1. **Darshan at Mamleshwar** — arrive early (before 8 am) for the shortest queue.
2. **Cross to Omkareshwar** — the bridge is a short walk from Mamleshwar. Alternatively, take the government ferry across the Narmada.
3. **Parikrama of the island** — walk the 3–4 km path around Omkareshwar island. Stop at the ghats for a dip in the Narmada.
4. **Visit the other island shrines** — the island has several smaller temples dedicated to Annapoorna, Gupteshwar, and Siddheshwar.
5. **Return to Mamleshwar** — complete the darshan at Mamleshwar before departing.
6. **Evening aarti** — if timing allows, stay for the evening aarti at either temple.

## Combining with Shegaon

Many pilgrims visiting the Vidarbha region combine Shegaon (Gajanan Maharaj) with Omkareshwar (Jyotirlinga) in a single trip. The practical route is:

- Shegaon → Akola (75 km, 1.5 hours) → Khandwa (130 km, 3 hours) → Omkareshwar (60 km, 1.5 hours)
- Or: Shegaon → Nagpur (110 km) → Indore (by train or flight, ~6–8 hours) → Omkareshwar (77 km, 2 hours)

The combined visit is best planned as a 4 to 5 day trip. Our [Shegaon travel guide](/shegaon-travel-guide) has accommodation options at Shegaon, and the [Pandharpur route guide](/shegaon-to-pandharpur-route-guide) covers the Shegaon-Pandharpur-Omkareshwar triangular route.

## Mahashivaratri at Omkareshwar

Mahashivaratri is the biggest festival at Omkareshwar. The temple remains open through the night, and the Jyotirlinga abhishek continues without interruption. The island fills with hundreds of thousands of devotees during the 24-hour festival. The Mamleshwar temple on the mainland runs parallel celebrations. If you plan to attend, book accommodation at least 30 days in advance and arrive the day before.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Which temple has the Jyotirlinga — Omkareshwar or Mamleshwar?**
A: Both temples are Jyotirlingas in their own right and are part of the same Omkareshwar pilgrimage. The Omkareshwar temple (island) is the primary Jyotirlinga, and the Mamleshwar temple (mainland) is the secondary Jyotirlinga at the same site. Devotees visit both.

**Q: Can I visit both temples in one day?**
A: Yes. Most pilgrims visit both in a single day. Arrive early (before 8 am), do Mamleshwar darshan, cross to the island, do Omkareshwar darshan, complete the parikrama, and return for evening aarti.

**Q: Is there a fee for darshan?**
A: Darshan at both temples is free. There are optional paid darshan queues at Omkareshwar (special entry that bypasses the regular queue for a small donation). The free queue is always available and moves steadily.

**Q: Can I take a bath in the Narmada?**
A: Yes, and it is part of the ritual practice for many devotees. There are ghats near the Mamleshwar temple and along the island path. Bathing before darshan is considered auspicious.

**Q: How do I get from the mainland to the island?**
A: Two options: walk across the pedestrian bridge (10 minutes) or take the government ferry (15 minutes, frequent departures). The bridge is the more common route for pilgrims with mobility.

# Shegaon Samadhi Mandir: History, Architecture & Inside Guide

When Shri Gajanan Maharaj entered samadhi on September 8, 1910 — on the day of Rishi Panchami — the town of Shegaon built around him the same quiet simplicity he had lived. The structure that marks his resting place is the Samadhi Mandir: a domed white marble temple at the center of the Sansthan complex. Devotees enter for darshan of the paduka (footprints) and leave with a small laddu prasad, a practice unchanged for over a century. This guide explains the history of the mandir, its architecture, what darshan is like, and how to plan your visit.

## The 1910 samadhi

Shri Gajanan Maharaj passed into samadhi on Rishi Panchami, a day in the Hindu lunar calendar dedicated to honoring the rishis and the ancestors. The date is significant within the tradition not only because of its ritual associations but because devotees report that the saint had foretold the date to close associates. The samadhi itself was calm: the saint was seated in meditation when the transition occurred, and no announcement was made. The local community learned of it gradually and began gathering.

The site of the samadhi is a small structure that existed before 1910 as a simple shelter. In the years immediately following, devotees built a modest temple around it. Over subsequent decades, as the number of visitors grew, the Sansthan rebuilt and expanded the structure into the white marble mandir that stands today.

## Architecture of the mandir

The current Samadhi Mandir is a single-domed white marble structure with four entrances — one on each side — consistent with the temple-architecture convention of openness to all directions. The central focus is the paduka (footprints) of Shri Gajanan Maharaj installed within a raised enclosure. Above the paduka sits a silver chatri (umbrella-shaped canopy) that has been added over the years by devotees.

The mandir's simplicity is architectural intentionality. There is no elaborate sculptural program on the outer walls, no towering spire beyond the single central dome, and no separation between the space where devotees stand and the space where the paduka is. The absence of ornamentation is itself the statement: the saint wanted no monument, and the mandir that was built after him respects that.

The complex surrounding the mandir has expanded significantly. The Anand Sagar gardens (open 10 am to 5 pm, free entry) cover a large area behind the mandir and include a lake, walking paths, a children's area, and the cultural center. The main road leading to the mandir is lined with shops selling prasad, books, photographs, and offering accommodation booking counters.

## Inside the mandir: the darshan experience

Darshan at the Samadhi Mandir is a short, standing experience. Devotees enter through the main east-facing gate, join the queue, and walk in a slow file past the central paduka. The paduka is installed on a raised platform behind a silver railing. There is no prostration required, but many devotees place their hands on the railing and bow briefly.

The experience is deliberately brief — a minute or two for each devotee, depending on the crowd. During peak periods (Pragat Din, Samadhi Utsav, weekends), the queue extends outside the main gate and the darshan time is extended into the evening to accommodate the volume. On ordinary days, the morning darshan (starting around 5:30 am) and evening darshan (around 6 pm) are quick and personal.

A small laddu prasad is distributed after darshan. The laddu is made fresh at the Sansthan's kitchen. Visitors with dietary restrictions should note that it contains gram flour, ghee, and sugar — no preservatives, no artificial flavors.

## Anand Sagar: the mandir's garden and lake

The Anand Sagar complex is the landscaped campus adjacent to the mandir. The lake — the centerpiece — is fed by a well rather than the nearby Painganga River, and its water level is maintained year-round by the Sansthan. The gardens have walking paths, seating areas, a small zoo for children, and a light-and-sound show in the evening.

Anand Sagar is open from 10 am to 5 pm. Entry is free. The complex is most pleasant during the winter months (October to February) when the gardens are in bloom and the temperature is comfortable for walking.

## Rishi Panchami and Samadhi Utsav

Rishi Panchami falls on the fifth day of the waxing moon in the month of Bhadrapada (August–September). This is the anniversary of Shri Gajanan Maharaj's samadhi, and the Sansthan observes a ten-day utsav (festival) around this date. The central events are:

- **Paduka abhishek:** The paduka is bathed with milk, honey, and ghee on Rishi Panchami morning.
- **Granth parayan:** Organized reading of the Gajanan Vijay Granth over seven days.
- **Annadan:** Free meals served to thousands of devotees throughout the utsav period.
- **Night darshan:** The mandir remains open longer during the utsav; the night atmosphere is particularly peaceful.

Planning a visit around Rishi Panchami means booking accommodation well in advance — Shegaon's Bhakta Niwas fills up within weeks of the announcement of the utsav dates. See our [Rishi Panchami Samadhi Utsav guide](/rishi-panchami-shegaon-samadhi-utsav-guide) for detailed planning.

## Pilgrimage route: Shegaon and the nearby circuit

Shegaon sits at the junction of several important pilgrimage routes. Devotees coming from Nagpur, Akola, or Amaravati can make a single stop at Shegaon and continue to the Nashik-Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga or to the Pandharpur Vitthal Temple. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is the Samadhi Mandir the same as the main temple?**
A: The Samadhi Mandir is the specific structure that houses the paduka of Shri Gajanan Maharaj at the site of his samadhi. The main temple complex also includes the Gopal Krishna Mandir, the Annapurna Mandir, and several smaller shrines within the same campus. Most visitors go directly to the Samadhi Mandir as the primary destination.

**Q: Can I visit the samadhi at any time?**
A: Morning and evening darshan are available every day. The mandir has limited hours on certain festival days — check the Sansthan website or call the office before planning a visit on a festival date.

**Q: Is there an entry fee?**
A: No. Darshan at the Samadhi Mandir is free. Entry to Anand Sagar is also free. The only costs are accommodation, prasad items, and donations if you wish to contribute.

**Q: What is the difference between pragat din and samadhi utsav?**
A: Pragat Din (February 12) commemorates the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj began public darshan in 1878. Samadhi Utsav (around Rishi Panchami, August–September) commemorates his passing in 1910. Both are major festivals at Shegaon, but they mark different moments in the saint's life.

**Q: How do I get to the Samadhi Mandir from the railway station?**
A: Shegaon railway station is on the Nagpur–Bhusaval line and is about 1.5 km from the mandir. Shared rickshaws, local buses, and a Sansthan shuttle connect the station to the temple complex.

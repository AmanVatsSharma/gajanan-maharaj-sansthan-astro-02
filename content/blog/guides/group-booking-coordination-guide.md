# Group Booking Coordination Guide | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

When fifteen families decide to visit the Samadhi Mandir at Shegaon together, or a temple trust organises a fifty-person group for the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga, the booking process stops being a simple individual conversation and becomes a coordination exercise. Someone has to collect names, compile room requirements, negotiate shared costs, communicate with the Sansthan office, manage last-minute changes, and make sure everyone arrives on the same day with confirmed rooms. Group bookings are entirely welcome at all four Sansthan locations, and the booking desk is experienced in handling them — but the quality of the outcome depends heavily on how well the coordination is done on the devotee side. This guide covers the full group booking workflow: selecting a coordinator, compiling the member list, communicating with the Sansthan, assigning rooms, managing payments, and troubleshooting the common issues that arise when many people travel together.

## Choosing a group coordinator

Every group booking needs a single point of contact with the Sansthan office. This is the person who collects everyone's details, sends the booking request, tracks confirmations, handles changes, and distributes information to the group. The coordinator does not have to be the most senior member of the group, but they do need to be: detail-oriented (names, dates, ID numbers do not tolerate approximation), responsive (the Sansthan office may send follow-up questions), and patient (group bookings take longer to process than individual ones).

For temple trust groups, the coordinator is typically the trust secretary or a designated committee member. For family groups, it works well to assign this role to someone who is comfortable with phone calls and message management. For corporate devotional outings, the HR or admin team member handling the logistics should serve as the coordinator. Whichever approach you choose, make sure the entire group knows who the coordinator is and that all communications about the booking go through that single person.

## Compiling the member list

The Sansthan needs a complete list of every guest before they can allocate rooms. The minimum information required per guest is: full name, age, and a government-issued photo ID number. If you are booking as a group, compile this list in a simple spreadsheet or table before you even make the initial enquiry. A well-organised member list speeds up the booking process significantly — the Sansthan can confirm your request in a single response rather than going back and forth for missing details.

Here is the minimum data the Sansthan needs:

- Group name or trust name (if applicable)
- Total number of guests, broken down by adults, children, and seniors
- Check-in and check-out dates
- Room type preferences (number of each room type needed)
- Special requests (ground-floor rooms, vegetarian meals, wheelchair access, adjacent rooms)
- Primary contact name, phone number, and WhatsApp number

The [booking process guide](/blog/bhakta-niwas-booking-process) explains the standard information requirements in more detail. For groups of more than 25 people, it is helpful to also provide a brief note about the group's purpose — a temple trust yatra, a family gathering, a corporate devotional outing — as this helps the Sansthan staff allocate the right rooms and provide appropriate support during the stay.

## Communicating with the Sansthan office

Group booking requests should be sent as a single, well-organised message — not as a series of individual questions from multiple group members. The preferred channels are WhatsApp (+91 80531 90691) or the [booking form](/booking). If you are using WhatsApp, structure your message clearly:

1. State that you are requesting a group booking and give the group name or size.
2. Provide the check-in and check-out dates.
3. List the number and type of rooms needed.
4. Provide the total guest count with age breakdown.
5. Mention any special requests.

Sending this information in a single message — rather than splitting it across multiple replies — reduces the back-and-forth and speeds up the confirmation process. The [phone and WhatsApp booking best practices guide](/blog/phone-and-whatsapp-booking-best-practices) covers message formatting in detail.

## Room allocation strategy

The most common room allocation question for group coordinators is: how many of each room type do we need? The answer depends on the group's composition and your budget.

For groups of 10 to 15 people: A mix of 4-bed AC or Non-AC rooms works well. Most groups book two 4-bed rooms and one 2-bed room for the coordinator or trust secretary. This provides comfortable shared accommodation with a private room for one person.

For groups of 20 to 40 people: A combination of 4-bed and 3-bed rooms, with a few 2-bed rooms for seniors or group leaders. The [best accommodation for large groups guide](/blog/best-accommodation-for-large-groups) provides room allocation templates for groups of different sizes.

For groups of 50 or more people: The common hall dormitory is often the most practical option, supplemented by a few private rooms for group leaders. See our dedicated [50-plus devotees guide](/blog/group-booking-for-50-plus-devotees) for the full logistics of very large group bookings.

When allocating rooms, consider group dynamics carefully. Not every combination of guests works well in the same room. Older devotees should be paired with other older devotees if possible, families with young children should have rooms with access to bathrooms without long corridors, and guests with mobility issues should be on the ground floor.

## Managing payments

For group bookings, the payment arrangement depends on the size of the group and the nature of the group:

- **Family groups:** It is common for each family to pay for their own rooms individually. The coordinator collects the money and settles the bill with the Sansthan at check-in. This keeps things simple and avoids one person having to carry the entire group's cash.
- **Temple trust groups:** The trust typically pays a lump sum to the Sansthan, and individual devotees contribute to the trust fund. The coordinator provides the trust with a single invoice covering all rooms and meals, which the trust settles in advance or at check-in.
- **Corporate groups:** The corporate admin or HR team handles payment on behalf of all employees. The Sansthan can provide a single consolidated bill.

In all cases, confirm the payment method (cash, UPI, bank transfer) and the payment deadline before your arrival. Some locations require advance payment for groups of more than 10 people. Ask the booking desk about this when you confirm the reservation.

## Handling last-minute changes

The most common last-minute issue with group bookings is a change in headcount — someone cannot make it, or an additional person joins at the last minute. The best approach is to notify the Sansthan as soon as you know about the change, even if it is only a day or two before arrival. The booking desk will adjust the room allocation accordingly, though changes within 24 hours of check-in may not always be possible if the Bhakta Niwas is fully booked.

If your group size increases unexpectedly, check availability immediately. During regular seasons, additional rooms are usually available on short notice. During festival periods, availability is tight and last-minute additions may require staying at a nearby private hotel instead. The [last-minute booking options guide](/blog/last-minute-booking-options-guide) covers contingency plans when Bhakta Niwas rooms are unavailable.

## Group booking checklist

Use this checklist to make sure nothing is missed:

- Appoint a single coordinator with a phone and WhatsApp number.
- Compile a complete member list with names, ages, and ID details.
- Decide on check-in and check-out dates as a group.
- Determine the room type mix needed for your group composition.
- Send a single, organised booking request to the Sansthan via WhatsApp or the [booking form](/booking).
- Confirm the booking with written confirmation from the Sansthan.
- Distribute the confirmed details (dates, address, arrival instructions) to all group members.
- Arrange payments and confirm the payment method with the Sansthan.
- Share emergency contact information with all group members. The [emergency contact guide](/blog/emergency-contact-during-pilgrimage) lists the key numbers.
- Share the itinerary and logistics with the group at least one week before departure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Is there a minimum group size for a group booking?**

A: There is no formal minimum group size — any group of two or more people can request a group booking. However, the Sansthan's group booking process becomes most efficient at six or more people. For smaller groups of two to four people, an individual booking works just as well. The advantages of a formal group booking are most apparent at 10 people and above, where coordinated room allocation and consolidated billing save significant administrative time.

**Q: Can different families in the group stay in separate rooms?**

A: Yes. In fact, this is the standard arrangement for family groups. The group booking process simply means that all rooms are booked, confirmed, and managed through a single coordinator. Each family can have their own room or rooms, and the coordinator handles the collective communication with the Sansthan.

**Q: What happens if the Bhakta Niwas does not have enough rooms for the full group?**

A: If the Sansthan cannot accommodate the full group, the booking desk will inform you as soon as they know — usually at the time of initial confirmation or shortly before your arrival. They will offer the maximum available rooms at the Bhakta Niwas and suggest nearby private accommodation options for the remaining guests. Some groups split between Bhakta Niwas and a nearby hotel to accommodate larger numbers. Plan for this contingency when booking very large groups.

**Q: Do group bookings get priority during festival periods?**

A: Group bookings that are submitted well in advance (6 to 8 weeks before the festival) are given priority consideration. However, during major festivals like Pragat Din or Ashadhi Ekadashi, demand across all categories is extremely high, and even early group bookings may face availability constraints. The [festival rush booking strategies guide](/blog/festival-rush-booking-strategies) explains how to maximise your chances of securing rooms during peak periods.

**Q: Can the Sansthan arrange a special program for our group, such as a guided temple tour or a special aarti?**

A: Yes. The Sansthan is often able to arrange special programs for visiting groups, including guided tours of the Samadhi Mandir, special aarti arrangements, and spiritual discourses. Discuss your requirements with the coordinator at the time of booking. Advance notice (at least 2 to 3 weeks) is essential for arranging special programs.

**Q: Should I use the web form or WhatsApp for a group booking?**

A: Both channels work, but WhatsApp (+91 80531 90691) is generally faster for group bookings because it allows for a direct, real-time conversation with the booking desk. The web [booking form](/booking) is useful for submitting the initial details in a structured format, and many coordinators use both: the web form for the structured data, and WhatsApp for follow-up communication. See the [phone and WhatsApp booking best practices guide](/blog/phone-and-whatsapp-booking-best-practices) for formatting tips.

# Sansthan Four Location Room Booking Overview

Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan offers devotee rooms at four locations — Shegaon, Pandharpur, Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar — on one principle: the same honest tariffs (₹1,650 to ₹2,550 per night across five room rows), the same booking flow through the [booking page](/booking), the same office confirmation, and the same full-day stay cycle counted from your recorded entry. What differs is everything devotional: a Samadhi Mandir town in Vidarbha, a wari-hearted math on the Chandrabhaga, a Jyotirlinga island in the Narmada, and a Jyotirlinga at a hill source above Nashik. This overview puts the four side by side — stay by stay, tariff by tariff, purpose by purpose — so a family can choose its threshold in one reading.

This is the cluster's capstone: the wide view the individual guides build towards. Each centre has its own deep pages; this one's job is comparison, and its tables and links are laid out for exactly that.

## One Sansthan, four thresholds

A devotee who has stayed at one Sansthan centre arrives at any other already knowing the essentials — where the desk is, what the register asks, how the day-cycle runs, what a receipt looks like. That portability is deliberate: the network is one household with four addresses, and the rooms, the staff culture and the booking discipline travel with the devotee. The five room rows are common everywhere; the attached bathroom with hot water is standard in every row at every centre; and the requests that matter — ground floors, proximity, adjoining rooms — are heard in the same way at all four offices. What the tables below add is the local truth each address holds, because a pilgrimage chooses a town before it chooses a room.

The network's shape also has a history worth knowing in one paragraph. Shegaon is the source — the Samadhi Mandir town where the Sansthan's own story lives, and the centre from which the devotee-service culture spread to the other thresholds. Pandharpur, Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar extend the same household to the state's other devotional poles: the wari's destination, one Jyotirlinga in the river's embrace, another at the hill's source. A family that understands this shape understands the network's promise — that whichever devotion calls, the same honest room waits near it — and can plan a lifetime of yatras against one consistent standard of stay.

Reachability belongs in the same reading, because a threshold is only gentle if the journey to it is. Shegaon lives on the rail map, which is why it serves devotees from every corner of the state; Pandharpur and Trimbakeshwar sit within comfortable road distance of their regions' cities, the Nashik side especially; Omkareshwar draws its devotees across the Indore belt and the northern routes. None of these journeys is arduous by pilgrimage standards, but each rewards a family that plans the last leg as carefully as the room — an evening arrival, a rested night, and the first darshan taken in the morning's strength rather than the journey's exhaustion. The route guides across this site hold each town's approach in detail; the overview's counsel is only the principle: let the room be the journey's reward, not its epilogue.

## Two-centre and three-centre journeys

Because the booking discipline and room rows are common, multi-centre yatras are logistically simple even when they are devotionally ambitious. The classic patterns are three: Shegaon plus Trimbakeshwar, for a Samadhi Mandir darshan folded into a Nashik-side pooja; Pandharpur plus Shegaon, the two devotional hearts of Maharashtra in one rail arc; and the Jyotirlinga pair, Omkareshwar and Trimbakeshwar, for families collecting the darshan rather than the distance. The planning rules transfer from this cluster's other pages — book each centre as its own request with its own reference, allow rest days between centres, and let the month-wise calendar keep both windows honest. Families assembling such circuits will find company in the network's multi-location guides, and comparison-minded devotees can take the long view in the [Bhakta Niwas across locations comparison](/blog/bhakta-niwas-across-locations-comparison).

The cost of a two-centre journey is easier to plan than most families expect, precisely because the tariffs are common. A two-night stay at each of two centres in the 4 Bed Non-AC row totals ₹9,000 across the whole pilgrimage — four nights, four beds, two thresholds — and the same journey in the 4 Bed AC row totals ₹10,200. The arithmetic holds because the network holds: no centre prices itself above another, no season surcharge appears because a town is famous, and the family's budget question reduces to nights and rows rather than locations. That constancy is the quiet generosity of a sansthan's hospitality, and families who build multi-centre habits around it return to the network for decades.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are the rooms and tariffs the same at all four locations?

Yes — all five room rows are network-wide, ₹1,650 per night for the 2 Bed AC through ₹2,550 for the 4 Bed AC, with attached bathrooms and hot water standard in every row at every centre.

### Is one booking valid across centres?

No — each centre is its own request, its own reference and its own confirmation, which keeps every office's chart accurate for the town it serves.

### Which centre should a first-time family choose?

Shegaon is the gentlest first threshold — rail-side arrival, the complex's self-contained rhythm, and the network's fullest library of guides around it.

### Can we book two centres for one trip?

Yes — send a separate request for each centre with its own dates, and allow a rest day between towns; the multi-centre patterns above are well travelled.

### Where do the four centres' differences actually matter?

In terrain and festival calendars — Omkareshwar's ghats and Trimbakeshwar's gradients ask for thoughtful room requests, and each town's peaks move its booking window, as the month-wise calendar shows.

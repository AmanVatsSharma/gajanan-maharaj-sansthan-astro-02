# Shegaon And Ajanta Ellora Combined Trip | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

The combination of a spiritual pilgrimage to Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan at Shegaon with a visit to the Ajanta and Ellora caves near Aurangabad is one of the most culturally rich itineraries available in central India. Shegaon offers the devotional depth of the Samadhi Mandir and the Anand Sagar complex — a living, active spiritual tradition that continues to draw devotees every day. The Ajanta and Ellora caves, just 200 kilometres from Shegaon, offer a completely different but equally profound experience: ancient Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain rock-cut monuments that span more than a thousand years of Indian art and architecture. Together, the two destinations create a yatra that engages both the devotional and the cultural dimensions of Indian heritage — a combination that appeals not only to regular pilgrims but also to families, students, history enthusiasts, and NRI devotees who want to experience both the living spiritual tradition of Shri Gajanan Maharaj and the ancient artistic heritage of the Deccan. This guide provides a complete itinerary for combining the Shegaon pilgrimage with the Ajanta and Ellora cave visits, with transport options, accommodation recommendations, timing advice, and practical tips for each destination.

## The two destinations compared

**Shegaon (Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan):** A living spiritual centre with an active temple, daily darshans, the Mahaprasad canteen, and the Anand Sagar complex. The experience is devotional, communal, and ongoing — the temple is not a monument but a functioning place of worship that draws thousands of devotees every day.

**Ajanta and Ellora Caves (near Aurangabad):** Ancient rock-cut monuments carved into the basalt cliffs of the Sahyadri hills. Ajanta (approximately 30 caves, 2nd century BCE to 6th century CE) is famous for its Buddhist murals and sculptures. Ellora (approximately 34 caves, 5th to 10th century CE) includes Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain monuments, including the remarkable Kailasa temple — a monolithic structure carved from a single rock. Both sites are UNESCO World Heritage sites and are among the most important cultural monuments in India.

The combination works because the two experiences complement each other. The devotional intensity of Shegaon is balanced by the contemplative awe of the cave complexes, and the practical logistics are straightforward — both destinations are accessible from the same transport hub (Aurangabad) and can be visited in a single extended itinerary.

## The 5-day combined itinerary

### Day 1: Arrival at Shegaon

**Travel to Shegaon:** From Mumbai, take the 12139 Sewagram Express overnight (departs 8:55 PM, arrives 8:00 AM). From Pune, take an overnight bus or drive. From Nagpur, the 240 km drive takes 4 to 5 hours. The [complete transport guide](/blog/complete-transport-guide-for-devotees) covers all transport options.

**Accommodation:** Check in at the Shegaon Bhakta Niwas or a nearby hotel. Book through +91 80531 90691 or the [booking form](/booking).

**Darshan:** Attend morning darshan at the Samadhi Mandir (8:00 AM), visit Anand Sagar (10:00 AM to 12:00 PM), lunch at the Mahaprasad canteen, afternoon rest, evening darshan and aarti (5:00 PM to 8:00 PM). The [darshan timing guide](/blog/shegaon-darshan-timing-guide) covers the complete schedule.

**Overnight:** Shegaon Bhakta Niwas.

### Day 2: Shegaon full day, depart for Aurangabad

**Morning:** Wake up for the 5:00 AM Mangala Aarti. Spend the morning at the temple and Anand Sagar. Breakfast at the Mahaprasad canteen.

**Mid-morning:** Visit the Anandanand museum for a deeper understanding of Shri Gajanan Maharaj's life and teachings.

**Lunch (12:30 PM):** Lunch at the Mahaprasad canteen.

**Departure (1:30 PM):** Check out of the Bhakta Niwas and start the drive to Aurangabad. The 200-kilometre journey takes approximately 4 hours via Jalna. The road passes through the Ajanta hill range, and the landscape becomes more hilly as you approach Aurangabad.

**Arrival at Aurangabad (5:30 PM):** Check in at a hotel in Aurangabad. The city has a wide range of hotels from budget (INR 800–1,500) to luxury (INR 3,000–8,000). For a pilgrimage trip, a mid-range hotel (INR 1,500–3,000) near the CBS (Central Bus Stand) or the railway station is most convenient.

**Evening:** Rest and dinner at the hotel. Aurangabad has excellent vegetarian restaurants if you want a change from the Mahaprasad canteen food.

**Overnight:** Aurangabad hotel.

### Day 3: Ajanta Caves

**Travel to Ajanta:** The Ajanta caves are approximately 100 km from Aurangabad, and the drive takes 2 to 2.5 hours. Hire a taxi for the day from Aurangabad (INR 1,500–2,500 for the round trip including the driver's waiting time). Start early — leave Aurangabad by 7:00 AM to arrive at Ajanta by 9:30 AM, before the crowds build up.

**Ajanta Caves visit (9:30 AM to 2:00 PM):** The Ajanta complex has approximately 30 caves, carved into a horseshoe-shaped cliff overlooking the Waghora River. The caves are numbered 1 to 29 and are grouped into two phases: the earlier Hinayana phase (2nd century BCE) and the later Mahayana phase (5th to 6th century CE). Cave 1 is the most famous — it contains the celebrated mural of the Bodhisattva Padmapani, one of the most revered paintings in Indian art history. Cave 2 has equally impressive murals. Cave 26 is a chaitya (prayer hall) with a large carved Buddha image. Allow at least 3 to 4 hours for a thorough visit.

**Tips for Ajanta:**
- Hire a government-approved guide at the entrance (INR 200–300). The guide's explanations bring the caves to life and are well worth the cost.
- Wear comfortable shoes — the caves involve walking up and down stone steps.
- Carry water and sun protection — the caves are open to the sun, and the cliff-top walk is exposed.
- Photography is allowed in most caves (no flash).

**Lunch (2:00 PM):** Lunch at the MTDC restaurant near the Ajanta caves or at a restaurant on the way back to Aurangabad.

**Return to Aurangabad (3:00 PM):** Drive back to Aurangabad. Rest at the hotel in the afternoon.

**Evening:** Visit the Bibi Ka Maqbara (the Taj of the Deccan) if you have time. The Bibi Ka Maqbara is a Mughal mausoleum built by Aurangzeb's son and is architecturally similar to the Taj Mahal. It is approximately 3 km from the city centre and can be visited in 1 to 2 hours.

**Overnight:** Aurangabad hotel.

### Day 4: Ellora Caves, depart for Shegaon

**Travel to Ellora:** The Ellora caves are approximately 30 km from Aurangabad, and the drive takes 45 minutes to 1 hour. Hire a taxi for the day or take an MSRTC bus from Aurangabad CBS to Ellora. Leave Aurangabad by 8:00 AM.

**Ellora Caves visit (9:00 AM to 1:00 PM):** The Ellora complex has approximately 34 caves, representing three religious traditions: Buddhist (caves 1–12), Hindu (caves 13–29), and Jain (caves 30–34). The most remarkable structure is Cave 16 — the Kailasa temple, a monolithic structure carved from a single rock downward (the excavators started from the top and worked their way down). The Kailasa temple is one of the most astonishing architectural achievements in human history — a full-sized Hindu temple with towers, halls, and sculptures, carved from a single rock face over several decades. Cave 29 (Dumar Lena) is another impressive Hindu cave with a large Shiva lingam. The Buddhist caves (particularly Cave 10, the Vishvakarma cave with its massive carved Buddha) are equally impressive.

**Tips for Ellora:**
- Hire a government-approved guide (INR 200–300).
- The Kailasa temple is the highlight — spend at least 45 minutes there.
- Wear comfortable shoes — the Ellora complex is larger than Ajanta and involves more walking.
- Photography is allowed (no flash).

**Lunch (1:00 PM):** Lunch at a restaurant near Ellora or on the way back to Aurangabad.

**Return to Aurangabad (2:00 PM):** Collect luggage from the hotel and start the drive to Shegaon.

**Drive to Shegaon (200 km, 4 hours):** The 200-kilometre drive from Aurangabad to Shegaon takes approximately 4 hours via Jalna and Khamgaon. Arrive at Shegaon by approximately 6:00 PM.

**Evening darshan (6:00 PM):** Attend the evening darshan and aarti at the Samadhi Mandir. The return to Shegaon after the cave visits creates a powerful contrast — from the ancient stone carvings of Ellora to the living devotional tradition of the Samadhi Mandir.

**Overnight:** Shegaon Bhakta Niwas.

### Day 5: Shegaon return darshan and departure

**Morning:** Wake up for the 5:00 AM Mangala Aarti. Spend the morning at the temple and Anand Sagar.

**Breakfast:** Mahaprasad canteen.

**Departure:** Check out of the Bhakta Niwas by 10:00 AM. Board the 12139 Sewagram Express from Shegaon at 8:55 PM (arrives Mumbai CST at 7:00 AM the next morning). Alternatively, take an overnight bus back to Mumbai.

## Alternative itineraries

**3-day itinerary (Shegaon + Ellora only):** If you have less time, skip Ajanta and visit only Ellora. The Shegaon-Ellora-Shegaon itinerary fits into 3 days: Day 1 at Shegaon, Day 2 at Ellora and Aurangabad, Day 3 return to Shegaon and departure.

**6-day itinerary (Shegaon + Ajanta + Ellora + Omkareshwar):** If you have more time, add Omkareshwar to the itinerary after Shegaon. The route would be: Shegaon (Days 1–2), Aurangabad and Ajanta/Ellora (Days 3–4), Omkareshwar (Days 5–6). This creates a 6-day itinerary that covers the Samadhi Mandir, the Buddhist and Hindu cave monuments, and the Narmada Jyotirlinga.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: How many days do I need for the Ajanta and Ellora caves?**

A: One full day for each cave complex is the minimum comfortable duration. Ajanta requires 4 to 5 hours for a thorough visit, and Ellora requires 3 to 4 hours. If you have only one day, Ellora is more compact and can be visited more quickly. Ajanta is more spectacular for the murals.

**Q: Is the Ajanta-Ellora combined trip suitable for children?**

A: Yes, but it requires careful planning. The cave visits involve a lot of walking and climbing stone steps, which can be tiring for young children. Plan the cave visits for the morning when energy levels are high, and include rest breaks. The Ellora caves are more manageable for children than Ajanta, which involves a longer walk along the cliff.

**Q: Should I hire a guide at Ajanta and Ellora?**

A: Yes. Government-approved guides are available at both sites for INR 200–300. The guides are knowledgeable and bring the history and art to life in a way that self-guided visits cannot match. The cost is minimal compared to the value of the experience.

**Q: Can I visit both cave complexes in one day?**

A: It is possible but rushed. Ajanta is 100 km from Aurangabad (2 to 2.5 hours each way), and Ellora is 30 km from Aurangabad (45 minutes each way). Visiting both in one day would require leaving Aurangabad at 6:00 AM, spending 3 hours at Ajanta, returning to Aurangabad for lunch, and then spending 2 to 3 hours at Ellora in the afternoon. This works for devotees who are short on time but does not allow for a relaxed, contemplative experience at either site.

**Q: Is the road from Shegaon to Aurangabad safe?**

A: Yes, the road via Jalna on NH 753A and NH 753F is well-maintained and safe. The stretch through the Ajanta hills is scenic but requires careful driving in the monsoon season when the roads can be slippery.

**Q: What is the best time of year to visit the caves?**

A: October to February is ideal — the weather is cool and comfortable for the outdoor walks at the cave complexes. The monsoon season (July to September) makes the approach roads slippery and the caves humid. Summer (March to June) is hot but manageable if you visit early in the morning.

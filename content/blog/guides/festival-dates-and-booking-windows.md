# Festival Dates And Booking Windows | Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan

The rhythm of pilgrimage at Shri Gajanan Maharaj Sansthan is set by its annual festival calendar. Each major festival draws devotees from across Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and beyond, and each one creates its own accommodation pressure, transport demand, and darshan crowd pattern. Knowing when these festivals fall — and when to book accommodation for them — is one of the most practical pieces of knowledge a regular pilgrim can have. A devotee who understands Pragat Din's booking timeline can secure a comfortable room weeks before someone who waits until the last minute. Pragat Din in February is the Sansthan's flagship event, drawing tens of thousands of devotees to Shegaon. Ashadhi Ekadashi in July transforms Pandharpur into the centre of the Warkari universe. Mahashivratri lights up Trimbakeshwar in a way that no other night of the year does. Each festival has a specific booking window, and arriving inside that window — rather than scrambling a week before — is the difference between a confirmed room and a last-minute accommodation crisis. This guide provides the festival calendar, the recommended booking timelines for each event, and the specific planning considerations that apply to each one.

## What is a booking window and why does it matter

The booking window is the period during which the Sansthan's Bhakta Niwas rooms are most likely to be available. The Bhakta Niwas at Shegaon has a limited number of rooms — typically between 40 and 60 depending on the wing and room type. During Pragat Din, these rooms are booked by devotees who have been planning their visit for months. By the time Pragat Din is 6 weeks away, the booking desk has already confirmed most of the rooms, and new requests are placed on a waitlist.

The same principle applies at Pandharpur during Ashadhi Ekadashi. The Pandharpur Bhakta Niwas is smaller than Shegaon's and is primarily allocated to Dindi participants. Individual devotees who book within 8 weeks of Ashadhi Ekadashi are unlikely to get a room at the Sansthan property and will need to book a private hotel in Solapur or Pandharpur town.

The booking windows in this guide are based on the Sansthan's actual booking patterns over the past several years. They represent the point at which room availability begins to decline significantly. Booking before the window opens is fine — you can submit a request early and confirm closer to the date. Booking after the window closes is risky — you may be placed on a waitlist with no guarantee of a room.

## The major festival calendar

### Pragat Din (Shegaon)

Pragat Din is the most important annual festival at the Sansthan. It commemorates the divine appearance of Shri Gajanan Maharaj to his devotees in physical form, and it draws the largest annual gathering to Shegaon. The festival typically falls in February (on the specific date specified by the Sansthan for that year).

**What to expect:** The Samadhi Mandir is open for extended darshan hours, special aartis are performed throughout the day, and the Anand Sagar complex hosts cultural programs, devotional music, and community meals. The Bhakta Niwas operates at full capacity, and the Shegaon town fills with devotees arriving by train, bus, and private vehicle from across Maharashtra and beyond.

**Booking window:** 10 to 12 weeks before the festival date. By 6 weeks before Pragat Din, most rooms at the Bhakta Niwas are fully allocated. The common hall dormitory is particularly in demand and is typically fully booked by the 8-week mark.

**Booking strategy:** Submit your request as soon as the booking window opens. Provide a complete member list, room requirements, and indicate your willingness to pay an advance deposit. If you are visiting with a group, coordinate a single group booking request through one coordinator. The [festival rush booking strategies guide](/blog/festival-rush-booking-strategies) covers Pragat Din booking tactics in detail.

### Samadhi Utsav (Shegaon)

The Samadhi Utsav commemorates the day Shri Gajanan Maharaj entered samadhi, and it is observed with a deep sense of reverence across the Sansthan. The festival typically falls in August or September (the exact date depends on the Hindu calendar).

**What to expect:** Special darshan arrangements, extended temple hours, devotional discourses, and community meals. The atmosphere at Samadhi Utsav is more contemplative than Pragat Din, but the accommodation demand is still significant.

**Booking window:** 8 to 10 weeks before the festival date. Samadhi Utsav is slightly less crowded than Pragat Din, which means rooms are somewhat easier to secure, but the booking window is still narrow.

### Ashadhi Ekadashi (Pandharpur)

Ashadhi Ekadashi is the most significant festival in the Warkari calendar, and it transforms Pandharpur into one of the largest devotional gatherings in India. The festival falls in June or July, on the eleventh day of the waxing moon in the Hindu month of Ashadha.

**What to expect:** Hundreds of thousands of Warkaris walk from Alandi and Dehu to Pandharpur over several days, carrying the paduka (footprints) of saint-poets Dnyaneshwar and Tukaram. The Bhima river ghats are filled with pilgrims, and the Lord Vitthal temple has continuous darshan throughout the day and night. The atmosphere is electric — devotional singing, clapping, and community participation on a scale that is difficult to experience at any other time of year. Our [Ashadhi Ekadashi guide](/blog/ashadhi-ekadashi-pandharpur-wari-guide) covers the festival in detail.

**Booking window:** 12 to 16 weeks before the festival date. Ashadhi Ekadashi is the most challenging festival to book accommodation for. The Sansthan's Pandharpur Bhakta Niwas is primarily allocated to Dindi participants, and individual devotees should book at the earliest possible opportunity. Consider booking a backup hotel in Solapur or Pandharpur town as well.

**Booking strategy:** Submit your request as soon as you know your dates — ideally 4 months before the festival. Provide a complete booking request with all details, and be flexible on room type. If you are attending as part of a Dindi group, coordinate through your Dindi organisers who may have pre-arranged accommodation.

### Kartik Ekadashi (Pandharpur)

Kartik Ekadashi, also known as Dev Deepavali, falls in October or November and is the second major festival at Pandharpur after Ashadhi Ekadashi. It marks the end of the Chaturmas period and is celebrated with special darshan at the Vitthal temple, Bhima river aarti, and lamps lit on the ghats.

**What to expect:** A large but manageable crowd. The atmosphere is more contemplative than Ashadhi Ekadashi, and the darshan queues are shorter. The Kartik Ekadashi festival is well-suited for families and senior devotees who find the Ashadhi Ekadashi crowds overwhelming. Our [Kartik Ekadashi guide](/blog/kartik-ekadashi-pandharpur-darshan-guide) covers the festival schedule and darshan planning.

**Booking window:** 6 to 8 weeks before the festival date. Kartik Ekadashi is less crowded than Ashadhi Ekadashi, and accommodation is more readily available.

### Mahashivratri (Trimbakeshwar)

Mahashivratri is the most important festival at Trimbakeshwar, the Jyotirlinga temple at the source of the Godavari. It falls in February or March, on the fourteenth day of the dark half of the Hindu month of Phalguna.

**What to expect:** The temple is open throughout the night, with continuous aarti and darshan. Devotees gather in large numbers, and the Brahmagiri hill and the Godavari spring are the focus of all-night devotional activity. Many devotees combine a ritual dip in the Godavari with the temple darshan, though the water remains cold even in late February.

**Booking window:** 6 to 8 weeks before the festival date. Trimbakeshwar Bhakta Niwas is small, and Mahashivratri rooms fill up quickly. Book early and consider Nashik as a backup accommodation option.

### Other notable dates

**Gudi Padwa (March-April):** The Marathi New Year marks the first day of the Chaitra month and is observed at Shegaon with special morning programs and community gatherings. Accommodation demand during Gudi Padwa is moderate — the festival is celebrated locally rather than drawing large outside crowds. A 4 to 6 week advance booking is sufficient for most room types.

**Ganesh Chaturthi (August-September):** A ten-day celebration across Maharashtra, Ganesh Chaturthi brings special programs to the Sansthan, including Ganesh puja at the Bhakta Niwas and community meals. Demand is moderate, and the 4 to 6 week booking window is usually enough.

**Diwali (October-November):** India's festival of lights creates peak travel demand across the country, and the Sansthan locations are no exception. Families often combine Diwali celebrations with a temple visit, and all Bhakta Niwas properties see high occupancy during Diwali week. Book at least 4 to 6 weeks in advance for Diwali, and consider that train and flight availability is also very tight during this period.

**Kartik Ekadashi at Trimbakeshwar (October-November):** A smaller but meaningful festival at the Jyotirlinga temple, coinciding with Dev Deepavali when lamps are lit across the Godavari ghats. The 4 to 6 week booking window applies here as well.

**Shravan month (July-August):** The monsoon month of Shravan is a spiritually significant period across Maharashtra, with increased pilgrim traffic at all Sansthan locations. Every Monday during Shravan is considered auspicious, and the temples see larger than normal crowds. No specific festival booking window applies, but accommodation can fill up faster than usual during Shravan weekends.

## Planning around the Hindu lunar calendar

One of the challenges with festival planning is that Hindu festivals follow a lunar calendar, and their Gregorian dates shift by 10 to 15 days each year. Pragat Din falls in February every year, but the exact date changes. Ashadhi Ekadashi shifts between June and July. Mahashivratri can fall in February or March.

To avoid confusion:

- **Keep a running Hindu calendar** on your phone or at your home. Many apps and websites provide accurate festival date predictions based on the Panchang.
- **Confirm dates with the Sansthan** as soon as they are announced. The booking desk at +91 80531 90691 can provide the confirmed dates for the current year.
- **Book flexible transport** when possible. Train tickets can be cancelled and rebooked (with a small cancellation fee), giving you some flexibility if festival dates shift slightly.

## How to check festival dates for the current year

The Hindu calendar is lunar, and festival dates shift by 10 to 15 days each year on the Gregorian calendar. To confirm the exact dates for the current year:

1. **Contact the Sansthan office:** The booking desk at +91 80531 90691 can provide the current year's festival calendar.
2. **Check the Sansthan website:** Festival announcements and date confirmations are published on the website in the months leading up to each festival.
3. **Follow Sansthan social media channels:** Official announcements about Pragat Din and other major festivals are typically shared through the Sansthan's communication channels well in advance.

## Combining festival visits with multi-location yatras

Many devotees plan their multi-location yatras around festival dates, visiting two or more Sansthan locations during the same trip. This approach works well when the festivals fall in different months (e.g., Pragat Din at Shegaon in February and Kartik Ekadashi at Pandharpur in October), but it requires careful planning when multiple festivals fall in the same season.

The key principle is to book each location's accommodation independently, using the specific booking window for that festival. Do not assume that booking one location's festival accommodation will help you secure another location's rooms — each property operates its own booking system with its own capacity and demand.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Are festival dates announced in advance?**

A: Yes. The Sansthan announces major festival dates several months in advance, typically 4 to 6 months before Pragat Din and other major events. The exact dates depend on the Hindu lunar calendar and are confirmed by the Sansthan's calendar committee. Contact the [booking desk](/booking) or the [contact page](/contact) for the current year's festival dates.

**Q: Can I attend Pragat Din without a Bhakta Niwas booking?**

A: Yes. Pragat Din is a public festival, and devotees can attend the darshan, aartis, and programs without staying at the Bhakta Niwas. Many devotees stay in nearby Khamgaon or Buldhana and commute to Shegaon for the festival day. The darshan at the Samadhi Mandir is open to all visitors regardless of accommodation status.

**Q: Which festival is the most crowded?**

A: Pragat Din at Shegaon and Ashadhi Ekadashi at Pandharpur are the two most crowded events. Pragat Din draws the largest single-day crowd to Shegaon, and Ashadhi Ekadashi draws the largest gathering to Pandharpur. If you prefer a more contemplative festival experience, Kartik Ekadashi at Pandharpur or Mahashivratri at Trimbakeshwar are excellent alternatives with smaller crowds.

**Q: Should I avoid visiting during festival periods if I dislike crowds?**

A: Not necessarily. Each festival has a different crowd character. Pragat Din at Shegaon is well-organised with crowd management, and the darshan queues are managed efficiently. Ashadhi Ekadashi at Pandharpur is genuinely crowded and may not be suitable if you are uncomfortable with large gatherings. Kartik Ekadashi at Pandharpur offers the same spiritual significance with significantly fewer people. Choose the festival that matches your comfort level with crowds.

**Q: Can I book festival accommodation through the website form?**

A: Yes. The [booking form](/booking) on this website accepts festival-period booking requests. However, for the highest-demand festivals (Pragat Din, Ashadhi Ekadashi), WhatsApp (+91 80531 90691) is often faster because it allows for a direct, real-time conversation with the booking desk. Many devotees use both: the web form for the structured data and WhatsApp for follow-up communication.

**Q: Is there a way to get notified when festival bookings open?**

A: Yes. Contact the Sansthan booking desk at +91 80531 90691 and ask to be notified when the booking window for your preferred festival opens. The booking desk maintains a list of interested devotees for each festival and sends notifications when bookings become available. This is the most reliable way to ensure you do not miss the booking window.
